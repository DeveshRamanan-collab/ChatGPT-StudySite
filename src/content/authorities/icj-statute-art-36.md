title: ICJ Statute art 36
kind: treaty
text: |-
  Outlines the ways in which a state can express consent to the Court's jurisdiction: (1) an explicit agreement
  (compromis) submitting a particular dispute; (2) becoming party to a treaty specifying that disputes can be brought
  before the Court; (3) a declaration under art 36(2) accepting the Court's jurisdiction over future international
  legal disputes with another state accepting the same obligation (the "optional clause"); and, with these, the
  doctrine of forum prorogatum. Art 36(6): the Court has competence to rule on its own jurisdiction. (As summarised on
  the Week 8 slides; the materials do not reproduce the article's exact wording.)
gloss: "The ICJ has no compulsory jurisdiction: art 36 sets out how states consent, and the Court decides disputes about its own jurisdiction."
