text: |
  Issues of admissibility. Having regard to paragraph 10 of the Preamble and article 1, the Court shall determine
  that a case is inadmissible where: (a) the case is being investigated or prosecuted by a State which has jurisdiction
  over it, unless the State is unwilling or unable genuinely to carry out the investigation or prosecution; (b) the case
  has been investigated by a State which has jurisdiction over it and the State has decided not to prosecute the person
  concerned, unless the decision resulted from the unwillingness or inability of the State genuinely to prosecute; (c)
  the person concerned has already been tried for conduct which is the subject of the complaint, and a trial by the
  Court is not permitted under article 20, paragraph 3; (d) the case is not of sufficient gravity to justify further
  action by the Court. Unwillingness: having regard to the principles of due process recognized by international law —
  proceedings or a national decision for the purpose of shielding the person from criminal responsibility; unjustified
  delay inconsistent with an intent to bring the person to justice; proceedings not conducted independently or
  impartially, in a manner inconsistent with that intent. Inability: whether, due to a total or substantial collapse or
  unavailability of its national judicial system, the State is unable to obtain the accused or the necessary evidence
  and testimony or otherwise unable to carry out its proceedings. The ICC is "complementary to national criminal
  jurisdictions" (art 1).
