plain: If a plaintiff chose to get too drunk or drugged to take proper care, no damages unless the harm was likely anyway; even then, contributory negligence is presumed and at least 25% comes off.
formal: CLA s 50 applies where the person was intoxicated to the extent that their capacity to exercise reasonable care and skill was impaired (s 50(1)). No damages unless the harm was likely even without intoxication (s 50(2)); then contributory negligence is presumed unless the intoxication did not contribute at all (s 50(3)), cutting damages by 25% or more (s 50(4)). Not if not self-induced (s 50(5)).
terms:
  - term: Intoxicated (s 48)
    gloss: Under the influence of alcohol or a drug, whether or not taken for a medicinal purpose and whether or not lawfully taken.
  - term: Self-induced
    gloss: Section 50 does not apply where the court is satisfied the intoxication was not self-induced (s 50(5)).
  - term: Presumption of contributory negligence
    gloss: Under s 50(3), once the harm was likely anyway, the intoxicated person is presumed contributorily negligent unless the intoxication did not contribute in any way.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Any alcohol in the plaintiff's system triggers s 50.
    tempting: The section is headed "No recovery where person intoxicated".
    correction: Section 50(1) requires intoxication to the extent that the person's capacity to exercise reasonable care and skill was impaired.
  - claim: Under s 50 an intoxicated plaintiff always recovers, less 25%.
    tempting: The 25% reduction is the best-known part of the section.
    correction: "First comes the s 50(2) gate: no damages unless the harm was likely to have occurred even without intoxication. Only then is contributory negligence presumed, with at least 25% off."
  - claim: The 25% reduction applies to contributory negligence generally.
    tempting: 25% is the figure most remembered from this topic.
    correction: The 25% floor belongs only to the s 50(4) intoxication presumption. Ordinary contributory negligence is reduced by what is just and equitable.
socratic:
  - ask: Leo, drunk by choice so that his capacity for care is impaired, trips on a negligently unlit step. What is the first question under s 50?
    hint: s 50(2).
    answer: Whether the injury is likely to have occurred even if he had not been intoxicated. If not, no damages are awarded.
  - ask: The court finds he would likely have tripped anyway. What follows?
    hint: s 50(3).
    answer: He is presumed contributorily negligent unless the court is satisfied his intoxication did not contribute in any way to the cause of the injury.
  - ask: If the presumption applies, how much comes off?
    hint: s 50(4).
    answer: 25%, or a greater percentage the court determines to be appropriate in the circumstances of the case.
  - ask: His drink was spiked without his knowledge. Does s 50 apply?
    hint: s 50(5).
    answer: "No: the section does not apply where the court is satisfied that the intoxication was not self-induced."
  - ask: How does this box differ from the volenti box on intoxicated drivers?
    hint: Whose intoxication?
    answer: Section 50 concerns the injured plaintiff's own intoxication (contributory negligence). The volenti box concerns a passenger relying on an intoxicated driver.

what-if:
  - change: The court finds the plaintiff would not have been hurt had he been sober.
    effect: Section 50(2) is not satisfied, so no damages are awarded at all.
  - change: The court is satisfied the intoxication did not contribute in any way to the cause of the injury.
    effect: No presumption under s 50(3). Any reduction then depends on ordinary contributory negligence, on what is just and equitable.
  - change: The plaintiff had been drinking, but her capacity to take care was not impaired.
    effect: "Section 50 does not apply: s 50(1) requires intoxication to the extent that capacity to exercise reasonable care and skill was impaired."
