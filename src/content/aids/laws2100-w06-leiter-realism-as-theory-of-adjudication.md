plain: Leiter argues that legal realism is not a rival theory of what law is, but a descriptive theory of why judges decide as they do, so it can sit alongside positivism.
concrete: "Realists noticed that decisions fall into patterns by situation type: cases about one industry, say mining, decided one way; fishing cases another, whatever doctrine was cited. If rules do not guide judges to particular results, they cannot explain decisions; the factual patterns can, and they allow prediction. That is a claim about adjudication, not about what counts as law."
