plain: If the plaintiff and defendant were committing a crime together when the plaintiff was hurt, the defendant may escape liability, but only where the court cannot sensibly set a standard of care between them.
formal: Where the plaintiff was injured and plaintiff and defendant were involved in the criminal enterprise, courts have often found it not possible or feasible to determine an appropriate standard of care, so a defence of illegality is available (eg Gala v Preston). It is not available where, on the facts, the court is able to determine the relevant standard of care (eg Progress & Properties Ltd v Craft).
terms:
  - term: Criminal enterprise
    gloss: "A crime in which plaintiff and defendant were both involved when the plaintiff was injured: the setting for the illegality defence."
  - term: Standard of care (in illegality)
    gloss: The level of care owed. Illegality turns on whether, on the facts, the court can determine an appropriate standard between the participants.
