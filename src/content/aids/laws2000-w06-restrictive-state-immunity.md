plain: Under the restrictive approach a state is immune for sovereign acts — war, legislation, policing — but not for ordinary commercial dealings, judged by what the deal is, not why the state made it.
concrete: "A Cuban state-owned shipping company agreed to deliver sugar to Chile. After a coup in Chile, Cuba ordered the ships diverted for political reasons. Sued in England, Cuba claimed immunity: the diversion served a foreign-policy objective. The House of Lords rejected the claim (Playa Largo, 1983). Delivering sugar under a sale contract was commercial in nature — jure gestionis — and the political motive for breaking it did not make it a sovereign act."
formal: "Acts jure imperii are immune; acts jure gestionis are not (Empire of Iran (1963); Trendex (1977)). 2004 Convention art 10: a state in a commercial transaction with a foreign natural or juridical person \"cannot invoke immunity\" in proceedings arising from it, save state-to-state transactions or express agreement. Art 2(2): look \"primarily to the nature\" of the transaction; purpose only if the parties agreed. Arts 11–17 and 12 add further exceptions."

terms:
  - term: acta jure imperii
    gloss: Acts of a sovereign nature — foreign and military affairs, legislation, police power, administration of justice. Immunity applies.
  - term: acta jure gestionis
    gloss: Acts of a non-sovereign nature, such as commercial activities. Immunity does not apply.
  - term: commercial transaction (art 2(1)(c))
    gloss: Sale of goods or supply of services; loans and other financial transactions, including guarantees; other commercial, industrial, trading or professional dealings — not employment contracts. Profit is irrelevant.
  - term: nature test (art 2(2))
    gloss: Classify a transaction by what it is, not its policy purpose; purpose counts only if the parties so agreed.
misconceptions:
  - claim: A transaction made for an official purpose is a sovereign act and immune.
    tempting: States always act for public ends, and the purpose seems to reveal the act's character.
    correction: Art 2(2) looks primarily at the nature of the transaction. Buying office supplies for an embassy is commercial, whatever its official purpose (lecture example; Playa Largo).
  - claim: An organ of the state, such as a central bank, is always immune.
    tempting: Organs of government fall within the definition of 'State'.
    correction: Being the state is only the first step. In Trendex the Central Bank of Nigeria was an organ of the state, yet had no immunity for failing to honour a letter of credit — a commercial act.
  - claim: Every state now accepts the restrictive approach.
    tempting: It is codified in the 2004 Convention and applied in the leading cases.
    correction: About one-third of states, including China, do not recognise the jure imperii / jure gestionis distinction, and the Convention is not in force.
