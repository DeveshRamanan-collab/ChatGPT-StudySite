plain: "The International Bill of Rights is the 1948 Universal Declaration plus the two 1966 Covenants: one on civil and political rights, the other on economic, social and cultural rights."
concrete: Women delegates played a key role in getting women's rights into the Universal Declaration. formal: "UDHR (10 Dec 1948) art 2: rights for everyone \"without distinction of any kind\" — race, colour, sex, language, religion, opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status. ICCPR (16 Dec 1966): life (6), torture (7), slavery (8), liberty (9), detention (10), contract debt (11), movement (12), aliens (13). terms:
  - term: International Bill of Rights
    gloss: The UDHR (1948), the ICCPR and the ICESCR (both 16 December 1966).
  - term: UDHR art 2
    gloss: Everyone is entitled to the Declaration's rights without distinction of any kind, including on the status of the country or territory to which they belong.
  - term: ICCPR / ICESCR
    gloss: "The two 1966 Covenants: civil and political rights; economic, social and cultural rights. The same article number means a different right in each."
    example: "Art 11: no imprisonment for contract debt (ICCPR); adequate food, clothing and housing (ICESCR)."
misconceptions:
  - claim: The right to "adequate food, clothing and housing" is ICCPR art 11.
    tempting: Both Covenants have an art 11.
    correction: It is ICESCR art 11. ICCPR art 11 is the bar on imprisonment for breach of contract — the same number means a different right in each Covenant.
  - claim: The International Bill of Rights is the UN Charter, the UDHR and the ICCPR.
    tempting: The Charter is where human rights enter the UN system.
    correction: The Bill is the UDHR, the ICCPR and the ICESCR. The Charter's human rights provisions (arts 1(3), 55(c), 56) are not part of it.
  - claim: The UDHR and the Covenants were adopted together in 1966.
    tempting: They are always named together.
    correction: The UDHR dates from 10 December 1948; both Covenants from 16 December 1966.
