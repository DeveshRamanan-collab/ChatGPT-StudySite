plain: Players accept the ordinary risks of their sport, but others can still owe them a duty for risks beyond those. The CLA removes liability for some obvious or warned-of risks.
concrete: "Rootes, an experienced waterskier, was doing a \"crossover\" on the Macquarie River when he collided with a stationary boat. Spray briefly blinded him, but the towing boat's observer had also failed to warn the skiers of objects ahead. The High Court held that the boat driver owed Rootes a duty of care and had breached it: a failure to warn was not simply a risk inherent in water-skiing."
formal: "Participants \"may be held to have accepted risks which are inherent in that sport or pastime … but this does not eliminate all duty of care of the one participant to the other\" (Barwick CJ, Rootes v Shelton). Agar v Hyde: no duty to change rugby's rules. CLA s 5L: no liability for obvious risks of dangerous recreational activities; s 5M: no duty once warned."

terms:
  - term: Inherent risk
    gloss: A risk that is part of the sport or pastime itself, which participants may be held to have accepted.
  - term: Recreational activity (s 5K)
    gloss: Includes any sport, any pursuit for enjoyment, relaxation or leisure, and activities at places such as beaches or parks.
  - term: Dangerous recreational activity (s 5K)
    gloss: A recreational activity that involves a significant risk of physical harm.
  - term: Risk warning (s 5M)
    gloss: A warning, oral or written, general or specific, given in a way reasonably likely to warn people beforehand; it need not be received or understood.
