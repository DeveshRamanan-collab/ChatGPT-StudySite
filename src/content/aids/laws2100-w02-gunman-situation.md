plain: "Hart's test case: a gunman orders a clerk to hand over money. The clerk is obliged to comply, but has no obligation to. Austin's theory treats law as this situation writ large."
concrete: "A gunman orders a bank clerk to hand over the money and threatens to shoot if he refuses. The clerk complies. We would say he was obliged to hand it over, but not that he had an obligation to: he acted from fear, not from any recognition of authority. And the moment the gunman leaves the bank, it is all over."
formal: Orders backed by threats, or coercive orders, are orders which, like the gunman's, are 'supported only by threats' (CL 19); 'to command is characteristically to exercise authority over men, not power to inflict harm' (CL 20). Add generality, enduring character, a general habit of obedience, and supremacy and independence (CL 24), and laws become 'general orders backed by threats' from the sovereign or subordinates (CL 25).
