terms:
  - term: Will theory
    gloss: A person has a right if they have control or choice over whether others must or must not act in certain ways; its value is autonomy (slide 5).
    example: A party to a contract who may insist on performance or release the other side.
  - term: Interest theory
    gloss: A person has a right if it serves to protect an important interest; rights are justified by promoting or safeguarding well-being (welfare).
    example: An infant's rights, which protect her well-being though she cannot choose.
  - term: Universality
    gloss: Human rights claim to apply to each and every human being; a contract binds only its parties.
  - term: High priority
    gloss: Human rights have priority over the 'general interest' (slide 5). Constitutional rights also have priority, but a right can be constitutional without being a human right.
