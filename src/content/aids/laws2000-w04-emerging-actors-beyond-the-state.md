plain: International law once dealt only with states. Since the Second World War, individuals, groups and corporations have gained some rights, and some obligations, but none has a state's full personality.
concrete: Eight Torres Strait Islanders complained to the UN Human Rights Committee under the ICCPR's Optional Protocol, after exhausting their options at home. They argued that Australia's failure to implement and mitigate climate commitments violated their rights to enjoy their culture and to be free from arbitrary interference with family and private life. In 2022 the Committee found Australia had violated those rights, and Australia accepted at least part of its recommendations — something individuals could not have done 60 years earlier.
formal: "Individuals were not originally subjects. After WWII they gained rights exercisable against states: the non-binding Universal Declaration of Human Rights 1948, then binding treaty law (ECHR 1950; ICCPR 1966). Individuals bear obligations under international criminal law (Milošević, indicted by the ICTY, 1999). Non-state armed actors are bound by IHL, including common art 3 of the Geneva Conventions. Corporations: investment rights, but \"to date, no legally binding obligations\"."

    gloss: Non-binding UN principles on corporate human rights responsibility. Multinationals still have no legally binding obligations under international law.

    correction: The lecture calls it a non-binding instrument. It gave way to binding treaties — the ICCPR, the ICESCR and the ECHR — which bind states that ratify them.

    tempting: The Guiding Principles address business and human rights directly.
    correction: They are not binding. The slide says that "to date" there are no legally binding obligations of multinationals under international law; that gap drives the push in this field.

    correction: Non-state actors in armed conflict are bound by certain IHL obligations (for example on prisoners and civilians); common art 3 of the Geneva Conventions applies to them, though their duties may differ from a state's.
