plain: Conceptual analysis clarifies concepts, usually by setting necessary and sufficient conditions that mark a category's boundaries. The boundaries help discussion, but where they go is often contested.

formal: Conceptual analysis has been a key element/method of analytical philosophy. Conceptual theories generally define terms by necessary and sufficient conditions; they 'set the boundaries of categories', which can help establish 'a common ground for investigation and discussion, but the placement of the boundaries is often contested' (Bix, p 18). Purely conceptual theories contrast with non-conceptual theories in the natural and the social sciences.

terms:
  - term: Conceptual analysis
    gloss: The philosophical effort to clarify or analyse concepts; a key method of analytical philosophy that Hart imported into jurisprudence.
  - term: Necessary and sufficient conditions
    gloss: Features every member must have (necessary) and features that together guarantee membership (sufficient). Together they fix where a category begins and ends.
  - term: Semantic vs conceptual disagreement
    gloss: "Semantic: a disagreement about the meaning of a term. Conceptual: the parties do not share the same concept at all."
    example: "'Football' is semantic: 'I meant soccer' settles it."
  - term: Analytical jurisprudence
    gloss: Jurisprudence as the analysis of general ideas relevant in the legal domain; a tradition older than Hart, which he combined with Oxford ordinary-language philosophy.
