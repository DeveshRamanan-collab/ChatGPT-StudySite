plain: The reasonable person is an imaginary, ordinary careful person. Children are compared with children of their age; other personal traits, such as illness or inexperience, do not lower the bar.
concrete: "Watson, a 12-year-old, threw a sharpened piece of scrap metal at a tree; it bounced off and injured McHale's eye. The High Court held by majority that a child's standard of care is that of a normal child of comparable age. Kitto J: being abnormally slow-witted or quick-tempered is no answer, but a child may rely on the limited foresight characteristic of humanity at his stage of development."
formal: "Section 5B(1)(c) uses \"a reasonable person in the person's position\", a legal fiction for the objective test (Vaughan v Menlove (1837): \"the man of ordinary prudence\"). McHale v Watson: a child, by a normal child of comparable age. Carrier v Bonham: mental illness does not affect the standard. Imbree v McNeilly: learner drivers owe the licensed standard. Professional skill raises it (Rogers v Whitaker; s 5O)."

terms:
  - term: Reasonable person
    gloss: A legal fiction created by judges to apply the objective test, because there are too many subjective variables.
  - term: Comparable-age standard
    gloss: "For a child defendant: what a normal child of the same age would have done (McHale v Watson)."
