plain: Remedies for trespass to land are damages, an injunction, or self-help using reasonable force. Because no damage need be proved, at least nominal damages are always available once trespass is shown.
formal: "Remedies: damages (nominal, compensatory, aggravated, exemplary), injunctions (prohibitory or mandatory), and self-help (e.g. removing a trespasser with reasonable force). Damages put the plaintiff where she would be had no wrong been committed: generally the diminution in the property's value, in suitable cases the higher cost of reinstatement, plus consequential injury such as psychiatric injury. The flowchart also notes a \"user fee\" basis."
terms:
  - term: Diminution in value
    gloss: "The general measure of damages for trespass to land: the reduction in the property's value caused by the trespass."
  - term: Reinstatement
    gloss: The cost of restoring the land; in suitable cases ordered even though it is higher than the diminution in value.
  - term: Prohibitory and mandatory injunctions
    gloss: A prohibitory injunction restrains conduct, such as further entry; a mandatory injunction requires positive action.
  - term: Self-help
    gloss: Acting without a court order, e.g. physically removing a trespasser using reasonable force; the nuisance counterpart is abatement.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Damages for trespass to land are always the cost of repairing the land.
    tempting: Fixing the harm seems the natural measure.
    correction: Damages are generally assessed as the diminution in the property's value; the higher cost of reinstatement is ordered only in suitable cases (Lecture 1, slide 18).
  - claim: A trespass that caused no loss earns nothing.
    tempting: With no loss there is nothing to compensate.
    correction: Trespass to land is actionable per se, so nominal damages are available without proof of loss (Lecture 1, slides 6, 17).
  - claim: An occupier may use any force needed to eject a trespasser.
    tempting: It is their land, and the trespasser is in the wrong.
    correction: Self-help allows physical removal of a trespasser using reasonable force only (Lecture 1, slide 17).
