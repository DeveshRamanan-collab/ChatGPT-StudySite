plain: In arbitration, states that have consented set up their own temporary tribunal, choose its members and rules, and are bound by its final award.
concrete: The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea provides for disputes about the Convention to be settled by arbitration, so its parties consent in advance. Relying on that, the Philippines brought a claim against China over China's activities in the South China Sea. The arbitral tribunal sided with the Philippines, finding that China's conduct violated international law. As an arbitral award, the decision is final and binding upon the parties.
formal: Arbitration is founded on consent, given by becoming party to a treaty providing for arbitration of future disputes (e.g. UNCLOS) or by a special agreement (compromis) after a dispute arises. States control composition, the rules to be applied and confidentiality; procedure is flexible and can be faster; tribunals are ad hoc; arbitral awards are final and binding upon the parties.
terms:
  - term: compromis
    gloss: A special agreement by which the parties submit a particular, existing dispute to arbitration (or to the ICJ).
  - term: arbitral award
    gloss: "The final decision of an arbitral tribunal: final and binding upon the parties."
  - term: ad hoc tribunal
    gloss: A temporary tribunal set up for the dispute, unlike a standing court such as the ICJ.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Consent to arbitration can only be given in a treaty before the dispute arises.
    tempting: Treaty-based consent is the common route.
    correction: States can also consent after a dispute has arisen, by a special agreement (compromis) submitting that particular dispute.
  - claim: An arbitral award is a recommendation the parties may use in further talks.
    tempting: Arbitration is flexible and controlled by the parties.
    correction: "That describes conciliation. Arbitration is adjudicatory: its awards are final and binding upon the parties."
  - claim: A compromis is a settlement the parties reach by mutual concessions.
    tempting: The word looks like "compromise".
    correction: A compromis is an agreement to submit a particular dispute to a tribunal, not a settlement of the dispute itself.
