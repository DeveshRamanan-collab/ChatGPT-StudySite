plain: A state that commits a wrongful act must stop it, give assurances it won't recur where needed, and fully repair the harm — by giving back, paying, or acknowledging the wrong.
concrete: "In 2004 the ICJ found Israel's construction of a wall in the Occupied Palestinian Territory internationally wrongful. The consequences came in two layers. Cessation: Israel had to stop building, dismantle the portions already built, and offer appropriate assurances and guarantees of non-repetition. Reparation: it had to compensate the natural and legal persons affected by the construction — returning land or paying for the damage."
formal: "Art 30 ARSIWA: the responsible state must (a) cease the act, if continuing, and (b) offer appropriate assurances and guarantees of non-repetition, if circumstances so require. Art 31: full reparation for the injury, material or moral. Factory at Chorzów (PCIJ 1928): reparation must, as far as possible, wipe out all the consequences of the illegal act. Forms: restitution (art 35), compensation (art 36), satisfaction (art 37)."

terms:
  - term: restitution (art 35)
    gloss: "Reparation in kind: re-establishing the prior situation, e.g. returning property."
  - term: compensation (art 36)
    gloss: Usually money where restitution is not possible; covers material, financially assessable damage — not moral damage.
  - term: satisfaction (art 37)
    gloss: "Repairs moral damage: an apology, an expression of regret, or a court's judgment declaring the conduct wrongful."
  - term: moral damage
    gloss: Non-material injury, such as an affront to a state's dignity. Part of 'injury' under art 31(2); repaired by satisfaction.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Reparation punishes the wrongdoing state in proportion to its fault.
    tempting: Large damages can feel punitive.
    correction: "Chorzów: reparation restores. It must, as far as possible, wipe out the consequences and re-establish the situation that would in all probability have existed."
  - claim: Compensation can repair any injury, including moral damage.
    tempting: Money seems able to price any harm.
    correction: Compensation covers only material damage that is financially assessable. Moral damage is repaired through satisfaction (art 37).
  - claim: Assurances of non-repetition are owed in every case.
    tempting: Non-repetition sounds universal.
    correction: Art 30(b) requires them only "if circumstances so require"; cessation under art 30(a) applies where the act is continuing.
