plain: For a long time, careless but honest words that cost someone money gave no claim. Since Hedley Byrne, a duty can arise for negligent words where there is a special relationship.
concrete: Hedley Byrne, an advertising agency, was about to extend credit to a client and asked its bank to obtain a credit reference from the client's bank, Heller. The reference came back favourable but was marked "without responsibility". The client collapsed and the agency lost money. The House of Lords described what a duty for negligent words would need, but found none on the facts because of the disclaimer.
formal: "Derry v Peek (1889): negligent misstatement will not suffice for deceit; it must be intentional or reckless. Hedley Byrne v Heller [1964]: a duty of care for negligent words where a special relationship exists. Evatt (1968), Barwick CJ: information or advice on a serious matter; the speaker realises (or should) they are being trusted; reasonable for the recipient to act on it; a duty of reasonable care follows."
terms:
  - term: Deceit
    gloss: An intentional tort covering fraudulent misstatement; it needs an intentional or reckless misstatement. Outside this unit.
  - term: Negligent misstatement
    gloss: Careless (not fraudulent) words, such as advice or information, causing loss; actionable in negligence where a special relationship gives rise to a duty.
  - term: Special relationship
    gloss: "Hedley Byrne's requirement for a duty for negligent words: trust, reasonable reliance, and a speaker who knows or ought to know of the reliance."

  - claim: Hedley Byrne found that the bank's reference created a duty of care.
    tempting: Hedley Byrne is the founding case for duties for negligent words.
    correction: The House of Lords recognised such duties in principle, but the reference was given "without responsibility", so no duty arose on the facts.
  - claim: Because words differ from acts, careless words can never found liability in negligence.
    tempting: Lord Reid said the law must treat negligent words differently from negligent acts.
    correction: Different treatment is not no liability. Lord Reid's point is that opinions on social or informal occasions attract no duty; with a special relationship, a duty can arise.
