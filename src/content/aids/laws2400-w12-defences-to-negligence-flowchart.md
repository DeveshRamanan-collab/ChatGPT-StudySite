plain: Once negligence is proved, the defendant may point to the plaintiff's own conduct. Contributory negligence cuts the damages down; voluntary assumption of risk, if proved, defeats the claim altogether.
formal: "Contributory negligence results in a partial reduction in the damages otherwise recoverable, on what is 'just and equitable having regard to [their] share in the responsibility for the damage' (Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1965 (NSW) s 9(1)). Volenti non fit injuria operates as a complete defence: the defendant must show the plaintiff perceived, fully appreciated and voluntarily accepted the specific risk."

terms:
  - term: Contributory negligence
    gloss: The plaintiff's own lack of reasonable care for their safety, which reduces the damages otherwise recoverable.
  - term: Volenti non fit injuria
    gloss: "Voluntary assumption of risk: a complete defence where the plaintiff perceived, fully appreciated and voluntarily accepted the specific risk."
  - term: Just and equitable
    gloss: The measure of the reduction for contributory negligence, having regard to the plaintiff's share in the responsibility for the damage (1965 Act s 9(1)).
  - term: Illegality
    gloss: A defence where plaintiff and defendant were in a criminal enterprise and no appropriate standard of care can be determined (Gala v Preston).
