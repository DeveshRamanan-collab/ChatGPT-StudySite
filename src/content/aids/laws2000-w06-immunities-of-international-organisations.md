plain: International organisations such as the UN are immune from local courts so they can work without interference and alike everywhere. Their staff are protected only for official acts. Victims may be left without a remedy.
concrete: In 2010 a cholera outbreak in Haiti killed nearly 10,000 people. A UN-appointed panel concluded in 2011 that contamination from a UN peacekeepers' camp was a likely cause, though not the fault of any group or individual. The Secretary-General apologised, but the UN has not accepted legal responsibility. When human rights lawyers sued the UN in the United States, the federal courts upheld the UN's immunity in 2016.
formal: "IO immunities rest on treaties. UN Charter art 105: the UN has the privileges and immunities \"necessary for the fulfillment of its purposes\"; representatives and officials, those for \"the independent exercise of their functions\". 1946 Convention art II s 2: immunity \"from every form of legal process\" unless expressly waived, but \"no waiver of immunity shall extend to any measure of execution\"; premises and archives inviolable (ss 3–4)."
terms:
  - term: functional immunity of IO officials
    gloss: IO officials are immune only for acts carried out in their functions — never personal immunity.
  - term: representatives, officials, experts on missions
    gloss: "The 1946 Convention's three categories (arts IV, V s 18, VI): each immune for official words and acts; experts' immunity continues after the mission."
  - term: alternative mode of settlement (s 29)
    gloss: Because local courts are closed, the UN must provide another way to settle private law disputes — usually an arbitration clause in the contract.
  - term: privilege
    gloss: A benefit distinct from immunity from jurisdiction.
    example: UN officials pay no tax on their UN employment income in the country where they are posted.
misconceptions:
  - claim: International organisations' immunities come from customary international law, like state immunity.
    tempting: State immunity is customary, and IOs are also subjects of international law.
    correction: "IO immunities are based on treaty law: states grant them, e.g. UN Charter art 105 and the 1946 Convention."
  - claim: Once the UN waives immunity and loses, the claimant can seize UN assets to enforce the judgment.
    tempting: A state that waives immunity cannot reclaim it after losing.
    correction: "Art II s 2: \"no waiver of immunity shall extend to any measure of execution\", and UN property and assets are immune wherever located."
  - claim: Senior UN officials enjoy personal immunity like a head of state.
    tempting: Some UN officials are very senior and travel like diplomats.
    correction: "IO officials' immunity is functional only: words spoken or written and acts performed in their official capacity (art 105(2); art V s 18)."
