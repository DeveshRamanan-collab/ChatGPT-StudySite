plain: Human rights grew from national charters and Enlightenment ideas of freedom and dignity, then narrow early treaties, and became a general international concern only with the UN Charter after World War II.
concrete: The first international steps were narrow. Britain abolished slavery in 1807, other European countries followed, and the 1926 Slavery Convention carried the issue onto the international plane; the other early concern was limited women's rights — trafficking in women for prostitution. Only after the Second World War and the Nazi regime did the UN Charter make respect for human rights a general purpose of the international community.
formal: "Early domestic instruments: Magna Carta (1215), English Bill of Rights (1689), French Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789), US Constitution and Bill of Rights (1789, 1791). UN Charter: promoting respect for human rights \"for all without distinction as to race, sex, language, or religion\" is a Purpose (art 1(3)); the UN shall promote their universal respect and observance (art 55(c)); members pledge joint and separate action (art 56)."
