plain: A state can bind itself by publicly promising something even though no other state promises anything back; unlike a treaty, it needs no counterpart and no writing.
concrete: "Australia and New Zealand took France to the ICJ over its atmospheric nuclear tests in the Pacific, fearing radioactive fallout. Before a hearing on the merits, France's President and Foreign Minister made public statements, including at press conferences, that France would cease atmospheric testing. The Court held those statements showed an intention to be legally bound: France had given a legal undertaking without anyone accepting it."
formal: "\"A state may evidence a clear intention to accept obligations vis-à-vis certain other states by a public declaration which is not an offer or otherwise dependent on reciprocal undertakings from its addressees\" (Crawford, Brownlie's Principles). Unilateral statements can create binding obligations (Eastern Greenland (1933) PCIJ Ser A/B No 53; Nuclear Tests [1974] ICJ Rep 253). Unlike a treaty, there is no reciprocity and no writing requirement."

terms:
  - term: unilateral statement
    gloss: A public declaration by which one state commits itself, without any reciprocal undertaking, capable of binding it in international law.
  - term: reciprocity
    gloss: "Mutual undertakings between parties: present in a treaty, absent from a unilateral statement."
  - term: addressee
    gloss: The state or states a unilateral declaration is directed to; their acceptance or reply is not needed for it to bind.
misconceptions:
  - claim: A unilateral statement binds only once the addressee accepts it.
    tempting: That is how treaties and contracts work.
    correction: "Crawford: the declaration \"is not an offer or otherwise dependent on reciprocal undertakings from its addressees\". No other state's acceptance is needed."
  - claim: A unilateral statement and a reservation are the same thing, since both are unilateral.
    tempting: Art 2(1)(d) VCLT itself calls a reservation a 'unilateral statement'.
    correction: A reservation operates inside a treaty and its effect depends on other states' acceptance or objection (art 20); a unilateral statement stands alone.
  - claim: A unilateral statement must be in writing to bind.
    tempting: Treaties under the VCLT must be written.
    correction: "It need not be in writing: Norway's foreign minister spoke to his Danish counterpart (Eastern Greenland), and France's statements were made at press conferences (Nuclear Tests)."

  - ask: Does it matter that the statement was not written down?
