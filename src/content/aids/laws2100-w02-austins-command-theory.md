plain: "For Austin, laws properly so called are commands: an expressed wish that someone act or forbear, backed by a threatened evil (a sanction), from someone with the power to inflict it."
concrete: "Look at any human society, however it is organised: someone is in a position of power. They express a wish, in words or signs, to influence how others behave, and the others know they are liable to an evil, a sanction, if they do not comply. For Austin, wherever such a command is signified a duty is imposed; and a law is a command that obliges generally, to a class of acts."
formal: "Law in general: 'a rule laid down for the guidance of an intelligent being by an intelligent being having power over him' (PJD 18). Laws properly so called are a species of commands: a wish, an evil for non-compliance, and an expression of the wish. Command and duty are correlative; command, duty and sanction are inseparable. A law obliges generally to acts or forbearances of a class; superiority means might."

terms:
  - term: Command
    gloss: An expressed wish that another do or forbear, with an evil to be incurred from the commander if they do not comply.
  - term: Sanction (evil)
    gloss: "The evil threatened for non-compliance: an enforcement of obedience. Punishments are only one class of sanctions."
  - term: Superiority
    gloss: "Might: the power to affect others with evil and, through fear of it, force them to comply with one's wishes."
  - term: Positive law
    gloss: "Law 'simply and strictly so called': law set by political superiors to political inferiors, the matter of jurisprudence (PJD 18)."

    tempting: It echoes Hart's point that commanding is an exercise of authority.
    correction: "Austin needs no acceptance: command and duty are correlative. Wherever a command is signified, backed by an evil, a duty is imposed."
  - claim: Austin's 'evil' means moral wrongdoing, or else only criminal punishment.
    tempting: "'Evil' has a moral ring, and punishment is the obvious sanction."
    correction: Evil means sanction, an enforcement of obedience. The slide notes that punishments are only a class of sanctions.
  - claim: Austin counts international law as positive law.
    tempting: States make treaties and can pressure one another.
    correction: "It falls in his third kind: objects improperly but by close analogy termed laws, rules set and enforced by mere opinion (positive morality)."

objections:

    objection: "Hart: 'to command is characteristically to exercise authority over men, not power to inflict harm' (CL 20); a theory built on might cannot explain legal obligation."
    reply: "Schauer: law as experienced is overwhelmingly coercive; whether Austin was right depends on what a theory of law is designed to accomplish."

    evidence: He opens with a general definition of law and derives his four kinds of laws from it (PJD 18).
    objection: "The lecture says this is not what Austin is doing: he compares human societies and finds a common denominator, a power relation in which someone issues commands."
