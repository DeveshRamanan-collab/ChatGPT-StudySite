plain: If non-forcible measures would fail or have failed, the Council can authorise military action. With no UN army, it authorises coalitions of states or regional organisations, in clear words like "all necessary means".
concrete: "In 1990, after Iraq's illegal occupation of Kuwait, the Council adopted Res 678, authorising member states co-operating with Kuwait — an ad hoc coalition led by the US — to use \"all necessary means\" to restore Kuwait's sovereignty. That became Operation Desert Storm. The UN had no army of its own to send: art 43 was never implemented, so the Council authorised states to act."
formal: "Art 42: should the Council consider art 41 measures \"would be inadequate or have proved to be inadequate, it may take such action by air, sea, or land forces as may be necessary to maintain or restore international peace and security\", including demonstrations and blockade. It is an exception to art 2(4), a last resort, relying on member states (art 43 never implemented); regional organisations may be authorised (art 53)."
terms:
  - term: "\"all necessary means\""
    gloss: The usual Chapter VII authorising formula for force, used in Res 678 (Kuwait) and Res 794 (Somalia).
  - term: art 43
    gloss: The Charter's plan for forces made available to the Council; never implemented — hence no standing UN army and reliance on coalitions.
  - term: revived or implied authorisation
    gloss: A claim to revive an earlier authorisation, or to imply one from resolutions that do not expressly authorise force.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Res 1441 (2002) authorised the use of force against Iraq.
    tempting: It threatened "serious consequences" and gave Iraq a "final opportunity".
    correction: Res 1441 contained no authorising words. The coalition instead claimed that Iraq's material breach revived Res 678's authority — a claim most Council and UN members, and most scholars, rejected.
  - claim: Art 42 force is available only after sanctions have been tried and failed.
    tempting: Force is a last resort.
    correction: Art 42 also applies where the Council considers art 41 measures "would be inadequate" — they need not have been tried first.
  - claim: A regional organisation such as NATO may enforce peace on its own initiative.
    tempting: NATO acted in Libya; ECOWAS in Liberia.
    correction: "Regional organisations enforce Council decisions only when the Council authorises them (art 53, Chapter VIII): the permissible use of force stays centralised in the Council."
