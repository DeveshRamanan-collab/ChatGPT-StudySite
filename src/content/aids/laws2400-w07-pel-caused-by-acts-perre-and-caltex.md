plain: When a careless act (not words) costs someone money without hurting them or their property, Australian courts may find a duty by weighing salient features case by case.
concrete: Apand sold potato seed infected with bacterial wilt to Sparnon's farm, 3 km from the Perres' farm. Western Australia prohibited potatoes grown within 20 km of the disease. The Perres' potatoes were tested and disease-free, and they had no contract with Apand, but they could no longer sell into WA. They lost in the lower courts; the High Court held Apand owed them a duty.
formal: "In England, no duty is generally imposed for PEL caused by an act; Australian courts may find one by applying the salient factors approach. Perre v Apand (1999): duty owed. McHugh J asked, in order: foreseeability; indeterminate liability; unreasonable burden on autonomy; vulnerability; knowledge. Caltex Oil v Dredge 'Willemstad' (1976): a duty for PEL even before the salient factors approach was settled."
terms:
  - term: Ascertained class
    gloss: A defined group of potential plaintiffs, such as farms within the 20 km quarantine radius; it answers the indeterminacy concern.
  - term: Autonomy
    gloss: The defendant's freedom to pursue its own legitimate interests; a duty must not unreasonably burden it (McHugh J).
  - term: Control
    gloss: Who was in control of the situation; the Perres had none over which seed their neighbours planted, which made them vulnerable.

    correction: The High Court held a duty was owed despite the absence of any contract, applying the salient features.
  - claim: After Perre and Caltex Oil, PEL caused by an act attracts a duty.
    tempting: Both leading High Court cases found a duty.
    correction: It is definitely not the case that every PEL-by-act case attracts a duty. Courts take an incremental, case-by-case approach; Mallonland found no duty.
  - claim: McHugh J began with indeterminacy, the key PEL concern.
    tempting: Indeterminacy is the most famous reason courts hesitate over PEL.
    correction: Foreseeability came first; indeterminacy was asked only if the loss was foreseeable, then autonomy, vulnerability and knowledge.
