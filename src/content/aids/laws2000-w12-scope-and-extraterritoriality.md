plain: A state owes human rights to everyone in its territory or under its jurisdiction, whatever their nationality or legal status — and that can include people abroad, as in territory it occupies.
concrete: Uganda occupied Ituri, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The ICJ held Uganda responsible both for its soldiers' violations and for its lack of vigilance in preventing violations by other actors there, including rebel groups acting on their own account (para 179). Human rights instruments applied "in respect of acts done by a State in the exercise of its jurisdiction outside its own territory", particularly in occupied territories (para 216).
formal: States must respect the rights of people within their territory and subject to their jurisdiction (ICCPR art 2(1)), irrespective of nationality or legal status. Jurisdiction is "primarily territorial" but may be exercised abroad, and the drafters "did not intend to allow States to escape from their obligations" there (Wall, para 109; DRC v Uganda, para 216). The slides cite Lopez Burgos (para 12.3) for application even sometimes without effective control.
