plain: Judges sometimes agree on all the facts and on which sources count, yet disagree about what the law is. Dworkin says Hart's theory cannot explain this.
concrete: "In Riggs v Palmer nobody disputed the facts: Elmer poisoned his grandfather, and the will was valid. Nobody disputed that the will statutes were valid law. Yet the judges disagreed about whether Elmer was legally entitled to the estate. That is not empirical disagreement about what happened, nor moral disagreement about whether the law is just, but disagreement about what the law is."
formal: "'Lawsuits always raise, at least in principle, three different kinds of issues: issues of fact, issues of law, and the twinned issues of political morality and fidelity' (LE 3). Disagreement about the pertinent law is 'theoretical'. Law's Empire 'takes up the internal, participants' point of view', studying formal legal argument 'from the judge's viewpoint' (LE 14)."

terms:
  - term: Empirical disagreement
    gloss: "Disagreement about what happened: the facts of the case."
  - term: Theoretical disagreement
    gloss: Disagreement about what the pertinent law is, among participants who agree on the facts and on the validity of the sources.
  - term: Moral disagreement
    gloss: "Disagreement about whether the law, once identified, is just: for example, if it denies compensation."
  - term: Argumentative practice
    gloss: "Dworkin's distinctive feature of law: unlike many rule-governed social practices, legal practice consists in arguing about what the law requires."
