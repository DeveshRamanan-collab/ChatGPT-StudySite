plain: Even when a state's own conduct breaches an obligation, six circumstances can excuse it, working like defences. None of them can excuse breaching a peremptory norm.
concrete: "Hungary and Czechoslovakia agreed by treaty to build a joint system of locks on the Danube for hydroelectricity and navigation. In 1989 Hungary stopped work on its side over ecological concerns. Czechoslovakia responded by diverting the Danube into its own territory and damming it in 1992. The ICJ accepted Hungary had breached the treaty, but held the diversion was not a lawful countermeasure: taking unilateral control of a shared resource deprived Hungary of its equitable share — disproportionate."
formal: "Arts 20–25 ARSIWA: consent; self-defence in conformity with the UN Charter (art 21, with art 51); countermeasures (arts 22, 49–53: against a responsible state, to induce compliance, proportional, after procedural steps); force majeure (art 23: \"absolute and material impossibility\", Rainbow Warrior); distress (art 24); necessity (art 25). Art 26: none precludes the wrongfulness of an act contrary to a peremptory norm."
terms:
  - term: countermeasure
    gloss: An otherwise wrongful act taken against a responsible state solely to induce it to comply; must be proportional and respect jus cogens and procedure (arts 22, 49–53).
  - term: reprisal
    gloss: The lecture's label for a measure that fails the conditions for lawful countermeasures; it does not preclude wrongfulness.
  - term: force majeure (art 23)
    gloss: An irresistible force or unforeseen event beyond the state's control making performance materially impossible. Not if the state contributed or assumed the risk.
  - term: necessity (art 25)
    gloss: "Framed in the negative: only way to safeguard an essential interest against a grave and imminent peril, without seriously impairing another's essential interest."
misconceptions:
  - claim: Countermeasures may be used to punish the wrongdoing state.
    tempting: They are a response to a wrong already done.
    correction: "Art 49(1): a countermeasure's only legitimate purpose is to induce the responsible state to comply with its obligations — no other motive or agenda."
  - claim: A natural disaster always counts as force majeure.
    tempting: Floods and tsunamis are the classic example.
    correction: Not if the state contributed to the situation or assumed the risk, and the bar is "absolute and material impossibility" (Rainbow Warrior). In Serbian Loans even the First World War did not excuse repayment.
  - claim: Force majeure and necessity are the same excuse under two names.
    tempting: Both excuse non-performance in emergencies.
    correction: "Force majeure is involuntary: performance is materially impossible (art 23). Necessity is a choice to breach a lesser obligation to safeguard an essential interest against grave and imminent peril (art 25)."
socratic:
  - ask: Czechoslovakia diverted the Danube after Hungary stopped work. Which ground might it invoke?
    hint: A response to another's wrong.
    answer: "Countermeasures (art 22): an otherwise wrongful act taken against a responsible state to induce it to comply."
  - ask: Was there a prior wrong by Hungary?
    hint: Art 49(1).
    answer: Yes. The ICJ accepted that Hungary's refusal to complete the project breached the treaty, so a countermeasure was possible in principle.
  - ask: Why then did the plea fail?
    hint: Art 51.
    answer: Proportionality. Unilaterally taking control of the shared Danube deprived Hungary of its equitable share, with ecological impact — disproportionate.
  - ask: What else must every countermeasure respect?
    hint: Arts 50, 52–53.
    answer: No breach of jus cogens obligations such as the use of force; procedural steps — negotiate, notify, give a chance to cease; and termination once the wrongful act has ceased.
  - ask: Could Czechoslovakia have relied on necessity instead?
    hint: Art 25, read narrowly.
    answer: Only if diversion was the only way to safeguard an essential interest against a grave and imminent peril, without seriously impairing Hungary's essential interest, and it had not contributed to the situation.
