plain: An occupier must take reasonable care for people on its land, but need not fence off or warn about dangers that are obvious to anyone taking ordinary care.
concrete: Fifteen-year-old Nadia Romeo fell from the Dripstone Cliffs near Darwin at night and was seriously injured; she had been drinking. The cliffs were in a reserve controlled by the Conservation Commission. She argued it should have fenced the cliff edge or warned of it. The High Court held the Commission owed entrants a duty, but reasonable steps did not extend to fencing about 2 km of obvious cliff.
formal: "An occupier owes entrants a duty of reasonable care against foreseeable risks, but \"the critical question is commonly the measure or scope of that duty\" (Kirby J, Romeo). The danger is assessed before the event \"according to its obviousness and the care ordinarily exercised by the public\" (Toohey and Gummow JJ). CLA: s 5F defines obvious risk; s 5G presumes awareness; s 5H removes the duty to warn, with exceptions."

terms:
  - term: Obvious risk (s 5F)
    gloss: A risk that would have been obvious to a reasonable person in the plaintiff's position, including patent risks and matters of common knowledge, even if unlikely or not conspicuous.
  - term: Scope of duty
    gloss: What the duty actually requires on the facts. Romeo shows this, not existence, is usually the critical question.
  - term: Presumed awareness (s 5G)
    gloss: A plaintiff is presumed aware of an obvious risk unless they prove, on the balance of probabilities, that they were not.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Romeo lost because the Commission owed her no duty of care.
    tempting: The Commission was not liable for her fall.
    correction: The Commission did owe entrants a duty to take reasonable care against foreseeable risks; that duty did not extend to fencing an obvious cliff. Scope, not existence, decided the case.
  - claim: A risk cannot be "obvious" if it was unlikely to happen or hard to see.
    tempting: In ordinary speech, "obvious" means prominent and likely.
    correction: "Section 5F(3)–(4): a risk can be obvious even if it has a low probability of occurring, and even if it is not prominent, conspicuous or physically observable."
  - claim: Section 5H means a defendant never has to warn of an obvious risk.
    tempting: Section 5H(1) says plainly there is no duty to warn of an obvious risk.
    correction: "Section 5H(2) exceptions: the plaintiff requested advice or information, a written law requires a warning, or a professional's service carries a risk of death or personal injury."
