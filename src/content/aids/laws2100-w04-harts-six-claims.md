plain: Hart splits the vague idea of a 'necessary connection' between law and morality into six separate claims, judges each on its own, and concedes two of them.
concrete: "You accept the rules of chess for the purpose of playing chess, without passing moral judgment on them. Hart says officials accept a legal system's secondary rules in the same way: allegiance may rest on long-term interest, concern for others, an inherited attitude, or wanting to do as others do. formal: "Six claims (CL ch IX): (1) power and authority; (2) influence of morality on law; (3) interpretation; (4) criticism of law; (5) principles of legality and justice; (6) legal validity and resistance to law.
