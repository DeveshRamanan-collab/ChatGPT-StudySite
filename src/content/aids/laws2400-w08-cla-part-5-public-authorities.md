plain: "A NSW statute adds extra protection for public authorities: limited budgets count, and some claims succeed only if the authority acted so unreasonably that no sensible authority would have."
concrete: "On the F5 freeway near Sydney, third parties dropped a large piece of concrete from an overpass, killing Evans, a truck driver employed by Refrigerated Roadways. The RTA knew people dropped objects from overpasses. The Court of Appeal held the RTA owed a duty. Section 43A did not help it, because erecting protective screens is not a special statutory power: anybody can erect a screen. But the RTA had not breached its duty."
formal: "CLA Part 5 reflects the Ipp Committee's aim: liability only for acts so unreasonable that no reasonable authority would take them (Wednesbury). Section 42: functions are limited by resources reasonably available; their general allocation is not open to challenge. Section 43: no breach of statutory duty unless so unreasonable that no authority with those functions could properly consider it reasonable; s 43A applies the same test to special statutory powers."
terms:
  - term: Public or other authority (s 41)
    gloss: Includes the Crown, government departments, local councils, statutory authorities, and any person with public official functions, as to those functions.
  - term: Special statutory power (s 43A)
    gloss: A statutory power of a kind persons generally cannot exercise without specific statutory authority.
    example: Back-burning; issuing firearm licences.
  - term: Wednesbury unreasonableness
    gloss: "An administrative law idea: conduct so unreasonable that no reasonable authority would engage in it. A far higher bar than ordinary carelessness."
misconceptions:
  - claim: Under s 43, an authority breaches its statutory duty whenever a reasonable person would have acted differently.
    tempting: That is the ordinary negligence standard.
    correction: "Section 43 sets a much higher bar: the act or omission must be so unreasonable that no authority with those functions could properly consider it a reasonable exercise of its functions."
  - claim: Anything a public authority does under statute is a special statutory power for s 43A.
    tempting: Everything an authority does is authorised by statute.
    correction: "A special statutory power is one persons generally cannot exercise without specific statutory authority. Erecting screens on a bridge is not: anybody can erect a screen (RTA v Refrigerated Roadways)."
  - claim: Once an authority exercises a function, it must keep exercising it with care.
    tempting: Starting to help seems to create an obligation to continue.
    correction: "Section 46: exercising, or deciding to exercise, a function does not of itself indicate a duty to exercise it, or to exercise it in a particular way."
