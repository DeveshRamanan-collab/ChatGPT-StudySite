plain: International law has no world parliament, police or compulsory court, yet most states obey it most of the time, mainly because cooperation depends on it and breaking it costs reputation.

formal: Enforcement "is irrelevant to the binding quality of international law as a system of law" (slide 19). States operate within it because it is necessary to achieve anything meaningful; "the basis of obligation is the same in every legal order" — a consciousness that the order is needed (Hoffman 1987). Compliance comes from fear of "loss of reputation" (Chayes & Chayes 1995) and quasi-enforcement (UN Charter arts 5–6).
