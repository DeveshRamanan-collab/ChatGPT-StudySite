plain: "The first element is a breach: the state's act or omission falls short of an international obligation that bound it when it acted. Treaty or custom, either counts; domestic law is irrelevant."
concrete: "In October 1946 British warships entered the North Corfu Strait in Albanian waters, a passage swept for mines in 1944 and check-swept in 1945. Two ships struck mines: 45 officers and sailors died and 42 were wounded. Who laid the mines was never established — probably not Albania. But the mine-laying would have been visible from the coast, so Albania knew. Its breach was an omission: it failed to notify shipping and warn the approaching warships."
formal: A breach concerns a primary rule irrespective of its source (art 12 ARSIWA). The obligation must bind the state when the conduct occurs — no retroactivity (arts 13–14; Chagos Advisory Opinion, 2019). Several acts may together amount to a breach (art 15, composite acts). Conduct may be an act or an omission (art 2; Tehran; Corfu Channel, where Albania had to notify shipping and warn the British warships).
terms:
  - term: breach by omission
    gloss: Failing to do what an obligation requires — e.g. Iran failing to protect the US embassy under the VCDR; Albania failing to warn of mines.
  - term: composite act (art 15)
    gloss: A series of acts that individually may not breach an obligation but together do — e.g. pollution that adds up over time.
  - term: no retroactivity (arts 13–14)
    gloss: A state breaches an obligation only if it was bound by it when the conduct occurred; becoming bound later does not reach back.
misconceptions:
  - claim: If the state's own parliament authorised the conduct by statute, it cannot be a breach.
    tempting: Domestic authorisation looks like a complete defence.
    correction: "Only international law decides whether there is a breach (art 12). The lecturer: we are not interested in whether domestic law allowed the conduct."
  - claim: Once a state ratifies a treaty, it answers under it for its earlier conduct too.
    tempting: Ratification expresses consent to the whole treaty.
    correction: "There is no retroactivity: the obligation must bind the state at the time of the conduct (arts 13–14; Chagos Advisory Opinion)."
  - claim: Albania was responsible in Corfu Channel because it laid the mines.
    tempting: Albania lost the case.
    correction: "Who laid the mines remained conjecture — probably not Albania. It was responsible for its own omission: knowing of the minefield, it failed to notify shipping and warn the British warships."
socratic:
  - ask: British warships hit mines in Albanian waters that Albania probably did not lay. Where do you look for a breach?
    hint: Act or omission?
    answer: At Albania's own omission. Under art 2, conduct can consist of an act or an omission.
  - ask: What, according to the ICJ, was Albania obliged to do?
    hint: Corfu Channel, p 22.
    answer: Notify shipping in general of the minefield in its waters and warn the approaching British warships of the imminent danger.
  - ask: How could the Court conclude that Albania knew of the mines?
    hint: Two kinds of evidence.
    answer: Evidence of the Albanian Government's attitude, and the fact that the laying of the mines would have been visible from the Albanian coast.
  - ask: Would it matter whether the duty to warn came from custom rather than a treaty?
    hint: Art 12.
    answer: No. A breach concerns a primary rule of international law irrespective of its source — custom or treaty.
  - ask: Suppose the only treaty on point bound Albania from 1950. Could it breach that treaty in 1946?
    hint: Arts 13–14.
    answer: No. The state must be bound by the obligation when the conduct occurs; there is no retroactivity.
