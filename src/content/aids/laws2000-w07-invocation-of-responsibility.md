plain: "Normally only the state owed the broken obligation can complain. Rarely, others can too: members of a group the obligation protects collectively, or any state when the obligation is owed to everyone."
concrete: "The lecturer's example: states in a region make a treaty keeping the region free of nuclear weapons. If one party tests a weapon, another party may suffer no direct harm — yet it can invoke the tester's responsibility, because the obligation is owed to the group and protects their collective interest. And if a state commits genocide, any state may invoke its responsibility: the prohibition of genocide is owed to the international community as a whole."
formal: "Art 42 ARSIWA: only the state or states whose individual rights were violated — those owed the obligation — may invoke responsibility. Art 48(1): any other state may invoke it if (a) the obligation is owed to a group including it and established for the group's collective interest, or (b) it is owed to the international community as a whole (erga omnes: Barcelona Traction, ICJ 1970)."
terms:
  - term: injured state (art 42)
    gloss: A state to which the breached obligation was owed, e.g. under a bilateral or multilateral treaty. It may demand cessation, reparation and so on.
  - term: obligation erga omnes
    gloss: "Owed to the international community as a whole: the right it protects is so important that all states have a legal interest in its protection (Barcelona Traction)."
  - term: collective-interest obligation (art 48(1)(a))
    gloss: Owed to a group of states and established to protect the group's collective interest — e.g. a regional nuclear-free zone treaty or regional human rights system.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Any state economically affected by a breach is an 'injured state'.
    tempting: "'Injury' sounds like suffering loss."
    correction: Under art 42 injury turns on to whom the obligation was owed. A state buying fish from a party to a bilateral fisheries treaty is not injured by the other party's breach.
  - claim: Any state may invoke responsibility for any breach anywhere.
    tempting: Erga omnes norms concern everyone.
    correction: "The rule limits invocation to injured states (art 42). Art 48 applies only in very limited circumstances: collective-interest or erga omnes obligations."
  - claim: A non-injured state invoking an erga omnes breach must show it suffered material harm.
    tempting: Standing usually follows harm.
    correction: "Art 48(1)(b) rests on legal interest, not material injury: the right protected is so important that all states have a legal interest in it (Barcelona Traction)."
socratic:
  - ask: State P breaches a bilateral fisheries treaty with State Q. Who may invoke P's responsibility?
    hint: To whom was the obligation owed?
    answer: "Q, as the injured state under art 42: the obligation was owed to it."
  - ask: State R, not a party, buys fish from Q and loses money. Can R invoke?
    hint: Owed, not merely affected.
    answer: No. The obligation was owed to Q, not R; and a bilateral fisheries obligation is neither owed to a group for a collective interest nor erga omnes.
  - ask: State S commits genocide against part of its own population. Can unconnected State T invoke S's responsibility?
    hint: Art 48(1)(b).
    answer: Yes. The prohibition of genocide is owed erga omnes — to the international community as a whole — so any state may invoke it.
  - ask: Why do all states have standing there?
    hint: Barcelona Traction.
    answer: "By its very nature the obligation concerns all states: the right it protects is so important that all have a legal interest in its protection."
