plain: The plaintiff must show the negligence probably contributed to the harm. Showing that it only cost them a chance, or increased a risk, is not enough.
concrete: "A six-year-old girl, Tabet, suffered permanent brain damage after a delay in performing a CT scan during hospital treatment. She claimed the doctor's negligence cost her a chance of a better medical outcome. But that chance was under 50%: a possibility, not a probability. The High Court refused recovery. In personal injury cases, Australian common law does not compensate the loss of a chance of a better outcome."
formal: "Tabet v Gett (Gummow ACJ): in personal injury cases the common law of Australia does not entertain an action where the damage is characterised as the loss of a chance of a better outcome. Increased risk is not enough (Amaca v Ellis). A negligent contribution that is more than negligible is enough (Bonnington Castings v Wardlaw), and may be inferred from the evidence (Amaca v Booth)."

terms:
  - term: Loss of a chance
    gloss: A claim that negligence destroyed a possibility, not a probability, of a better outcome. Not actionable in Australian personal injury cases (Tabet v Gett).
  - term: Increased risk of harm
    gloss: "Conduct that made harm more likely: probabilistic, forward-looking, and saying nothing about what actually happened to this claimant."
  - term: Material contribution to harm
    gloss: "Conduct that was part of how the harm came about: causal, looking back after the harm, and more than negligible."
  - term: De minimis
    gloss: Negligible or trivial. A negligent contribution must be more than de minimis (Bonnington Castings v Wardlaw).
misconceptions:
  - claim: A lost chance of a better medical outcome is compensated in proportion to its size.
    tempting: Paying 30% of the damages for a 30% chance seems fair.
    correction: Tabet v Gett refused recovery for the loss of a chance of a better outcome in personal injury cases. A less than even chance is a possibility, not a probability.
  - claim: Proof that asbestos exposure increased the risk of the plaintiff's cancer establishes causation.
    tempting: Fairchild (UK) treated materially increased risk as enough, where science could not identify the cause.
    correction: "Amaca v Ellis: increased risk is not sufficient. The plaintiff must prove on the balance of probabilities that the exposure actually contributed. Australia has not adopted Fairchild."
  - claim: A negligent exposure must be the main cause of the disease.
    tempting: The dust also came from other sources or other employers.
    correction: "Bonnington Castings v Wardlaw: a negligent contribution that is more than de minimis is enough. It need not be the sole or main cause."
