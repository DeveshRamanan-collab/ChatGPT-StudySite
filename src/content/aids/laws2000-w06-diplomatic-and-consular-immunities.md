plain: Diplomats cannot be arrested or tried in the country they are posted to, and embassies cannot be entered without consent. Consuls get less protection. The host state's answer to abuse is expulsion, not prosecution.

Iranian security forces did not intervene. The militants were not Iranian officials, yet the ICJ held Iran in breach of the Vienna Conventions: it knew its duty to protect the mission, was asked for help, had the means to act — and did nothing."
formal: "VCDR art 31: a diplomatic agent is immune from the receiving state's criminal jurisdiction, and from civil and administrative jurisdiction save for private immovable property, private succession, and professional or commercial activity outside official functions. Person (art 29), residence and papers (art 30) are inviolable; the agent must obey local law (art 41). Premises are inviolable (art 22); persona non grata (art 9). Consuls: VCCR arts 41, 43."

misconceptions:
  - claim: A diplomat's criminal immunity has the same three exceptions as civil immunity under art 31.
    tempting: Art 31 lists exceptions right after the immunities.
    correction: The exceptions apply only to civil and administrative jurisdiction. Immunity from the receiving state's criminal jurisdiction is absolute.
  - claim: Embassy inviolability only stops the receiving state's own police from entering.
    tempting: Inviolability is usually pictured as 'keep out'.
    correction: Art 22(2) adds a positive duty to protect the premises against intrusion or damage. Iran breached it by failing to stop the militants in Tehran Hostages.
  - claim: Consuls have the same immunities as diplomats.
    tempting: Both are covered by Vienna Conventions and represent the sending state.
    correction: "Consular immunities are more restricted: a consul can be arrested for a 'grave crime' (VCCR art 41) and has only functional immunity in criminal and civil matters (art 43)."

    hint: VCDR art 22.
    answer: No. The mission premises are inviolable and may not be entered without the head of mission's consent, whoever is inside. Assange's own lack of immunity does not change that.
  - ask: Protesters then threaten to break into the embassy. What must the UK do?
    hint: Art 22(2); Tehran Hostages.
    answer: "Protect the premises against intrusion or damage. The duty is positive: Iran breached it by doing nothing while militants seized the US Embassy."
  - ask: A diplomat at the embassy runs a private consultancy on weekends and is sued by a client. Immune?
    hint: Art 31(c).
    answer: No. Civil immunity does not cover professional or commercial activity outside official functions. A criminal charge, by contrast, would be barred completely.
  - ask: A diplomat commits a serious crime and cannot be prosecuted. What can the receiving state do?
    hint: Slide 37.
    answer: Declare the diplomat persona non grata (art 9), sever relations, take other unfriendly acts, do nothing — or ask the sending state to waive immunity.
  - ask: Would the answer differ for a consul?
    hint: VCCR arts 41 and 43.
    answer: Yes. A consul can be arrested for a grave crime and has only functional immunity in criminal and civil matters.
