plain: International criminal law makes individual people — not only states — criminally liable for the gravest breaches of international rules, and sets the procedures for trying them.
concrete: "At Nuremberg, the IMT Charter provided for the individual criminal responsibility of leaders, organisers, instigators and accomplices: the defendants answered personally for crimes against peace, war crimes and crimes against humanity. That is ICL's hallmark — a breach of international rules entailing the personal criminal liability of the individual — and a different question from whether Germany, as a state, was responsible for internationally wrongful acts."
formal: "International crimes are \"breaches of international rules entailing the personal criminal liability of the individual(s) concerned\". ICL, a branch of public international law, has two limbs: substantive law, which governs the four core international crimes and demands that states prosecute them; and procedural law, regulating the stages of international criminal proceedings, found in the statutes and rules of the international(ised) tribunals."
terms:
  - term: international crime
    gloss: A breach of international rules entailing the personal criminal liability of the individual(s) concerned.
  - term: substantive limb
    gloss: The crimes' acts and subjective elements, when an accused may nevertheless not be liable, and when states may prosecute or bring accused persons to trial.
  - term: procedural limb
    gloss: Rules for pre-trial, trial and appeals, rights of the accused, evidence, sentencing and victims' rights, found in each tribunal's statute and rules.
  - term: hybrid branch
    gloss: ICL draws unique characteristics from international humanitarian law, international human rights law and national criminal laws.
