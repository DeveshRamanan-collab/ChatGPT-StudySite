plain: Using force to stop mass atrocities in another state has no basis in the UN Charter. R2P recasts it as a responsibility — but force still runs through the Security Council.
concrete: In 1999, with ethnic cleansing of Albanians in Kosovo and Russia threatening a veto, NATO bombed Yugoslavia without Security Council authorisation. The UK argued the action was legal "as an exceptional measure on grounds of overwhelming humanitarian necessity". A draft resolution calling it a flagrant violation of the Charter's arts 2(4), 24 and 53 did not pass. The Independent International Commission on Kosovo called the intervention "illegal, yet legitimate".
formal: "Humanitarian intervention is not addressed in the UN Charter and appears to conflict with art 2(4). The ICISS (2001) shifted from a right to intervene to a responsibility to protect: sovereignty implies responsibility to protect one's people from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity; if the state is unwilling or unable, an international responsibility arises. 2005 World Summit Outcome: three pillars. ILA (2018): a \"legal grey area\"."
terms:
  - term: humanitarian intervention
    gloss: Force to protect people in another state from gross and systematic human rights violations; not addressed in the UN Charter.
  - term: responsibility to protect (R2P)
    gloss: Sovereignty implies responsibility to protect one's population from four crimes; if the state is unwilling or unable, the international community responds through the UN.
  - term: three pillars
    gloss: (1) the state's primary duty to its population, citizens or not; (2) international assistance and capacity-building; (3) response through UN mechanisms.
  - term: prevent, react, rebuild
    gloss: The international responsibility in the ICISS report; within "react", military force is a last resort.
