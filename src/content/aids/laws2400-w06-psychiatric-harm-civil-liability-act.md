plain: "In NSW, a mental harm claim must pass the statute: a person of normal fortitude must foreseeably develop a recognised psychiatric illness, and pure shock claims are limited to witnesses and close family."
concrete: "In January 2003 a passenger train derailed near Waterfall, killing seven people and injuring many. Police officers Wicks and Sheehan were called to the scene, spent hours helping, and later suffered psychiatric injury. The lower courts held they had not witnessed victims being killed, injured or put in peril. The High Court disagreed: survivors remained in peril until rescued, so the officers could satisfy s 30(2)(a)."
formal: "CLA Part 3: s 27 defines pure and consequential mental harm. Section 32: no duty unless the defendant ought to have foreseen a person of normal fortitude might suffer a recognised psychiatric illness, considering shock, witnessing at the scene, relationship to the victim and any pre-existing relationship. Section 31: pure mental harm must be a recognised psychiatric illness. Section 30: pure shock claims need a scene witness or close family."

  - term: Mental harm (s 27)
    gloss: Impairment of a person's mental condition.
  - term: Pure vs consequential mental harm
    gloss: Consequential mental harm is a consequence of a personal injury of any other kind; pure mental harm is any other mental harm.
  - term: Normal fortitude
    gloss: "The s 32 benchmark: would a person of ordinary mental robustness foreseeably suffer a recognised psychiatric illness?"
  - term: Close member of the family (s 30(5))
    gloss: A parent or person with parental responsibility, spouse or partner, child or stepchild, or a sibling, half-sibling or step-sibling of the victim.
