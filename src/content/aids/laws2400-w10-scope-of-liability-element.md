plain: Even if the negligence caused the harm, the court still asks whether the defendant ought to answer for it. A later event can put the harm outside the defendant's liability.
concrete: "McKew's leg, injured through the defendant's negligence, was prone to give way. He went down a steep staircase without a handrail, unaided; the leg gave way and he suffered a severe ankle fracture. The first injury set up the fall, yet his own unreasonable conduct was a novus actus interveniens: the chain was broken, and the defendant was not liable for the second injury."
formal: "CLA s 5D(1)(b): 'that it is appropriate for the scope of the negligent person's liability to extend to the harm so caused'. Section 5D(4): the court is to consider (amongst other relevant things) whether or not and why responsibility for the harm should be imposed on the negligent party. The test comes from the common law: new intervening acts and remoteness (Topic 11)."
terms:
  - term: Normative question
    gloss: "A question about what ought to be: is it appropriate for liability to extend to this harm? Scope of liability is normative; factual causation is not."
  - term: Novus actus interveniens
    gloss: "A later event that exacerbates the original harm or causes new harm: the plaintiff's own act, a third party's act, or a natural event."
  - term: Breaking the chain
    gloss: If an intervening act breaks the chain of causation, the original wrongdoer is not liable for harm after the break.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Scope of liability is another way to prove causation when factual causation fails.
    tempting: The flowchart does have a fallback route for gap cases.
    correction: That fallback is s 5D(2), inside factual causation. Scope of liability is a second element, needed as well as factual causation, not instead of it.
  - claim: Any careless act by the plaintiff after the breach breaks the chain of causation.
    tempting: McKew's own conduct broke the chain.
    correction: Not every later careless act severs the chain. In March v Stramare the defendant's conduct generated the very risk that materialised, so the drunk driver's negligence did not break it.
  - claim: If the chain is broken, the original wrongdoer escapes liability for all the harm.
    tempting: "'Breaking the chain' sounds total."
    correction: The original wrongdoer is not liable for harm after the break. In McKew the defendant was not liable for the second injury, the ankle fracture.
