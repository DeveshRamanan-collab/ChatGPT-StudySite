misconceptions:
  - claim: "Article 38(1) ranks the sources: treaty beats custom, and custom beats general principles."
    tempting: They are listed (a) to (d), which looks like an order of authority.
    correction: The ordering tracks primary (law-creating) versus secondary (law-interpreting). A treaty and a customary rule can bind the same states at once; general principles fill gaps.
  - claim: Article 38(1) lists every source of international law.
    tempting: It is the first place of reference and courts rely on it constantly.
    correction: It was drafted to tell the ICJ what to apply, not to catalogue sources. Unilateral statements, jus cogens, IO decisions and soft law all sit outside it.
  - claim: Because judicial decisions appear in Article 38(1), an ICJ decision creates law like a treaty.
    tempting: In common law systems, case law is a primary source through precedent.
    correction: "Judicial decisions are secondary sources. Article 59: a decision has no binding force except between the parties and in respect of that particular case."
