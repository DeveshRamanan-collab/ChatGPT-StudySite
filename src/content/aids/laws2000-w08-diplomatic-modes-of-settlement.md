plain: Diplomatic modes — negotiation, good offices, mediation, inquiry, conciliation — help states settle disputes themselves. Third parties may help, but nothing they produce binds the states.
concrete: Timor-Leste and Australia disagreed over their maritime boundary in the Timor Sea. A Conciliation Commission was set up under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. It assessed the context and each party's position and interests, and proposed a resolution. Its recommendations bound neither state, but the parties used them, and the process led to a treaty delimiting the boundary and sharing transboundary resources.
formal: "Negotiation: good faith, a constructive atmosphere, refraining from undermining conduct (GA Res 53/101, 1998); it presupposes \"a genuine attempt … to engage in discussions … with a view to resolving the dispute\" (North Sea Continental Shelf, para 85). Good offices facilitate direct negotiations; mediation suggests a framework of settlement, not binding; inquiry finds facts; conciliation reports facts and positions and suggests a solution in non-binding recommendations."

terms:
  - term: good offices
    gloss: A neutral third party trusted by both sides facilitates efforts to enter into direct negotiations (e.g. the UN good offices mission in Lebanon).
  - term: mediation
    gloss: A third party takes a more direct role and suggests a framework of settlement or possible solutions — not binding (Richard Holbrooke, Bosnian War).
  - term: inquiry (fact-finding)
    gloss: Used when the parties disagree over the facts; usually ends in a non-binding report (UN Commission of Inquiry in Syria).
  - term: conciliation
    gloss: A third party, e.g. a group of experts, reports on facts and positions and suggests a solution in non-binding recommendations.
misconceptions:
  - claim: A mediator's proposals bind the parties, whereas good offices do not.
    tempting: Mediation is the more active role.
    correction: The solutions offered by a mediator are not binding. Neither mode binds; they differ in how far the third party goes — facilitating talks, or suggesting a framework.
  - claim: A conciliation report binds once both states have appointed the conciliators.
    tempting: The parties chose the experts themselves.
    correction: Conciliation ends in non-binding recommendations; parties may use the report to negotiate but are not bound. A binding result needs arbitration or judicial settlement.
  - claim: The modes must be used one at a time, in the order art 33(1) lists them.
    tempting: Art 33(1) starts with negotiation and the lecture follows that order.
    correction: No sequence is required, and the modes are often combined — e.g. mediation with a fact-finding mission.
