plain: When two wrongdoers are liable for the same damage, the plaintiff can recover everything from either one. The one who pays can then claim a share from the other.
concrete: Chapman's car collided with another vehicle near Adelaide, and he was thrown onto the road, unconscious. Dr Cherry stopped to help him and was struck and killed by Hearse's car. Dr Cherry's executor sued Hearse, who joined Chapman as a third party, claiming a contribution from him. The trial judge found Hearse negligent and held Chapman liable to contribute one-fourth of the damages; the High Court dismissed Chapman's appeal.
formal: "Where multiple tortfeasors are jointly and severally liable for damage, the plaintiff may recover the whole amount against only one of them, except where proportionate liability applies to property damage and economic loss claims (Tort Law Principles [12.170]). A tortfeasor liable for the full amount may seek contribution from another: in NSW, Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1946 s 5(1)(c), (2)."

terms:
  - term: Joint and several liability
    gloss: Each tortfeasor liable for the same damage can be made to pay the whole amount; the plaintiff may choose whom to recover from, eg the one with "deeper pockets".
  - term: Contribution
    gloss: The statutory mechanism by which a tortfeasor held liable for the full amount seeks a contribution from another tortfeasor. It in no way affects the plaintiff.
  - term: Concurrent tortfeasors
    gloss: Joint or separate wrongdoers whose torts contribute to the same damage. The risk of one being unable to pay lies with the others, not the plaintiff.
  - term: Proportionate liability
    gloss: The textbook's footnoted qualification for property damage and economic loss claims; the provisions themselves are not in these materials.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Contribution reduces what the plaintiff recovers by the other tortfeasor's share.
    tempting: Contribution divides the burden of the damages between the wrongdoers.
    correction: "Contribution in no way affects the plaintiff: it is a matter between the tortfeasors themselves. The plaintiff may recover the whole amount from one."
  - claim: If one tortfeasor is insolvent, the plaintiff loses that share of the damages.
    tempting: That tortfeasor's share simply goes unpaid.
    correction: Where there are joint or separate concurrent tortfeasors, the risk that one cannot pay lies with the other tortfeasors, not the plaintiff.
  - claim: Contribution is the same thing as contributory negligence.
    tempting: Both come from 'contribute', and both shift who bears the loss.
    correction: Contributory negligence reduces the plaintiff's damages for their own share of fault (the 70% finding at trial in March v Stramare). Contribution is a claim between tortfeasors.

boundary:

    why: "A different mechanism: each defendant pays the plaintiff a portion matching its market share, because no one can say whose device caused the harm. Contribution assumes each is liable for the same damage."
transfer:
  - scenario: A ride operator and the company that serviced the ride are both negligent, and a rider is injured. The rider sues only the operator, which is insured, and recovers in full. The servicing company has since gone out of business.
    answer: If they are jointly and severally liable for the same damage, the rider may recover the whole amount from the operator alone. The operator can seek contribution from the servicing company under the Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1946 (NSW) s 5(1)(c), (2); if that company cannot pay, the risk lies with the operator, not the rider. Contribution in no way affects the rider.
