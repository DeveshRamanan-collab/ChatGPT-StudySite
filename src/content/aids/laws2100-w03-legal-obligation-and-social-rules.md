plain: "Being forced is not the same as being under an obligation. Hart explains legal obligation through social rules: standards people accept and use to criticise deviation, not just threats or habits."
concrete: "A gunman and a tax officer both demand your money, with bad consequences if you refuse. Factually they look alike. Yet only the tax demand creates an obligation, and someone who never pays tax is still under an obligation to pay. Hart's explanation: the tax demand rests on a rule people accept as a standard; the gunman's rests on fear alone."
formal: "Where there is law, 'human conduct is made in some sense non-optional or obligatory', yet there is 'a difference, yet to be explained' between being obliged and having an obligation (CL 82).
