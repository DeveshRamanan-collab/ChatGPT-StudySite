plain: Acting under a lawful power, such as a valid arrest or a lawful entry onto land, is a defence to what would otherwise be a trespass.
concrete: A police officer entered the curtilage of Ms Roy's unit to check compliance with a domestic violence order. The High Court held 3:2 in Roy v O'Neill that this entry was within the implied licence from Halliday v Nevill. At the door, the officer observed that Roy was intoxicated. That observation enlivened a statutory power to remain for a breath test (Police Administration Act 1978 (NT) s 126(2A)), giving independent authority to stay.
formal: Lawful arrest is a defence to trespass to the person. Under LEPRA s 99(1), arrest without warrant requires BOTH suspicion on reasonable grounds that the person is committing or has committed an offence AND satisfaction that arrest is reasonably necessary for a listed reason; ss 230–231 allow only reasonably necessary force. Entry may rest on the implied licence (Halliday v Nevill); remaining may need a separate power (Roy v O'Neill).
terms:
  - term: Reasonable suspicion
    gloss: "Suspicion on reasonable grounds: more than a possibility, less than a belief."
  - term: Implied licence
    gloss: Permission for anyone, including police, to use an unobstructed path or driveway for lawful communication with the occupier (Halliday v Nevill).
  - term: Independent authority to remain
    gloss: A separate statutory power that justifies staying on land, so the stay no longer depends on the implied licence.
    example: "Roy v O'Neill: the NT breath-test power, enlivened by observing intoxication."
misconceptions:
  - claim: Reasonable suspicion of an offence is enough for a lawful arrest without warrant.
    tempting: Reasonable suspicion is the familiar trigger for police action.
    correction: Section 99(1) LEPRA requires BOTH reasonable suspicion AND satisfaction that arrest is reasonably necessary for one of the listed reasons.
  - claim: Once an arrest is lawful, police may use whatever force it takes to complete it.
    tempting: A lawful arrest seems to authorise the steps needed to carry it out.
    correction: Sections 230–231 LEPRA permit only such force as is reasonably necessary.
  - claim: The implied licence let the officer in Roy v O'Neill stay on the land to breath-test the occupier.
    tempting: The High Court upheld both the entry and the officer's staying.
    correction: The implied licence (Halliday v Nevill) covered the entry; the power to remain came independently from the statutory breath-test power, enlivened by observing Roy's intoxication.

  - scenario: A council officer walks up a resident's open driveway to hand over a notice, then, relying on no other power, goes into the fenced backyard to photograph the trees.
    answer: Walking up an unobstructed driveway for lawful communication with the occupier is within the implied licence (Halliday v Nevill), so the approach is excused. Unless a separate statutory power gives independent authority, as in Roy v O'Neill, legal process gives no defence to trespass there.
