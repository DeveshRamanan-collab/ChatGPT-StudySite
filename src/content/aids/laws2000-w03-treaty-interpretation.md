plain: To interpret a treaty, read its words in their ordinary meaning, in context and in light of its purpose; only if that leaves things unclear or absurd do you turn to the drafting history.
concrete: "In the South China Sea Arbitration the tribunal had to decide whether features such as the Spratly Islands were 'islands' or 'rocks' under art 121 of UNCLOS. It mattered enormously: a rock gets only 12 nautical miles, an island a full 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone, and other features nothing. Using both art 31 and art 32 means, the tribunal held the Spratly Islands were not legally islands."
formal: "VCLT art 31(1): \"A treaty shall be interpreted in good faith in accordance with the ordinary meaning to be given to the terms of the treaty in their context and in the light of its object and purpose.\" Art 32: supplementary means — preparatory work and circumstances of conclusion — to confirm that meaning, or where art 31 leaves it ambiguous, obscure, manifestly absurd or unreasonable. Both reflect custom."

terms:
  - term: ordinary meaning
    gloss: The common sense of a treaty's terms, read in good faith — the starting point under art 31(1); a special meaning applies if the parties intended one.
  - term: context
    gloss: The treaty text as a whole, its preamble and annexes, and any agreement or instrument made in connection with its conclusion.
  - term: travaux préparatoires
    gloss: The treaty's preparatory work — drafts and negotiating records — a supplementary means of interpretation under art 32.
  - term: teleological interpretation
    gloss: Reading a treaty's terms in the light of its overall object and purpose.

    tempting: "'Good faith' sounds like sincerity."

    correction: They reflect customary international law, recognised by the ICJ, so they govern the interpretation of treaties involving non-parties too.
