plain: "Hart describes two extremes about judges: the Nightmare, that judges always make law; and the Noble Dream, that they never do. Hart thinks both exaggerate."
concrete: Litigants come to court expecting the existing law to be applied to their dispute, not new law made for them. The Nightmare, pressed by American legal realism, is that this picture of the judge as different from the legislator is an illusion. The Noble Dream answers that even when statutes and precedents give no determinate guide, there is still an existing law somewhere for judges to apply.
