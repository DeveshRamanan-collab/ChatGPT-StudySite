plain: The same rule can live in a treaty and in custom at once; a treaty can record an existing custom, finish forming a developing one, or start a new one.
concrete: "If Australia were attacked by another state tomorrow, the lecturer says, it would have a right of self-defence. That right exists as customary international law and is also written into Article 51 of the UN Charter. Because the customary rule exists in parallel, even a state not party to the Charter would have a right of self-defence: the treaty is not the rule's only home."
formal: A rule may be contained in a treaty and be a rule of custom simultaneously (Nicaragua, ICJ Rep 1986, paras 175–178; see also North Sea Continental Shelf). A treaty may (a) codify a pre-existing rule of custom, (b) crystallise a developing rule of custom, or (c) give rise to a new rule of customary law. The two versions keep separate existences and may differ in content.

terms:
  - term: codification
    gloss: A treaty writing down a pre-existing customary rule; the customary rule continues to exist alongside it.
  - term: crystallisation
    gloss: A treaty prompting the intense state practice that completes the formation of a developing customary rule.
  - term: generating new custom
    gloss: Practice developing around a treaty provision on a genuinely new issue, giving rise to a new customary rule that can bind non-parties.
