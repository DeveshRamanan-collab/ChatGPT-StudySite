plain: The ICC applies its own Statute first, then international law, then general principles drawn from national laws. It has no police, so it depends on states that are bound to co-operate.
concrete: In Resolution 1970 (2011) the Security Council referred Libya to the ICC Prosecutor. It decided that the Libyan authorities "shall cooperate fully" with the Court; but, recognising that states not party to the Rome Statute have no obligation under it, it only "urges" all states and concerned organisations. It also recognised that the UN would bear none of the referral's expenses.
formal: "Art 21: the Court applies first the Statute, Elements of Crimes and Rules; second, applicable treaties and principles and rules of international law; failing that, general principles derived from national laws — consistently with internationally recognised human rights. Art 86: States Parties \"cooperate fully\"; art 88: national procedures. Art 12(3) declarants co-operate \"without any delay or exception\". UN Charter art 25 binds members to Council decisions."
terms:
  - term: Elements of Crimes
    gloss: One of the Court's own instruments, applied "in the first place" with the Statute and the Rules of Procedure and Evidence (art 21).
  - term: general principles of law
    gloss: Principles the Court derives from national laws of the world's legal systems; the last, gap-filling source in art 21.
  - term: duty to co-operate
    gloss: States Parties must co-operate fully (art 86); declarants under art 12(3) and states bound by a Chapter VII decision must also co-operate.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Every UN member must co-operate with the ICC under art 86.
    tempting: All states had to co-operate with the ICTY.
    correction: Art 86 binds States Parties. A non-party is bound only by an art 12(3) declaration or by a Chapter VII Security Council decision (art 25 UN Charter) — Res 1970 merely urged other states.
  - claim: General principles of law rank above the Rome Statute.
    tempting: They are a primary source under art 38(1) of the ICJ Statute.
    correction: "Art 21 ranks its sources: the Statute, Elements and Rules first; treaties and international law second; general principles from national laws only \"failing that\"."
  - claim: The ICC is strictly bound by its previous decisions, as in common-law precedent.
    tempting: Australian courts follow precedent.
    correction: Art 21(2) says the Court "may apply" principles and rules as interpreted in its previous decisions — permissive, not binding.
