plain: For Hart, a legal system is the union of primary rules, which impose duties, and secondary rules, which say how the primary rules are identified, changed and enforced.
concrete: Imagine an island community run only by customs. When people disagree about what a custom requires, they ask the elders, who know the traditions (recognition). When they discover they can fish, they call a general assembly to make a new fishing rule (change). When disputes drag on, they appoint a third party to decide them (adjudication). Each fix is a rule about the rules.
formal: "A regime of primary rules of obligation alone is uncertain, static and inefficient. 'The remedy for each of these three main defects ... consists in supplementing the primary rules of obligation with secondary rules which are rules of a different kind' (CL 94): uncertainty, rule of recognition; static quality, rules of change; inefficiency, rules of adjudication. The union is 'the heart of a legal system' (CL 98)."

terms:
  - term: Primary rules of obligation
    gloss: Rules that impose duties, requiring or forbidding conduct.
  - term: Secondary rules
    gloss: "'Rules of a different kind' (CL 94): rules about primary rules, saying how they are identified, changed and authoritatively applied."
  - term: Rules of change
    gloss: Rules empowering someone to create new rules or change existing ones, remedying the static quality of primary rules.
  - term: Rules of adjudication
    gloss: Rules authorising a third party to settle disputes about breaches of the rules, remedying inefficiency.
