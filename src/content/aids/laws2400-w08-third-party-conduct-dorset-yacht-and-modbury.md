plain: You can owe a duty for harm someone else causes, but usually only if you controlled that person, had a special relationship, or created the risk yourself.
concrete: "Boys escaped from detention run by the Home Office and caused significant damage to several yachts in the nearby harbour. The yacht owners sued the Home Office. The House of Lords held a duty was owed: damage on escape was reasonably foreseeable, and the officers had control over the boys. For policy reasons, the duty covered damage in the vicinity immediately after the escape, not later damage while the boys remained at large."
formal: "A duty for a third party's conduct is found only in certain circumstances, usually a pre-existing relationship (defendant–plaintiff or defendant–third party) or a situation of control. Home Office v Dorset Yacht [1970]: duty owed through control, limited in scope by policy. Modbury Triangle v Anzil (2000): no duty to protect an employee from a third party's crime; the centre had not created the risk, and there was no special relationship."

terms:
  - term: Third party
    gloss: The person whose conduct actually causes the plaintiff's harm, but who is not the defendant sued, often because they have no money.
  - term: Creating vs failing to prevent
    gloss: "Modbury's distinction: a defendant who creates a risk may owe a duty; one who merely fails to prevent harm caused by someone else usually does not."
misconceptions:

    tempting: Foreseeability is the paradigm test for duty.
    correction: "Foreseeability is not enough: Modbury shows a relationship or control is needed. There is general reluctance to make defendants protect others from third parties' criminal acts."
  - claim: Dorset Yacht makes a custodian liable for everything escapees do until caught.
    tempting: Control founded the duty, so it seems to last as long as the escape.
    correction: Policy limited the scope to damage in the vicinity immediately after escape; no duty for further damage while at large (indeterminate liability).
  - claim: Third-party duty is vicarious liability for the third party's tort.
    tempting: The defendant pays for what someone else did.
    correction: It is the defendant's own duty, arising from control or a relationship. The Home Office did not employ the boys; it controlled them.

  - ask: In Modbury, the centre switched off the car park lights and an employee was attacked. Why no duty?
    hint: Who created the risk, and was there control or a relationship?
