plain: An act that would normally be a tort can be excused if it was needed to prevent serious harm and was a reasonable, proportionate response. Courts keep this defence narrow.
concrete: "In Proudman v Allen, the defendant saw a driverless car rolling down a slope towards another car. He turned its steering wheel to avoid the collision, and the runaway car rolled into the sea. Its owner sued. The defendant succeeded on necessity: he acted to protect property from harm, and the defence is available even to strangers who act to protect the property of others."
formal: Necessity may be available if the action was necessary to prevent serious harm to person or property, and was reasonable in the circumstances and proportionate to the risk. The doctrine "must be carefully circumscribed" (Southwark v Williams). In medicine (In re F), it must not be practicable to communicate with the assisted person, and the action must be what a reasonable person would take in their best interests.

  maps:
    - from: A real fire

  limits:

    - In medicine, necessity can justify ongoing treatment of a permanently incapacitated patient, not just a one-off emergency (In re F).
terms:
  - term: Public necessity
    gloss: Action to save lives or protect the public, such as throwing luggage from a sinking ship to save lives.
  - term: Necessity to protect property
    gloss: Action to protect property from harm, such as trespassing on a neighbour's land to rescue your cat.
  - term: Best interests
    gloss: "Lord Goff in In re F: the action must be what a reasonable person would take, acting in the best interests of the assisted person."
  - term: Temporary vs permanent incapacity
    gloss: "Temporary: do no more than is necessary before the patient recovers consciousness. Permanent: no point waiting for consent; act in the patient's best interests."
misconceptions:
  - claim: Desperate need, such as homelessness, justifies entering empty property under necessity.
    tempting: The need is real and serious, and the houses were standing empty.
    correction: "Southwark v Williams: the doctrine must be carefully circumscribed. If homelessness were admitted as a defence to trespass, \"no one's house would be safe\"; necessity is confined to urgent situations of imminent peril."
  - claim: Only the owner of threatened property can rely on necessity to protect it.
    tempting: Protecting property sounds like the owner's business alone.
    correction: "Proudman v Allen: even volunteers or strangers who act to protect the property of others can use the defence."
  - claim: Necessity is just self-defence applied to property.
    tempting: Both excuse force used to avert harm, and both were pleaded in NSW v McMaster.
    correction: Necessity also covers public necessity and medical treatment (In re F), needs no unlawful attacker, and its test is necessity plus reasonableness and proportionality to the risk.
