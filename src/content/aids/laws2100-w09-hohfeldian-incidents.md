plain: "Hohfeld splits 'right' into four positions: claim (someone owes me a duty), privilege (I have no duty not to act), power (I can change your position), immunity (you cannot change mine)."
concrete: "Take the lecture's example of rights we create: you and I sign a contract. You are under a duty to me to deliver the goods, so I hold a claim-right against you. Nothing obliges me not to inspect them on arrival: a privilege. If I can release you from your duty, I hold a power over your position. If you cannot alter my entitlement on your own, I hold an immunity against you."
formal: "Hohfeld (1879–1918), slide 6: X has a claim-right against Y with respect to φ iff Y is under a duty to X to φ; a privilege to φ iff X has no duty not to φ; a (normative) power over Y iff X can alter, through volitional action, Y's normative position; an immunity against Y iff Y lacks the ability to alter X's Hohfeldian incidents."
terms:
  - term: Claim-right
    gloss: X has a claim-right against Y with respect to φ if and only if Y is under a duty to X to φ.
    example: "Under a lease, the landlord must repair the roof: the tenant holds a claim-right."
  - term: Privilege
    gloss: X has a privilege to φ if and only if X has no duty not to φ. The definition is silent on whether others must let X φ.
    example: No law forbids walking on a public beach.
  - term: Power
    gloss: X has a (normative) power over Y if and only if X can alter, through volitional action, Y's normative position.
    example: Making a will changes what the executor will be required to do.
  - term: Immunity
    gloss: X holds an immunity against Y (in respect of X's normative position) if and only if Y lacks the ability to alter X's Hohfeldian incidents.
    example: A constitution bars Parliament from removing freedom of religion.
misconceptions:
  - claim: A privilege means others are under a duty not to interfere with what I do.
    tempting: We usually think of freedoms as protected.
    correction: That would be a claim-right against others. On slide 6, X has a privilege to φ iff X has no duty not to φ; the definition says nothing about anyone else's duty.
  - claim: An immunity is X's ability to alter Y's normative position.
    tempting: Power and immunity both concern altering positions.
    correction: "That is a power. X holds an immunity against Y when Y lacks the ability to alter X's Hohfeldian incidents: the mirror case."
  - claim: "Hohfeld's analysis is just the will theory: having a right means controlling others."
    tempting: Control sounds like a power.
    correction: The will theory says what makes something a right (control or choice). Hohfeld shows that 'right' names four distinct positions, of which a power is only one.
