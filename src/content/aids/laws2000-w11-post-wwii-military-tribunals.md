plain: After World War II the Allies tried Axis leaders before two international military tribunals — Nuremberg and Tokyo — and held thousands of further trials in occupied zones and across the Asia-Pacific.
concrete: The Tokyo tribunal (IMTFE) was set up by proclamation of US General Douglas MacArthur, implementing the Potsdam Declaration, to try Japanese officials. Twenty-eight defendants faced 55 counts of crimes against peace, conventional war crimes and crimes against humanity. All were found guilty, though not on every count; seven were sentenced to death and the rest to imprisonment. It largely agreed with Nuremberg on the law.
formal: The London Agreement, with the IMT Charter annexed, created the Nuremberg IMT; art 6 listed crimes against peace, war crimes and crimes against humanity "for which there shall be individual responsibility" of leaders, organisers, instigators and accomplices. The IMTFE rested on MacArthur's proclamation (IMTFE Charter art 5(a)–(c)). Control Council Law No 10 allowed domestic prosecutions in the Allied zones of occupation.
terms:
  - term: crimes against peace
    gloss: Planning, preparing, initiating or waging a war of aggression or a war in violation of treaties, or conspiring to do so (IMT Charter art 6(A)).
  - term: war crimes
    gloss: Violations of the laws or customs of war — e.g. ill-treatment of civilians or prisoners of war, killing hostages, plunder, wanton destruction (art 6(B)).
  - term: crimes against humanity
    gloss: Murder, extermination, enslavement, deportation and other inhumane acts against any civilian population, or persecutions on political, racial or religious grounds (art 6(C)).
  - term: Control Council Law No 10
    gloss: The basis for domestic prosecutions in the Allied zones of occupation — best known for twelve US trials by the Nuremberg Military Tribunals.

    tempting: Genocide is the crime most associated with the Holocaust.
    correction: Art 6 listed crimes against peace, war crimes and crimes against humanity. Genocide appears in later instruments — the 1948 Genocide Convention, the ICTR Statute, the Rome Statute.

    tempting: Criminal law usually follows the law of the place.
    correction: Art 6(C) covered such acts "whether or not in violation of the domestic law of the country where perpetrated".
