plain: Lawyers argue with more than rules. Rules apply all-or-nothing; principles give reasons with weight, grounded in justice or fairness; policies set goals for the community.
concrete: "Dworkin's pair: 'the standard that automobile accidents are to be decreased is a policy, and the standard that no man may profit by his own wrong a principle'. The second is observed not because it makes society better off but because justice requires it. In Riggs v Palmer it weighed against the will statutes, which read literally gave Elmer his murdered grandfather's estate."
formal: "Principle: a standard observed 'not because it will advance or secure an economic, political, or social situation deemed desirable, but because it is a requirement of justice or fairness or some other dimension of morality'. Policy: a standard 'that sets out a goal to be reached' (TRS 22). Rules and principles differ by 'a logical distinction' in 'the character of the direction they give' (TRS 24)."

terms:
  - term: Principle
    gloss: A standard observed because it is a requirement of justice, fairness or another dimension of morality, not because it secures a desirable social state (TRS 22).
  - term: Policy
    gloss: A standard setting a goal, generally an improvement in an economic, political or social feature of the community; some protect a present feature from adverse change.
  - term: All-or-nothing
    gloss: "How rules apply: if A then B. If the facts meet the rule, it settles the outcome; if not, it contributes nothing."
  - term: Dimension of weight
    gloss: "The importance principles have and rules lack: conflicting principles are weighed; with conflicting rules, one must give way."
