plain: Jus ad bellum asks whether a state may resort to force at all; jus in bello governs how force is used once fighting has begun. This unit studies the first.
concrete: "The lecturer's way to remember it: \"ad bellum\" is the law before war — when a state is allowed to go to war and use force. \"In bello\" is the law in war — what is permitted once hostilities have started, the territory of the Geneva Conventions, also called international humanitarian law or the law of armed conflict. So whether State A may strike State B at all is an ad bellum question; whether A's pilots must spare civilians is in bello."
formal: "Jus ad bellum: the law relating to the resort to force and war. Jus in bello: the laws regulating behaviour during hostilities (international humanitarian law). Earlier limits on war: Aquinas's just war conditions; the Hague Conventions (1899, 1907); the League of Nations (1920–1945); the Kellogg-Briand Pact (1928). The UN Charter (1945) aims \"to save succeeding generations from the scourge of war\"."
terms:
  - term: jus ad bellum
    gloss: "The law on the resort to force: whether a state may use force at all — today arts 2(4), 42 and 51 of the UN Charter."
  - term: jus in bello
    gloss: The law regulating behaviour during hostilities — international humanitarian law, or the law of armed conflict.
  - term: just war doctrine
    gloss: "Aquinas (13th century): a war is just with the right authority (auctoritas), a just purpose (causa justa) and the right intent (recta intentio)."

misconceptions:
  - claim: Jus ad bellum governs how hostilities are conducted.
    tempting: Both are Latin phrases about war.
    correction: That is jus in bello (international humanitarian law). Jus ad bellum asks whether force may be resorted to at all.
  - claim: A state acting in lawful self-defence may fight however it likes.
    tempting: Its resort to force was lawful.
    correction: Whether it could resort to force (jus ad bellum) and how it conducts hostilities (jus in bello) are separate questions; conduct is judged by international humanitarian law.
  - claim: The Kellogg-Briand Pact prohibited the threat or use of force.
    tempting: It outlawed war.
    correction: It outlawed war as a means of national policy, except for self-defence. The "threat or use of force" formula is art 2(4) of the UN Charter (1945).
