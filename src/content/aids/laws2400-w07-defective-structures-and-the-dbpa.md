plain: When a hidden building defect lowers a later owner's property value, the common law asked whether a duty was owed. In NSW, a 2020 statute now gives owners that duty directly.
concrete: A builder built a house with defective foundations. Years later Mrs Maloney, the third owner since construction, noticed cracks in the walls caused by the foundations. Her loss was the reduced value of the house, not an injury. In Bryan v Maloney the High Court held the builder owed a subsequent purchaser a duty to take reasonable care in building the house, using the proximity principle and treating homeowners as more vulnerable.
formal: "Bryan v Maloney (1995): a house builder owed a subsequent purchaser a duty. Woolcock v CDG (2004): no duty to a commercial purchaser: no reliance or assumption of responsibility, and not vulnerable. Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 (NSW) s 37: a person who carries out construction work has a duty to exercise reasonable care to avoid economic loss caused by defects, owed to each owner and each subsequent owner."
terms:
  - term: Latent defect
    gloss: A defect not identifiable at or soon after the build, discovered years later, often by a later owner; the reason tort comes into the picture.
  - term: Construction work (s 36)
    gloss: "Defined broadly: building work, preparing designs, manufacturing or supplying building products, and supervision, coordination or project management."
  - term: Non-delegable duty
    gloss: A duty that stays with the person even if they use subcontractors (s 39); the s 37 duty also cannot be contracted out (s 40).
misconceptions:

    tempting: Both cases involve a later owner and a latent defect.
    correction: "Woolcock went the other way: no duty, because there was no reliance or assumption of responsibility and the commercial purchaser was not vulnerable."
  - claim: Bryan v Maloney applied the salient features approach and the statutory duty.
    tempting: Both appear in this topic.
    correction: Bryan (1995) used the proximity principle. The statutory, non-delegable duty comes from the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020, decades later.
  - claim: Under the DBPA, only the original owner who paid the builder can sue.
    tempting: Building disputes are usually contract disputes between builder and client.
    correction: "Section 37(2): the duty is owed to each owner of the land and to each subsequent owner."
