plain: While a case is pending, the ICJ can order interim steps to prevent irreparable harm to the rights in dispute. Those orders bind the parties.
concrete: "The tutorial's facts: State A sues State B under the Genocide Convention, alleging B has incarcerated disproportionate numbers of a minority group, forcibly displaced large parts of it, and enabled the destruction of its villages and sacred sites. A final judgment may take years. If the destruction continues meanwhile, the judgment could come too late to matter. Provisional measures fill that gap: interim orders preserving the rights in dispute until the Court decides."
formal: "Art 41 ICJ Statute: the Court may \"indicate, if it considers that circumstances so require, any provisional measures which ought to be taken to preserve the respective rights of either party\". Conditions: absence of jurisdiction not manifest; rights at least plausible; a link between those rights and the measures; a real and imminent risk of irreparable harm before the final decision. They bind (LaGrand, 2001, para 102)."

terms:
  - term: provisional measures
    gloss: Interim orders under art 41 preserving the parties' rights pending the final decision, so the judgment is not rendered meaningless.
  - term: prima facie jurisdiction
    gloss: At this stage the Court need only be satisfied that the absence of jurisdiction is not manifest.
  - term: irreparable harm
    gloss: Harm the final judgment could not undo; there must be a real and imminent risk of it before the decision.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Provisional measures are only recommendations, because art 41 says "indicate".
    tempting: "\"Indicate\" sounds weaker than \"order\"."
    correction: "LaGrand (2001, para 102): the power to indicate measures \"entails that such measures should be binding\"; the contrary would defeat the object and purpose of art 41."
  - claim: The Court must first establish its jurisdiction over the merits.
    tempting: Jurisdiction normally comes first.
    correction: It need only be satisfied that the absence of jurisdiction is not manifest, and that the rights claimed are at least plausible.
  - claim: Any risk of financial loss justifies provisional measures.
    tempting: Loss is harm.
    correction: There must be a real and imminent risk of irreparable harm before the final decision; ordinary, compensable loss does not suffice.

  - ask: State A fears State B will destroy the minority group's villages and sacred sites before judgment. What can A ask for?
    hint: Art 41.
    answer: Provisional measures to preserve the rights in dispute pending the final decision.
  - ask: Must the Court first decide that it has jurisdiction under art IX?
    hint: Manifest?
    answer: No — only that the absence of jurisdiction is not manifest. If both are parties to the Convention, art IX gives at least that.
  - ask: What must A show about its rights?
    hint: Plausible.
    answer: That the rights for which protection is sought — here under the Genocide Convention — are at least plausible.
  - ask: Where is the urgency?
    hint: Irreparable.
    answer: "Destroyed villages and sacred sites, or harm to the group, cannot be undone by a later judgment: a real and imminent risk of irreparable harm."
  - ask: A also asks the Court to halt B's unrelated national elections. Likely?
    hint: Link.
    answer: No. There must be a link between the rights claimed and the measures; the measures must be appropriate and necessary to protect those rights.
