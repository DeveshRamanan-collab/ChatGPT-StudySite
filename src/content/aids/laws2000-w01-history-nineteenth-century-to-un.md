plain: From the 1800s, law between states was seen as coming only from state consent; after two world wars, states built institutions — the League, the PCIJ, then the UN — to keep the peace.
concrete: "After the First World War, the League of Nations (1919) did not ban war, but required members to submit disputes likely to lead to conflict to adjudication or inquiry. The 1928 Kellogg-Briand Pact went further: states committed to refrain from war as an instrument of national policy. The Second World War still followed, and in 1946 the League was replaced by the United Nations, whose Charter bans the use of force."

The 1928 Kellogg-Briand Pact obliged states to refrain from war as a means of settling controversies and as an instrument of national policy. The UN Charter entrenched sovereign equality, self-determination and a ban on force.
