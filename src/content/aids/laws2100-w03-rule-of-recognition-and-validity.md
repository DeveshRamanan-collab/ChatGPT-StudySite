plain: "For Hart, a rule is valid law if it passes the tests set by the rule of recognition: the accepted practice, kept up by officials and judges, that says where law is found."
concrete: "An NSW lawyer says 'this regulation is valid law in New South Wales'. How does she know? She checks it against the sources the system treats as authoritative: the federal and state constitutions, the relevant legislation, the case law. Why those sources and not others? Because a rule, practised by officials and above all judges, supplies those criteria. That practised rule is the rule of recognition."
formal: A rule of recognition provides 'authoritative criteria for identifying primary rules of obligation' (CL 100). A legal system exists where valid rules are generally obeyed and officials accept the secondary rules (CL 109).
