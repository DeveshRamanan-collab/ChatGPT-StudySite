plain: An international organisation can do only what its members gave it power to do, expressly or by necessary implication — and it answers for its own wrongful acts, separately from its members.
concrete: "UN peacekeeping forces are subsidiary organs of the UN, acting under the control of the Security Council and General Assembly. But they are made up of troops contributed by member states, which keep jurisdiction and residual control over them. So when peacekeepers act wrongfully, who is responsible? ARIO art 7 asks who had effective control over the specific conduct: the UN, or a contributing state that kept control of its soldiers."
formal: "Speciality: IOs \"do not, unlike States, possess a general competence\" (Nuclear Weapons [1996] [25]). Implied powers: \"conferred upon it by necessary implication as being essential to the performance of its duties\" (Reparation, 182). ARIO art 3: every internationally wrongful act of an IO entails its responsibility; art 6: organs' and agents' conduct; art 7: a seconded organ's conduct is the IO's if it \"exercises effective control over that conduct\"."

terms:
  - term: principle of speciality (attribution, conferred powers)
    gloss: "IOs have no general competence: only the powers their members give them, limited by the common interests entrusted to them (Nuclear Weapons [25])."
  - term: implied powers
    gloss: Powers not expressly provided but conferred by necessary implication as essential to the IO's duties (Reparation, 182) — for the UN, bringing a claim.
  - term: effective control (ARIO art 7)
    gloss: "The test for attributing a seconded organ's conduct to an IO: factual, assessed for the specific conduct, in the full circumstances (ILC commentary)."
  - term: ARIO
    gloss: "The ILC's 2011 draft articles on the responsibility of IOs: not binding, modelled on state responsibility, criticised for treating very different IOs alike."
misconceptions:
  - claim: International organisations, like states, have a general competence over any matter not excluded.
    tempting: IOs have international personality, and personality is the state's model.
    correction: IOs "do not, unlike States, possess a general competence" (Nuclear Weapons [25]). They have only the powers their members conferred — the principle of speciality.
  - claim: An IO can only exercise powers expressly written into its constitutive instrument.
    tempting: Speciality limits IOs to conferred powers.
    correction: "Conferred powers include implied ones: the UN \"must be deemed to have those powers which, though not expressly provided in the Charter, are conferred upon it by necessary implication\" (Reparation, 182)."
  - claim: Peacekeepers are UN organs, so their conduct is always attributed to the UN alone.
    tempting: Peacekeeping forces are subsidiary organs acting under Security Council control.
    correction: Contributing states keep jurisdiction and residual control. Under ARIO art 7 the conduct is the UN's only if it had effective control; a state that kept control over the specific conduct can be responsible.

    hint: Does the body have a general competence?
    answer: Not as a general competence — IOs have only conferred powers. But the power may be implied if inspection is essential to performing its quota duties (Reparation, 182).
  - ask: Why could the WHO not have the ICJ address the legality of nuclear weapons?
    hint: Nuclear Weapons [25].
    answer: That question could not be deemed a necessary implication of the WHO's constitution; attributing the competence to it would disregard the principle of speciality.
  - ask: The body's own inspectors wrongfully damage a vessel. Who is responsible — the body or its members?
    hint: Distinct personality; the International Tin Council.
    answer: The body. Every internationally wrongful act of an IO entails its own responsibility (ARIO art 3), and its organs' and agents' conduct in performing their functions is its act (art 6).
  - ask: Now the inspectors are a member state's coast guard placed at the body's disposal, and that state's ministry gave the specific order. Who answers?
    hint: ARIO art 7.
    answer: Art 7 attributes the conduct to the IO only if it exercised effective control over that conduct. Here the state kept control, so the conduct is likely the state's.
  - ask: How much weight do the ARIO carry?
    hint: Their status, and the critique.
    answer: They are draft articles, not binding, and criticised for modelling one state-responsibility framework for very different IOs. Cite them as the ILC's framework and note those limits.
