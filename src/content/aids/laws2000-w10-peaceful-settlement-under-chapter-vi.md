plain: Before any coercion, states in a dispute that could endanger peace must first try peaceful means. The Security Council can investigate, urge and recommend under Chapter VI — but it cannot compel.

formal: "Art 33(1): parties to a dispute likely to endanger international peace and security \"shall, first of all, seek a solution\" by negotiation, enquiry, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, judicial settlement, regional agencies or other peaceful means. The Council may call upon them to do so (art 33(2)), investigate (art 34) and recommend procedures (art 36(1)); legal disputes generally go to the ICJ (art 36(3)); unsettled disputes are referred to it (art 37(1))."
terms:
  - term: Chapter VI
    gloss: The Charter's chapter on pacific settlement of disputes. The Council investigates, calls upon the parties and recommends; no art 39 determination is needed.
  - term: art 33(1) means
    gloss: Negotiation, enquiry, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, judicial settlement, regional agencies or arrangements, or other peaceful means of the parties' choice.
  - term: situation (art 34)
    gloss: Something short of a dispute that might lead to international friction or give rise to a dispute; the Council may investigate it.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Under Chapter VI the Council can impose binding sanctions on a party that refuses to settle.
    tempting: The Council's decisions bind under art 25.
    correction: Chapter VI powers are to investigate, call upon the parties and recommend (arts 33(2), 34, 36(1)). Sanctions are Chapter VII art 41 measures, needing an art 39 determination first.
  - claim: Parties who fail to settle by art 33 means must take the dispute to the ICJ.
    tempting: Art 36(3) points legal disputes to the ICJ.
    correction: "Art 37(1) requires them to refer it to the Security Council. Art 36(3) is only a consideration for the Council: legal disputes should as a general rule be referred to the ICJ by the parties."
  - claim: Chapter VI is the General Assembly's job; the Council only acts under Chapter VII.
    tempting: Members may also bring disputes to the Assembly (art 35).
    correction: Arts 33–37 set out the Security Council's role in peaceful settlement. Art 35 merely lets any member raise a dispute with the Council or the Assembly.
