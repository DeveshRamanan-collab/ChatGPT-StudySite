terms:
  - term: Corrective justice
    gloss: "Weinrib's view: tort allocates responsibility for harm; whoever breaches another's moral rights must correct the wrong by paying compensation, resting on free human agency."
  - term: Distributive justice
    gloss: Tort as a system to distribute risks and losses in society, through insurance or by allocating losses to a public authority and so to taxpayers.
  - term: No-fault liability
    gloss: A mechanism (e.g. for road accidents) requiring proof of causation rather than fault, spreading losses through insurance.
  - term: Law and economics
    gloss: "Calabresi and Posner: tort aims at the most efficient outcome by allocating the costs of accidents; Posner sees a cost–benefit structure underpinning it."
misconceptions:

    tempting: Both are compatible with the victim receiving compensation.
    correction: Corrective justice (Weinrib) ties payment to the individual wrongdoer's responsibility; distributive justice spreads losses across society, e.g. no-fault schemes needing causation, not fault (Lecture 2, slides 9–11).
  - claim: Under a no-fault scheme, nothing has to be proved.
    tempting: "\"No-fault\" sounds like \"no proof\"."
    correction: A no-fault mechanism still requires proof of causation; it drops only the need to prove fault (Lecture 2, slide 11).

    tempting: Payment of compensation is one of the listed options.

  - ask: Why is that hard to achieve in practice?
