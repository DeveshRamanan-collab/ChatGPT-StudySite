plain: Consent protects the defendant only if it was freely given, by someone able to give it, for the very act done, and only up to the limits of what was agreed.
concrete: "During an Australian Rules match, the defendant struck the plaintiff on the head with a raised elbow moments after he had kicked the ball away, fracturing his skull. The defendant said this was contact a footballer must expect. Fox J disagreed in McNamara v Duncan: players consent to forcible contact justified by the rules and usages of the game, but this blow was deliberate and a serious infringement of the rules."
formal: "Consent may be express or implied. To be valid it must be voluntary/free (sufficient knowledge; no force, fraud, threat or intimidation), come from a competent person, and relate to the act complained of. A false inducement does not vitiate consent if the plaintiff understands the \"nature and character\" of the interference. Conduct exceeding the consent is not protected: McNamara v Duncan; Dean v Phung."

terms:
  - term: Express or implied consent
    gloss: Consent may be given in words or inferred from conduct, such as taking part in a contact sport.
  - term: "\"Nature and character\""
    gloss: What the interference actually is. If the plaintiff understands this, a false inducement about something else does not invalidate consent.
    example: A false promise of marriage used to induce sexual relations.
  - term: Vitiate
    gloss: To destroy the legal effect of consent. Force, fraud, threat or intimidation vitiate consent.
  - term: Scope of consent
    gloss: The limits of what was actually agreed to; conduct beyond them is not protected.
    example: "Dean v Phung: more than 53 consultations of unnecessary treatment for minor chipping."
misconceptions:
  - claim: Any lie by the defendant cancels the plaintiff's consent.
    tempting: Fraud is listed among the things that stop consent being freely given.
    correction: Not every misrepresentation nullifies consent. If the plaintiff understands the "nature and character" of the interference, consent is valid despite a false inducement, such as a false promise of marriage.
  - claim: By playing a contact sport, a player consents to any injury that happens during the game.
    tempting: Forcible contact is part of the game, and everyone knows fouls happen.
    correction: "McNamara v Duncan: players consent to contact justified by the rules and usages of the game, not to a deliberate blow in serious breach of the rules, even if such acts may well occur."
  - claim: Consent must be in writing, or at least stated in words, to be valid.
    tempting: Signed medical consent forms make written consent look like the norm.
    correction: Consent may be express or implied. What matters is that it is free, comes from a competent person, and relates to the act complained of.

elements:
  - name: Consent given (express or implied)
    test: Did the plaintiff agree to the interference, in words or by conduct?
  - name: Voluntary / free
    test: Did she have sufficient knowledge of what she was agreeing to, with no force, fraud, threat or intimidation? A false inducement does not vitiate if she understood the nature and character of the act.
  - name: Competent person
    test: Did the person have sufficient understanding to comprehend the act and its consequences? Adults are presumed competent.
    cases:
      - brightwater-care-group-v-rossiter
      - gillick-v-west-norfolk-and-wisbech-area-health-authority
  - name: Related to the act complained of
    test: Was the consent given for this specific act?
    cases:
      - dean-v-phung
  - name: Not exceeded
    test: Did the defendant's conduct stay within the scope of what was actually agreed to?
    cases:
      - mcnamara-v-duncan
      - dean-v-phung
