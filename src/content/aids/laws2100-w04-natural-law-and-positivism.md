plain: "Natural law theorists say law has an essentially moral dimension: law is social facts plus conformity to moral standards. Positivists say law's existence is simply a matter of social facts."
concrete: "Augustine asked: 'What are states without justice but robber-bands enlarged?' It is the gunman situation writ large. A robber band and a state both make demands backed by force. Many natural law thinkers say what separates them is justice: law reflects, or should reflect, justice, and that is what the gunman situation lacks. Hart pushes against this and explains the difference through accepted rules instead."
formal: Hart takes positivism to mean 'the simple contention that it is in no sense a necessary truth that laws reproduce or satisfy certain demands of morality, though in fact they have often done so' (CL 185–186). Natural law contends that between law and morality there is a connection 'in some sense "necessary"', which deserves to be taken as central in analysing law (CL 155).
terms:
  - term: Necessary connection between law and morality (NCLM)
    gloss: The natural law contention that law and morality are connected in some sense 'necessarily', not just in fact, and that this is central to analysing law (CL 155).
  - term: Positive law
    gloss: Human-made law, posited by human authorities. Finnis notes that Aquinas first put the term into wide philosophical circulation.
  - term: Procedural and substantive natural law
    gloss: Procedural theories (Fuller) connect law with moral standards of form; substantive theories (Finnis) connect it with moral standards of content, such as basic goods.
  - term: Conceptual separability
    gloss: "What positivists actually claim: law can be identified without moral criteria. 'Separation of law and morals' is imprecise, since real laws and morals overlap."
