plain: The General Assembly — every member, one vote each — can discuss peace and security and recommend, but not decide. It holds back while the Council deals with a matter, unless the Council is deadlocked.
concrete: In 1950, with the Cold War deadlocking the Council, the Assembly adopted Resolution 377(V), "Uniting for Peace". If the Council, because the permanent members cannot agree, fails to exercise its primary responsibility where there appears to be a threat to the peace, breach of the peace or act of aggression, the Assembly will consider the matter immediately and recommend collective measures to members.
formal: "The Assembly may discuss peace and security questions and, except as provided in art 12, recommend; questions needing action go to the Council (art 11(2)). It may recommend measures for peaceful adjustment (art 14; Certain Expenses). While the Council exercises its functions on a matter, it shall not recommend unless asked (art 12(1); Wall, paras 27–28). Uniting for Peace: GA Res 377(V). The Secretary-General may alert the Council (art 99)."
