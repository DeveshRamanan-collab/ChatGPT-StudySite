plain: In a genuine national emergency a state may suspend some rights, but only as far as strictly needed, and some rights never. In normal times, any limit must be lawful, legitimate and necessary.
concrete: In Aksoy v Turkey, the European Court of Human Rights accepted that investigating terrorist offences presents special problems, but could not accept that it was necessary to hold a suspect for fourteen days without judicial intervention. The period was "exceptionally long", leaving him vulnerable to arbitrary interference with liberty and to torture, and Turkey gave no detailed reasons why judicial intervention was impracticable (para 78).
formal: "ICCPR art 4(1): in an officially proclaimed \"public emergency which threatens the life of the nation\", a state may derogate \"to the extent strictly required by the exigencies of the situation\", consistently with other obligations, without listed discrimination. Art 4(2): none from arts 6, 7, 8(1)–(2), 11, 15, 16, 18. ACHR art 27 is similar. Otherwise restrictions need law, a legitimate aim and necessity in a democratic society."

terms:
  - term: derogation
    gloss: Temporarily suspending treaty obligations in a public emergency threatening the life of the nation, to the extent strictly required (ICCPR art 4(1)).
  - term: non-derogable rights
    gloss: Rights that cannot be suspended even in emergency — ICCPR arts 6, 7, 8(1)–(2), 11, 15, 16 and 18; ACHR art 27(2) lists its own.
  - term: limitation
    gloss: "A restriction in normal times: provided by law, serving a legitimate aim in the limitation clause, and necessary in a democratic society."
  - term: notification
    gloss: One of the four substantive requirements for a valid derogation.
misconceptions:
  - claim: A state may derogate whenever its government considers national security requires it.
    tempting: Security is the usual reason given.
    correction: Art 4(1) needs a public emergency threatening the life of the nation, officially proclaimed, and measures strictly required by the situation.
  - claim: The right to liberty (ICCPR art 9) can never be suspended.
    tempting: Liberty feels as fundamental as life.
    correction: Art 9 is not in art 4(2), so it is derogable — but only to the extent strictly required, as Aksoy shows for fourteen-day detention without a judge.
  - claim: Outside an emergency, a state may restrict a limited right whenever convenient.
    tempting: Many rights are not absolute.
    correction: The restriction must be provided by law, serve a legitimate aim in the limitation clause, and be necessary in a democratic society — a public need, proportionate.
