plain: "In international law, a state's own law is no excuse: if the state breaks an international obligation, it is in breach, whatever its domestic law says."

terms:
  - term: internal law
    gloss: A state's own domestic law — constitution, statutes, regulations, court decisions. Its absence or contrary content does not justify failing to perform a treaty (art 27).
  - term: manifest violation (art 46(2))
    gloss: A breach of internal competence rules that would be objectively evident to any state conducting itself in accordance with normal practice and in good faith.
  - term: ILC Draft Articles on State Responsibility (2001)
    gloss: Not binding as an ILC product, but mostly recognised as representing customary international law — which is why art 3 matters.
