plain: The Security Council has 15 states, five of them permanent with a veto. Members gave it main responsibility for peace and security, agreed to carry out its decisions, and let those decisions override conflicting treaties.
concrete: "In 2017 the Council, acting on North Korea's nuclear programme, adopted S/RES/2397: lower caps on refined petroleum supplied to the DPRK, bans on its exports of food, machinery and other goods, and a duty to de-register vessels involved in illicit activities. Every UN member — not just the 15 at the table — must carry out those decisions (art 25), and a member's trade treaty with the DPRK cannot excuse it (art 103)."
formal: "Art 23: 15 members; five permanent (US, China, Russia, UK, France) with the veto (art 27(3)); ten elected by the General Assembly for two years along regional lines. Art 24(1): members confer \"primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security\", and the Council acts on their behalf; its powers lie in Chapters VI, VII, VIII and XII (art 24(2)). Art 25: decisions bind; art 103: Charter obligations prevail."
terms:
  - term: primary responsibility
    gloss: "Art 24(1): members confer on the Council the main — not exclusive — responsibility for international peace and security, for prompt and effective action."
  - term: veto
    gloss: The power of each permanent member to block a Council decision (art 27(3)).
  - term: elected members
    gloss: "Ten non-permanent members chosen by the General Assembly for two-year terms, five each year: Africa 3, Asia 2, Latin America and Caribbean 2, WEOG 2, Eastern Europe 1."
  - term: art 103
    gloss: Where Charter obligations conflict with obligations under any other international agreement, the Charter obligations prevail.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Art 24 gives the Council exclusive responsibility for peace and security, so no other organ may touch it.
    tempting: The Council is the UN's peace-and-security organ.
    correction: "Art 24(1) says \"primary\", not exclusive. The General Assembly has a subsidiary responsibility: it may discuss and recommend (arts 11(2), 14), subject to art 12."
  - claim: A Council decision binds only the members that voted for it.
    tempting: Treaty obligations usually depend on each state's consent.
    correction: Under art 25 every member has agreed in advance to accept and carry out the Council's decisions. Voting against, or not sitting on the Council, changes nothing.
  - claim: A treaty concluded before a Council decision prevails over the decision.
    tempting: It feels as if the earlier commitment should come first.
    correction: Art 103 gives Charter obligations — including carrying out binding decisions — priority over obligations under any other international agreement, whatever its date.

    hint: Art 27(3).
    answer: The veto. Any one of them can block a decision — one reason the slides give for the Council not acting in every threat.
  - ask: Why does the Council, rather than each state, act on threats to peace?
    hint: Art 24(1).
    answer: Members conferred primary responsibility on it, to ensure prompt and effective action, and agreed that it acts on their behalf.
  - ask: Must a state that voted against a decision carry it out?
    hint: Art 25.
    answer: Yes. Members agree to accept and carry out the Council's decisions, so the decision binds all members.
  - ask: What if carrying it out breaches that state's treaty with another state?
    hint: Art 103.
    answer: The Charter obligation prevails over the obligation under the other agreement.
