plain: "Human rights are monitored rather than enforced like court orders: the UN Human Rights Council reviews every state and appoints independent experts, and treaty committees examine reports and complaints."
concrete: Under the Universal Periodic Review, every UN member state is reviewed every 4.5 years. The state — and NGOs — submit reports; a working group of 47 members reviews them and issues an "Outcome Report" with recommendations to the state under review. The state then accepts or merely notes each recommendation. Nothing in the process makes the recommendations binding.
formal: "Charter-based: the Human Rights Council replaced the Commission on Human Rights in 2006 (UNGA res 60/251): 47 states elected by the General Assembly for three-year terms (maximum two consecutive), working through sessions, the Universal Periodic Review and special procedures. Treaty-based: committees for each core treaty — state reporting, individual complaints (\"views\"), inter-state complaints, general comments. Outputs are not binding but carry significant weight (Diallo, para 66)."

terms:
  - term: Universal Periodic Review
    gloss: Peer review of every UN member state every 4.5 years by a 47-member working group; the state accepts or notes the recommendations.
  - term: special procedures
    gloss: Independent experts — special rapporteurs, independent experts, working groups — with thematic and country mandates; they visit (with consent), receive complaints and report.
  - term: treaty body
    gloss: The committee supervising a core treaty, e.g. the Human Rights Committee for the ICCPR.
  - term: general comment
    gloss: A treaty body's interpretation of treaty provisions, considered authoritative — e.g. General Comment No 31.
misconceptions:

    tempting: The names are almost identical.
    correction: The Council is a Charter-based body of 47 states (UNGA res 60/251). The Committee is the ICCPR's treaty body of independent experts, issuing views and general comments.
  - claim: Treaty body decisions bind states like ICJ judgments.
    tempting: Individual complaints are decided in a judicial style.
    correction: They are not binding but carry significant weight. In Diallo (para 66) the ICJ was "in no way obliged" to follow the Committee but ascribed "great weight" to its interpretation.
  - claim: UPR recommendations bind the state under review.
    tempting: They come out of a formal review.
    correction: The state under review accepts or notes the recommendations; the UPR is peer review, not adjudication.
