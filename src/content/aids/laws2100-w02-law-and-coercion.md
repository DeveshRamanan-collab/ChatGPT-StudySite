plain: Is coercion part of what law is? Some say law is a kind of coercion; others that law regulates it. The key question is whether coercion is necessary to law, or only salient in it.
concrete: "Imagine a society of angels: everyone complies with the rules, so nothing ever needs enforcing. Raz argues that even angels face coordination problems, so their community still needs laws, just not coercive ones. If a legal system without coercion can be imagined, coercion is not a necessary feature of the concept of law. Schauer accepts this might be so, but says the law we actually live under is coercive."
formal: "Lamond: interest in law and coercion is 'clarificatory' and 'evaluative' (2001, 36). Coercion is law's genus (Austin); law is a coercive social order (Kelsen); coercion is law's most salient aspect (Bentham, Holmes, Raz); law regulates coercion (Bobbio); legal practice guides and constrains state coercion (Dworkin). Lamond: coercion is at best 'the most salient aspect of certain legal systems' (p 56)."
