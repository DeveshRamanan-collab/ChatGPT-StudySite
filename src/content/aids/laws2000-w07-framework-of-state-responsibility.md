plain: "A state is internationally responsible when conduct attributable to it — an act or an omission — breaches an international obligation it owes. Responsibility brings consequences: stop the wrong and repair it."
concrete: "The lecturer's examples of primary rules are the prohibition on using force against another state, or a treaty on the good management of shared water. Those rules say what states must do. State responsibility is the second layer: it tells you when a state has breached such a rule and what follows. In the tutorial, DFAT's lawyers advising on a pandemic convention walk that path — obligation, breach, attribution, excuses, who may invoke, consequences."
formal: "Art 1 ARSIWA: \"every internationally wrongful act of a State entails its international responsibility\". Art 2: an internationally wrongful act exists when conduct consisting of an act or omission is attributable to the state and constitutes a breach of an international obligation owed by it. ARSIWA (ILC, adopted 10 August 2001) is not binding, but many articles appear to codify customary international law."

terms:
  - term: primary rule
    gloss: A substantive obligation found in treaty, custom or general principles — e.g. the prohibition on using force. It says what a state must or must not do.
  - term: secondary rule
    gloss: "A rule of state responsibility: it determines when a primary obligation has been breached by a state and the legal consequences of that breach."
  - term: internationally wrongful act
    gloss: Conduct (act or omission) attributable to a state that breaches its international obligation (art 2). Covers crime-like and tort-like wrongs alike.
  - term: ARSIWA
    gloss: The ILC's Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts, adopted 10 August 2001. Not a treaty and not binding as such; many articles reflect custom.
misconceptions:
  - claim: ARSIWA binds states as treaty law because it was adopted in 2001.
    tempting: It is cited article by article, just like a treaty.
    correction: "It was adopted by the ILC's commissioners, not by states: it is not a treaty and not binding as such. It matters because many articles appear to codify customary international law, which does bind."
  - claim: An internationally wrongful act needs harm to another state and fault by the responsible state.
    tempting: Domestic tort law asks about damage and fault.
    correction: "Art 2 names only two elements: conduct attributable to the state, and a breach of an international obligation. The type of damage matters later, for the form of reparation."
  - claim: State responsibility rules are 'secondary' because they rank below treaties and custom as sources.
    tempting: "'Secondary' sounds like a lower rank in art 38."
    correction: "The label is functional: primary rules set substantive obligations; secondary rules determine when they are breached and with what consequences. State responsibility presupposes a primary rule."
socratic:
  - ask: Australia asks whether another party has breached the pandemic Convention. What is the first question?
    hint: Step 1.
    answer: What primary obligation binds that state — here the Convention's articles, such as art 21 on access to vaccines. State responsibility rules are secondary and need a primary rule to work on.
  - ask: Is a breach on the face of the facts enough to make a state responsible?
    hint: Art 2 has two elements.
    answer: No. The conduct must also be attributable to the state. In principle a state answers only for its own conduct, so the prima facie breach must be linked to it.
  - ask: The breach is attributable. Is the state now necessarily responsible?
    hint: Step 4.
    answer: "Not yet: check whether a circumstance precluding wrongfulness (arts 20–25) applies, such as consent, countermeasures or force majeure."
  - ask: If no ground applies, what follows, and who may complain?
    hint: Step 5.
    answer: Cessation and non-repetition (art 30) and full reparation (art 31). The injured state may invoke responsibility (art 42); non-injured states only in limited cases (art 48).
  - ask: A foreign ministry says ARSIWA "is just an ILC document, so nothing in it binds us". Reply?
    hint: Custom.
    answer: It is not a treaty and is not binding as such, but many of its articles appear to codify customary international law — courts including the ICJ have said so — and custom binds the state.
