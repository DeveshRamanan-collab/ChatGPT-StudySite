plain: Every state counts equally in law, however big or small, and no state is bound by a rule of international law unless it has agreed to be bound.
concrete: "In the UN General Assembly, China, with 1.4 billion people, has one vote — exactly the same as a state of half a million people. Equality is legal, not practical: influence still depends on a state's resources and on the skill of its diplomats. The lecturer's example is Malta, a small state that played a large role in reforming the law of the sea, which eventually produced the UN Law of the Sea Convention."
formal: "Article 2 of the UN Charter prescribes that UN members are equal (sovereign equality): each has the right to be considered on an equal basis, with one vote in the General Assembly. Separately, no treaty or rule binds a state unless it has consented — expressly (ratifying a treaty) or implicitly (following a practice in the belief that it is legally binding)."

terms:
  - term: sovereign equality
    gloss: Each state, however large or small, has equal legal standing; in the General Assembly each has one vote (Art 2 UN Charter).
  - term: state consent
    gloss: A state's agreement to be bound. Without it, no treaty or rule of international law binds that state.
  - term: express consent
    gloss: Consent given explicitly, such as by ratifying a treaty.
  - term: implicit consent
    gloss: Consent shown by following a customary practice in the belief that it is legally binding — the basis of customary international law.
misconceptions:
  - claim: Once most states ratify a treaty, it binds every state.
    tempting: Organisations such as the General Assembly decide by majority vote, so majorities seem to bind everyone.
    correction: "Majority voting describes decision-making inside organisations. A treaty binds only states that consented: no state can compel another to sign up to a treaty it does not want."
  - claim: Sovereign equality and state consent are two names for the same idea.
    tempting: Both protect a state's independence and the lecture discusses them together.
    correction: Sovereign equality concerns equal standing (one state, one vote, Art 2); consent concerns whether a particular rule binds a particular state. They answer different questions.
  - claim: Sovereign equality means every state has equal influence.
    tempting: Equal votes suggest equal power.
    correction: The equality is legal. Influence depends on political, financial and military resources and on the personal qualities of a state's representatives, as Malta's role in the law of the sea shows.
