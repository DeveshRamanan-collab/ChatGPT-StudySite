plain: Barristers cannot be sued in negligence for how they run a case in court, or for closely connected preparation. The protection serves the court system, not lawyers, and is narrow.
concrete: "The lecturer's example: a solicitor acting on a claim worth about $10 million was investigating and gathering evidence but forgot to diarise the limitation period. The time limit passed, the claim became statute barred, and the client lost the right to sue. The client could then sue the solicitor, because the advocates' immunity covers the conduct of cases in court, not this kind of file management."
formal: "As a matter of public policy, barristers are immune from liability for negligence in the conduct of cases in court, and in pre-trial matters intimately connected with the case in court. Giannarelli v Wraith gave the policy reasons: the advocate's duty to the court may conflict with the duty to the client; court time may be wasted on defensive arguments; and the finality of judgment."
terms:
  - term: Intimately connected
    gloss: Describes pre-trial work so closely tied to the case in court that it shares the immunity; general advice is not.
  - term: Finality of judgment
    gloss: The principle that a decided case should stay decided; a negligence suit about how it was run would reopen it. Later cases treat this as the key reason.
  - term: Limitation period
    gloss: A time limit for bringing a case, after which the claim is statute barred.
misconceptions:
  - claim: All lawyers are immune from negligence claims for litigation work.
    tempting: The immunity is described as an advocates' immunity, and solicitors run litigation too.
    correction: The immunity concerns barristers' conduct of cases in court and intimately connected pre-trial matters. Solicitors definitely can be sued for negligence in acting for clients.
  - claim: A barrister's written advice months before any proceedings is covered by the immunity.
    tempting: The advice comes from a barrister.
    correction: The immunity attaches to the courtroom, not to who the lawyer is. Advice falls under pure economic loss caused by negligent misstatement (Topic 7).
  - claim: The immunity exists to protect barristers from the cost of claims.
    tempting: Immunities usually benefit the person immune.
    correction: "It rests on public policy about the court process: conflicting duties to court and client, wasted court time, and the finality of judgment."
