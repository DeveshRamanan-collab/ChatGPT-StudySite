plain: "Jurisdiction is a state's power over people and conduct, in three forms: making law, enforcing it and judging cases. Laws may reach abroad; enforcement on another state's soil needs its permission."
concrete: In 1960 a Mossad team went to Argentina, captured the former Nazi officer Adolf Eichmann and smuggled him to Israel without the Argentine authorities' consent. He was tried in Israel for crimes against humanity and war crimes, and executed in 1962. Whatever Israel's power to prosecute, the capture was unauthorised enforcement in another state's territory — a serious violation of Argentina's sovereignty. The Security Council asked Israel to offer compensation.
formal: "Jurisdiction is \"the lawful power of a State to define and enforce the rights and duties, and control the conduct, of natural and juridical persons\" (Oxman). Prescription: international law leaves states \"a wide measure of discretion\", limited by prohibitive rules (Lotus (1927) 19). Enforcement: failing \"a permissive rule to the contrary\", a state \"may not exercise its power in any form in the territory of another State\" (18)."

terms:
  - term: jurisdiction to prescribe
    gloss: Making national law applicable to persons, property and activities — by legislation, executive act, regulation or court determination.
  - term: jurisdiction to enforce
    gloss: "The executive putting laws into operation: arrest, seizure, freezing assets, fines, remand and imprisonment. The most intrusive form."
  - term: jurisdiction to adjudicate
    gloss: Domestic courts' power to hear and bindingly decide legal disputes. In principle territorial.
  - term: sovereignty
    gloss: Supreme authority within a territory, subject to international requirements; a description of statehood, not a precondition for it (Besson).
misconceptions:
  - claim: Lotus says a state may not extend its laws abroad unless a permissive rule allows it.
    tempting: Lotus does mention a permissive rule.
    correction: That is the enforcement rule. For prescription, Lotus says international law leaves states "a wide measure of discretion", limited only by prohibitive rules. Prescription abroad is free unless prohibited; enforcement abroad is prohibited unless permitted.
  - claim: Imprisoning a convicted thief is adjudicative jurisdiction, because a court imposed the sentence.
    tempting: Courts hand down sentences.
    correction: Slide 38 lists imposing imprisonment and custody on remand as enforcement. The prosecution in court is adjudication; criminalising theft is prescription.
  - claim: A state's courts can never sit outside its territory.
    tempting: Adjudicative jurisdiction is in principle territorial.
    correction: "By agreement they can: after Lockerbie, a special Scottish court sat at Camp Zeist in the Netherlands, applying Scottish law, under a 1998 UK–Netherlands agreement."
