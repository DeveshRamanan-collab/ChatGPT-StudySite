plain: You may use force to protect yourself, others or property, but only if you reasonably fear force and respond in proportion. In NSW, the statute adds that what you respond to must be unlawful.
concrete: Fontin wrongly accused Katapodis of not paying for goods, and a heated argument followed. Katapodis picked up a wooden T-square and broke it on Fontin's shoulder. Fontin threw an off-cut of glass at Katapodis's face, injuring his hand. The High Court held Fontin had the right to defend himself, but throwing the glass "was out of all reasonable proportion to the emergency confronting" him, so self-defence failed.
formal: "Common law: the defendant must prove a reasonable apprehension of physical force to the defendant or others, and that the force used was proportionate to the threat (Zecevic v DPP). Section 52 CLA: no liability for conduct believed necessary for a listed purpose that is a reasonable response in the circumstances as perceived, but only if the conduct responded to was unlawful. The regimes run in parallel."

terms:
  - term: Reasonable apprehension
    gloss: A reasonably held expectation that physical force is about to be used against the defendant or others (Zecevic v DPP).
  - term: Proportionality
    gloss: The force used must be in proportion to the threat; a response "out of all reasonable proportion to the emergency" fails.
    example: "Fontin v Katapodis: glass thrown at the face after a blow with a T-square."
  - term: Unlawful conduct (s 52)
    gloss: The conduct responded to must be unlawful (held to include criminal and tortious conduct), or would have been but for a mental health or cognitive impairment.
    example: "NSW v McMaster: running at police was a negligent trespass to the person."
  - term: Reasonable response as perceived
    gloss: Under s 52(2), the response is judged in the circumstances as the defendant perceived them.

  - scenario: A shop security guard sees a customer grab a phone and run. He tackles the customer, then keeps punching him while he lies pinned and still.
