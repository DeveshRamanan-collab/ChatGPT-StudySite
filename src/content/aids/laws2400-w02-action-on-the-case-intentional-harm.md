plain: "When harm is indirect, an action on the case may lie: for a prosecution brought maliciously without proper cause, or for deliberately or recklessly causing someone physical or psychiatric injury."
concrete: In Wilkinson v Downton [1897] 2 QB 57 the defendant, as a practical joke, told the plaintiff her husband had been "smashed up" in an accident. The false news caused her nervous shock. There was no touching, so no battery; the court held that a cause of action lies for wilfully doing an act calculated to cause physical harm, which actually causes it.
formal: "Malicious prosecution: the defendant initiated proceedings against the plaintiff; they terminated in the plaintiff's favour; the defendant acted maliciously and without reasonable and probable cause; plus damage to life, liberty, reputation or property (A v New South Wales). Intentional infliction of personal injury: wilfully doing an act calculated to cause physical harm, and causing it (Wilkinson v Downton); reckless indifference suffices (Nationwide News v Naidu)."
