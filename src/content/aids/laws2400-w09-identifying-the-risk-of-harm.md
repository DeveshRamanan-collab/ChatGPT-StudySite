plain: Before asking what precautions were needed, name the risk. Describe it as a kind of injury from its real source, not too broadly and not as the exact injury suffered.
concrete: "A 14-year-old dived off a bridge into an estuary and suffered partial paraplegia. People had jumped from the bridge for years without injury, but a shifting sandbar changed the depth, and \"no diving\" pictographs were displayed. The Court of Appeal described the risk as serious spinal injury from diving off the bridge. Gummow J said that obscured the true source: impact on jumping into potentially shallow water over shifting sands."
formal: "Benic v NSW (Garling J): first identify a risk of harm; it is sufficient \"if the risk of harm is described as a class of injury, as distinct from the particular injury actually suffered by the plaintiff\". Then consider the s 5B(1) elements. RTA v Dederer: the risk must correspond to the precautions that should have been taken, not cover fundamentally different scenarios."
