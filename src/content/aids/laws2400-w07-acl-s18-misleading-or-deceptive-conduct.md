plain: A business that misleads people can be liable under a statute, the Australian Consumer Law, without anyone proving a duty of care or carelessness.
concrete: "The ACCC sued Kimberly-Clark over its wipes on two counts: whether they were \"flushable\" and whether they were \"made in Australia\". The Federal Court found the \"made in Australia\" claim false and misleading, because the wipes were made elsewhere, but held the \"flushable\" claims were not false and misleading. The Full Federal Court upheld that ruling. Kimberly-Clark had settled similar US actions out of court."
formal: "Australian Consumer Law s 18: \"A person must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive or is likely to mislead or deceive.\" It covers conduct and words, negligent and intentional. No duty of care or negligence need be proved; a causal link is still needed, but nowhere near the extent required at common law. The common law remains relevant, e.g. outside trade or commerce."
