plain: Peoples have a right to control their own destiny. For colonised peoples that clearly includes independence; elsewhere it usually means a real say within the existing state, and a right to break away is contested.
concrete: Quebec held referendums on leaving Canada, and the question whether it could secede unilaterally went to the Supreme Court of Canada. In 1998 the Court held that unilateral secession is consistent with neither international law nor the Canadian Constitution. Distinguishing internal from external self-determination, it found that Quebec's people already enjoyed internal self-determination within Canada — so there was no ground to claim the external kind, secession.
formal: "UN Charter art 1(2) refers to the \"principle of equal rights and self-determination of peoples\" without framing it as a right; ICCPR art 1: \"All peoples have the right of self-determination.\" The 1960 Declaration on the Granting of Independence calls alien subjugation \"a denial of fundamental human rights\"; Chagos (2019) affirmed the right in decolonisation. Outside it, unilateral secession is \"highly contentious\" (Kosovo; Re Secession of Quebec)."
terms:
  - term: internal self-determination
    gloss: A people freely pursuing its political, economic, social and cultural development within an existing state. Quebec's was held to be fulfilled.
  - term: external self-determination
    gloss: A people leaving the existing state — independence or secession. Accepted for colonised peoples; outside decolonisation, highly contentious.
  - term: decolonisation context
    gloss: Peoples under colonial rule, where self-determination is generally accepted (1960 Declaration; Chagos).
  - term: unilateral secession
    gloss: Breaking away from an existing state without its agreement.
    example: Kosovo 2008; contested attempts in Iraqi Kurdistan and Catalonia in 2017.
misconceptions:
  - claim: The UN Charter makes self-determination a right of peoples.
    tempting: Self-determination is a foundational UN principle, named in art 1(2).
    correction: Art 1(2) refers to the principle of self-determination but did not frame it as a right. It was later enshrined as a human right in ICCPR art 1.
  - claim: If most people in a region vote to leave, international law gives them a right to secede.
    tempting: Self-determination sounds like majority choice, and referendums are its usual form.
    correction: "Re Secession of Quebec: unilateral secession is not consistent with international law where the people's internal self-determination is fulfilled. Quebec's referendums failed; a vote alone confers no right."
  - claim: The Kosovo Advisory Opinion confirmed a right to secede for oppressed peoples.
    tempting: Kosovo's supporters made exactly that argument, and the Court found the declaration lawful.
    correction: The Court said only that the declaration of independence was not in itself illegal; it refrained from saying unilateral self-determination was lawful.
