plain: A reservation lets a state join a multilateral treaty while excluding or changing certain provisions for itself — unless the treaty forbids it or it undermines the treaty's purpose.
concrete: "The lecturer's example: a treaty aims to eliminate ozone-depleting emissions, with strict obligations on certain gases. A state joining it cannot reserve that it will not limit its own ozone-depleting emissions — that conflicts with the treaty's very objective. The reservation is incompatible with the object and purpose, so it is invalid, and the state cannot rely on it to escape the treaty's obligations."
formal: A reservation is "a unilateral statement, however phrased or named, made by a State, when signing, ratifying, accepting, approving or acceding to a treaty, whereby it purports to exclude or to modify the legal effect of certain provisions of the treaty in their application to that State" (VCLT art 2(1)(d)). Art 19 permits one unless prohibited, outside specified permitted reservations, or incompatible with object and purpose.

terms:
  - term: reservation
    gloss: A unilateral statement, made on signing, ratifying, accepting, approving or acceding, purporting to exclude or modify certain provisions' legal effect for that state (art 2(1)(d)).
  - term: interpretative declaration
    gloss: A state's view of what a provision means, not put forward as a condition of being bound; only explanatory, so not a reservation.
  - term: object and purpose
    gloss: The treaty's core aim; a reservation incompatible with it is impermissible (art 19(c)).
  - term: severance
    gloss: "One view of an invalid reservation: ignore it and treat the state as bound by the whole treaty — criticised as contrary to consent."
misconceptions:
  - claim: A statement is only a reservation if the state calls it one.
    tempting: States often label such statements 'declarations'.
    correction: "Art 2(1)(d): \"however phrased or named\". The question is whether it purports to exclude or modify a provision's legal effect — unlike an interpretative declaration, which only explains meaning."
  - claim: If another state objects to a permissible reservation, the reserving state is out of the treaty altogether.
    tempting: An objection sounds like a rejection of membership.
    correction: As the lecture presents art 20(4), the treaty does not enter into force between the reserving and objecting states only; it can still operate with states that accept.
  - claim: The effect of an impermissible reservation is settled.
    tempting: The VCLT has detailed rules on reservations.
    correction: "It is unresolved. Three views: the state does not become a party; it is bound without the reservation (severance); or it is bound without it unless it expressed a contrary intention."
socratic:
  - ask: Novaria ratifies the Chemical Weapons Convention but declares it will not adhere to Article IV(5) on inspections. Is that a reservation?
    hint: Does it purport to exclude a provision's effect for Novaria?
    answer: Yes. However named, a statement on ratification excluding a provision's legal effect for that state is a reservation (art 2(1)(d)).
  - ask: What do you check first about the treaty itself?
    hint: "Art 19(a)–(b): read the whole treaty."
    answer: Whether the treaty prohibits reservations, or this one, or allows only specified reservations that exclude it. If so, the reservation fails.
  - ask: If the treaty is silent, is the reservation compatible with its object and purpose?
    hint: What is the Convention for, and what does Article IV(5) do?
    answer: Article IV(5) secures on-site verification of chemical weapons destruction facilities; excluding it arguably undermines the treaty's purpose, making the reservation impermissible under art 19(c).
  - ask: If the reservation were permissible and Armadonia objected, what would follow?
    hint: Art 20(4) as presented in the lecture.
    answer: The treaty would not enter into force between Novaria and Armadonia; if another state accepts, the reservation is effective in that relationship.
  - ask: If it is impermissible, where does Novaria stand?
    hint: Three possible solutions.
    answer: The reservation is void. Either Novaria is not a party; or it is bound in full (severance); or it is bound without the reservation unless it made the reservation a condition of consent.
elements:
  - name: Is it a reservation?
    test: A unilateral statement, however named, made on signing, ratifying, accepting, approving or acceding, purporting to exclude or modify a provision's effect — not merely an interpretative declaration.
    authorities:
      - vclt-art-2-1-d
  - name: Not prohibited by the treaty
    test: The treaty does not prohibit reservations or this reservation (art 19(a)), and does not allow only specified reservations that exclude it (art 19(b)).
    authorities:
      - vclt-art-19
  - name: Compatible with object and purpose
    test: Having passed (a) and (b), the reservation must not be incompatible with the treaty's object and purpose (art 19(c)).
    authorities:
      - vclt-art-19
  - name: Acceptance and objection
    test: "Expressly authorised: no acceptance needed (20(1)). Few parties, full application essential: all must accept (20(2)). Constituent instrument: the organ accepts (20(3)). Otherwise accept or object (20(4))."
    authorities:
      - vclt-art-20
  - name: If impermissible
    test: "Void from the start; no state need object. Three views: not a party; bound in full (severance); or bound without the reservation unless the state expressed a contrary intention."

boundary:
  - text: Bilateral treaties.
    why: "Reservations matter mainly for multilateral treaties: two states can usually negotiate disagreements into the text, while 160-plus states agreeing on every provision is unrealistic."
  - text: A treaty among few negotiating states whose full application is essential (art 20(2)).
    why: "Here a reservation needs acceptance by all the parties: the unanimity rule survives for this special class of treaties."
transfer:
  - scenario: A state ratifies a multilateral treaty on the safe disposal of nuclear waste, declaring it 'will not be bound by the provision requiring waste to be stored in sealed containers'. The treaty says nothing about reservations.
    answer: "It is a reservation: it purports to exclude a provision's legal effect for the state (art 2(1)(d)). With no prohibition (art 19(a)–(b)), the question is art 19(c): excluding sealed storage likely defeats a waste-safety treaty's object and purpose, like the lecturer's ozone example. If impermissible, it is void and one of the three unresolved solutions applies."
objections:
  - position: "Severance: a state that made an invalid reservation is bound by the whole treaty, including the reserved provision."
    evidence: An invalid reservation is null and void from the start, so the treaty's terms apply as if it had never been made.
    objection: It goes against the principle that a state is bound only by obligations it consented to; the lecture finds a third view — bound without the reservation unless consent was expressly conditional — more nuanced.
