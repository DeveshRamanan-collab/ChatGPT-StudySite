plain: "Careless words or acts that cost someone only money can ground a duty, but only within extra limits: the speaker took on responsibility, the plaintiff reasonably relied, and could not protect themselves."
concrete: "Hedley Byrne asked its bank to check the credit status of Easipower Ltd with Easipower's bank, Heller. Heller gave a reference \"without responsibility\". The House of Lords held that a duty of care can arise for pure economic loss caused by negligent words, but Hedley Byrne itself lost: by disclaiming responsibility for the reference, Heller had not assumed responsibility for it."
formal: Pure economic loss is economic loss not consequent upon physical injury to person or property; CLA s 5 includes economic loss in "harm". Foreseeability is necessary but not sufficient (Perre v Apand), given indeterminate liability and disproportion. Hedley Byrne requires a special relationship, voluntary assumption of responsibility, reliance and reasonable reliance. Vulnerability is also required (Woolcock Street Investments v CDG).

terms:
  - term: Pure economic loss

  - term: Known reliance

elements:
  - name: Pure economic loss
    test: Is the loss economic and not consequent upon physical injury to person or property?
    authorities:

  - name: Reasonable foreseeability

  - name: Known reliance
    test: Did the speaker know, or ought they to have realised, that the recipient would rely and act on the information?
    cases:
      - hedley-byrne-v-heller

  - name: Assumption of responsibility
    test: Did the speaker take it upon themselves to give the information or advice, in a matter of business or serious consequence, without disclaiming responsibility?
    cases:
      - hedley-byrne-v-heller
      - tepko-pty-ltd-v-water-board
  - name: Reasonable reliance
    test: Was reliance reasonable, given the subject matter, the occasion, the parties' relative knowledge and their capacity to judge?

      - hedley-byrne-v-heller
