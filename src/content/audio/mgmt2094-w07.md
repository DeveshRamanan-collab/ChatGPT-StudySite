This week is a tutorial with two charts, and the job is to keep three words apart: invention, innovation and creativity.

Start with invention against innovation. The slide says, let's understand the difference, and pay attention to the economic value. On the chart, an invention is a novel product, device, process, or concept. It needs knowledge, competence, and financial aid. The examples are the invention of the printing press, electricity and the telephone. Innovation is the introduction of a newer and better solution that meets new requirements or existing market needs. It needs technical knowledge, competence, knowledge about market needs and trends, and financial support. The examples are the introduction of the iPhone, the tablet and the flat-screen TV.

So notice what is shared and what is not. Both columns need competence and money, so effort and funding won't tell them apart. What only innovation has is the market: it meets requirements or market needs, it needs knowledge of market needs and trends, and its examples are introductions, not inventions. The slide doesn't spell out the economic value, but that's where the two columns differ.

Now creativity against innovation. The slide says, remember which one follows the other. Creativity is the use of imagination or original ideas to create something. It's related to imagination, it can't be measured, and it comes first. Innovation is the introduction of new or improved goods, services, processes and so on. It's related to implementation, it can be measured, and it follows creativity. Improved counts, not only brand new.

For a scenario, use the right chart for the right pair. A novel device sitting in a garage is an invention; once it's introduced to meet a need, that's innovation. Staff imagining ideas is creativity; putting one into practice is innovation, and innovation follows creativity.
