## Invention vs innovation

Let's understand the difference. Pay attention to the economic value.

| | Invention | Innovation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A novel product, device, process, or concept | The introduction of a newer and better solution that meets new requirements or existing market needs |
| What it requires | Knowledge, competence, and financial aid | Technical knowledge, competence, knowledge about market needs, trends, and financial support |
| Examples | The invention of the printing press, electricity, the telephone | The introduction of the iPhone, the tablet, the flat-screen TV |

:::definition
**Invention** is a novel product, device, process, or concept. **Innovation** is the introduction of a newer and
better solution that meets new requirements or existing market needs.
:::

Both columns need competence and money ("financial aid" for invention, "financial support" for innovation). Only the
innovation column is tied to the market: it is defined by meeting requirements or market needs, it adds knowledge
about market needs and trends, and its examples are introductions (the iPhone) where invention's are inventions (the
printing press). The slide does not spell out the economic value; this is where the chart's two columns differ.

## Creativity vs innovation

Remember which one follows the other. In which ways are they useful in organisations?

| | Innovation | Creativity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | The introduction of new or improved goods, services, processes, etc. | The use of imagination or original ideas to create something |
| Related to | Implementation | Imagination |
| Measurement | Can be measured | Cannot be measured |
| Order | Follows creativity | Precedes innovation |

:::rule
Creativity precedes innovation; innovation follows creativity.
:::

Innovation here covers improved goods, services and processes, not only brand-new ones. The question of how
creativity and innovation are useful in organisations is left for discussion in the tutorial; the materials do not
answer it, so start from the chart's rows: imagination and original ideas on one side, implementation of new or
improved goods, services and processes on the other.

:::examtip
Use the right chart for the right pair. Invention against innovation turns on whether something novel has been
introduced to meet requirements or market needs; creativity against innovation turns on imagination against
implementation, and on order — creativity comes first.
:::
