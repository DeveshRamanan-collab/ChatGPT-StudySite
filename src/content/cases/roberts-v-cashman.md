name: Roberts v Cashman
citation: "[2000] NSWSC 770"
aglc: Roberts v Cashman [2000] NSWSC 770
facts: The client's banana chair broke and she was injured; Depo Medrol injections made things worse. She joined a class action against Depo Medrol and filled out the law firm's questionnaire, but never had a conference (client interview) and was never asked about the original injury. When she found out she should have sued the chair's manufacturer, her claim was time-barred.
issue: Whether the firm breached its duty of care by relying on a questionnaire and never interviewing the client.
held: "Duty of care breached (NSW Supreme Court): the firm had expertise in personal injury and product liability; assessment of the original injury was inherent in the Depo Medrol claim (for causation); detailed information, including medical records, should have been obtained to build the case; the questionnaire (three years of problems, significant operations, unable to work) should have put the firm on notice that a conference was called for; there is no different standard of conduct in class actions or mass tort litigation; and a conference was not impracticable or uneconomic (the client lived in Sydney)."
ratio: Clients are notoriously unreliable in giving their own history in writing; a pre-planned questionnaire is not of itself a sufficient discharge of the solicitor's obligation, and a solicitor who does not interview the client in such a situation "does so at his own peril" [64].
significance: Shows that interviewing the client is part of a solicitor's duty of competence and diligence, not a courtesy.
why-here: The tutorial preparation slides use it to show why a proper client interview matters — failing to conduct one can itself be negligent.
