facts: |-
  The plaintiff operated a racecourse enclosed by a fence to keep race information private. The defendant
  broadcaster erected a platform on neighbouring land and broadcast live descriptions of the races, causing
  paying patrons to stay away and listen instead, and reducing the plaintiff's takings.
issue: Whether broadcasting a description of events visible from neighbouring land, causing loss of custom, is an actionable private nuisance.
held: |-
  By majority (Latham CJ, Dixon and McTiernan JJ; Rich and Evatt JJ dissenting), there was no actionable
  nuisance.
ratio: Private nuisance protects an occupier's use and enjoyment of rights incident to occupation of land; it does not protect against loss of profit caused by lawful competition, and the natural rights of an occupier do not include freedom from being overlooked.
significance: Leading Australian authority that overlooking, or losing customers to lawful competition, without more, is not private nuisance.
why-here: Use to reject a nuisance claim based only on being overlooked or on losing customers to lawful competition, while flagging that motive or hostility could change the analysis.
