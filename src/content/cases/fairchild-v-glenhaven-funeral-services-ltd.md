aglc: Fairchild v Glenhaven Funeral Services Ltd [2003] 1 AC 32
facts: A UK dust disease case in which the claimant could not prove which employer's exposure caused the disease.
issue: Whether materially increasing the risk of the disease was enough to establish causation when the claimant could not prove which employer's exposure caused it.
held: Materially increasing the risk was treated as enough.
ratio: "A policy-based exception to the ordinary causation rules: where the claimant cannot prove which employer's exposure caused the disease, materially increasing the risk can suffice."
significance: Australian common law has not adopted a risk-based test in that form. The Ipp Report looked at cases like it in recommending the exceptional case provision now in CLA s 5D(2).
why-here: "Use as a contrast: the UK risk-based exception that Australian common law has not adopted (compare Amaca v Ellis)."
