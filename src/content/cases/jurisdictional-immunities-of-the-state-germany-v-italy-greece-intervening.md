facts: |
  Civil proceedings were brought in Italian courts against Germany by victims' families with respect to serious
  crimes committed by German forces against civilian populations and prisoners of war during the occupation of
  Italy in World War II; Italian courts also took measures against Villa Vigoni, German property in Italy. Germany
  brought the case before the ICJ, claiming that Italian courts (and thus the Italian state) were violating its
  right to state immunity.
issue: |
  Whether customary international law deprives a state of immunity in foreign civil proceedings because of alleged
  serious violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian law, including norms of
  jus cogens.
held: |
  Italy had violated Germany's immunity. As of 2012, customary international law did not deprive a state of
  immunity because of alleged serious violations of international human rights law and IHL. "The rules of State
  immunity are procedural in character … and [are] thus entirely distinct from the substantive law which determines
  whether that conduct is lawful or unlawful" (para 93); state immunity for acta jure imperii "continues to extend
  to civil proceedings for acts occasioning death, personal injury or damage to property committed by the armed
  forces and other organs of a State in the conduct of armed conflict, even if the relevant acts take place on the
  territory of the forum State" (para 77).
ratio: |
  Because state immunity is procedural and distinct from the substantive rules breached, a state keeps its
  immunity for sovereign acts of its armed forces in armed conflict even where grave breaches are alleged.
significance: |
  The leading authority that there is (at present) no serious-violations or jus cogens exception to state
  immunity; widely criticised as promoting impunity.
