facts: |
  Denmark claimed sovereignty over the whole of Greenland; Norway argued that part of Eastern Greenland was
  terra nullius and occupied it. Denmark relied on a 1919 statement by the Norwegian Foreign Minister, Mr
  Ihlen, who told the Danish Minister that "the Norwegian Government would not make any difficulty in the
  settlement of this question."
issue: |
  Whether a unilateral statement made by a state's representative, in the absence of any treaty or reciprocal
  undertaking, could create a binding legal obligation for that state.
held: |
  The Permanent Court of International Justice held that the Ihlen declaration was "unconditional and
  definitive", and that Norway was under an obligation to refrain from contesting Danish sovereignty over
  Greenland as a whole, and from occupying any part of it.
ratio: |
  A unilateral statement by a state representative, evidencing a clear intention to accept an obligation, can
  itself create a binding international legal obligation, without any offer, reciprocity or acceptance by the
  other state being required.
significance: |
  The founding authority for unilateral statements/acts as a source of binding international obligations
  outside Article 38(1).
