facts: |
  An experienced water-skier, Rootes, was performing a "crossover" on the Macquarie River when he collided
  with a stationary boat after being briefly blinded by spray; the towing boat's observer had failed to warn
  skiers of the stationary obstacle.
issue: |
  Whether a fellow participant in a sporting activity (the boat driver/observer) owed a duty of care to
  another participant (the skier), and whether that duty was breached.
held: |
  The High Court held that the boat driver owed Rootes a duty of care and that the duty had been breached by
  the observer's failure to warn of the stationary obstacle.
ratio: |
  Barwick CJ: participants in a sport accept the risks inherent in it, but this does not eliminate all duty
  of care between participants — whether a duty arises, and its extent, depends on the circumstances of the
  case.
significance: |
  Establishes that a duty of care can arise between co-participants in a sport, and that the "inherent risk"
  of the sport must be distinguished from a separate failure to take reasonable care, such as a failure to
  warn.
