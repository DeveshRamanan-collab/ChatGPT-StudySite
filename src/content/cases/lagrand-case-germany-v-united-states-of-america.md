facts: |-
  Proceedings brought by Germany against the United States before the ICJ. The slides quote only the Court's reasoning
  on whether provisional measures indicated under art 41 of the ICJ Statute are binding (para 102).
issue: Are provisional measures indicated by the ICJ under art 41 of its Statute binding?
held: |-
  Yes. "The object and purpose of the Statute is to enable the Court to fulfil the functions provided for therein,
  and, in particular, the basic function of judicial settlement of international disputes by binding decisions in
  accordance with Article 59 of the Statute." Art 41 is to prevent the Court from being hampered because the parties'
  rights are not preserved, so "the power to indicate provisional measures entails that such measures should be
  binding"; the contention that they might not be binding "would be contrary to the object and purpose of that
  Article" (para 102).
ratio: Provisional measures indicated under art 41 are binding, as follows from the object and purpose of the Statute and the terms of art 41 read in context.
significance: Settles the debate created by the word "indicate" in art 41; tutorial question 1(b) asks exactly whether, in the ICJ's view, its provisional measures are binding.
why-here: The authority that provisional measures bind the parties.
