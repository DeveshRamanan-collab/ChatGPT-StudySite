facts: |
  In August 1928, Mrs Donoghue went with a friend to a café in Scotland for an ice-cream soda; the friend
  bought her the ice cream and a bottle of ginger beer. The ginger beer came in an opaque glass bottle bearing
  the manufacturer's name, David Stevenson. As the beer was poured into a tumbler, a decomposed snail floated
  out of the bottle. Donoghue suffered shock and severe gastro-enteritis and sued the manufacturer, with whom
  she had no contract (her friend had paid).
issue: Whether a manufacturer of a product, with whom the ultimate consumer has no contract, can owe that consumer a duty of care in negligence.
held: |
  The House of Lords, by a 3:2 majority, allowed Donoghue's appeal. A manufacturer who sells products intending
  them to reach the ultimate consumer in the form in which they left the manufacturer, with no reasonable
  possibility of intermediate examination, and knowing that a lack of reasonable care in preparing the product
  will injure the consumer, owes that consumer a duty to take reasonable care.
ratio: |
  Lord Atkin's neighbour principle: a person must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which they
  can reasonably foresee would be likely to injure their "neighbour" — a person so closely and directly
  affected by the act that the defendant ought reasonably to have them in contemplation when directing their
  mind to the act or omission in question.
significance: Founding authority of the modern tort of negligence; established that a manufacturer owes consumers a duty of care independent of any contract between them.
