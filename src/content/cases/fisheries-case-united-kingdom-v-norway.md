facts: |
  The UK and Norway disagreed over how to delimit Norway's territorial sea along its heavily indented
  coastline; the UK argued for a customary 10-mile rule for closing bays, while Norway calculated its
  territorial sea using straight baselines drawn between the outermost points of its coastal formations.
issue: |
  Whether the UK's claimed 10-mile rule for the closing line of bays had achieved the status of customary
  international law, and if so, whether Norway was nonetheless not bound by it.
held: |
  The Court held there was not enough state practice to support the 10-mile rule as a rule of customary
  international law at all; but added that, even had the rule existed, Norway would not have been bound by
  it, having always opposed its application to the Norwegian coast.
ratio: |
  Even where a rule has achieved the status of customary international law, a state that has persistently and
  consistently objected to it from the outset of its formation is not bound by it — the persistent objector
  rule.
significance: |
  The leading authority for the persistent objector exception to the general rule that customary
  international law binds all states.
why-here: The source of the persistent objector rule — used whenever a fact pattern raises whether a state can escape a customary obligation it has always rejected.
