aglc: Nuclear Tests Cases (Australia v France, New Zealand v France) [1974] ICJ Rep 253
facts: |
  Australia and New Zealand challenged France's atmospheric nuclear weapons testing in the Pacific; before a
  hearing on the merits could proceed, the French President and Foreign Minister made a series of public
  statements, including at press conferences, indicating that France would cease atmospheric testing.
issue: |
  Whether the French government's public statements themselves created a binding legal obligation to cease
  atmospheric nuclear testing, without any of the elements ordinarily required for a treaty.
held: |
  The ICJ found that the official French statements testified to an intention to be legally bound to cease
  atmospheric tests, and interpreted them as constituting a legal undertaking not to conduct such tests after
  1974; "nothing in the nature of a quid pro quo, nor any subsequent acceptance of the declaration, nor even
  any reply or reaction from other States, is required" for a unilateral statement to bind.
ratio: |
  A unilateral declaration, made with the intention to be bound according to its terms, confers on itself the
  character of a legal undertaking; the binding character of such statements rests on the principle of good
  faith, and no reciprocity or acceptance by other states is needed.
significance: |
  Confirms and applies the Eastern Greenland principle to modern unilateral statements made through official
  press declarations, and is reflected in the ILC's later Guiding Principles on unilateral declarations.
why-here: Applies the unilateral-statement doctrine to a modern example — used to show how courts assess the words, context and intention behind a state's public statement.
