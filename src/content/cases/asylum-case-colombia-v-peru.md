facts: |
  Colombia had granted diplomatic asylum, in its embassy in Lima, to a Peruvian political leader, and argued
  that as the state granting asylum it had the unilateral right to qualify the nature of the offence for the
  purposes of the asylum, binding on Peru, on the basis of an alleged regional customary rule of Latin
  American diplomatic asylum.
issue: |
  Whether a regional rule of customary international law existed, entitling the state granting diplomatic
  asylum to make a unilateral and definitive qualification of the offence, binding on the territorial state.
held: |
  The ICJ held that Colombia had not proven the existence of such a customary rule: the practice disclosed
  "so much uncertainty and contradiction, so much fluctuation and discrepancy... that it is not possible to
  discern in all this any constant and uniform usage, accepted as law."
ratio: |
  A party asserting a rule of customary international law (particularly a regional one) must show consistent
  and uniform practice accepted as law; inconsistent, contradictory or politically expedient practice does
  not establish a binding custom.
significance: |
  Leading illustration of the practice element of customary international law failing for want of consistency
  and uniformity, and of the extra difficulty of proving a regional (rather than universal) custom.
why-here: Used as the model of a custom claim failing for lack of consistent and uniform state practice.
