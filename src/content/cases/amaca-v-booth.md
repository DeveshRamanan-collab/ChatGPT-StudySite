citation: (2011) 246 CLR 36
aglc: Amaca v Booth (2011) 246 CLR 36
facts: Booth had mesothelioma and 30 years of exposure to asbestos in brake linings manufactured by Amaca (about 27 years of heavy brake-lining exposure; his other exposures were trivial by comparison). The Dust Diseases Tribunal drew an "overwhelming inference" of causation.
issue: Whether an inference that Amaca's asbestos caused or contributed to Booth's mesothelioma was open when science could not say which exposure triggered the disease.
held: The Court of Appeal and the High Court upheld the Tribunal's inference. French CJ held that Booth's exposure to asbestos in brake linings manufactured by Amaca not only prospectively increased the risk of his contracting the disease but, in the event, [causally] contributed to its development and continuation.
ratio: A causal connection may be inferred from medical and expert evidence (French CJ also referred to the 'Bradford Hill criteria' as circumstantial evidence of a causal relationship between asbestos and mesothelioma). The inference was reasonably open because asbestos is effectively the only known cause of mesothelioma, Booth's heavy exposure dwarfed his other exposures, and expert evidence accepted at trial was that cumulative exposure contributes to the disease.
significance: Material contribution established in a dust disease case; contrasted with Amaca v Ellis. The CLA is never mentioned because s 3B(1) excludes dust disease proceedings under the Dust Diseases Tribunal Act 1989 (NSW).
why-here: Cite where the evidence supports an inference that the defendant's exposure contributed to the disease, not merely increased the risk.
