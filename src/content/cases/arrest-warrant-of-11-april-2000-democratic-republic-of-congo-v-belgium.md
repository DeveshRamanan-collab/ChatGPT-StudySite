facts: |
  A Belgian court, under universal jurisdiction, issued an arrest warrant for Yerodia, the sitting foreign minister
  of the DRC, accused of inciting hatred in a war-torn region. The DRC brought the case to the ICJ, claiming the
  warrant violated the minister's immunity.
issue: |
  Whether an incumbent foreign minister enjoys immunity from foreign criminal jurisdiction, and whether there is an
  exception for international crimes.
held: |
  Issuing the warrant violated the foreign minister's immunity. "The immunities accorded to Ministers for Foreign
  Affairs are not granted for their personal benefit, but to ensure the effective performance of their functions
  on behalf of their respective States." Current state practice does not support an exception to immunity from
  foreign criminal jurisdiction for international crimes (paras 58 et seq). But immunity "does not mean that they
  enjoy impunity": immunity is procedural, criminal responsibility substantive, and prosecution remains possible
  in the official's own state, after waiver, by other states once they leave office (for acts before or after office
  and private acts during it), and before international criminal courts with jurisdiction (paras 58–61).
ratio: |
  Personal immunity of an incumbent foreign minister is a rule of customary international law, grounded in function,
  and has no exception for international crimes; immunity is not impunity.
significance: |
  The leading authority on personal immunity (ratione personae) of high-ranking officials and on the separation
  between immunity and individual criminal responsibility.
