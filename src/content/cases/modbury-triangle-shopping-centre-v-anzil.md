name: Modbury Triangle Shopping Centre v Anzil
citation: (2000) 205 CLR 254
aglc: Modbury Triangle Shopping Centre Pty Ltd v Anzil (2000) 205 CLR 254
facts: |
  Anzil, an employee of a video shop in the shopping centre, was attacked by unknown assailants at 10.30pm
  while walking to his car after the car park lights — usually left on until 11pm — went off on their
  10pm timer as usual.
issue: |
  Whether the shopping centre, as occupier, owed Anzil a duty of care to prevent physical injury inflicted by
  the criminal acts of third parties in its car park.
held: |
  The High Court, by majority, held that the occupier's duty of care did not extend to protecting Anzil from
  the criminal conduct of third parties; the occupier's duty over the state of the premises rested on its
  control of, and knowledge about, the premises, not on an assumed responsibility to guard against
  unpredictable criminal acts.
ratio: |
  An occupier does not, merely by controlling premises, owe a duty to prevent the independent criminal
  conduct of third parties on them, absent a high degree of foreseeability and predictability of that
  conduct, control over the third party, or an assumption of responsibility.
significance: |
  Confines occupiers' liability for omissions: control over premises is not the same as control over the
  people who use them, so no general duty arises unless foreseeability of the specific risk is very high.
why-here: Used to contrast with cases like Hargrave v Goldman, where the defendant's control over the risk itself founded a duty — here the defendant had no control over the third parties.
