facts: The defendant was authorised by the occupier to keep an eye on the house while the occupier was away, and was told where the spare key was kept; he later entered the house to steal property.
issue: Whether a person with express authority to enter for one purpose is a trespasser when they enter for a different, unauthorised purpose instead.
held: The defendant was a trespasser when he entered to steal, despite having been given the key and authority to enter for the purpose of keeping an eye on the house.
ratio: Authority or a licence to enter for one purpose does not extend to entry for a different, unauthorised purpose; entering outside the scope of the licence is trespass even where the entrant otherwise has permission and the means of access.
significance: Confirms that a licence is confined to its purpose, even where the entrant has been given a key and express permission.
why-here: Shows that even a keyholder with express permission becomes a trespasser once acting outside the purpose for which the key was given.
