facts: Crimmins worked as a waterside worker at Port Melbourne 1961–65. Stevedoring operations were regulated by the Australian Stevedoring Industry Authority, which directed workers where to work. The Authority was later abolished and the Stevedoring Industry Finance Committee (SIFC) assumed its liabilities. In 1997 Crimmins was diagnosed with mesothelioma caused by unloading asbestos cargoes. The Authority was not his employer.
issue: Did the Authority owe Crimmins a duty of care, and did its liability pass to the SIFC?
held: "The High Court held that a duty of care was owed, because the Authority/SIFC had an overarching supervisory and regulatory role regarding safety, and individual employers were required to supply safety equipment. (The lecture adds that the liability did pass to the SIFC, which stands in the Authority's shoes.) Hayne J dissented: no duty was owed in deciding whether to exercise the quasi-legislative power to order the use of respirators."
ratio: "McHugh J: the statutory authority owed a duty because it directed the worker to places of work with risks of injury of which it was, or ought to have been, aware, and it knew or ought to have known he was specially vulnerable — through the casual nature of his employment and his obligation to obey its directions."
