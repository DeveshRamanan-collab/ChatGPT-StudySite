facts: |
  Nigeria bought cement from Trendex, a UK company. Ending up with too much, it had the Central Bank of Nigeria
  stop the letter of credit, so Trendex was not paid. Trendex sued in the UK; Nigeria claimed immunity.
issue: |
  Whether the Central Bank of Nigeria, an organ of the state, could claim immunity in proceedings arising out of
  its failure to honour a letter of credit.
held: |
  The English Court of Appeal rejected immunity. The Central Bank was part of the state, but the nature of the
  transaction was commercial, not a sovereign act, so immunity did not apply.
ratio: |
  Immunity does not extend to acts of a commercial nature (jure gestionis), even when done by an organ of the
  state; the court looks at the nature of the transaction.
significance: |
  A key common law decision recognising the restrictive approach; the principle is replicated in art 10 of the UN
  Convention on Jurisdictional Immunities (2004).
why-here: Used to show that a state organ in an ordinary commercial transaction cannot claim immunity.
