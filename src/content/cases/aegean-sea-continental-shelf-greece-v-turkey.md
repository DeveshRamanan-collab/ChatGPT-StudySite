facts: |
  Greece and Turkey disputed the delimitation of the continental shelf in the Aegean Sea. Before the case
  reached the ICJ, the two states' Prime Ministers had issued a joint press communiqué after meeting in
  Brussels.
issue: |
  Whether the press communiqué was itself an "international agreement" capable of creating rights and
  obligations, even though it was not a polished, formal treaty document.
held: |
  The Court held (this week's slides record) that a press communiqué could be considered a legally binding
  international agreement, at para 96 of the judgment.
ratio: |
  A document does not need to be a formal, polished instrument to satisfy art 2(1)(a) VCLT's "written form" —
  substance and the parties' apparent intention matter more than the document's label or formality.
significance: |
  Cited on the slides as the first illustration that intention to create binding obligations can be found in
  an informal written document, not only in a treaty that calls itself one.
why-here: Used to show a treaty does not need to look like a treaty — even a joint press statement can count.
