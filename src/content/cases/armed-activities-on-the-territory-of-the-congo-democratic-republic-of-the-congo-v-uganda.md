  Whether Uganda's action was a lawful exercise of self-defence — in particular, whether it was necessary and
  proportionate, and whether attacks by armed groups operating from the Congo could be attributed to the Congo.
held: |-

  necessary to that end" (223 [147]). The slides also cite the case, with the Wall Advisory Opinion, for the importance
  the Court gives to attribution to the host state when self-defence is invoked against non-state actors.

  A defensive response whose geographical reach far exceeds the attacks it answers is neither proportionate nor
  necessary; and self-defence against attacks by non-state actors turns on attribution to the host state.
