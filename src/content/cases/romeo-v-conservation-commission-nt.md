facts: |
  Fifteen-year-old Nadia Romeo, affected by alcohol, fell from the Dripstone Cliffs onto Casuarina Beach
  near Darwin at night and was rendered paraplegic. The cliffs and beach reserve were managed by the
  Conservation Commission, which had not fenced or lit the cliff edge; Romeo sued alleging negligence in
  failing to warn of, or fence off, the cliff.
issue: |
  Whether the Commission, as occupier in control of a public reserve, owed and had breached a duty of care
  to entrants by failing to fence or warn of the cliff.
held: |
  The High Court (Gaudron and McHugh JJ dissenting) held that although the Commission owed entrants a duty
  to take reasonable care against reasonably foreseeable risks of injury, it was not in breach of that duty
  by failing to fence or illuminate the cliff edge, because the danger was obvious.
ratio: |
  Duty of care is not owed in the abstract — the scope of an occupier's duty is measured against the
  obviousness of the danger and the care ordinarily exercised by entrants; there is no duty to warn of, or
  guard against, an obvious risk.
significance: |
  Leading authority distinguishing the existence of a duty from its scope, and establishing that an occupier
  need not guard against obvious dangers — later codified by CLA ss 5F–5H on obvious risk.
why-here: The key case for arguing that an occupier's duty does not extend to protecting entrants from open and obvious dangers.
