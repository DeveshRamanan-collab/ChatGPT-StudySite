facts: Residents without a proprietary interest in the land they occupied argued they had title to sue in private nuisance, relying on Khorasandjian v Bush.
issue: Whether title to sue in private nuisance requires a proprietary interest in the land, or whether mere occupation suffices as Khorasandjian v Bush had held.
held: The House of Lords overruled Khorasandjian v Bush.
ratio: Only a person with a proprietary interest in the land — an owner or a tenant in possession — has title to sue in private nuisance; mere occupation as a licensee is not enough.
significance: Restored the traditional (Oldham v Lawson) position on title to sue, resolving the inconsistency created by Bush and Deasy Investments.
