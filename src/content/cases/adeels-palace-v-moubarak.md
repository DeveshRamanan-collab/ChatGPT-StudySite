facts: After a fight in a nightclub on New Year's Eve, Moubarak was shot by another patron. He argued that the club was negligent in not providing any security, resulting in his injury.
issue: Whether the club's failure to provide security was a cause of the shooting injuries under CLA s 5D(1), and if not, whether it was an exceptional case under s 5D(2).
held: "The 'but for' test was not satisfied: the evidence at trial did not show that the presence of security personnel would have deterred or prevented the re-entry of the gunman. There was no reason to treat the case as an exceptional case under s 5D(2)."
ratio: In contrast to what Mason CJ observed in March v Stramare, s 5D(1) 'treats factual causation and scope of liability as separate and distinct issues', and factual causation under s 5D(1) is determined by the 'but for' test. That changing 'any of the circumstances in which the shootings occurred might have made a difference does not prove factual causation'; it 'would be contrary to established principles' to hold the defendant responsible when negligence was not a necessary condition but 'might have' prevented the harm [57].
significance: The leading High Court statement that s 5D(1) separates factual causation from scope of liability, and the one case on the slides in which s 5D(2) was considered and found not to apply.
why-here: Cite for the 'but for' test under s 5D(1)(a), for keeping factual causation separate from scope of liability, and for "might have made a difference" not being enough — including under s 5D(2).
