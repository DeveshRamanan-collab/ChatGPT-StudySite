facts: |
  The Court had to consider whether the World Health Organization had competence to address the legality of the
  use of nuclear weapons in view of their health and environmental effects.
issue: |
  Whether the question fell within the WHO's powers, given the principle of speciality governing international
  organisations.
held: |
  "The Court need hardly point out that international organizations are subjects of international law which do
  not, unlike States, possess a general competence. International organizations are governed by the 'principle
  of speciality', that is to say, they are invested by the States which create them with powers, the limits of
  which are a function of the common interests whose promotion those States entrust to them" [25]. Attributing
  that competence to the WHO would disregard the principle of speciality, since it could not be deemed a necessary
  implication of the WHO's constitution.
ratio: |
  International organisations have only the powers conferred on them by their members (express or by necessary
  implication), limited by the common interests entrusted to them; they have no general competence.
significance: |
  The leading statement of the principle of attribution (speciality) and of its limit on implied powers.
why-here: Used for the principle of speciality — no general competence for international organisations.
