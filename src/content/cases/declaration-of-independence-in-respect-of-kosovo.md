facts: |
  Following UN administration of the territory, Kosovo declared independence from Serbia in February 2008. The
  secession was supported by many Western countries and opposed by Serbia, Russia and China. Kosovo is recognised
  by more than 100 UN member states and is a member of the IMF and World Bank.
issue: |
  Whether the unilateral declaration of independence was in accordance with international law.
held: |
  The declaration of independence was not in itself illegal. The Court refrained from deciding whether Kosovo was
  a state, and refrained from saying that unilateral self-determination (secession) in that case was lawful. The
  Court referred to declarations connected with "the unlawful use of force or other egregious violations of norms
  of general international law, in particular those of a peremptory character (jus cogens)" as ones that should
  not lead to recognition.
ratio: |
  A declaration of independence is not, as such, prohibited by international law; illegality attaches where it is
  connected with the unlawful use of force or egregious violations of general international law, especially jus
  cogens.
significance: |
  The leading illustration of contested statehood and of the controversy over external self-determination
  (unilateral secession) outside the decolonisation context.
why-here: Used for illegality as a ground of non-recognition and for the unresolved question of a right to secede.
