facts: Boys in juvenile detention operated by the Home Office (working on an island under the supervision of officers, per Topic 5) escaped and caused significant damage to several yachts in the nearby harbour. The yacht owners sued the Home Office for negligence.
issue: Whether the Home Office owed the yacht owners a duty of care in relation to damage caused by the escaped boys.
held: "The House of Lords held that the Home Office owed the yacht owners a duty of care: it was reasonably foreseeable that the boys might cause damage if they escaped, and the detention officers had control over the boys. The scope of the duty was limited to damage caused in the vicinity immediately after escape; for reasons of policy, no duty existed as regards further damage while the escapees remained at large."
ratio: A duty of care can arise where a defendant has control over a third party who poses a foreseeable risk of harm to others, although policy considerations may limit the scope of the duty.
significance: The leading illustration of a duty arising from control over a third party.
