facts: Woods suffered serious injury to one eye while playing indoor cricket. The defendant organised the game and did not provide any helmets as part of the supplied equipment; it argued there were no specific helmets for indoor cricket and that the rules of the game did not require them.
issue: Whether the organiser breached its duty of care by not supplying protective helmets.
held: The High Court held by majority that the duty was not breached.
ratio: "Gleeson CJ took the sporting context into account: the plaintiff was an adult and the risk of some injury was likely, though only two serious eye injuries per year were reported out of 12,500 people playing indoor cricket in WA. The \"burden of taking precautions\" limb is not confined to financial cost but extends to practical considerations such as the availability of suitable safety equipment, convenience and safety: headgear had not been designed for indoor cricket, was not worn by players elsewhere, was not required by the rules, and raised its own convenience and safety concerns, so a reasonable provider was not required to supply it."
significance: "The authority for s 5B(2)(c): the burden of precautions includes practical considerations, not just cost."
