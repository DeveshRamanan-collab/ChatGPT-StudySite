aglc: San Sebastian Pty Ltd v Minister (1986) 162 CLR 340
facts: Developers bought and planned in Woolloomooloo on the strength of a publicly exhibited council redevelopment scheme. The council later abandoned the scheme, and the developers lost money.
issue: Whether a duty of care arose in respect of a published planning scheme on which developers relied without any request for advice.
held: "A duty can arise without any request — for example, through known special competence, an assumption of responsibility, inviting or intending reliance, or an interest in the recipient acting. The claim failed: the statement was one of intention, and relying on a mere intention was not reasonable, even though the reliance was genuine."
ratio: "Brennan J's conditions: the representor realises the representee will trust their special competence; it is reasonable for the representee to accept and rely on the statement; and loss is reasonably foreseeable if the statement is wrong. Reliance on a statement of intention is not reasonable."
significance: Confirms a request is not essential for a duty, while showing that genuine reliance can still be unreasonable.
why-here: Use for unsolicited public statements and for the point that reliance on a statement of intention is not reasonable.
