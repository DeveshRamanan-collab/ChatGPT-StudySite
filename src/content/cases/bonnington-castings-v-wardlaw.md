aglc: Bonnington Castings v Wardlaw [1956] AC 613
facts: The claimant contracted pneumoconiosis from cumulative inhalation of silica dust. Only part of the dust came from the employer's negligence.
issue: Whether the claimant had to show that the negligent dust was the sole or main cause of the disease.
held: It was enough that the negligent exposure made a contribution that was more than de minimis (or negligible). The claimant need not show it was the sole or main cause.
ratio: A negligent exposure that materially contributes to the harm — a contribution more than de minimis — establishes causation, even though other, non-negligent exposure also contributed.
significance: The foundational material contribution case in the dust disease line (UK); followed in Amaca v Booth. The textbook flowchart lists it as an example of multiple sufficient causes, while the lecture slides treat it as a material contribution case.
