facts: |
  Two rugby players were injured in a scrum and argued that the International Rugby Football Board owed
  them a duty of care to monitor the rules of the game and change them to reduce "unnecessary risk" to
  players.
issue: |
  Whether a sport's governing body owed players a duty of care to change the rules of the game to reduce
  risks inherent in it.
held: |
  The High Court rejected the claim; the Board owed no such duty of care to the players.
ratio: |
  Gleeson CJ: rugby is based on activities that are, by the standards of most members of the community,
  obviously dangerous, and the existence and extent of any duty is very fact-specific to the relationship in
  issue.
significance: |
  Shows that even in sport, a duty is not owed to every participant by every body connected with the
  activity — the closer a case gets to a policy-laden rule-making decision, the less likely a duty is found.
why-here: Contrasts with Rootes v Shelton — used to show that a duty found between co-participants does not extend automatically to a remote regulatory body.
