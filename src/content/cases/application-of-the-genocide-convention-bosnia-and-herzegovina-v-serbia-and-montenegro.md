facts: |
  Proceedings before the ICJ in which the Court had to decide whether acts committed by persons and groups
  could be attributed to Serbia and Montenegro. The materials extract the Court's reasoning on attribution only.
issue: |
  When may persons or groups that are not state organs under internal law be equated with state organs, and
  should the ICTY's "overall control" test replace the effective control test for attributing private conduct?
held: |
  Persons, groups or entities may be equated with state organs even if that status does not follow from internal
  law, provided they act in "complete dependence" on the state, of which they are ultimately merely the
  instrument — but this "must be exceptional" (paras 392–393). The "overall control" test "has the major drawback
  of broadening the scope of State responsibility well beyond the fundamental principle … a State is responsible
  only for its own conduct"; it is "unsuitable, for it stretches too far, almost to breaking point, the connection
  which must exist between the conduct of a State's organs and its international responsibility". Attribution
  requires instructions or direction, or effective control over the action during which the wrong was committed,
  under the customary rule reflected in art 8 (para 398).
ratio: |
  Private conduct is attributable under art 8 only on instructions or effective control over the specific
  operation; de facto organ status requires complete dependence and is exceptional.
significance: |
  Rejects Tadić's overall control test and confirms effective control as "the law as it stands now", though
  some disagree with the Court's conclusion.
