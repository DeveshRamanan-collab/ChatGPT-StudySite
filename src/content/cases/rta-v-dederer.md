aglc: Roads and Traffic Authority of NSW v Dederer (2007) 234 CLR 330
facts: A 14-year-old dived off a bridge into an estuary with a shifting sandbar and suffered partial paraplegia. The bridge carried "fishing and climbing prohibited" signs and pictograph "no diving" signs. Jumping from it was a widespread practice that had never previously injured anyone, and he had seen others do it unharmed. The RTA knew of the jumping and that riverbed levels were continually altered. 1992 safety standards required vertical rather than horizontal railing bars, but the railings were not modified.
issue: Whether the RTA breached its duty of care — the plaintiff argued that the signs should have warned of the shifting sands and that the railings should have been replaced — and how the risk of harm should be identified.
held: By 3:2 (Gummow, Callinan and Heydon JJ), no breach of duty. The obligation was to exercise reasonable care, not to prevent harm; the risk was foreseeable but the probability low; the existing signs warning of, and prohibiting, diving were a reasonable response.
ratio: "Gummow J: the correct identification of the risk is critical. The Court of Appeal's \"serious spinal injury flowing from the act of diving off the bridge\" \"obscured the true source of potential injury [which arose] from impact upon jumping into the potentially shallow water and shifting sands of the estuary\" [60]. The risk must be specific enough to correspond to the precautions in issue. Probability is judged prospectively, not retrospectively; the magnitude of risk was grave but the probability low; given the expense, difficulty and inconvenience, a reasonable response did not demand the measures suggested. \"If the [R]TA exercised reasonable care, it would not be liable even if the risk-taking conduct continued.\""
