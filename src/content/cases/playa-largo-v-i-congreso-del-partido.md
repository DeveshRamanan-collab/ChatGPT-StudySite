facts: |
  Cuba's state-owned shipping company agreed to deliver sugar to a company in Chile but was ordered to divert the
  ships due to political tensions after a coup in Chile. Cuba claimed sovereign immunity, arguing the diversion had
  a foreign policy objective.
issue: |
  Whether the diversion was a sovereign act (jure imperii) attracting immunity because of its policy purpose, or a
  commercial act (jure gestionis) judged by its nature.
held: |
  The UK House of Lords rejected Cuba's immunity claim, holding that the shipping activity was commercial (jure
  gestionis), not sovereign (jure imperii).
ratio: |
  Whether conduct is sovereign or commercial depends on its nature, not on the state's policy motive; a
  foreign-policy reason for breaching a commercial contract does not make the act sovereign.
significance: |
  A landmark case on the nature-versus-purpose test later reflected in art 2(2) of the 2004 UN Convention.
why-here: Used to apply the nature test where a state claims a political purpose for commercial conduct.
