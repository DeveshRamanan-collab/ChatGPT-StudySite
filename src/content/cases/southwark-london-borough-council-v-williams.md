facts: Two homeless families, in desperate circumstances, made an orderly entry into empty council-owned houses in Southwark that were awaiting redevelopment; the council sought summary possession orders.
issue: Whether necessity is available as a defence to trespass where the defendants entered land because they were homeless.
held: The Court of Appeal unanimously held that necessity did not excuse the trespass.
ratio: The necessity defence is confined to cases of great and imminent danger to life, and does not extend to homelessness or housing need; admitting such a plea would mean "no one's house could be safe".
significance: Sets the outer limit of the necessity defence, and Lord Denning's warning that the doctrine "must be carefully circumscribed".
why-here: The leading case for how narrowly necessity is construed.
