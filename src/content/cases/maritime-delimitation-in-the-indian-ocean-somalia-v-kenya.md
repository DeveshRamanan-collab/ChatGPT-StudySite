facts: |
  Kenya and Somalia signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) concerning the delimitation of their maritime
  boundary. The MoU was signed, dated, and registered with the United Nations.
issue: |
  Whether a document styled as a Memorandum of Understanding — normally treated in Australian practice as
  "less than a treaty" — was nonetheless a treaty under international law, and whether it had entered into
  force on signature as it expressly provided.
held: |
  The Court held the MoU was a treaty under international law (at para 42, per the slides): it recorded an
  agreement by the parties, was signed, dated and registered with the UN, and had all the elements of a
  treaty. By contrast to the general rule in art 24, the MoU expressly stated it entered into force only upon
  signature.
ratio: |
  An instrument labelled a "memorandum of understanding" can still meet art 2(1)(a) VCLT and be a binding
  treaty at international law, regardless of the "less than treaty" status Australian domestic practice gives
  MoUs.
significance: |
  Directly used in the slides to contrast international law's treatment of MoUs with Australia's own DFAT
  practice of treating an MoU as having "less than a treaty" status.
why-here: Shows an MoU can be a treaty internationally even though Australia's own practice treats MoUs as non-treaties.
