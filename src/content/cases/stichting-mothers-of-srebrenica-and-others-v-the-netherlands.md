facts: |
  In 1995 the Srebrenica safe area, protected by a Dutch battalion of UNPROFOR, was overrun by Bosnian Serb forces;
  the ICJ and ICTY later found the Srebrenica operation amounted to genocide. Families of victims sued the UN and
  the Netherlands in Dutch courts for failing to prevent the genocide.
issue: |
  Whether the UN could rely on its immunity before domestic courts in proceedings over peacekeeping conduct.
held: |
  The Dutch Supreme Court (2012) threw out the case against the UN on the ground of immunity, and the ECtHR (2013)
  confirmed it: both recognised UN immunity before domestic courts. The case continued against the Netherlands,
  which the Dutch Supreme Court in 2019 found partially responsible, for the part over which it had effective
  control of the troops.
ratio: |
  The UN's immunity from domestic jurisdiction is upheld even for claims concerning a failure to prevent genocide;
  the responsibility of the troop-contributing state can be pursued separately.
