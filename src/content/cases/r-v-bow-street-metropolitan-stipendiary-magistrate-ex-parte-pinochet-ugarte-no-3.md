facts: |
  Augusto Pinochet, former Chilean president (in office 1973–1990), travelled to the UK for medical treatment.
  Spanish courts brought a criminal case against him under Spain's universal jurisdiction laws, including charges
  of torture, and requested extradition. He was arrested in the UK, giving rise to extradition proceedings.
issue: |
  Whether a former head of state retains (functional) immunity from foreign criminal proceedings for torture
  committed while in office.
held: |
  While there is absolute immunity for serving heads of state, there is no immunity for former heads of state in
  relation to acts which, at the time they occurred, constituted an international crime for which a form of
  universal jurisdiction has been established (e.g. torture as enshrined in the Convention against Torture). A few
  judges relied on the jus cogens nature of the prohibition on torture; the majority relied on the UK, Chile and
  Spain all being parties to the Convention against Torture. Pinochet was ultimately allowed to leave the country on
  medical grounds (he died in 2006).
ratio: |
  Functional immunity of a former head of state does not cover torture where, at the time, it was an international
  crime subject to universal jurisdiction under a treaty binding the relevant states.
significance: |
  The first domestic decision to accept an exception to functional immunity for international crimes; evidence of
  emerging state practice.
