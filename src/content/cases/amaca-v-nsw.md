name: Amaca v NSW
citation: "[2004] NSWCA 124"
aglc: Amaca v NSW [2004] NSWCA 124
facts: Hay sued his employer, Rolls Royce, and Pacific Power (the site owner) for exposure to asbestos. Both defendants cross-claimed against James Hardie, the manufacturer of asbestos; James Hardie was held liable, but it cross-claimed against the State of NSW. The cross-claim was rejected at trial and by the Court of Appeal; the High Court reversed on the principles and remitted; on remitter the Court of Appeal again held no duty was owed.
issue: Whether the State of NSW owed a duty of care in relation to the asbestos exposure.
held: "The Court of Appeal held that the State did not owe a duty of care: James Hardie and others had no less knowledge than the State of the dangers of asbestos dust; the State's control was much less than that of Rolls Royce (employer), Pacific Power (site owner) and James Hardie (supplier); the plaintiffs were not vulnerable to the State, as they could rely on their employers, the suppliers and the owners and occupiers; and a duty would impose a massive obligation on the State."
ratio: "Five propositions: the totality of the relationship decides the duty; relevant control includes control of any situation containing a risk of harm to others; no duty merely because an authority might have prevented harm by exercising a statutory power; statutory powers and their mere prior exercise create no duty to exercise them in future; knowledge that harm may result from a failure to exercise a power is not itself sufficient."
significance: A synthesis of the common law on public authority omissions; decided at common law because it is a dust disease case excluded from the Civil Liability Act by s 3B(1)(b).
why-here: Supplies the five propositions to test a claim that an authority should have used its powers.
