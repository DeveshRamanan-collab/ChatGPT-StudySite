facts: |
  A boy riding his bicycle on a public street was hit by a truck driven by the defendant and injured. He sued
  for damages in negligence rather than trespass to the person. The accident occurred on 7 May 1952, but the
  action was not started until 19 July 1955 — more than three years, but less than six years, after the
  accident. Trespass to the person had a three-year limitation period; negligence (an action on the case) had
  a six-year period.
issue: |
  Whether the plaintiff, whose facts could satisfy either trespass to the person or negligence, could choose to
  frame the claim as negligence in order to gain the benefit of the longer six-year limitation period.
held: |
  The High Court dismissed the appeal. The plaintiff was entitled to frame the action in negligence, so the
  six-year limitation period applied, even though the same facts might also have supported a trespass claim.
ratio: |
  The same set of facts can give rise to two distinct causes of action (trespass to the person and negligence)
  with different essential ingredients and different limitation periods; a plaintiff may choose which cause of
  action to plead, provided the elements of the chosen cause of action are proved.
significance: |
  Confirms that the trespass/action-on-the-case distinction still has real, practical consequences (such as
  limitation periods) in modern Australian law, not merely historical interest.
