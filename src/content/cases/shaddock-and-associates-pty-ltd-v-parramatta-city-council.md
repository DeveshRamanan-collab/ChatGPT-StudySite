name: Shaddock & Associates Pty Ltd v Parramatta City Council
citation: (1981) 150 CLR 225
aglc: Shaddock & Associates Pty Ltd v Parramatta City Council (1981) 150 CLR 225
facts: An intending purchaser's solicitor asked the council whether any road-widening proposal affected the land. The council said no, first orally by phone and then in a certificate; neither disclosed the scheme that existed. The purchase proceeded, the road-widening scheme emerged, and the land's value was destroyed.
issue: Whether the council owed the purchaser a duty of care in providing the information.
held: Duty found. The duty covers information as well as advice, and there is no separate requirement that the speaker possess special skill. The council was the only source of the information and the reliance on it was clear; the Court suggested the phone call alone might not have been enough, but the phone call together with the certificate was.
ratio: A public authority that provides factual information, knowing it will be relied on, can owe a duty of care; the duty extends to information as well as advice and does not depend on the speaker having special skill.
significance: Shows a public authority can owe a duty for negligent information, and removes any separate special-skill requirement.
why-here: The authority for a duty over factual information from a sole source (often a public body), without special skill.
