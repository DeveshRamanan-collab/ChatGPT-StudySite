facts: A police officer entered the open driveway of a house in order to arrest a disqualified driver who had driven onto the property.
issue: Whether entering an unobstructed driveway without express permission, to communicate with or apprehend someone there, is a trespass.
held: The officer had an implied licence to enter the driveway; there was no trespass.
ratio: Where access to a dwelling by path or driveway is unobstructed, unlocked and not marked by a prohibiting sign, the law implies a licence for any member of the public, including police, to go upon it for the purpose of lawful communication with an occupier.
significance: The leading Australian statement of the implied licence to enter, applied and elaborated in Roy v O'Neill.
why-here: The starting test for every implied-licence problem — establish the licence exists before asking whether the entrant went beyond it.
