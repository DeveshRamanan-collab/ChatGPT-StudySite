facts: |
  Portugal held a small enclave within Indian territory, accessible only by a route passing through Indian
  territory; Portugal had used that route for centuries, and argued it had a right of passage even though no
  treaty between Portugal and India established one, which India disputed.
issue: |
  Whether a rule of customary international law establishing a right of passage could exist as between only
  two states, in the absence of any treaty, and whether it did so here.
held: |
  The ICJ held that the long, consistent practice between Portugal and India supported a customary right of
  passage for Portugal over Indian territory to access its enclave, even though it bound only these two
  states.
ratio: |
  Customary international law is not limited to universal or general rules — a particular custom can exist
  and bind as few as two states, provided the practice between them meets the ordinary requirements of
  consistency and acceptance as law.
significance: |
  Establishes that particular (including bilateral) custom is possible, alongside general/universal customary
  international law.
