facts: Fontin incorrectly accused Katapodis of not paying for goods, leading to a heated argument. Katapodis picked up a wooden T-square and broke it on Fontin's shoulder; Fontin then threw an off-cut of glass at Katapodis's face, injuring his hand.
issue: Whether Fontin's response was a proportionate act of self-defence, and whether Katapodis's provocation reduced Fontin's liability.
held: |-
  The High Court held that while Fontin had the right to defend himself, throwing the glass was "out of
  all reasonable proportion to the emergency confronting Fontin"; provocation could not reduce the
  compensatory damages awarded against him.
ratio: Self-defence fails where the force used is disproportionate to the threat faced, and provocation is not a defence that reduces compensatory damages in tort, though it may affect exemplary damages.
significance: The leading illustration of the proportionality limb of self-defence, and of provocation's limited role in tort.
