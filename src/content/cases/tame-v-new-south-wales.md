facts: |
  A police officer completing an accident report mistakenly recorded the plaintiff's blood alcohol reading
  as 0.14 (it was in fact nil). Although the error was corrected within weeks, the plaintiff's solicitor told
  her about it over a year later and she developed a psychotic depressive illness.
issue: |
  Whether the police officer owed the plaintiff a duty of care not to cause her psychiatric injury.
held: |
  The High Court held (heard together with Annetts) that the officer did not owe Mrs Tame a duty of care,
  because it was not reasonably foreseeable that a person in her position would suffer a recognised
  psychiatric illness from an error that had been corrected and apologised for; her reaction was extreme and
  idiosyncratic.
ratio: |
  Reasonable foreseeability of a recognised psychiatric illness is the central test for a psychiatric-harm
  duty of care; the "control mechanisms" of normal fortitude, sudden shock, and direct perception (or its
  immediate aftermath) are relevant considerations, not fixed pre-conditions for liability.
significance: |
  Rejects rigid, categorical control mechanisms for psychiatric harm in favour of a single foreseeability
  inquiry; the common law test is now qualified for NSW cases by CLA Part 3 (ss 27–33).
why-here: The leading common law case for psychiatric harm — used together with Annetts to state and apply the general foreseeability test.
