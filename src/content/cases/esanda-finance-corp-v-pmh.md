name: Esanda Finance Corp v PMH
citation: (1997) 188 CLR 241
aglc: Esanda Finance Corp v PMH (1997) 188 CLR 241
facts: Esanda lent money to companies associated with Excel, taking Excel's guarantee in reliance on its accounts. PMH had audited and certified Excel's accounts. Esanda alleged PMH were negligent and that it consequently suffered economic loss after Excel was placed into receivership.
issue: Whether PMH, as auditors, owed Esanda (a financier relying on the audited accounts) a duty of care.
held: "The High Court held that no such duty existed — there were no circumstances which might serve to establish any relationship of proximity between Esanda and PMH. Pleading that Esanda belonged to a class who might reasonably rely on audited accounts was not enough: there was no request, no intention to induce Esanda to act, and no assumption of responsibility."
ratio: Mere foreseeability of pure economic loss does not give rise to a duty of care; the law requires, in addition, a special relationship (proximity) between the parties, shown by factors such as reasonable reliance, the nature of the relationship, special skill or knowledge, knowledge of reliance and assumption of responsibility (Dawson J).
significance: Leading High Court case on negligent misstatement; McHugh J's policy reasons against auditors' liability to third parties — higher auditing costs, more complex litigation, indeterminate liability and difficulty proving reliance.
why-here: The first case to go to for pure economic loss caused by negligent words.
