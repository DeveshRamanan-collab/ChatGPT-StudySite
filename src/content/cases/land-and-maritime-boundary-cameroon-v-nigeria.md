facts: |
  A boundary dispute between Cameroon and Nigeria, with Equatorial Guinea intervening, concerning their land
  and maritime boundary.
issue: |
  Whether a state can invoke a violation of its own internal (domestic) law about who had competence to
  conclude a treaty as a ground to invalidate its consent to be bound.
held: |
  The Court confirmed the art 46 VCLT rule: a state cannot invoke the fact that its consent to be bound by a
  treaty was expressed in violation of its internal law, unless the violation was "manifest and concerned a
  rule of its internal law of fundamental importance".
ratio: |
  Violating one's own domestic law on treaty-making competence does not, without more, invalidate consent to
  be bound — only a manifest violation of a fundamentally important internal rule can.

  treaty.
