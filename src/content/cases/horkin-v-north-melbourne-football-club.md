facts: Horkin was asked to leave a football club because he had had too much to drink; when he did not leave, club employees threw him out with more force than necessary, injuring him.
issue: Whether the plaintiff's own carelessness (intoxication, failing to leave when asked) should reduce the defendant's liability for an intentional battery.
held: The trial judge reduced the defendant's liability by 30% for contributory negligence; the Victorian Supreme Court held this was unfair and unjust, and restored full liability.
ratio: |-
  Contributory negligence is not a defence to an intentional tort: the difference between an intentional
  wrong and the plaintiff's carelessness "is not merely in degree but in kind", and the social
  condemnation attached to intentional wrongdoing differs markedly from carelessness.
significance: The leading Australian rejection of contributory negligence as a defence, or damages-reducing factor, for intentional torts.
