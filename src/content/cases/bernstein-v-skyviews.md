facts: |-
  Skyviews' aircraft flew several hundred feet above Lord Bernstein's estate, took a single aerial
  photograph of his house without permission, and offered it to him for sale. Bernstein sued in trespass,
  asserting ownership of the airspace above his land on the *usque ad coelum* maxim, and an invasion of
  his privacy.
issue: Whether flying an aircraft at a height that does not affect the use of the land beneath it is a trespass to the airspace above that land.
held: There was no trespass.
ratio: A landowner's rights in the airspace above land extend only to such height as is necessary for the ordinary use and enjoyment of the land and structures on it; above that height the owner has no greater rights in the airspace than any other member of the public.
significance: Rejects an unlimited application of the usque ad coelum maxim and fixes the modern test for how high "land" extends for the purposes of trespass.
why-here: Gives the height test that Kelsen and LJP Investments then apply at the low end of the airspace.
