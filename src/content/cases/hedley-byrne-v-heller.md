facts: |
  Hedley Byrne, an advertising agency, asked its bank, National Provincial, to check the credit status of
  Easipower Ltd with Easipower's own bank, Heller & Partners. Heller gave a favourable credit reference, but
  expressly "without responsibility". Relying on that reference, Hedley Byrne extended credit to Easipower and
  suffered loss when Easipower went into liquidation. Hedley Byrne sued Heller for negligent misstatement.
issue: Whether a duty of care can arise for negligent words causing pure economic loss (loss not derived from personal injury or property damage), and, if so, whether such a duty existed on these facts.
held: |
  The House of Lords held that a duty of care can arise where careless advice or information causes pure
  economic loss, provided there is a special relationship between the parties involving a voluntary assumption
  of responsibility by the party giving the advice and reasonable reliance by the other party. On the facts,
  however, Hedley Byrne's claim failed because Heller had expressly disclaimed responsibility for the
  reference.
ratio: A duty of care for negligent misstatement causing pure economic loss can arise where there is a special relationship between the parties — a voluntary assumption of responsibility by the adviser, reliance by the other party, and reasonableness of that reliance — but an effective disclaimer of responsibility negates the duty.
significance: Founding authority for duty of care in negligent misstatement and pure economic loss, extending negligence beyond the Donoghue v Stevenson paradigm of physical injury or property damage.
