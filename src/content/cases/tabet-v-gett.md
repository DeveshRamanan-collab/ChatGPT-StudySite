facts: A six-year-old child, Tabet, suffered permanent brain damage after a delay in performing a CT scan during hospital treatment. She alleged that the defendant doctor's negligence deprived her of a chance of a better medical outcome. The chance of a better outcome was less than 50%.
issue: Whether the court should 'permit recovery for physical injury not shown to be caused or contributed to by a negligent party, but which negligence has deprived the victim of the possibility (but not the probability) of a better outcome' [25].
held: No. In personal injury cases the law of negligence as understood in the common law of Australia does not entertain an action for recovery when the damage is characterised as the loss of a chance of a better outcome (Gummow ACJ [46]).
ratio: Loss of a chance of a better medical outcome, where the better outcome was only possible and not probable, is not compensable damage in personal injury cases. This does not require acceptance in absolute terms that destruction of the chance of obtaining a benefit or avoiding a harm can never supply the damage that is the gist of an action in negligence (Gummow ACJ [27]).
significance: "Keeps the balance of probabilities at the centre of factual causation in medical negligence: a less than even chance is a possibility, not proof of causation."
why-here: Cite where the plaintiff can show only a less than 50% chance that proper care would have produced a better outcome.
