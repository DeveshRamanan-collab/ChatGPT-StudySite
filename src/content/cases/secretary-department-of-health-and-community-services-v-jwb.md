facts: |-
  An application was brought by the parents of Marion, an intellectually disabled teenager, seeking court
  authorisation for her sterilisation.
issue: Whether parents could consent to non-therapeutic medical treatment (sterilisation) of their intellectually disabled child, and how consent to medical treatment by or for minors should be classified.
held: |-
  The High Court approved the Gillick test for a minor's capacity to consent, held that a child's
  disability does not of itself defeat capacity, and ruled that parents cannot consent to non-therapeutic
  treatment that is not in the child's interests. McHugh J reasoned in obiter that consent is a defence to
  trespass to the person, so the onus is on the defendant to prove it.
ratio: A minor can give informed consent once she has sufficient understanding and intelligence to understand fully what is proposed; parental consent does not extend to authorising non-therapeutic treatment against the child's interests.
significance: The leading Australian case adopting Gillick competence and marking the limits of parental consent, plus McHugh J's influential obiter on the onus question.
