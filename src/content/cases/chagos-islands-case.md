facts: |
  Mauritius claimed the Chagos archipelago, administered by the United Kingdom as the British Indian Ocean
  Territory (home to an important military base); the UK had "no doubt" of its sovereignty. The UK had separated
  the islands and removed their population before Mauritius became independent. The General Assembly requested
  an advisory opinion.
issue: |
  Whether the decolonisation of Mauritius had been lawfully completed, given the separation of the Chagos
  archipelago, and the consequences under international law.
held: |
  The Court affirmed the right of self-determination of the people concerned in the decolonisation context, found
  that the UK's separation of the islands and removal of the population before independence violated that right,
  and noted the UK's obligation to complete the decolonisation of Mauritius. In late 2022 Mauritius and the UK
  began negotiations on the future status of the Chagos.
ratio: |
  In the decolonisation context, the right of peoples to self-determination is an established rule of
  international law, and a colonial power's detachment of part of a colony before independence violates it.
significance: |
  The key modern authority for the right to self-determination in the decolonisation context.
why-here: Used as the clear case of self-determination in the decolonisation context, to contrast with contested secession.
