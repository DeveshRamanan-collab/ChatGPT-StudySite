facts: While driving a truck on the F5 freeway near Sydney, Evans, an employee of Refrigerated Roadways, was struck by a large piece of concrete dropped by third parties from an overhead bridge, and died. The RTA knew of the problem of people dropping objects from overhead bridges.
issue: Whether the RTA owed a duty of care in respect of erecting a structure to prevent objects being thrown off the bridge; the RTA relied on Modbury Triangle Shopping Centre v Anzil.
held: "The NSW Court of Appeal held that the RTA owed such a duty, distinguishing Modbury Triangle (where the harm could only arise through criminal conduct). Section 43A CLA would not have helped the RTA, as erecting protective screens is not an exercise of a special statutory power. But the RTA did not breach its duty: it had not been shown that it failed to take reasonable steps to prevent the risk of harm."
ratio: The RTA has "a unique role to play concerning any dangers of objects falling from an overpass onto cars on a freeway below"; freeway drivers are vulnerable, and many serious road accidents involve criminal conduct by one party.
significance: Shows the common law and CLA Part 5 working together, and that a public authority can owe a duty about third parties' conduct where it has a unique role, knowledge and vulnerable road users.
