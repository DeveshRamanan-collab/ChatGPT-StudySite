name: Strong v Woolworths Ltd t/as Big W

aglc: Strong v Woolworths Ltd t/as Big W (2012) 246 CLR 182
facts: Ms Strong, who walks with the aid of crutches because of a disability, slipped outside the entrance to a Big W store and suffered a spinal injury when her crutch slid on a greasy potato chip. Woolworths, trading as Big W, had no system to inspect and clean the area where she fell. The District Court found Woolworths negligent; the Court of Appeal reversed that finding.
issue: Whether Woolworths' failure to have an inspection and cleaning system was a necessary condition of Ms Strong's fall (CLA s 5D(1)(a)), proved on the balance of probabilities (s 5E), when no one knew when the chip had been dropped.
held: "Woolworths' negligence lay in its failure to employ a system for the periodic inspection and cleaning of the area (duty: occupier/entrant; an appropriate system = inspection and removal of slipping hazards at intervals of not greater than 20 minutes). 'The probabilities favoured the conclusion that the chip was deposited in the longer period between 8.00am and 12.10pm and not the shorter period between 12.10pm and the time of the fall' [38], so causation was established."
ratio: "Section 5D(1)(a) 'is a statutory statement of the \"but for\" test of causation': the defendant's negligence must be a necessary condition of the occurrence of the harm, decided on the balance of probabilities (s 5E)."
significance: Clarifies s 5D(1) and (2) and s 5E, and shows factual causation proved by inference about when a hazard arose.
why-here: Cite for s 5D(1)(a) as the statutory 'but for' test and for proving causation on the balance of probabilities when the timing of the hazard is unknown.
