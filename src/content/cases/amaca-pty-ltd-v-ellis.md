facts: The claimant developed lung cancer. He had been exposed to asbestos and had also smoked. The evidence was limited exposure, mainly epidemiological (risk) evidence, and a competing cause (smoking).
issue: Whether showing that the asbestos exposure increased the risk of lung cancer was enough to establish causation.
held: Showing that exposure increased the risk is not sufficient. The claimant must prove, on the balance of probabilities, that the exposure actually contributed to the cancer, and the evidence in the case could not establish this.
ratio: "Risk alone is not enough: an increase in the risk of harm does not establish factual causation without proof, on the balance of probabilities, that the defendant's conduct contributed to the harm."
significance: The High Court's rejection of a risk-based test of causation; contrasted on the slides with Amaca v Booth. The textbook flowchart lists it under material contribution to harm.
why-here: Cite where the evidence shows only an increased risk — especially with a competing cause — to argue causation fails.
