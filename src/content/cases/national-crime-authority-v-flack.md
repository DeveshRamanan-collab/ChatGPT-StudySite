aglc: National Crime Authority v Flack (1988) 86 FCR 16
facts: Mrs Flack sued for conversion after the National Crime Authority neither initiated any prosecution nor returned a briefcase containing money that had been on her premises, holding it for three years.
issue: Whether the finder or occupier of a chattel has the possession required to sue in trespass or conversion.
held: Mrs Flack had possession in law over all chattels on the premises, including those she was unaware of, giving her possessory rights against everyone except the true owner.
ratio: A person in occupation of premises has possession in law of chattels found there, sufficient to sue anyone but the true owner, even without knowledge of the chattel's existence.
significance: Establishes that a finder/occupier's possession, though not ownership, is enough to found an action for interference with goods.
why-here: Used to answer "does the plaintiff have possession" where the plaintiff found or held goods without owning them.
