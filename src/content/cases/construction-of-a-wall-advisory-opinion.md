facts: |
  The opinion concerned Israel's construction of a security barrier, or wall, in the Occupied Palestinian
  Territory.
issue: |
  Whether construction of the wall was an internationally wrongful act, whether necessity could preclude its
  wrongfulness, and what consequences followed.
held: |
  The ICJ found construction of the wall to be an internationally unlawful act. It was not necessary to Israel's
  essential interests: there were other means to protect those interests than building the barrier along that
  route. Israel was obliged to stop construction, dismantle portions already built, compensate natural or legal
  persons for damage caused (returning land or compensating), and "offer appropriate assurances and guarantees of
  non-repetition".
ratio: |
  Necessity fails where the conduct is not the only way to safeguard the essential interest; the consequences of
  a wrongful act are cessation, non-repetition and reparation.
significance: |
  Used for necessity (art 25), cessation and non-repetition (art 30) and reparation (art 31).
