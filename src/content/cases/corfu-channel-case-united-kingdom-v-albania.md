aglc: Corfu Channel Case (United Kingdom v Albania) [1949] ICJ
facts: |
  In October 1946, two British cruisers and two destroyers entered the North Corfu Strait and travelled into
  Albanian waters. The passage was regarded as safe: it had been swept in 1944 and check-swept in 1945. Two ships
  struck a mine and were gravely damaged; forty-five British officers and sailors lost their lives and forty-two
  others were wounded. Who laid the mines in Albanian territorial waters remained a matter for conjecture.
issue: |
  Whether Albania was responsible for the damage and loss of life even though it was not shown to have laid the
  mines itself.
held: |
  Evidence of the Albanian Government's attitude, and the fact that the laying of the mines would have been
  visible from the Albanian coast, led the Court to conclude that Albania knew about the minefield. "The
  obligations incumbent upon the Albanian authorities consisted in notifying, for the benefit of shipping in
  general, the existence of a minefield in Albanian territorial waters and in warning the approaching British
  warships of the imminent danger to which the minefield exposed them … every State [has an] obligation not to
  allow knowingly its territory to be used for acts contrary to the rights of other States" (p 22). Albania was
  responsible for failing to warn.
ratio: |
  A state may be responsible for an omission: failing to notify and warn of a known danger in its territory
  breaches its obligations, and a state may not knowingly allow its territory to be used for acts contrary to
  the rights of other states.
significance: |
  Used in the lecture as an example of a breach by omission, and on the slides to show that a state can be in
  breach of its obligations in relation to the conduct of others it did not itself carry out. The lecturer notes
  the "knowingly allow its territory" duty is a question of due diligence not examined further in this unit.
why-here: Shows breach by omission and that a state can answer for harm it did not directly cause by failing to warn.
