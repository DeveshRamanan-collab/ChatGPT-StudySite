facts: A husband and wife sued their neighbours for private nuisance caused by persistent noise. The wife held legal rights in the land; the husband occupied it only as her licensee.
issue: Whether a person without a proprietary interest in land, such as a spouse who is a mere licensee, has title to sue for private nuisance.
held: The wife, having a proprietary interest, could sue; the husband, being merely a licensee, had no title to sue.
ratio: Only a person with a proprietary interest in land — an owner or a tenant in actual possession — has title to sue in private nuisance; a mere licensee does not, even if they live in the property.
significance: States the traditional position on title to sue in private nuisance, later tested in Khorasandjian v Bush and settled by Hunter v Canary Wharf.
