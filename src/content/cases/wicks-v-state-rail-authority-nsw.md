facts: |
  Two police officers attended the scene of the Waterfall train derailment, where seven people were killed
  and many injured; State Rail admitted negligence. The officers spent several hours assisting the injured
  and later suffered psychiatric injury.
issue: |
  Whether the officers had "witnessed, at the scene, the victim being killed, injured or put in peril"
  within the meaning of CLA s 30(2)(a), and whether s 32 or s 30 should be considered first.
held: |
  The High Court allowed the appeal, holding that s 32 (which controls whether a duty of care arises) must
  be considered before the s 30(2) limitation, and that the derailment's consequences unfolded over time —
  the officers witnessed victims still being injured or in peril while they assisted, satisfying s 30(2)(a).
ratio: |
  Section 32 CLA governs whether a duty of care exists and must be addressed first; a person can be
  "witnessing ... a victim being ... put in peril" for the purposes of s 30(2)(a) even though the triggering
  event unfolds over an extended period rather than an instant.
significance: |
  Clarifies the relationship between the CLA's general mental-harm duty provision (s 32) and its specific
  limitation on recovery for pure mental harm (s 30), and gives "put in peril" a broad, ordinary meaning.
why-here: The leading case applying CLA Part 3 to rescuers and multi-victim disasters, and for the order in which ss 32 and 30 must be applied.
