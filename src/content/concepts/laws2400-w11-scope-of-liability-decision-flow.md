Causation under CLA s 5D(1) has two elements: factual causation (s 5D(1)(a)) and scope of liability — "that it
is appropriate for the scope of the negligent person's liability to extend to the harm so caused" (s 5D(1)(b)). The
flowchart is the map of that second element, read top to bottom.

1. **s 5D(1)(b)** — is it appropriate for liability to extend to this harm?
2. **s 5D(4)** — the court is to consider (amongst other relevant things) "whether or not and why responsibility for the
   harm should be imposed on the negligent party".
3. **Policy, said out loud** — this embraces policy-based considerations which the courts are required to articulate
   (*Wallace v Kam*), and raises normative questions: whether the defendant *ought* to be held liable (*Ruddock v
   Taylor*).
4. **Two common law concepts** — scope of liability raises two distinct ideas:
   - *new intervening acts* (*novus actus interveniens*): did a later event break the chain of causation?
   - *remoteness of harm*: was the damage of a reasonably foreseeable kind? (The egg-shell skull rule sits here.)

Either branch can take the harm outside the scope of the defendant's liability, even though factual causation is
satisfied; s 5D(4) keeps the question "should this defendant answer for this harm?" in view throughout.

Exam use: after factual causation, state s 5D(1)(b) and s 5D(4), then run only the branch the facts raise — a later
event points to novus actus; an unexpected kind, extent or manner of harm points to remoteness.
