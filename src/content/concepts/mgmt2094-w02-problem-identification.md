The first step of creative problem solving is to **identify an interesting problem**: find problems, identify
needs. Identifying meaningful problems, asking the right questions is a key characteristic of creative people.

Two quotations on the slide make the point. "If I had one hour to save the world, I would spend 59 minutes defining
the problem and one minute solving it" (attributed to Albert Einstein). "Computers are useless. They can only give
answers" (attributed to Pablo Picasso).

Some related qualities:

- listen to the unusual;
- identify consequences that make the subject essential.

Examples of important questions:

- Why is the speed of light the same everywhere? … led to the discovery of relativity by Einstein.
- How to use colours differently? … one of the essential questions that led to impressionism.
- How to make bus stop shelters viable? … led to the worldwide success of Decaux.

In each example the question comes first and the breakthrough follows. This is also the step that is hardest to
assess: the lecture notes there is no standard test for the ability to formulate interesting problems.

Exam use: in an applied answer, show the brief being turned into a question — a found problem or an identified need —
before any solution is offered, and cite one of the three examples.
