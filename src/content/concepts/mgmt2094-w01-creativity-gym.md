Developing creative skills is like working out at a gym: creativity can be trained, in stages.

1. **Starting off** — learning and developing the skills. This is the base level: if you want to create a business,
   you need to know and understand the industry you are entering and develop the skills required to run a business.
2. **Being proficient** — training your mind and getting inspiration, or knowing how to get it. Everyone, and every
   organisation, is slightly different; what matters is a systematic approach to training our minds and our teams for
   creativity and inspiration.
3. **Becoming an expert** — practising your creative thoughts and training your mind to automatically go into default
   creative mode, and then receiving feedback on those thoughts and actions. At this level creativity becomes part of
   who you are as a professional, so it comes much more easily when it is needed.

Example: at Disney, Imagineering's researchers have a yearly opportunity to formally present their wildest (and, with
any luck, economically viable) innovations to the CEO (Lev-Ram 2015): practised creative work put in front of someone
for feedback.

Exam use: identify the stage from what the person is doing — learning the basics, training the mind and seeking
inspiration, or practising by default and taking feedback.
