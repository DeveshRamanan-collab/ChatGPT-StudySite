There are now also statutory provisions which provide for liability for misrepresentation. Australian
Consumer Law s 18: "A person must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive or
is likely to mislead or deceive."

- Section 18 covers both **conduct and words**, and negligent as well as intentional misstatements.
- There is **no need to establish a duty of care or prove negligence** — what must be proved is the existence of
  misleading or deceptive conduct. A causal link is still needed, but nowhere near the extent required at common
  law.
- The **common law remains relevant** — for
  example where the defendant is not engaging "in trade or commerce" — and a plaintiff can argue both.

*ACCC v Kimberly-Clark Australia* [2019] FCA 992; [2020] FCAFC 107: the ACCC brought action on two counts —
whether wipes were "flushable" and whether they were "made in Australia". The Federal Court found the "made in
Australia" claim false and misleading (the wipes were made elsewhere), but ruled Kimberly-Clark had not made false
and misleading claims about the "flushable" wipes — upheld on appeal by the Full Federal Court. Kimberly-Clark had
faced similar actions in the US and settled out of court.

Exam use: where a misstatement is made in trade or commerce, flag s 18 as the easier route because no duty of
care is needed; where it is not (a friend at a barbecue, say), the claim must be run as negligent misstatement.
