**Divergent thinking** is the second step of creative problem solving, still in the prospective phase: find
solutions. Its enemy is "linear thinking" — the propensity to:

- settle for the first idea;
- logical reasoning, that locks you in the first solution.

The lecture's example is **the quota method**. Always ask at least three ideas from your team (& yourself!):

- different options, alternate solutions;
- or refuse to decide.

The claim behind the quota: when at least three good ideas have been found, many more are generally found. If the
alternatives are not there yet, the slide's answer is to refuse to decide rather than settle.

Divergent thinking is hard to measure: divergent thinking tests produce mixed results.

Idea-gathering techniques build the same rule into a meeting. Brainstorming (Alex Osborne, BBDO) generates as many
ideas as possible in a short time without exercising censorship on the participants, and only afterwards analyses and
prioritises them — the hand-over to convergent thinking.

Exam use: when a scenario shows a team adopting its first idea, name the linear-thinking trap and prescribe the quota
(at least three options, or refuse to decide) before any evaluation.
