Article 1 of the Montevideo Convention on Rights and Duties of States (1933): the state "should possess ...
a. a permanent population; b. a defined territory; c. government; and d. capacity to enter into relations with
other states". A regional convention with few parties, it is widely regarded as codifying custom.

Population: no minimum (Nauru about 12,000, China about 1.4 billion); a core population living there
permanently, whatever its nationality. Territory: no minimum size (Vatican about 0.5 sq km); boundaries must be
defined but need not be fixed or undisputed — "no rule that the land frontiers of a State must be fully
delimited" (*North Sea Continental Shelf* [46]). Effective government: the most tricky — a coherent, effective
exercise of public power; a colony would not qualify. Once statehood is established, a presumption of continuity
means ineffective government (Somalia, Syria, Yemen) does not end it, and the form of government is irrelevant.
Capacity for relations: arises from independence — Scotland may meet the first three but cannot conduct
international relations on its own.

Exam use: apply each criterion to the facts in turn, then note that new entities face a stricter effectiveness
test than established states.
