BCG studied more than 100 of the world's most innovative companies and found six distinct innovation models
(Manly, Grassl and Wilson 2017):

- **Creator** ("We know best") — led by a visionary, disrupts markets and makes focused big bets: Apple.
- **Solution builder** ("We're listening") — wins by understanding and delighting customers: Nike.
- **Leverager** ("We do it best") — innovates its core business model to drive ongoing advantage: Zara.
- **Expander** ("Seeking share to steal") — uses a core capability to enter and dominate adjacent markets: Amazon.
- **Defender** ("Protecting our turf") — focuses on points of advantage in mature or slow-changing sectors: Allstate.
- **Fast follower** ("Ready to react") — optimises its capabilities to react rapidly to competitive innovations:
  Reckitt Benckiser.

Innovators fall short either by pursuing the wrong model for their business and competitive context, or by not
supporting a good model with the capabilities it requires. Choosing is all about context. Industry: only a subset of
models succeeds in most industries — Sears relied on the defender model and lost its edge when agile online players
upended retail. Company: the model must align with strategy, strengths and capabilities — Amazon (expander) and Costco
(leverager) both succeed in retail. Look for a model rivals are not using, or are using poorly, and back it with a
blueprint: structure and culture, tools and processes, metrics and incentives.

Exam use: name the model from its characteristics, then judge its fit with the industry and the company.
