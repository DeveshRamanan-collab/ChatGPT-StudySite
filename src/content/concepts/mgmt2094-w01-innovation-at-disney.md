The week's reading, Michal Lev-Ram's 'Empire of Tech' (Fortune, 1 January 2015), profiles Bob Iger, Disney's CEO
since 2005, who has spent his near decade wearing an additional corporate hat: **CTO**. Its lesson: not only do
today's media companies need to start thinking like technology companies, their CEOs also need to start thinking like
CTOs.

What Iger did:

- **Big and early bets**, even ones seemingly at odds with the business model: ABC shows on iTunes (2005, the first
  deal of its kind); free full-length TV shows online (2006). "If you're going to win big, you have to get in early."
- **Three pillars**: investing in creative content, international expansion and technological innovation.
- **Decision-making moved to the business units**, with a CTO for each business instead of one corporate CTO, and a
  CTO council that runs hackathons and a "Best of Disney" symposium.
- **Acquisitions**: Pixar ($7.4 billion), Marvel ($4.3 billion), Lucasfilm ($4 billion); the brands are encouraged to
  "beg, borrow, and steal" technologies from each other.

Not every bet paid: the interactive division spent five years in the red, losing more than $1 billion, before its
first profit. Iger: "We lost a fair amount of money in the process, but we learned from that."

John Lasseter's line sums up the balance: "Art challenges technology, and technology inspires art."

Exam use: Disney is a worked example of a company that does both content and technology.
