The textbook ([12.170]) explains what happens where several tortfeasors are liable for the same damage.

Where multiple tortfeasors are **jointly and severally liable** for damage, the plaintiff may choose to recover the whole amount against only one of them — for example, the one with the "deeper pockets". A footnote qualifies this where the proportionate liability provisions apply, in respect of claims for property damage and economic loss; those provisions are not in these pages.

**Contribution** is the statutory mechanism by which a tortfeasor held liable for the full amount of damage may seek a contribution against another tortfeasor. For NSW the textbook cites the Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1946 (NSW) s 5(1)(c) and s 5(2).

Two consequences follow:

- Contribution in no way affects the plaintiff; it is a matter between the tortfeasors themselves.
- So where there are joint or separate, concurrent tortfeasors, the risk that one of them is insolvent or not able to pay the full amount of damages lies with the other tortfeasors, not the plaintiff.

Exam use: where two wrongdoers are liable for the same damage, say the plaintiff may recover the whole amount from either (checking whether proportionate liability applies to a property damage or economic loss claim), and that contribution and any insolvency are for the tortfeasors to sort out between themselves.
