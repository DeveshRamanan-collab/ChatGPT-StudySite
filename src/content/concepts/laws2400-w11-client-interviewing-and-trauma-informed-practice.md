Not doctrine: the Topic 11 tutorial preparation slides (a LAWS1100 skills deck) on interviewing a client.

Why it matters in a torts unit: in *Roberts v Cashman* [2000] NSWSC 770, a firm relied on a questionnaire and never
interviewed a class-action client; her original injury (a broken banana chair) was never explored and her claim against
the manufacturer became time-barred. The duty of care was breached. A solicitor who does not interview the client in
such a situation "does so at his own peril".

- **Initial interview duties**: explain confidentiality, establish the limits of the retainer, disclose costs.
- **Client-focused interviewing**: the lawyer controls the process, not the content; clients make their own decisions
  (ASCR rr 7 and 8); adjust the scaffolding to the client.
- **Active listening — LARS**: Listening, Acknowledging (door openers, minimal encouragers), Reflecting (and
  reframing), Summarising (facts and concerns). Hear the client before solving.
- **Questioning — the T-funnel**: broad open → general open → detailed → nitty-gritty, with clarification throughout.
  Don't start too soon; don't do too much; no leading, compound or double-negative questions.
- **Trauma-informed practice**: avoid re-traumatisation rather than trying to solve trauma; ask "What happened to you?"
  rather than "What is wrong with you?"; read fight, flight, freeze and fawn responses, and shame, as coping; work with
  safety, trustworthiness, choice, collaboration and empowerment.
