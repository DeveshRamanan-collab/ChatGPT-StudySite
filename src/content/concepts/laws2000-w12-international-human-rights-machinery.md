**Charter-based.** The Human Rights Council replaced the UN Commission on Human Rights in 2006 (UNGA resolution
60/251). It has 47 member states elected by the General Assembly for three-year terms (maximum two consecutive), with
regional distribution, and works through regular and special sessions, the Universal Periodic Review and the special
procedures.

- **Universal Periodic Review:** every member state is reviewed every 4.5 years. States (and NGOs) submit reports; a
  47-member working group reviews them and issues an outcome report with recommendations, which the state accepts or
  notes. Australia (January 2021) received 344 recommendations and supported 177.
- **Special procedures:** independent experts — special rapporteurs, independent experts, working groups — with 46
  thematic and 14 country mandates. They visit countries (with state consent), receive individual complaints and
  issue reports.

**Treaty-based.** Each core treaty has a committee, e.g. the Human Rights Committee for the ICCPR. Committees consider
state reports (then concluding observations), decide individual complaints (written, confidential, modelled on judicial
practice, ending in "views"), may hear inter-state complaints (extremely rare: three ever, all under ICERD) and issue
general comments, considered authoritative.

**Legal nature.** Treaty body decisions are not binding but carry significant weight. In *Diallo* (ICJ, 2010, para 66)
the Court was "in no way obliged" to follow the Committee's interpretation of the ICCPR but ascribed "great weight" to
it.
