The lecture lists nine core international human rights instruments. In date order:

1. International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (ICERD) — 21 Dec 1965
2. International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) — 16 Dec 1966
3. International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) — 16 Dec 1966
4. Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW) — 18 Dec 1979
5. Convention against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment (CAT) — 10 Dec 1984
6. Convention on the Rights of the Child — 20 Nov 1989
7. International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families —
   18 Dec 1990
8. Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities — 13 Dec 2006
9. International Convention for the Protection of All Persons from Enforced Disappearance — 20 Dec 2006

Human rights obligations come from two sources. Treaty law: the core instruments, and regional treaties such as the
European Convention on Human Rights and the American Convention on Human Rights. Customary international law: e.g.
the prohibitions of torture, genocide and racial discrimination. Some of these are considered jus cogens — norms
"from which no derogation is permitted" (VCLT art 53).

Exam use: where a state is not party to the relevant treaty, ask whether the norm is also customary (or jus cogens)
before concluding it is not bound.
