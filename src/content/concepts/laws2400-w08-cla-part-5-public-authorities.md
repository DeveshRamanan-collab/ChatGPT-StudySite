The Ipp Committee aimed to confine public authorities' liability to acts so unreasonable that no reasonable
authority would take them (Wednesbury unreasonableness). Part 5 applies to civil liability in tort:

- **s 41**: a "public or other authority" includes the Crown, government departments, local councils, statutory
  authorities, and any person with public official functions (psychiatrists under the Mental Health Act 2007, people
  putting up road warning signs, prison guards).
- **s 42**: functions are limited by the resources reasonably available; their general allocation is not open to
  challenge; functions are judged by the broad range of activities.
- **s 43**: no breach of statutory duty unless the act or omission was so unreasonable that no authority with those
  functions could properly consider it reasonable.
- **s 43A**: the same test for special statutory powers — powers persons generally cannot exercise without specific
  statutory authority (lecture examples: back-burning, firearm licences).
- **s 44**: no liability for failing to exercise a function to prohibit or regulate an activity (licensing included)
  unless the plaintiff could have required it in proceedings.
- **s 46**: exercising, or deciding to exercise, a function does not of itself create a duty to exercise it.

In *RTA v Refrigerated Roadways* (2009) the RTA owed a duty about concrete dropped from an overpass; s 43A did not
help it, because anybody can erect a screen — but there was no breach.

Exam use: start with the common law duty, then ask which Part 5 sections modify it.
