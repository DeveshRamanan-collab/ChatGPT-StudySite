A reflective paper is somewhat personal in that it includes your subjective feelings and opinions. So reveal
information wisely:

- Instead of revealing everything about yourself, carefully ask yourself if something is appropriate before including
  it in your writing.
- If you feel uncomfortable about a reflection, don't include it unless it did change the final outcome of your
  experience. If you do include it, indicate in your writing the concerns you have about such reveals.
- Always maintain a professional and academic tone. Yes, it is personal and subjective, but keep your thoughts in an
  organised fashion so they appear sensible.
- Avoid dragging someone else down in your writing. Whilst that person may well have made life difficult for you, keep
  a level of detachment as you describe their influence.

In the recording the lecturer says it is fine not to share a personal feeling you are uncomfortable with; suggests
jotting down notes first, perhaps as free writing, and then organising them into a structured piece; and warns against
badmouthing or accusing teammates.

Exam use: for any personal detail, ask in order — is it appropriate, and did it change the outcome? — then check the
tone, and the detachment with which other people are described.
