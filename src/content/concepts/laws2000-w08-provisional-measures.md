Art 41 ICJ Statute: the Court "shall have the power to indicate, if it considers that circumstances so require, any
provisional measures which ought to be taken to preserve the respective rights of either party." They protect
disputing states from actions that could render the final decision meaningless.

Conditions (the Court need not finally decide jurisdiction or the merits):

1. **Prima facie jurisdiction** — the absence of jurisdiction is not manifest.
2. **Plausible rights** — the rights for which protection is sought are at least plausible.
3. **Link** — between those rights and the measures requested; the measures must be appropriate and necessary to
   protect the alleged rights.
4. **Urgency** — a real and imminent risk that irreparable harm will occur before the final decision is given.

**Binding?** Yes. In *LaGrand (Germany v United States)* (2001, para 102) the Court reasoned from the object and
purpose of the Statute — to enable judicial settlement of disputes by binding decisions (art 59) — that art 41
exists so the Court is not hampered because the parties' rights are not preserved. The power to indicate provisional
measures "entails that such measures should be binding"; the contrary contention "would be contrary to the object
and purpose of that Article".

Exam use: run the four conditions against the facts, propose measures tied to the rights claimed, and state that
they bind (*LaGrand*).
