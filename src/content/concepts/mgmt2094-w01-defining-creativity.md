**Creativity is seeing what everyone else has seen, and thinking what no-one else has thought** (Einstein, as
quoted on the slides). We have an abundance of, and easy access to, information, so we can all see a lot; the key is
to think, or perceive, what no-one else has thought.

**Creative thinking** is seen simply as the ability to think, imagine or invent something new. The lecture keeps it
broad: imagining while reading a book, or taking a different route to campus, is already creative thinking, however
small. We all have this ability.

Can it be learnt? "Aren't we just that naturally or not? Some of us may well be, others aren't." Sir Ken Robinson
(2011): "When people say to me that they aren't creative, I assume they haven't learnt what is involved." The
lecturer's hunch is that people who are not naturally creative can improve, and that naturally creative people still
gain from study: structure lets them turn creativity into something practical and useful, such as a business.

Creativity isn't just about the arts. In business we admire the likes of Jeff Bezos and Elon Musk, and look at
creative ways of doing business, such as **dropshipping**: you don't physically hold the stock; the manufacturer or
wholesaler supplies your customer directly.

Exam use: quote Einstein's definition, then show it in a business example.
