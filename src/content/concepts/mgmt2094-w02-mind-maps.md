A **mind map** is a way to visually organise notes, thoughts, ideas and conversations, created around a single
word or text in the centre to which associated ideas, words and concepts are added. Tony Buzan, a British
psychologist, takes the credit for inventing modern mind mapping; different forms have been used over many centuries,
and Buzan popularised it.

Why it works: we think in **radial associations**. Asked how many windows are in your home, you picture the home and
navigate it, adding windows until you return to the start — a map, not a list.

Buzan's guidelines:

1. Start in the centre of a blank landscape page.
2. Use an image for the central idea.
3. Use colours throughout — three at minimum.
4. Connect main branches to the centre, and each level to the one before (the brain works by associations).
5. Curved branches, not straight.
6. One key word per line, upper and lower case.
7. Images throughout; each word or image alone on its line.
8. Lines thicker at the centre, thinner as they radiate out.
9. Lines the same length as their word or image.
10. Develop your own personal style.

Uses: note taking, documenting conversations, brainstorming, tweezing out a strategy; in business, mostly
collaborative. For marketing and sales: synthesis of information and collaborative brainstorming.

Exam use: critique a map against the guidelines — too many branches? images over words? a centre image taking too
much attention?
