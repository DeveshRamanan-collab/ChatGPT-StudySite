Tort law sits inside a wider "ecosystem" of law that regulates human behaviour and protects rights and
interests — it is not a watertight compartment. Against **criminal law**: tort deals with private wrongs,
criminal law with public wrongs (offences); tort's main purpose is compensation, criminal law's is
punishment; tort aims at redress/correction, criminal law at retribution/deterrence; tort actions are
initiated by the individual, criminal prosecutions by the State; and the standard of proof is the balance of
probabilities in tort versus beyond reasonable doubt in crime. The line is not always sharp — the same
conduct (e.g. assault) can be both a tort and a crime, and compensation can sometimes be obtained for
injuries suffered from crimes.

Against **contract law**: both deal with civil wrongs, and the same facts can found actions in both (e.g.
product liability, contracts for services). But duties in tort arise from the general law (the "neighbour
principle") and bind everyone, while contractual duties arise from the parties' agreement and law and bind
only the parties (privity). Motive can matter in some torts but not in contract, and tort damages are
unliquidated while contract damages are liquidated.

Tort law also overlaps with **human rights law**, protecting bodily integrity, reputation and property, but
Australia has no comprehensive federal human rights statute (only the ACT, Victoria and Queensland have
one), so tort's role in protecting human rights remains limited.
