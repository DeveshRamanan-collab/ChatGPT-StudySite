Popular views of innovation come mainly from Hollywood and Disney films such as *Back to the Future*. The lecture
then sets out four more concrete views of where innovation comes from:

- **Social deterministic school** — innovations result from a combination of external social factors and influences,
  such as demographic and cultural change. When conditions are right, innovation will occur: Covid and the increase of
  online offerings.
- **Individualistic school** — innovations result from unique individual talents, so innovators are born. This view
  is linked to serendipity.
- **Resource-based innovation** — the firm's own resources provide the context for innovative activity: Starbucks has
  innovated the style and type of coffee-based drinks it offers.
- **Market-based innovation** — market conditions provide the context that facilitates or constrains innovation, so
  the firm must recognise the opportunities that arise in the marketplace: the Share a Coke campaign made each person
  feel special and unique.

The first two ask whether society or the gifted individual drives innovation; the last two ask whether the firm's own
resources or the market's conditions give it its context. Mark's furniture business shows the market-based view in a
small firm: he describes marketing as seeing a hole in the market and filling it with what people want, at a price
they will pay.
