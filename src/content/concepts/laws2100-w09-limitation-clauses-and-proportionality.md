Are there any absolute human rights? Limitation clauses are constitutional provisions that permit the partial limitation of some constitutional rights.

- Canadian Charter s 1: rights are guaranteed "subject only to such reasonable limits prescribed by law as can be demonstrably justified in a free and democratic society".
- Victorian Charter s 7(2): the same test, "based on human dignity, equality and freedom", weighing the nature of the right, the importance of the limitation's purpose, its nature and extent, its relationship to the purpose, and any less restrictive means.
- ECHR art 15: derogation in war or public emergency, but none from art 2 (life, save lawful acts of war), 3 (torture), 4(1) (slavery) or 7 (no punishment without law).

Robert Alexy distinguishes rules (definitive commands) from principles (optimisation requirements), realised to the greatest extent possible given legal and factual constraints. Because rights are principles, proportionality is necessarily connected to adjudicating constitutional rights. The test:

1. **Suitability**: the measure must be capable of achieving the legitimate aim.
2. **Necessity**: there must be no less restrictive means to the same goal.
3. **Proportionality in the narrow sense (balancing)**: the benefits must outweigh the detriment to the right infringed.

His "weight formula" formalises balancing: the intensity of interference, the importance of the competing principle, and the certainty about these factors.

Exam use: run a limitation through all three stages, in order.
