Idea gathering is one of the four families of creativity techniques. The lecture gives it two strands.

**Brainstorming, brainwriting methods** (Alex Osborne, president and co-founder of BBDO): in order to answer a
question, generate in a meeting as many ideas as possible in a short time without exercising censorship on the
participants. Analyse and prioritise ideas afterwards. For marketing and sales, its usage is product ideas, solutions
to a problem, names and slogans.

**Participatory innovation — idea boxes, idea intranets** — has a long history:

- **1721** — the best ideas deposited in an "idea box" (*meyasu bako*) are rewarded (Shôgun Tokugawa Yoshimune);
- **Seventies** — Japanese companies develop quality circles and Kaizen;
- **2000s** — idea management software; participatory innovation as open procedures for the collection, analysis
  and application of ideas;
- **2010s** — companies seek new ideas outside with idea crowdsourcing: Crossing, YourEncore, CrowdSpirit,
  InnoCentive.

The direction of travel is outward: from a box inside one organisation, to software across it, to crowds beyond it.

Exam use: distinguish the in-meeting technique (brainstorming: quantity first, no censorship, sort later) from
standing systems that collect ideas over time (idea boxes, intranets, crowdsourcing). Both are idea gathering, and
both still need analysis and prioritising — collection alone is not participatory innovation.
