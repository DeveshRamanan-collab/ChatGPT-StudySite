Art 25: members "agree to accept and carry out the decisions of the Security Council in accordance with the
present Charter". The lecture stressed that this binding force attaches to decisions, not to everything the Council
says. A single resolution may mix recommendations, which do not bind, and decisions, which do — and many resolutions
contain both. Which is which usually turns on the words the Council uses in each paragraph, chiefly its verbs, and
borderline wording is debated.

The Charter draws the same line. Under Chapter VI the Council recommends "appropriate procedures or methods of adjustment"
(art 36(1)). Under art 39, once it has determined a threat to the peace, breach of the peace
or act of aggression, it "shall make recommendations, or decide" what measures are taken under arts 41 and 42 — a
recommendation (not binding) or a decision (binding).

A binding decision is a Charter obligation, so art 103 gives it priority: "In the event of a conflict between the
obligations of the Members of the United Nations under the present Charter and their obligations under any other
international agreement, their obligations under the present Charter shall prevail". A member cannot rely on a
conflicting treaty to avoid a binding sanctions decision.

The General Assembly, by contrast, recommends (arts 11(2), 14).

Exam use: before saying a state must comply, find the operative paragraph and ask whether it is a decision (binding
under art 25, prevailing under art 103) or only a recommendation.
