Industrial innovation is not only major (radical) innovation; it also includes minor (incremental) technological
advances. Incremental improvements to an existing product happen far more often than breakthroughs: a Braille Rubik's
cube adapts a familiar puzzle so that people with vision impairment can use it. And "technological" is not only about
technology — a firm can innovate how it does business as well as what it sells.

The lecture names seven types, each with its slide example:

- **Product** — a new or improved product (the Braille Rubik's cube).
- **Process** — a new process, such as a manufacturing process or online recruiting in HR; mostly internal, so less
  visible.
- **Organisational** — a new venture division; a new accounting procedure.
- **Management** — Total Quality Management (TQM).
- **Production** — quality circles; just in time (JIT).
- **Commercial/marketing** — guerrilla marketing campaigns.
- **Service** — internet-based financial services, which change how the customer is served.

Exam use: name the type precisely (online recruiting is process, not service; JIT is production, not management), say
whether the change is radical or incremental, and support it with the slide example.
