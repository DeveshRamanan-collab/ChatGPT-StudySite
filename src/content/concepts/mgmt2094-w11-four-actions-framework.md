Yellow Tail is the lecture's example of innovation: a budget wine taking on the crowded US wine market. Before it
entered, premium and budget wines competed on the same factors — price, enological terminology, marketing, ageing,
vineyard prestige, wine complexity and range — at different levels. Plotting those levels gives the industry's
strategy canvas.

Kim and Mauborgne: to fundamentally shift the strategy canvas of an industry, reorient your strategic focus from
competitors to alternatives, and from customers to non-customers. The four actions framework asks:

1. **Eliminate** — which factors that the industry takes for granted should be eliminated? (enological terminology,
   ageing qualities, above-the-line marketing)
2. **Reduce** — which factors should be reduced well below the industry's standards? (wine complexity, wine range,
   vineyard prestige)
3. **Raise** — which factors should be raised well above the industry's standard? (price versus budget wine, retail
   store involvement)
4. **Create** — which factors should be created that the industry has never offered? (easy drinking, ease of
   selection, fun and adventure)

Yellow Tail's resulting curve sits apart from both groups. The lessons: understanding consumers' needs is key to
innovation; challenge existing business models; focus is more important than ticking all the boxes; and look across
industries and across time.

Exam use: apply the four questions to the scenario's industry, factor by factor, and describe the new curve.
