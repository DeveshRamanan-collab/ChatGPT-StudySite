**ArmorAll**, one of Dow Corning's major customers, approached it with the idea of taking on **Thompson's WaterSeal** with a
**silicone modified organic (SMO)** product. As permutations with the siloxane backbone were limited, SMO was Dow
Corning's strategy for growth; this would have been its first commercialised SMO product. ArmorAll wanted it
developed and commercialised within **18 months**, and the project became the **pilot for DfSS**.

- The chemist worked on a product to meet or exceed the competitor's performance criteria, namely waterproofing, and
  came up with an SMO emulsion.
- To find the mix of silane, wax and surfactant that met price *and* performance, a **six-factor, two-level design of
  experiment** was run; samples were tested for waterproofing durability and the results compiled with economic and
  profitability data.
- **Process engineering commenced concurrently**: a new process was designed on ArmorAll's volumes.
- At one key meeting ArmorAll chose the formulation and price; it was extremely impressed with the detail.

**Results**: in 12 months over 4,500 tonnes produced and US$40 million of revenue; a Technical Achievement Award and a
Manufacturing Achievement Award; the project became the basis for all new commercialisation projects.

The guest lecturer's explanation: the team spent far more time up front on experiments, financial analysis and
process engineering, so the customer only had to choose.

Exam use: DFSS front-loads customer requirements, statistics and economics, set against a usual four-year cycle.
