The present interpretive regime is arts 31–32 VCLT, recognised by the ICJ as reflecting customary
international law (so it binds non-parties too). Article 31(1): a treaty is interpreted "in good faith in
accordance with the ordinary meaning to be given to the terms of the treaty in their context and in the
light of its object and purpose" — context includes the text, preamble, annexes, and any related agreement
drawn up in connection with its conclusion. Article 32 supplementary means — preparatory work (*travaux
préparatoires*) and the circumstances of conclusion — are reached only "when the interpretation according to
article 31 (a) leaves the meaning ambiguous or obscure; or (b) leads to a result which is manifestly absurd
or unreasonable."

This is sequential, not a menu: courts first seek the text's objective meaning under art 31, using art 32
only to confirm it if the text is unclear or absurd — never to rewrite the treaty. *South China Sea
Arbitration* applied both means to art 121 UNCLOS's "regime of islands" and found the Spratly Islands and
other features were not "islands" — critical, since a rock gets only 12 nautical miles against an island's
full 200.

Exam use: always start with art 31 (ordinary meaning, in context, in light of object and purpose); only cite
art 32 preparatory work once you have shown art 31 leaves real ambiguity, absurdity or unreasonableness —
citing travaux préparatoires without first exhausting art 31 is a common, marked-down shortcut.
