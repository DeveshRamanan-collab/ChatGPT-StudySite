Under the pressure of the silicone breast implant litigation (lawsuits from 1984, a multibillion-dollar class action
settlement in 1998, nine years of bankruptcy protection to June 2004), Dow Corning re-examined how it worked.

- **Focus on strengths.** In 1994 it started building its own Legal Entity Ordering Payments software (LEOP). After
  12 months and significant money spent unsuccessfully, it stopped developing software and focused on its strength:
  silicone products. In 1995 it announced it would implement SAP, and announced its program of transformation.
- **Transformation.** The goal was a 'world class', customer-focused, process-oriented organisation: a four-year
  journey (1997–2000) built in layers from hardware and enabling software, through workflow re-engineering, to culture
  and a customer-driven company. Three areas needed re-engineering; **commercialisation of new products** was one.
- **Change management.** In 1997 a consulting company redesigned the commercialisation process. Implementation
  failed: **poor change management practices led to poor take-up**. In 1999 leadership abandoned it to focus on
  **Lean Six Sigma**, and in 2001, as part of that program, decided to implement **Design for Six Sigma**.

Exam use: a well-designed process is worth nothing if people do not take it up. Changing how a firm innovates is
itself a change that needs leaders to drive it, as the later Six Sigma program had.
