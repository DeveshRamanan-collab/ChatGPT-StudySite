Why can't we create a standardised test measuring individual creativity? The lecture gives three reasons:

- **creativity is a process** — the three steps, not a single ability;
- **creativity is often a group phenomenon** — so an individual score misses it;
- **creativity implies domain knowledge**.

It then hangs a test under each step of the process:

- identify an interesting problem: **no standard test** for the ability to formulate interesting problems;
- divergent thinking: **divergent thinking tests produce mixed results**;
- convergent thinking: **IQ tests** and **emotional intelligence**. IQ tests measure deductive capacity: deduce the
  consequences.

The pattern matters. The step the lecture calls a key characteristic of creative people — asking the right questions
— is the one with no test at all, while IQ tests reach only convergent thinking (identify benefits and risks, keep
the best and eliminate wrong answers, evaluation and selection).

The lecture's own "tests of creativity" are exercises rather than measures. An ambiguous drawing (a duck or a rabbit):
how easy was it to see both? Did it require real mental strain? The ease to flip between the two is related to
creativity (Wiseman et al. 2011). Then: think about a door — how many uses can you write down in one minute?

Exam use: if asked how an organisation could assess its people's creativity, argue from the three reasons and the
step-by-step gaps, rather than proposing a single score.
