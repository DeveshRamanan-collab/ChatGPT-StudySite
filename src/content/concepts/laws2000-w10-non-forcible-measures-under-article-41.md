Art 41: the Council "may decide what measures not involving the use of armed force" are to be employed,
including "complete or partial interruption of economic relations and of rail, sea, air, postal, telegraphic, radio,
and other means of communication, and the severance of diplomatic relations". The word "include" makes the list
non-exhaustive: it gives the Council a wide scope of non-military coercive measures, economic and diplomatic.

Comprehensive sanctions are financial, trade and other economic measures against states — e.g. Iraq during the 1990s,
and North Korea until today because of nuclear proliferation (S/RES/2397 (2017)). They are controversial because of
their impact on populations (Iraq, 1990s). In response, the trend is towards targeted or "smart" sanctions: specific
commodities (e.g. conflict diamonds), or particular groups, corporations or individuals (travel bans, asset freezes).
Sanctions committees often set the details.

Art 41 measures can also include ad hoc international criminal tribunals to try people responsible for serious crimes
under IHL — the ICTY and ICTR. Whether a political organ could create a tribunal, which art 41 never mentions, was
debated at first; members accepted that it could, and that is no longer questioned. The ICC is not part of the UN,
but the Council, acting under Chapter VII, can refer a situation to the ICC Prosecutor (Rome Statute art 13(b)) —
e.g. Darfur, 2005.

Exam use: match the measure to the target — a whole state, a commodity, named individuals — and say why targeted
measures may be preferred.
