Because law is argumentative, *Law's Empire* builds a theory of law on interpretation. We interpret texts and
also practices. Dworkin's example is courtesy:

1. **Pre-interpretive stage**: identify the actions we associate with courtesy (letting people go first, removing a
   hat).
2. **Interpretive stage**: ask what the point of the practice is.
3. **Post-interpretive stage**: revisit the examples in light of that point and decide which really count.

The crucial question in law is: what is the point of the law? For Dworkin, a liberal, the point of the legal system is
to justify the exercise of state coercion. Hart's conditions for a legal system are about structure and say nothing
about its point.

**Law as integrity**: integrity → consistency in principle. The principles of the system's political morality, not its
structure, give it unity.

- The chain novel (LE 228): Dworkin's analogy for interpretation in the legal context.
- Judge Hercules, "an imaginary judge of superhuman intellectual power and patience who accepts law as integrity"
  (LE 239).
- Dimension of fit + dimension of justification (LE 255).

The **one-right-answer thesis**: even in hard cases there is one correct legal answer. Positivists and realists reject
it; Finnis, 'On reason and authority in Law's Empire' (1987), pushes back on the incommensurability of fit and
justification.

Exam use: ask whether integrity is general jurisprudence or particular — does it fit an evil system such as apartheid
South Africa?
