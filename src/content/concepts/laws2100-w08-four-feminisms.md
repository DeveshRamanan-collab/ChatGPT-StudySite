Feminists share interests and political commitments but not the same theoretical approach. Four categories, each responding to the one before:

1. **Liberal feminism**: the goal is equal treatment. Remove all formal barriers to women's equal participation in social, economic and political life; assess individuals on their merits, not group membership. Most legal gains of the 60s and 70s used it (eg the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth)). Problem: formal equality did not become substantive equality, and it implicitly assumes male experiences as the norm/benchmark.

2. **Cultural/difference feminism** embraces the differences between men and women, reacting to a formal equality that forces women to assimilate to patriarchal structures. Carol Gilligan: ethics of care vs ethics of justice. Law should recognise women's perspective and values. Contra: it can reinforce the stereotypes behind women's subordination.

3. **Radical feminism** (Catharine MacKinnon): women do not so much speak with a different voice; they have no voice at all. Women as a class are dominated by men as a class; sex oppression is the most fundamental form of domination. Pornography is a political issue, not a private preference. Contra: does it see women only as victims?

4. **Postmodern feminism**: distrust of grand narratives; deconstruction of binaries (male/female, reason/emotion, nature/culture). Essentialism is the flaw of assuming women share an essence and common interests; intersectionality: race, culture, religion and class intersect. Contra (Naffine): when do we know enough, and who will speak for women?

Exam use: name the approach before criticising 'feminism'.
