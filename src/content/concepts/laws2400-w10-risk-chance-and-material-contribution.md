Factual causation needs proof on the balance of probabilities; a possibility or an increased risk is not enough.

**Loss of a chance.** In *Tabet v Gett* (2010) 240 CLR 537 a child suffered brain damage after a delayed CT scan. Her chance of a better outcome was under 50% — possible, not probable. Gummow ACJ: in personal injury cases the common law of Australia does not entertain an action where the damage is characterised as the loss of a chance of a better outcome — without accepting, in absolute terms, that a lost chance can never be the damage.

**Dust disease cases**, where science cannot say which exposure caused the disease:
- *Increased risk* — probabilistic, looks forward, says nothing about what happened to this claimant.
- *Material contribution to harm* — causal, looks back, must be more than negligible.

*Bonnington Castings v Wardlaw*: a negligent contribution to cumulative silica dust that was more than de minimis was enough; it need not be the sole or main cause. *Amaca v Ellis* (2010): showing asbestos exposure increased the risk of lung cancer (he also smoked) was not sufficient. *Amaca v Booth* (2011): mesothelioma (asbestos effectively its only known cause), long heavy exposure, trivial other exposures and cumulative-mechanism evidence — contribution inferred. *Fairchild* (UK) treated materially increased risk as enough; Australian common law has not adopted it.

*Amaca v Booth* never mentions the CLA: s 3B(1) excludes dust disease proceedings from it.

Exam use: contribution, or only risk or chance?
