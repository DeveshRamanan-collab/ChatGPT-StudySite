It is one of the few academic papers where you can use "I" in your writing. But it is NOT a personal journal
(diary) entry:

- Relate your subjective feelings and opinions using specific evidence to explain them.
- Avoid slang, jargon etc, and always proofread.
- Keep your sentences focused; don't ramble. Avoid squeezing multiple ideas into one sentence.
- Avoid sentence fragments.
- Vary your sentence length, but not too long; it makes the writing more conversational.
- Use paragraphs.

**Transitional phrases** shift the argument and introduce specific details. They let you illustrate how one experience
or detail directly links to a conclusion or understanding. Common ones: *for example*, *for instance*, *as a result*,
*an opposite view is*, *a different perspective is*.

You must relate all this to your group project and experiences, with theoretical underpinnings.

The recording adds that feelings should not come out of the blue: think critically about the experience and give
specific evidence. The paper is structured in paragraphs, not a stream of consciousness, and "I" is acceptable in other
academic writing too.

Exam use: to fix a weak reflective sentence, name the rule it breaks (fragment, ramble, slang, unsupported feeling) and
the transitional phrase that would link it to a conclusion.
