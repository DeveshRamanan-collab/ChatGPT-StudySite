**Creativity is thinking up new things. Innovation is doing new things** (Theodore Levitt). The unit is built
on this split: in the first half of semester (Weeks 1–7) you will be thinking up new things; in the second half
(Weeks 8–13) you will be doing new things.

The two are different but support each other; the lecture's hint is "one for the other". Creative thinking is the
starting point, and the question is whether we can leverage it to make innovation: something practical and useful that
brings value, such as a business. The slides add that innovation isn't just about new things; Levitt's line puts the
weight on doing.

The slides show the semester's path as **vision → imagination → creativity → innovation**.

Examples from the materials:

- Disney Imagineering's Dish, a virtual reality chamber for testing future rides, is "blue sky" experimentation: the
  technology is still too expensive and cumbersome for the parks (Lev-Ram 2015). Thought up, not yet done.
- MagicBands, RFID wristbands rolled out at Disney World (more than 9 million given out), are a new thing actually done.
- Dropshipping is a simple idea someone came up with and was able to implement, and it brings value.

Exam use: test any example with two questions — was something new thought up, and was it actually done?
