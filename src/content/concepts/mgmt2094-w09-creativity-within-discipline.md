Six Sigma is about discipline — disciplined people, processes and actions — but the guest lecturer's point was that
**you can still be disciplined and still be creative**.

- **Space to play.** At the small Australian R&D unit (Blacktown), Friday afternoons were free for any experiment,
  amid laughter and noise. The new central R&D building in Midland was sterile — lab coats, no noise — and the
  creativity was missing. The host lecturer added that most free-time ideas go to waste, but the few that work may be
  a goldmine, and the time builds morale and sharing. Atlassian was given as a later example of a day a week on
  one's own projects.
- **Diversity.** Bring people who think differently into headquarters: with creativity you really need diversity.
- **Reuse.** Dr Kortian's patented emulsion process came from applying an existing technique (emulsion
  polymerisation) in a new way. DFSS uses **TRIZ**, the theory of inventive problem solving: Altshuller's study of
  patents found only a small share were truly novel, so many contradictions (eg strength versus weight) have been
  solved before.
- **Balance.** The host lecturer: creativity alone gives chaos; structure alone gives nothing new.

Exam use: argue that a structured innovation process needs protected space, diversity and reuse of known solutions to
stay creative.
