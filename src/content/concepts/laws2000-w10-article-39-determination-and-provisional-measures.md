Chapter VII lays down the Council's powers with respect to threats to the peace, breaches of the peace and acts
of aggression. It opens with art 39: "The Security Council shall determine the existence of any threat to the peace,
breach of the peace, or act of aggression and shall make recommendations, or decide what measures shall be taken in
accordance with Articles 41 and 42, to maintain or restore international peace and security." This determination
separates Chapter VII from Chapter VI: without it, arts 41 and 42 are closed. Its interpretation has evolved in
practice (proliferation, terrorism, internal conflict, Ebola 2014).

Art 40: to prevent an aggravation of the situation, the Council may, before recommending or deciding measures under
art 39, call upon the parties to comply with provisional measures — e.g. a ceasefire, a cessation of hostilities or
letting humanitarian aid in. They are "without prejudice to the rights, claims, or
position of the parties", and failure to comply is taken into account. The wording
suggests they come first, but in practice the Council often makes such demands alongside sanctions or force. Most
scholars say an art 39 determination must precede them, though this is debated.

Then art 41 (no armed force) and art 42 (force, the last resort, where art 41 measures "would be inadequate or have
proved to be inadequate").

Exam use: (1) art 39 determination, (2) any art 40 measures, (3) art 41, (4) art 42 only if art 41 is inadequate.
