The flowchart's intoxication box: there is now a legislative presumption of contributory negligence where
the plaintiff is intoxicated (CLA s 50; see also Civil Law (Wrongs) Act 2002 (ACT) ss 95, 96). Section 50 works as a
sequence.

1. **Trigger (s 50(1)).** The person whose death, injury or damage is the subject of the proceedings was, at the time
   of the act or omission that caused it, intoxicated to the extent that their capacity to exercise reasonable care
   and skill was impaired.
2. **No damages unless (s 50(2)).** A court is not to award damages unless satisfied that the death, injury or damage
   (or some other injury or damage) is likely to have occurred even if the person had not been intoxicated.
3. **Presumption (s 50(3)).** If so satisfied, the person is presumed to have been contributorily negligent unless
   the court is satisfied that the intoxication did not contribute in any way to the cause.
4. **Minimum reduction (s 50(4)).** Where the presumption applies, damages are reduced by 25% or a greater percentage
   the court determines to be appropriate.
5. **Exception (s 50(5)).** The section does not apply where the court is satisfied that the intoxication was not
   self-induced.

Exam use: take s 50 in order — impaired capacity, self-induced, likely to have happened anyway, any contribution —
and state the 25% floor. Keep it apart from the *volenti* box on passengers of intoxicated drivers: there the driver
is intoxicated, not the plaintiff.
