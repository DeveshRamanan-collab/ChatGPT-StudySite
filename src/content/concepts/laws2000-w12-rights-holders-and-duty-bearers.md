Right bearers are the individual and groups (collective rights). Duty bearers are primarily states.

- **Vertical obligations:** the state's duties toward individuals under its jurisdiction.
- **Horizontal obligations:** the state's duty to protect individuals from violations by other private actors — e.g.
  torture, non-discrimination in housing.

The Human Rights Committee's General Comment No 31 (para 8) explains the horizontal duty: the Covenant "cannot be viewed
as a substitute for domestic criminal or civil law", but the positive obligations on states parties to ensure Covenant
rights "will only be fully discharged if individuals are protected by the State, not just against violations of
Covenant rights by its agents, but also against acts committed by private persons or entities".

International organisations can also be duty bearers (e.g. in peacekeeping). In *Interpretation of the Agreement of
25 March 1951 between the WHO and Egypt* (Advisory Opinion, ICJ, 1980, para 37): "International organizations are
subjects of international law and, as such, are bound by any obligations incumbent upon them under general rules of
international law, under their constitutions or under international agreements to which they are parties".

Exam use: where a private actor causes the harm, frame the state's breach as a failure of its positive obligation to
protect (GC 31, para 8), not as the private actor breaching the treaty.
