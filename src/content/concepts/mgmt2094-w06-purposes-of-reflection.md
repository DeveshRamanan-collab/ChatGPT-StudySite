The slides list five purposes of reflection:

1. **Consider the process of our own learning** — a process of recognition.
2. **Critically review something** — our own behaviour, that of others, etc.
3. **Build on theory from observation.**
4. **Engage in personal or self development.**
5. **Make decisions or resolve uncertainty.**

In the recording the lecturer glosses them. Recognition is step one: we sit down and recognise that we want to learn
something from this experience. Critical review means looking critically at our own behaviour or other people's.
Building theory means we observe and come up with an explanation for what we observe. And, based on our reflection, we
can make decisions.

In respect to your self-reflective writing, the purposes relate to your group experiences, theory on the innovative
project you presented, and how it may have affected the type and outcome of the project. The lecturer's prompts: what
did I learn from this, how could I — and others — have done things differently, and how would I do it next time?

Exam use: when asked what a piece of reflective writing is doing, name the purpose it serves (critically reviewing how
the team handled a conflict, say) rather than reciting all five.
