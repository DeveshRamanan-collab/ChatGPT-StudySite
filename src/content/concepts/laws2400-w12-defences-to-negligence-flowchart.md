The textbook gives two flowcharts for defences to negligence (Tort Law Principles 3e, pp 478–479):
"Defences to Negligence 2 – Contributory Negligence" and "Defences to Negligence 3 – Voluntary Assumption of Risk".
Both turn on the plaintiff's own conduct, but they end in different places.

**Contributory negligence** results in a *partial reduction* in the damages otherwise recoverable, based on what is
"just and equitable having regard to [their] share in the responsibility for the damage" (Law Reform (Miscellaneous
Provisions) Act 1965 (NSW) s 9(1)). Its boxes: what is just and equitable; the relevant standard of care (CLA s 5R);
the causal requirement; momentary thoughtlessness by employees; agony of the moment; rescuers; and intoxication
(CLA s 50).

**Voluntary assumption of risk** (*volenti non fit injuria*) operates as a *complete defence*. The defendant must
show that the plaintiff perceived the risk, fully appreciated its nature and extent, and voluntarily accepted that
specific risk. Its boxes: knowledge of risk; intoxicated drivers and passengers; legislative reform (CLA s 5L); and
illegality.

Exam use: when the facts show the plaintiff's own carelessness or risk-taking, walk both branches — contributory
negligence reduces the damages; *volenti*, if proved, defeats the claim.
