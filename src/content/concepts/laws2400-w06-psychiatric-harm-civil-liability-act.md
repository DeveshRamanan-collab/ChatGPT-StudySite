Part 3 CLA governs mental harm claims in NSW. Section 27 splits "mental harm" into "pure" (standalone) and
"consequential" (from another personal injury). Section 31 bars damages for pure mental harm unless it is
a recognised psychiatric illness. Section 32 sets the duty test: no duty to avoid causing mental harm
unless the defendant ought to have foreseen a person of *normal fortitude* might suffer a recognised
illness, having regard to four circumstances — sudden shock, witnessing the victim killed/injured/put in
peril at the scene, the plaintiff's relationship to the victim, and any pre-existing relationship with the
defendant. Section 30 then limits recovery for *pure* mental harm from shock to scene-witnesses and the
victim's close family.

*Wicks v State Rail Authority (NSW)* establishes the order of analysis: s 32 (duty) precedes s 30(2) (the
limitation), since s 30 only matters once a duty exists. It reads "witnessed ... being ... put in peril"
broadly — officers who spent hours assisting derailment victims still "in peril" satisfied s 30(2)(a), as
not every such event begins and ends in an instant.

Section 32 differs from *Tame*: it expressly requires foreseeability to be judged against a person of
*normal fortitude*, whereas *Tame* treated that as one relevant consideration, not a fixed standard.

Exam use: for a NSW mental-harm problem, apply s 32 first (duty), then s 31 (illness), then — only for pure
mental harm from shock — s 30 (scene-witness or close family).
