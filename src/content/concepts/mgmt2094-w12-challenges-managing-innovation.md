The slides list seven challenges faced while managing innovation:

1. **Why change?** Only innovation matters.
2. **What to change?** Ranging from changes in product and service to the ways things are done (i.e. process innovation).
3. **Understanding innovation.** Understanding the common problems associated with partial views of innovation.
4. **Building an innovation culture.** Managing innovation is all about creating firm-specific routines (i.e. repeated, reinforced patterns of behaviour) which define the firm's particular approach to the problem.
5. **Continuous learning.** Firms constantly need to develop their routines to deal with environmental challenges.
6. **High involvement innovation.** A higher level of participation in innovation represents a competitive advantage.
7. **Managing connections.** Business organisations are now required to operate in relationship with others rather than in splendid isolation.

Routines are the thread: challenge 4 builds them, and challenge 5 keeps developing them as the environment changes.

Example: Gilbert + Tobin faced "why change?" when NewLaw disruptors began taking lower-value legal work, and met "managing connections" by partnering with one, LegalVision, rather than standing alone. Shelley Simpson describes continuous learning: as her business grew she had to learn logistics, packaging, freight and compliance.
