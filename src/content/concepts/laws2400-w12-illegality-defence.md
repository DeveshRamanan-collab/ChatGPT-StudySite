The last box under the *volenti* flowchart's general principles is illegality.

Where the plaintiff has been injured and the plaintiff and defendant were involved in the criminal enterprise, the
courts have often found that it would not be possible or feasible to determine an appropriate standard of care. In
such cases, the courts have found that a defence of illegality is available to the defendant — see, eg, *Gala v
Preston*.

However, the defence of illegality will not be available to a defendant where, on the facts, the court is able to
determine the relevant standard of care — see, eg, *Progress & Properties Ltd v Craft*.

So the illegality alone does not decide the matter. Two questions do:

1. Were the plaintiff and the defendant involved in the criminal enterprise when the plaintiff was injured?
2. On the facts, is the court able to determine the relevant standard of care?

The flowchart's illegality defence is available only where the first answer is yes and the second is no.

Exam use: in a joint-crime fact pattern, argue whether a standard of care can be set on these facts, citing *Gala v
Preston* for the defence and *Progress & Properties Ltd v Craft* for its limit.
