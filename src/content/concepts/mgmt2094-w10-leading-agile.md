Rigby, Sutherland and Takeuchi find that the greatest impediment to agile is not methodology or evidence but
the behaviour of executives, who unwittingly keep managing in ways that undermine it — overturning team decisions
and adding review layers. They set out six practices:

1. **Learn how agile really works** — it is neither anarchy nor 'doing what I say, only faster'.
2. **Understand where agile does or does not work** — it is not a panacea.
3. **Start small and let the word spread** — at John Deere agile began in corporate IT in 2004 and spread over years
   to R&D and marketing, helped by a coach and weekly one-page articles.
4. **Allow 'master' teams to customise their practices** — as in shu-ha-ri, master the proven disciplines first;
   Spotify's squads may drop burndown charts because their tested changes improve results.
5. **Practise agile at the top** — executive teams at Systematic, GE Digital and Mission Bell Winery ran themselves as
   agile teams and rank-ordered enterprise backlogs.
6. **Destroy the barriers to agile behaviours** — get everyone on the same page, change roles rather than structures,
   name only one boss for each decision, focus on teams not individuals, and lead with questions, not orders.

The stalled app pilot shows why practice 6 matters: an agile team built a praised app, but annual funding and waterfall
testing queues left the total time to release barely improved.

Exam use: when agile stalls in a scenario, diagnose which practice the leaders are breaking.
