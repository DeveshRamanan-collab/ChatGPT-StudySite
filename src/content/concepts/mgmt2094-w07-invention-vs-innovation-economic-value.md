The tutorial's first comparison: let's understand the difference between invention and innovation, and pay
attention to the economic value.

- **Invention** is a novel product, device, process, or concept. It requires knowledge, competence, and financial
  aid. Examples: the invention of the printing press, electricity, the telephone.
- **Innovation** is the introduction of a newer and better solution that meets new requirements or existing market
  needs. It requires technical knowledge, competence, knowledge about market needs, trends, and financial support.
  Examples: the introduction of the iPhone, the tablet, the flat-screen TV.

Both columns need competence and money. Only innovation is tied to the market: it is defined by meeting
requirements or market needs, it adds knowledge about market needs and trends, and its examples are introductions
rather than inventions. The slide does not spell out the economic value; this is where the two columns differ.

**Exam use:** ask whether the thing is simply novel (invention) or has been introduced as a newer, better solution
that meets a requirement or market need (innovation), and cite the chart's examples.
