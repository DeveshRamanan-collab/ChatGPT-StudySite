The tutorial's second comparison: remember which one follows the other.

- **Creativity** is the use of imagination or original ideas to create something. It is related to imagination,
  it cannot be measured, and it precedes innovation.
- **Innovation** is the introduction of new or improved goods, services, processes, etc. It is related to
  implementation, it can be measured, and it follows creativity.

The order is fixed: creativity first, innovation second. The two columns pair off — imagination against
implementation, cannot be measured against can be measured — and innovation covers improved goods, services and
processes, not only brand-new ones. The first chart's examples of innovation (the introduction of the iPhone, the
tablet, the flat-screen TV) are introductions of exactly this kind.

The slide ends with a question for discussion: in which ways are they useful in organisations? The materials leave
it open; the chart's rows are the place to start.

**Exam use:** when a scenario describes original ideas being imagined, call it creativity; when something new or
improved is introduced and put into practice, call it innovation, and say that innovation follows creativity.
