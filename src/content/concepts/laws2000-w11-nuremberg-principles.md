In Resolution 95(I) of 11 December 1946 the UN General Assembly affirmed the "Principles of International Law
Recognized in the Charter of the Nürnberg Tribunal and in the Judgment of the Tribunal". Each principle closes a door
an accused might try to leave through.

Principle I — individual responsibility: "Any person who commits an act which constitutes a crime under international
law is responsible therefor and liable to punishment."

Principle II — internal law is no shield: that internal law imposes no penalty for an act which is a crime under
international law "does not relieve the person who committed the act from responsibility under international law".

Principle III — official position is no shield: acting "as Head of State or responsible Government official does not
relieve him from responsibility under international law".

Principle IV — superior orders are no shield: acting "pursuant to order of his Government or of a superior does not
relieve him from responsibility under international law, provided a moral choice was in fact possible to him". The
qualifier matters: the plea fails where a moral choice was in fact possible.

Principle III is about responsibility, which is substantive. Week 6's immunities are procedural: they may stop a
particular foreign court from proceeding, but immunity does not mean impunity.

Exam use: when an accused pleads "my country's law allowed it", "I was head of state" or "I followed orders", answer
with Principles II, III and IV in turn.
