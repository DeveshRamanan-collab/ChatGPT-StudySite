**Design thinking** (Brown and Wyatt, 2010) incorporates consumer insights in depth and rapid prototyping, aimed at
getting beyond the assumptions that block effective solutions. It is human centred and crosses public, for-profit and
non-profit sectors. Its process is **a system of overlapping spaces rather than a sequence of orderly steps**:
- **Inspiration** — the problem or opportunity that motivates the search: a brief, then observing what people
  actually do. Kara Pecknold reframed a website brief for Rwandan weavers who had little access to computers.
- **Ideation** — synthesising insights, then divergent thinking by diverse, T-shaped teams; brainstorming defers
  judgement.
- **Implementation** — prototyping, testing, iterating and refining. VisionSpring found children were at ease when
  they screened each other's eyes.

Shanti shows the cost of skipping it: a nearby, affordable water centre needed a 5-gallon jerrican she could not carry
and a monthly card for more water than she needed.

The lecture's **ideation process** follows the same shape: a sufficiently diverse team (age, seniority, experience,
customer knowledge); an immersion stage; as many ideas as possible (quantity, not quality); criteria for prioritising
(eg revenue potential, impact on competitive advantage); and a shortlist of three to five business models.

**Exam use:** contrast the overlapping spaces with a linear, milestone process, and ground each space in an example.
