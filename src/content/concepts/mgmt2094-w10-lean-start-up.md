Lean Start-Up (LSU) is associated with Steve Blank and Eric Ries. Its key principles on the slides are the
**minimum viable product (MVP)** and **lean–agile development**.

Steve Blank's hypotheses:

- Entrepreneurs and investors are generally executing on guesses.
- Start-ups aren't small versions of large organisations.
- They are about learning/discovery, not execution.
- They need to surface hypotheses/assumptions and test them against facts.
- However, the facts live outside the building.

The core concepts are the **MVP** — a product with just those features that allow you to sell to early adopters and
validate demand; **pivoting** — course correction to the proposed set of hypotheses, based on market learnings; and the
**business model canvas**.

The lean start-up method = **customer development + agile development** (fast, iterative and incremental product
development): MVP 1, 2, 3 and 4, each shaped by customer feedback, lead by rapid deployment to the final product, with
no waste of time and money and a culture of continuous improvement. The 'lean' comes from Japan's manufacturing
industry (Toyota's Production System and kanban); scrum, extreme programming and the 2001 Agile Manifesto supply the
agile stream; Blank's *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (2005) and Ries's *Lean Startup* (2011) bring them to start-ups.

Exam use: explain why a start-up should test its assumptions with an MVP before scaling.
