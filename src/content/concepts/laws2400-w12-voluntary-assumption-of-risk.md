*Volenti non fit injuria* (voluntary assumption of risk) operates as a *complete defence* to negligence.
The essence of the defence lies in a plaintiff's willingness to assume a risk which the plaintiff knew existed in a
particular activity.

The defendant must show that the plaintiff not only perceived the existence of the risk, but fully appreciated the
nature and extent of that risk and voluntarily accepted that specific risk. The defence is narrowly confined to the
specific risk and does not extend to an awareness of the general nature of the risk.

**Knowledge of risk.** The plaintiff must fully appreciate the nature and likely manifestation of the risk (*Banovic
v Perkovic*; *Carey v Lake Macquarie City Council*). Effective knowledge extends beyond the mere existence of a risk
to an acceptance of materialisation of that risk (*Carey*; *ICI v Shatwell*).

Set it beside contributory negligence: that defence reduces damages in part, on what is just and equitable; *volenti*,
if proved, defeats the claim entirely.

Exam use: identify the specific risk that materialised, then ask whether the plaintiff perceived it, fully
appreciated its nature and extent, and voluntarily accepted it. Knowing only that an activity is dangerous in
general is not enough.
