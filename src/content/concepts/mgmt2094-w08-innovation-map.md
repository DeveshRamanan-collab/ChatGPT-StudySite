Innovations come in many types — management, service, experiential, value, business model, structural, market,
process, strategic, evolutionary, revolutionary, application, marketing, user-led, product, technological, supply
chain, brand and disruptive innovation — from small incremental changes to revolutionary ones that change an industry
or a culture.

**Characteristics.** There is an object or target being changed (a product, a process, a lifestyle, a strategy, a
culture); innovations vary in extent or magnitude, the degree to which one deviates from the past; innovation is
closely related to problem solving, because ideas for change never transpire without difficulty; and it has an
impact, the significance or range of its effects.

**The innovation map** places types on two axes, internal–external and continuous–discontinuous:
- **Operational innovation** (internal, continuous): processes, technological innovation — ongoing, largely unseen.
- **Management innovation** (internal, discontinuous): internal structures and processes, people management,
  networking, partner interfaces — changed once in a while, then left to settle.
- **Product & service innovation** (external, continuous): brand, technological innovation — continuous.
- **Strategic innovation** (external, discontinuous): disruptive innovation, new markets, new offerings, channels,
  supply chain, customer interfaces, Blue Ocean Strategy — changed when called upon. The class activity's cases
  (Netflix, Airbnb, Uber and others) are tested for disruption here.

**Exam use:** place the scenario on both axes before naming the quadrant.
