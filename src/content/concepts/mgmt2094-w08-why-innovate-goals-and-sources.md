**Why innovate?** In a turbulent, rapidly changing economy, organisations must prepare to innovate on a continuing
basis, otherwise their survival chances are seriously threatened (the slide's example: Apple). Innovation is hard, so
organisations rarely do it without pressure: customers want new things, competitors fight for them, technology moves.

**Goals:** improving quality; creating new markets; extending the product range; reducing labour costs; improving the
production process; reducing materials; reducing environmental damage; replacing products/services; reducing energy
consumption; conformance to regulations. Goals can work together, and Lego's plan for plant-based or recycled bricks
(Reed, 2018) pursues reduced environmental damage while keeping the product the same.

**Sources of innovation:**
1. **Organisational structure** — organic structures (lower vertical differentiation, formalisation and
   centralisation) facilitate flexibility, adaptation and cross-fertilisation.
2. **Long tenure in management** — provides legitimacy and knowledge of how to get things done; a long-serving leader
   can also become complacent.
3. **Slack resources** — let the organisation afford to purchase innovations, bear the cost of instituting them and
   absorb failures; abundance can also make it feel unthreatened, so it stops innovating.
4. **Intra-communications** — committees, task forces and cross-functional teams that work across departmental lines.

**Exam use:** name the goal, then ask which sources the organisation has — and whether tenure or slack is cutting the
wrong way.
