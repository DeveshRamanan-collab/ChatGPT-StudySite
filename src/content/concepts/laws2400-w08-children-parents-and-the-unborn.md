1. **Parents' duty to others.** Parents may owe others a duty to control their child. *Smith v Leurs* (1945): a
   13-year-old's sling-shot stone injured the plaintiff's eye; a duty was owed but not breached, because the parents had
   told their son not to use the sling shot outside their home.
2. **Parents' duty to the child.** Generally no duty in feeding, clothing or raising a child — parents may raise their
   child as they see fit. But they must drive carefully so as not to injure the child (or even a foetus), and must not
   create danger or risk for the child: a parent may be liable if the child is led into danger by their actions. *Hahn v
   Conley* (1971): a grandfather minding a 3-year-old answered her call from across the road; she crossed and was struck
   by a car. In loco parentis, he owed no duty to protect her against that danger — he did not call her to come to him.
3. **Pre-natal injuries.** A child injured in the womb may sue for pre-natal injuries if later born alive.
4. **Wrongful conception, birth and life.** Parents have sued for wrongful conception (the mother would not have
   conceived but for the negligence) and wrongful birth (the pregnancy would have been terminated but for the
   negligence); children have sued for wrongful life. These actions are controversial.

Exam use: identify who is suing whom, and in which direction the duty runs.
