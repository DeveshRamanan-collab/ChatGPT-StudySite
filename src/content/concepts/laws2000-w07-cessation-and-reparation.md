Where there is an internationally wrongful act and no circumstance precludes wrongfulness, two basic
consequences arise.

**Cessation and non-repetition (art 30):** the responsible state must (a) cease the act, if it is
continuing, and (b) offer appropriate assurances and guarantees of non-repetition, if circumstances so
require. In the *Wall* advisory opinion Israel "must offer appropriate assurances and guarantees of
non-repetition".

**Full reparation (art 31):** the responsible state must make full reparation for the injury caused;
injury includes any damage, material or moral. *Factory at Chorzów* (PCIJ, 1928): reparation must, as far
as possible, wipe out all the consequences of the illegal act and re-establish the situation which would,
in all probability, have existed had it not been committed. Three forms:

- **Restitution (art 35):** in kind — e.g. the return of property.
- **Compensation (art 36):** usually money where restitution is not possible (the property was damaged);
  covers material damage that is financially assessable, not moral damage.
- **Satisfaction (art 37):** repairs moral damage — an apology, an expression of regret, or a court's
  judgment declaring the conduct wrongful.

In the *Wall* opinion (2004) Israel had to stop construction, dismantle what had been built and compensate
affected persons; in *Chagos* (2019) the UK had to end its administration and return the islands to
Mauritius as rapidly as possible.

Exam use: state cessation first, then match each head of damage to the right form of reparation.
