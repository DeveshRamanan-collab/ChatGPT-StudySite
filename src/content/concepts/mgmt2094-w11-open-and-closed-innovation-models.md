The lecture's closed and open models describe how concept development and implementation are timed.

- **Closed model**: the steps run consecutively. The project starts, the concept is developed, the concept is frozen,
  and only then does implementation begin, ending in market introduction.
- **Open model**: the project starts and concept development begins, but implementation starts somewhere in the middle
  of concept development, before the concept is frozen. Implementing early is a "trial by fire": you see how the market
  and your users respond, keep developing the concept, and keep tweaking the product or service as implementation
  continues to market introduction.

The difference is the overlap. In the closed model nothing is implemented until the concept is fixed; in the open
model development and implementation run side by side, so market feedback can still change the concept.

Mark's furniture business shows the open habit in a small firm: if a material for a planned piece proves too
expensive he looks for an alternative, and he reorders priorities as things change — even big companies, he notes,
plan to go from A to B and end up at C.

Exam use: ask when the concept is frozen relative to implementation. Before implementation starts → closed; after it
has started → open.
