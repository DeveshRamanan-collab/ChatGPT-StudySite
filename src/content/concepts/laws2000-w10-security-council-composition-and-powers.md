The Security Council has 15 UN member states (art 23). Five are permanent members with veto powers (the P-5):
the US, China, Russia, the UK and France; the veto is in art 27(3). Ten are non-permanent members elected for a
two-year term — five elected each year by the General Assembly — divided along regional lines: three for Africa, two
for Asia, two for Latin America and the Caribbean, two for Western Europe and Other States, and one for Eastern Europe.

Art 24(1): "In order to ensure prompt and effective action by the United Nations, its Members confer on the Security
Council primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security", and agree that it acts on
their behalf. Under art 24(2) it must act in accordance with the Purposes and Principles of the UN; its specific
powers are in Chapters VI, VII, VIII and XII. Chapter VII sets out its powers with respect to threats to the peace,
breaches of the peace and acts of aggression.

Its decisions are legally binding upon all members: they "agree to accept and carry out the decisions of the Security
Council" (art 25). Read art 25 with art 103: where Charter obligations conflict with obligations under any other
international agreement, "their obligations under the present Charter shall prevail".

Exam use: cite art 24 for why the Council acts, art 25 for why its decisions bind, and art 103 for why they override
a conflicting treaty.
