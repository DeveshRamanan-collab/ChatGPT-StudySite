Herrmann's Whole Brain Model is based on learning / thinking / doing. It divides thinking into four quadrants:

- **A** — logical, analytical, fact based, quantitative.
- **B** — organised, sequential, planned, detailed.
- **C** — interpersonal, feeling based, kinaesthetic, emotional.
- **D** — holistic, intuitive, integrating, synthesising.

The model looks at how you first look at a problem: it creates a thinking profile of you and how you respond to each
quadrant. **Preferred mode is 1**, **competent mode is 2** (the way you function, but may not be your first choice)
and **avoidance is given a 3**. You may have more than one quadrant with the same number.

It links to the creative process: the **A and B sides are preparation and verification**; the **C and D sides are
incubation and illumination**.

The lecture's point: your personality pulls you towards some quadrants and away from others, but the task decides
which quadrant you need — a maths problem needs A. If it is one you avoid, be aware of that and consciously push
yourself there, or work with people whose preferences complement yours. The lecturer cautioned that the quadrants do
not necessarily correspond to how the brain actually works.

Example: Poincaré's ideas arrived during incubation (C–D), and he then verified them consciously and logically (A–B).

Exam use: name the quadrant a task needs, the person's code for it, and what to do about a 3.
