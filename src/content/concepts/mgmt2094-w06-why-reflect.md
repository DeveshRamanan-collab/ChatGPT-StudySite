It is not sufficient to merely have experience in order to learn. Without reflecting, an experience may be quickly
forgotten or its learning potential lost. Moon (1999) suggests reflection is "a form of mental processing with a
purpose", and the slide sets beside it a line attributed to John Dewey: "We do not learn from experience… we learn from
reflecting on experience."

The lecture introduces reflection with Dumbledore's Pensieve from *Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire* (Rowling, 2000).
When Dumbledore has too many thoughts and memories crammed into his mind, he siphons the excess into a stone basin and
examines them at his leisure; in that form it becomes easier to spot patterns and links. In the recording the lecturer
draws the parallel: reflective writing is the container for your thoughts, ideas and memories, and putting them there
helps you spot patterns in your thinking and link ideas together.

The lecturer also says why this matters now. We are constantly distracted and bombarded with information by phones,
computers and social media, so we have lots of experiences but rarely sit down to reflect on them, and so we don't
really learn from them.

Exam use: define reflection with Moon's phrase, then explain why experience alone is not enough — the point of
reflecting is learning, not just remembering.
