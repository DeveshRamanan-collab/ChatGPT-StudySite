**Derogation in an emergency.** ICCPR art 4(1): in a public emergency which threatens the life of the nation
and is officially proclaimed, a state may derogate "to the extent strictly required by the exigencies of the
situation", if the measures are consistent with its other international obligations and do not discriminate solely on
the ground of race, colour, sex, language, religion or social origin. Art 4(2): no derogation from arts 6, 7, 8(1) and
(2), 11, 15, 16 and 18. ACHR art 27 is similar (war, public danger or other emergency threatening a state's
independence or security) and lists non-suspendable rights such as life, humane treatment and freedom from slavery.

Four substantive requirements:

1. Necessity and proportionality (nature, duration, impact). In *Aksoy v Turkey* (ECtHR, para 78), holding a suspect
   for fourteen days without judicial intervention was "exceptionally long" and not necessary.
2. Consistency with the state's other obligations under international law, especially jus cogens.
3. No effect on absolute or non-derogable rights.
4. Notification.

**Restrictions outside an emergency** must be provided by law; serve a legitimate aim described in the limitation
clause (e.g. protection of rights and freedoms); and be necessary in a democratic society — responding to a public
need, proportionate, with a legitimate aim.

Exam use: first decide whether the state is derogating in an emergency (art 4) or limiting a right in normal times,
then test each condition in turn.
