Art 42: should the Council consider art 41 measures "would be inadequate or have proved to be inadequate, it
may take such action by air, sea, or land forces as may be necessary to maintain or restore international peace and
security". It is an exception to the prohibition on the use of force in art 2(4), a last resort, and relies on member
states.

Art 43 was never implemented, so the Council authorises coalitions of states. In 1990, after Iraq's illegal
occupation of Kuwait, Res 678 authorised member states co-operating with Kuwait to use "all necessary means"
(Operation Desert Storm). The Council may also authorise regional organisations to enforce its decisions (art 53,
Chapter VIII) — e.g. ECOWAS in Liberia, NATO in Libya. Authorisations have covered humanitarian relief in internal
conflict (Somalia 1992, Res 794), protection of civilians (Libya and Côte d'Ivoire 2011, Res 1973 and 1975), no-fly
zones (Res 1973) and piracy (Somalia 2008, Res 1816).

Revived or implied authorisation? In March 2003 a US- and UK-led coalition, including Australia, invaded Iraq,
claiming that Iraq's material breach of Res 687's ceasefire conditions revived the authority to use force under Res
678; Res 1441 had threatened "serious consequences". Most other Council and UN members, and most legal scholars,
rejected this.

Exam use: look for express Chapter VII authorising words such as "all necessary means"; do not imply an
authorisation from vaguer language.
