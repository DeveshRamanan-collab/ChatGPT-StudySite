Parties can deal with obvious building defects by contract; torts comes in for **latent** defects, found
years later, often by a later owner. A defect causing physical injury is the paradigm; a defect that only
diminishes the building's value has been viewed as PEL, whether it was negligently planned, designed or built.

*Bryan v Maloney* (1995): a builder built a house with defective foundations; Mrs Maloney, the third owner, found
cracks in the walls. **Duty owed** to a subsequent purchaser to take reasonable care in building a house. It used
the proximity principle and distinguished dwellings — a homeowner is more vulnerable.

*Woolcock Street Investments v CDG* (2004): CDG designed a commercial complex; one owner refused to pay for a
geotechnical inspection; Woolcock bought from the original owners and the building settled.
**No duty**: no reliance or assumption of responsibility, and Woolcock was not vulnerable — a commercial buyer
could have protected itself.

In NSW the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 now gives owners a statutory cause of action:

- s 37: a person who carries out construction work has a duty to exercise reasonable care to avoid economic loss
  caused by defects, owed to each owner and each subsequent owner, with damages as if at common law;
- s 36: "construction work" includes designs, building products, supervision and project management;
- s 39: the duty is non-delegable; s 40: it cannot be contracted out.

Exam use: in NSW, start with s 37 — no common law duty needed.
