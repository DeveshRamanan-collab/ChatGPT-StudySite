Two pairs of theories on the slides answer where creative ideas come from.

**Creativity by grace**: the idea of genius — creative people are endowed with a superhuman potential; creativity is
seen as a divine gift. **Creativity by accident**: creativity rises by chance — accidental discoveries such as
immunisation, arising from an interruption in work, and radioactivity, from a wrong hypothesis.

**Creative association**: applying procedures from one area to another. It underlies the justification for many
divergent thinking techniques such as lateral thinking and brainstorming. **Cognitive creativity**: creativity is a
normal human activity — about reasoning, recognition and understanding.

The readings qualify grace and pure accident. Sadler-Smith (citing Cropley) notes that Becquerel could not have
discovered radioactivity by chance had he not already been immersed in the research: chance favours the prepared mind.
Darwin, a biologist, drew on Malthus' political economy — knowledge from an unrelated domain. Claman (2017) takes the
cognitive view: everyone has creativity when confronted with new problems; it is "not just for the talented few",
though it fades in a rut. Her fixes — think new, look for intersections, capitalise on obstacles, share what you know
— treat creativity as a habit that can be rebuilt.

implies for whether creativity can be developed.
