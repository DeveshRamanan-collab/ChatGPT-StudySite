The first element of an internationally wrongful act is conduct in breach of an international obligation.
Breach is synonymous with violation.

- **Any source** (art 12): the breach concerns a primary rule of international law irrespective of its
  source — custom or treaty, it doesn't matter. It must be an international obligation, not a domestic one;
  whether domestic law allowed the conduct is irrelevant. Failing to comply with an ICJ decision could also
  engage responsibility.
- **In force at the time** (arts 13–14): the state needs to be under the obligation at the time of the
  breach — there is no retroactivity (*Chagos* Advisory Opinion, 2019).
- **Composite acts** (art 15): one or two actions may not amount to a breach, but taken together they
  eventually will — for example, pollution that adds up over time.
- **Act or omission** (art 2): a breach can arise from an action (use of force, pollution) but also from an
  omission. In *Tehran*, Iran failed to protect the US embassy and its staff, as the Vienna Convention on
  Diplomatic Relations required. In *Corfu Channel* (1949), Albania knew of mines in its waters but failed to
  notify shipping and warn the approaching British warships; 45 sailors died.

Exam use: identify the precise obligation, confirm it bound the state when the conduct occurred, then state
whether the breach is an act, an omission or a composite of acts.
