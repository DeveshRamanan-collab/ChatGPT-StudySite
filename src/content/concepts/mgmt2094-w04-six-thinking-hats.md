In *Six Thinking Hats* Edward de Bono separates thinking into six distinct modes, each identified with a coloured hat.

- **Blue — control thinking.** Process, control, taking an overview: think of the blue sky over us, a wider, higher perspective to ensure you are addressing the right issue. *What is our focus? What do we do next?*
- **White — information, facts.** Neutral, objective and non-emotional; white is effectively colourless. Facts and figures, trends from historical data, gaps in the knowledge. *What information do we have? What do we need?*
- **Yellow — benefits, harmony.** Sunny and optimistic: see all the benefits and the value in the decision. *What are the strengths and pluses? Why will it work?*
- **Green — growth, creativity.** The creation of ideas, concepts and perceptions — a tree branching out; escaping old ideas to find better ones, with no criticism or judgement. *What is possible? What would happen if?*
- **Red — emotion, intuition.** Views based on intuition and instinct, without explanation or justification. *What is your gut reaction?*
- **Black — problems, caution.** The risks, and why a proposed solution will fail; it makes plans tougher and more resilient and spots fatal flaws, but it can be over-used. *What might go wrong?*

The colours are de Bono's interpretation; what matters is the direction each one names.

Exam use: identify the hat from the kind of thinking a question asks for, not from its topic.
