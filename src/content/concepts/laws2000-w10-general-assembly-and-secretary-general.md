The General Assembly is the parliamentary body of the UN, made up of all member states (currently 193). It is
seen as the most "democratic" international organ but has a minimal role in enforcement. One state, one vote (art 18).

It has a subsidiary responsibility for international peace and security. It may discuss any question on it and,
except as provided in art 12, make recommendations; a question on which action is necessary must be referred to the
Council (art 11(2)). It may recommend measures for the peaceful adjustment of any situation (art 14; *Certain
Expenses*, 1962). But while the Council is
exercising its functions in respect of a dispute or situation, the Assembly shall not make any recommendation on it
unless the Council so requests (art 12(1); *Wall* Advisory Opinion, paras 27–28). The lecture explained that the
old view — no Assembly involvement while the Council deals with a matter — has evolved: the Assembly may now consider
it in parallel, usually taking a broader view while the Council works on detail.

Uniting for Peace (GA Res 377(V), 1950): if the Council, because of lack of unanimity of the permanent members, fails
to exercise its primary responsibility, the Assembly shall consider the matter immediately with a view to
recommending collective measures. The Cold War widened its role.

The Secretary-General, appointed by the Assembly for five years, may bring any threat to the Council's attention
(art 99) and offers good offices.

Exam use: the Assembly recommends; the Council decides.
