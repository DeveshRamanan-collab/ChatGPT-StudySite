*Shaddock & Associates v Parramatta City Council* (1981): the council told an intending purchaser, by phone and
then by certificate, that no road-widening proposal affected the land; one did. **Duty found.** The duty covers
information as well as advice, and there is no separate requirement that the speaker possess special skill. The
council was the only source of the information; the phone call alone might not have been enough.

*San Sebastian v Minister* (1986): developers bought in Woolloomooloo on the strength of a publicly exhibited
redevelopment scheme the council later abandoned. A duty can arise without any request — known special
competence, assumption of responsibility, inviting or intending reliance, or an interest in the recipient acting.
Brennan J: the representor realises the representee will trust their special competence; it is reasonable to
accept and rely; loss is reasonably foreseeable if the statement is wrong. **No duty**: the statement was one of
intention, and relying on a mere intention was not reasonable.

*Tepko v Water Board* (2001): a reluctant "ball-park" costing, shown to the plaintiff's bank, was overstated and
the arrangements collapsed. **No duty**: the Board neither knew nor should have known of the reliance, and
reliance on a reluctant, provisional estimate was not reasonable. Needed: known reliance, an assumption of
responsibility, or both.

Exam use: ask what kind of statement it was, how it was given, whether the defendant knew or ought to have known
it would be relied on, and whether relying on it was reasonable.
