The reading's infographic (adapted from Designworks' Luke van O and Herbert Simon's *The Sciences of the
Artificial*, 1969) runs five stages: **Empathy → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test**, under three headings —
Understand ("understanding ends in insight"), Create ("creation ends in ideas") and Deliver ("delivery ends in
reality").

Its eight steps say how to behave along the way:

1. Be empathetic in defining the problem (decide the issue, agree the audience, establish a glossary of terms).
2. Be explorative in researching the problem (review its history and other attempts; talk to your end users).
3. Be creative in generating ideas (as many as possible; do not judge or debate them).
4. Be iterative in refining your ideas (multiple drafts, diverse feedback, working prototypes).
5. Be rational in choosing an idea (set aside emotion and ownership; avoid consensus thinking).
6. Be practical in delivering it to the client (plan, resource, assign, execute).
7. Be honest in evaluating performance (did it meet its goals? measure success).
8. Repeat.

The line is drawn straight, but the process is iterative: the reading says its iterative nature can open alternative
paths or even redefine the initial problem, and the guest lecture stresses that a team may go back a stage at any point
or change direction.
