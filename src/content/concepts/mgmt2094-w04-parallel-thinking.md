**Parallel thinking** is an alternative to argument. In parallel thinking each thinker puts forward his or her thoughts in parallel with the thoughts of others, rather than attacking the thoughts of others. It guides thought processes in one direction at a time, so we can effectively analyse issues, generate new ideas and make better decisions — a constructive alternative to arguments.

Hats help a group use parallel thinking. You can "put on" and "take off" a hat: hats are directions of thinking, so the whole group looks the same way at the same time.

Argument is how we usually think, even inside our own heads: put a point, wait for the counter-argument, go back and forth. In a meeting the most extroverted voices fill the room and quieter people hold back. Parallel thinking gives everyone a voice; the benefits slide lists removal of ego (reduce confrontation).

De Bono (quoted by Kivunja 2015) names three difficulties in practical thinking that the method answers:

- **Emotions** — relying on gut feeling and prejudice: the hats use emotions at the right place.
- **Helplessness** — "I don't know what to do next": the hats give definite next steps.
- **Confusion** — keeping everything in mind at once: one direction at a time.

Exam use: define parallel thinking against argument in one sentence, then show how a hat points everyone in the same direction.
