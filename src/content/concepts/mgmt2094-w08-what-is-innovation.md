An innovation is an idea, practice or object that is **perceived as new** by an individual or other unit of
adoption — a use of new knowledge to offer a new product or service that customers want. In short:
**invention + commercialisation**.

*Perceived*: newness is judged by the adopter — usually customers, but for an NGO any stakeholder (the people it
helps, donors, partner organisations). *Commercialisation*: you can be extremely creative, but if you cannot
commercialise it, it is creativity, not innovation.

**Invention vs innovation.** Invention is the creation of a new product, service or process; it may not be
commercialised, can be autonomous or induced, can have a non-economic motive, usually stays in the R&D centre and
precedes innovation. Innovation introduces it into the marketplace: it results in commercialisation, is usually
induced, has an economic motive, spreads across the organisation, brings organisational change and succeeds
invention.

**Three circles.** Innovation sits where what is **desirable** to users, what is **possible** with technology and
what is **viable** in the marketplace overlap. The jetpack has long been desirable, but the technology is only now
arriving and it is extremely expensive. Lego's plastic-free brick (Reed, 2018) must look and feel the same, around 200
alternative materials have so far fallen short, and the chief executive could not yet say what it would cost.

**Exam use:** define with both halves, then test the idea against all three circles.
