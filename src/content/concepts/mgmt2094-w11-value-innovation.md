Value innovation (VI) is one of the most prominent programmatic approaches to innovation currently in use. It is
different from building layers of competitive advantage, and it does not mean segmenting the market and accommodating
customer needs. Its main focus is offering products and services that create superior buyer value in existing markets
and enable a quantum leap for the firm to create new markets. It also differs from technology innovation: new
technology does not become a value innovation unless it is cheap enough for mass buyers.

Value innovation changes all three basic building blocks of strategy:

- **Competition** — not outperforming the competition, but seeking radically superior value to capture the entire
  mass market.
- **Customers** — not retaining and better satisfying existing customers, but targeting the mass of buyers by following
  non-customers closely, willingly losing some existing customers.
- **Corporate capabilities** — not just leveraging and extending current capabilities, but being willing to combine a
  company's capabilities.

Yellow Tail is the lecture's example: still just wine, in a market full of budget and premium wines, but by rethinking
what wine and wine drinking are it repositioned the product and created value for people who were not wine buyers.
Before innovating, consider the whole business model too: value proposition, customers, customer relationships,
channels, revenue, key partners, key activities, key resources and costs.

Exam use: test each building block — who are the non-customers, and what superior value reaches the mass market?
