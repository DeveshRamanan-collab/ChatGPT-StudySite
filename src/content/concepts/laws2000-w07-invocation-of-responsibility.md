**The injured state (art 42).** The rule is that only the state or states whose individual rights have
been violated are entitled to invoke international responsibility — to demand cessation, reparation and so
on. A state is injured if the obligation breached was owed to it, for instance under a bilateral or a
multilateral treaty. It may be a state individually or states as a group.

**Non-injured states (art 48).** In very limited circumstances, any state other than an injured state may
invoke the responsibility of another state if:

- (a) the obligation breached is owed to a group of states including that state, and is established for
  the protection of a collective interest of the group — e.g. a regional nuclear-free zone treaty or a
  regional system for the protection of human rights; or
- (b) the obligation breached is owed to the international community as a whole — an obligation erga
  omnes (*Barcelona Traction*, ICJ 1970).

An obligation erga omnes is so important that by its very nature it concerns all states: the right it
protects is so important that all states have a legal interest in its protection. Examples in the slides:
the non-use of force principle, the prohibition of aggression and genocide, and breaches of international
environmental law.

Exam use: ask "to whom was this obligation owed?" — if to the claimant, it is injured (art 42); if to a
collective or to everyone, art 48 may still give it standing.
