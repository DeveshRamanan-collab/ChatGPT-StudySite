ARIO art 2(a) defines an international organisation as one "established by a treaty or other instrument
governed by international law and possessing its own international legal personality", which may include
entities other than states as members. Being governed by international law distinguishes IOs from NGOs such as
Greenpeace or Amnesty International. Almost all IOs are created by treaty (the UN Charter); some by resolutions
(OPEC; the ICTY and ICTR by Security Council resolution).

The constitutive instrument sets membership (UN Charter art 4: "peace-loving states"; the EU is a member of the
FAO), withdrawal (Rome Statute art 127: effective one year after notification; the UN Charter has no withdrawal
clause) and expulsion (UN Charter art 6, for persistent violation).

International legal personality is distinct from the members': concluding treaties, sending and receiving
delegations, bringing international claims, and being responsible for wrongdoing. It may be conferred
explicitly (WTO art VIII; the norm for the last 30 years) or implicitly: *Reparation for Injuries* (1949) found
the UN an international person although the Charter is silent. UN Charter art 104 confers only domestic legal
capacity — renting offices in New York, employing staff.

Exam use: go to the constitutive instrument first; if it is silent on personality, argue implied personality
from the functions the organisation was intended to exercise.
