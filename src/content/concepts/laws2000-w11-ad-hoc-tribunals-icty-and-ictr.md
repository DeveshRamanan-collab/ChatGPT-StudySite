Both ad hoc tribunals were created by the UN Security Council acting under Chapter VII of the UN Charter ("Action
with respect to Threats to the Peace, Breaches of the Peace, and Acts of Aggression") — not by treaty.

ICTY: Resolution 827 (25 May 1993) decided that "all States shall cooperate fully" with the Tribunal and take any
measures necessary under their domestic law, including complying with requests for assistance or Trial Chamber
orders. Article 1 of its Statute: power to prosecute persons responsible for serious violations of international
humanitarian law in the territory of the former Yugoslavia since 1991. Milestones: the 1995 Tadić Interlocutory Appeal
Decision on the legality of its creation; the 1999 indictment of Slobodan Milošević. 161 charged, 90 convictions, 19
acquittals; closed 31 December 2017.

ICTR: established on 8 November 1994 under Chapter VII (the slide gives "Resolution 855" — verify the number), seated
in Arusha. Article 1: serious violations of IHL in Rwanda, and by Rwandan citizens in neighbouring states, in 1994;
arts 2–4: genocide, crimes against humanity, violations of Common Article 3 and Additional Protocol II. Kambanda
pleaded guilty to genocide (May 1998); Akayesu was convicted of genocide, with sexual violence found to constitute the
actus reus (September 1998). Closed December 2015.

The Residual Mechanism (MICT) was established in 2010. Both tribunals contributed to ICL; both are criticised for
delays and distance from affected communities.

Exam use: Chapter VII decisions bind every UN member (art 25), so all states had to cooperate.
