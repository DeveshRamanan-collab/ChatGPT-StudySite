Chapter VI of the Charter deals with the pacific settlement of disputes. Art 33(1): the parties to any dispute
"the continuance of which is likely to endanger the maintenance of international peace and security, shall, first of
all, seek a solution by negotiation, enquiry, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, judicial settlement, resort to
regional agencies or arrangements, or other peaceful means of their own choice". Under art 33(2) the Security Council
shall, when it deems necessary, call upon the parties to settle their dispute by such means.

The Council's role:

- it can investigate any dispute, or any situation which might lead to international friction or give rise to a
  dispute, to see if it is a danger to international peace and security (art 34);
- any UN member may bring such a dispute or situation to the attention of the Council (or the General Assembly)
  (art 35);
- it can recommend appropriate procedures or methods of adjustment (art 36(1));
- it should consider that legal disputes should as a general rule be referred by the parties to the ICJ (art 36(3));
- parties who fail to settle a dispute by the art 33 means shall refer it to the Council (art 37(1)).

Peaceful settlement — good offices, inquiry, mediation, conciliation, adjudication (ICJ) or arbitration (PCA) — is the
first of the Council's tools, before collective security without force (art 41) or with force (art 42).

Exam use: open a Security Council problem with Chapter VI; Chapter VII coercion comes after.
