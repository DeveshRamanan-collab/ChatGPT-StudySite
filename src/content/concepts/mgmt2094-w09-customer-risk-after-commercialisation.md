After 12 months of a very successful campaign the Deck Protector project **stopped**. ArmorAll had run TV commercials
that Thompson's challenged; a legal battle ensued and ArmorAll lost; the financial impact of the court case forced
ArmorAll to be taken over by Clorox. In the guest lecture: ArmorAll's marketing people ran the ads without checking
with the legal people, and Dow Corning, as a B2B supplier, had done everything its customer wanted — yet the customer
could still undo the project.

The case's **lessons learned**:

1. The new Lean (Six Sigma) approach to commercialisation was a complete success.
2. It opened up the use of complex statistical and mathematical modelling in product development.
3. Existing processes were challenged and, when identified as non-value-added, removed.
4. No matter how good your commercialisation process, **poor risk management by your customers** can lead to project
   failure.

The host lecturer tied this to the unit's distinction: coming up with the product is the **creativity** part;
bringing it to market is the **innovation** part, and every component — marketing, legal, HR, culture and strategy —
has to come together.

Exam use: when evaluating an innovation, separate the quality of the development process from the risks in the wider
chain (customers, partners, legal) that the innovator does not control.
