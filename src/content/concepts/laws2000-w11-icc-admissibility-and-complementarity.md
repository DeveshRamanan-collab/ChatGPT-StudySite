Jurisdiction is not enough: the case must also be admissible. The ICC is "complementary to national criminal
jurisdictions" (art 1), so national courts come first.

Under art 17 the Court shall determine that a case is inadmissible where:
(a) it is being investigated or prosecuted by a state which has jurisdiction over it, unless the state is unwilling or
unable genuinely to carry out the investigation or prosecution;
(b) such a state has investigated it and decided not to prosecute, unless that decision resulted from unwillingness
or inability genuinely to prosecute;
(c) the person has already been tried for the conduct, and a trial by the Court is not permitted under art 20(3); or
(d) it is not of sufficient gravity to justify further action by the Court.

Unwillingness: having regard to the principles of due process recognised by international law, the Court considers
whether the proceedings or decision were for the purpose of shielding the person from criminal responsibility;
whether there has been unjustified delay inconsistent with an intent to bring the person to justice; and whether the
proceedings were not conducted independently or impartially, in a manner inconsistent with that intent.

Inability: whether, due to a total or substantial collapse or unavailability of its national judicial system, the
state is unable to obtain the accused or the necessary evidence and testimony, or otherwise unable to carry out its
proceedings.

Exam use: after jurisdiction, ask whether a state with jurisdiction is acting, and genuinely; then gravity.
