The goal of **divergent thinking** is to generate many different ideas about a topic in a short period of time. It
involves breaking a topic down into its component parts to gain insight into its various aspects, and it typically
happens in a spontaneous, free-flowing manner, so ideas arrive in a random, unorganised fashion (Week 3 creativity
exercises).

The exercises train it:

- **Uses for a paper clip** in 60 seconds.
- **Think in threes**: A = usual solution, B = different solutions, C = radical solutions.
- **Asking new questions**: two minutes listing the existing questions, then at least five new and varied ones — no
  solutions.
- **Place two unrelated things together** to find a new opportunity.
- **Who are your future competitors?** — current and future, direct and indirect; the usual answer is only the
  current, direct one.
- **Speed thinking**: idea ping-pong, a mind map at speed, as many ideas as possible in 120 seconds, 120 seconds on why
  the ideas will NOT work, and nine different perspectives on one problem.

The reading's process sets the rules: when generating, produce as many ideas as possible, log the session, do not judge
or debate ideas, and hold one conversation at a time; when choosing, set aside emotion and ownership of ideas and avoid
consensus thinking. Deloitte's summary: diverging, before converging.

Exam use: pick the exercise that fits a stuck team, and keep generating separate from judging.
