Nickless (2017) shows a firm tackling disruption head on. Sam Nickless joined Gilbert + Tobin (G+T) as chief operating officer in 2015 to future-proof the firm.

**The threat.** NewLaw disruptors such as the start-up LegalVision focused on lower-value, higher-volume transactional work (wills, loan documents, first-round document reviews), done faster and cheaper with standard processes and digital automation. G+T's focus was bespoke, high-value work. But disruption tends to **move up the market** from the lower-value work towards the higher value, where the bulk of G+T's business lay.

**The options.** Change the business model; retreat into a specialist niche; develop online platforms to automate simple activities; outsource (perhaps to lower-cost labour overseas); or partner with a disruptor. G+T considered launching its own version, but getting the incentives right to motivate people and create the right culture would be very hard alongside a traditional legal model.

**The choice.** G+T bought a 20 per cent stake in LegalVision for about A$4 million: exposure to a high-growth market and the ability to learn from disruption as it arrived. Due diligence included mystery shoppers and a review by G+T's top lawyers. The partnership opened the underserviced small-to-medium enterprise market and lets G+T craft combined solutions, sometimes taking a smaller share of the work.
