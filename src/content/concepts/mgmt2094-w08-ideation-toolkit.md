According to Mattimore (2012) there are seven tools:
1. **Questioning assumptions** — frame the challenge, list 20–30 assumptions (true or false), use several as thought
   starters. Selling more women's razors: what if we targeted teenage girls with an inexpensive razor?
2. **Opportunity redefinition** — pick the three most interesting words of the opportunity statement, generate 8–10
   alternatives for each (who, what, where, when, how), and combine them: "How do we sell more insurance to
   Catholics?" becomes "How do we get practising young Catholics incentivised to sell life insurance to Catholic
   grandparents?"
3. **Wishing** — wish for the impossible (20–30 wishes), then use the most fantastic as stimuli for novel but
   realistic ideas; maybe Uber came from wishing for more convenient transport.
4. **Triggered brainwalking** — ensures everyone contributes and combines with other techniques, like vanilla ice
   cream with toppings.
5. **Semantic intuition** — combine categories of key words to name a new idea before knowing what it is: improve +
   teamwork → "upgrade information exchange" → upgrade information software.
6. **Picture prompts** — visuals surface intuitions, emotions and feelings (good for personnel and management
   challenges); customise them, and add some random pictures.
7. **Worst idea** — list really terrible ideas, then turn them into good ones by thinking of the opposite or finding
   something of value; it takes the creative pressure off.

**Exam use:** name the tool from its mechanism — assumptions listed, three words recombined, keywords merged into a
name.
