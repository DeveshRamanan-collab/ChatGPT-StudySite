Creative problem solving moves through six stages, alternating **divergent thinking** (coming up with many
possible solutions to the one problem) and **convergent thinking** (solving a problem with only one single correct
solution):

1. **Objective finding** — defines the problem area. Divergence lists problems; convergence picks the most relevant,
   using hits and hotspots found by questioning ownership (is one motivated to solve it?), priority (how important?)
   and critical nature (how urgent?).
2. **Fact finding** — gather the information. It increases comprehension, helps new ideas, and may show the problems
   from a new perspective.
3. **Problem finding** — uses the previous stage's hits to define the problem correctly: the most productive problem
   definition possible.
4. **Idea finding** — mainly divergent: many ideas, using a variety of idea generation aids.
5. **Solution finding** — the choice of ideas that can be transformed into workable solutions.
6. **Acceptance finding** — a divergent activity to implement solutions successfully: list obstacles and ways to
   overcome them, develop preventive actions and contingency plans, generate an action plan.

The process is intuitive and natural (it builds on our creative processes rather than replacing them), research
based, generalisable to all aspects of life, and a higher order thinking skill with sub-skills. The slides also list
five conditions of creative thinking: receptivity, immersion, seeing questions, utilisation of errors and detached
devotion.

Exam use: name the stage and say whether the move is divergent or convergent.
