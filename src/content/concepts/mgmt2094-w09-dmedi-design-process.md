Dow Corning's **Six Sigma Design Process** ran in five steps, each ending in 'execute':

1. **Define** — understand the purpose; develop a charter, a multi-generational product/service plan and a project plan
   to manage the project (tools: charter, Gantt chart, in-and-out-of-scope tool, communications plan).
2. **Measure** — research customers; capture the **voice of the customer**; translate needs into
   **critical-to-customer requirements (CCRs)** using **Quality Functional Deployment (QFD)**; benchmark competitor
   performance; set target sigma performance.
3. **Explore** — translate CCRs into functions; use creative techniques to develop alternative concepts; evaluate them
   (eg with a Pugh matrix) and select the **best-fit concept**; assess the risk of the high-level design.
4. **Develop** — detailed designs, capability assessments, FMEA, **design of experiments (DOE)**, process control plans
   and pilot plans.
5. **Implement** — execute the pilot and validation plans; make the **scale-up decision**; transition to process
   owners; document lessons learned; reward and recognise.

Running alongside every step: change management, customer alignment, strategy alignment, project management, project
case development and process management.

The guest lecturer stressed the market-research focus: the aim was a product the market wanted, made in the most
efficient way possible.

Exam use: place a scenario in its step — customer needs and CCRs → Measure; generating and choosing concepts → Explore;
experiments and pilots → Develop; scale-up and hand-over → Implement.
