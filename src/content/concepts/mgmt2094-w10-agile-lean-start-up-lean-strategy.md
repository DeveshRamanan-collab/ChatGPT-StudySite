The tutor slides close Week 10 by joining its three approaches: they **complement one another** to help
organisations innovate more effectively. Each answers a different question.

- **Agile** — how do we organise teams to develop and improve innovations? Organise work into short, iterative cycles
  (sprints), enable collaboration and quick adaptation, and continuously improve the product.
- **Lean start-up** — how do we test whether our innovation creates value for customers? Test assumptions with real
  users, learn from feedback and adapt, and develop solutions that create value for customers.
- **Lean strategy** — how do we ensure experimentation remains aligned with strategic direction? Set direction, scope
  and guardrails for experimentation, prioritise the right opportunities and allocate resources effectively.

The tutorial's UniConnect challenge (a teaching case: a university-backed start-up building a platform for students)
practises three levels of decision-making: **managing tasks** (prioritise work based on value and capacity),
**managing change** (test assumptions and adapt based on evidence) and **managing barriers** (remove organisational
barriers and enable cross-functional collaboration).

Can an organisation be agile and still fail to innovate? The materials say yes. A financial services company's agile
pilot built an app customers praised, yet annual funding and waterfall testing and integration meant the time to
release improved very little. For Collis, entrepreneurship without strategy leads to chaos.
