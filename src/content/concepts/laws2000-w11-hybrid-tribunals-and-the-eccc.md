Alongside the ad hoc tribunals and the ICC sit other international(ised) criminal tribunals: the Special Court for
Sierra Leone, the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (ECCC), the Special Tribunal for Lebanon, the
Special Panels for Serious Crimes, the Iraqi High Tribunal and the Kosovo Specialist Chambers. The ECCC is the model
the slides explain in full.

Establishment: to try individuals responsible for serious crimes under the Khmer Rouge regime from 1975 to 1979. After
a request by Cambodia to the UN, it was established through an international agreement in 2004, following lengthy
negotiations. It sat within the Cambodian court structure but received international assistance through the United
Nations Assistance to the Khmer Rouge Trials (UNAKRT).

Jurisdiction: crimes committed between 17 April 1975 and 6 January 1979 — genocide under the 1948 Genocide
Convention, crimes against humanity as per the Rome Statute, grave breaches of the Geneva Conventions, and crimes under
Cambodian law — against senior leaders of Democratic Kampuchea and those most responsible.

Distinctive features: part of the Cambodian judiciary, applying national and international law; two Co-Prosecutors,
one national and one international; a majority of national judges in all Chambers; a structure more akin to civil
law, reflecting Cambodia's legal system, so Co-Investigating Judges, not Prosecutors, investigated; and civil parties
had a right to participate.

Exam use: contrast the ECCC — built by agreement with the state and sitting in its courts — with the ICTY and ICTR,
created by the Security Council under Chapter VII.
