Applicable law (art 21). The Court shall apply:
1. in the first place, the Statute, the Elements of Crimes and its Rules of Procedure and Evidence;
2. in the second place, where appropriate, applicable treaties and the principles and rules of international law,
   including the established principles of the international law of armed conflict;
3. failing that, general principles of law derived from national laws of the world's legal systems (as appropriate,
   those of states that would normally exercise jurisdiction), if consistent with the Statute and international law.

The Court may apply principles and rules of law as interpreted in its previous decisions. Application and
interpretation must be consistent with internationally recognised human rights, without adverse distinction on
grounds such as gender, age, race, religion or other status.

Cooperation. States Parties shall "cooperate fully with the Court in its investigation and prosecution of crimes"
(art 86) and ensure procedures are available under their national law for all forms of cooperation (art 88). A
non-party that accepts jurisdiction by declaration under art 12(3) must cooperate "without any delay or exception" in
accordance with Part 9. A non-party that is a UN member can also be bound through the Security Council: members
"agree to accept and carry out the decisions of the Security Council" (art 25 UN Charter), including decisions taken
under Chapter VII.

Exam use: for cooperation, ask whether the state is a Party, a declarant, or bound by a Chapter VII decision.
