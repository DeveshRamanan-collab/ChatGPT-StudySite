The diplomatic (non-adjudicatory) modes leave the outcome in the parties' hands: none of them produces a binding
result.

1. **Negotiation** — the parties retain control without third parties; the most common mode and usually the first
   step. GA Resolution 53/101 (1998): good faith, a constructive atmosphere, and refraining from conduct that might
   undermine the negotiations. It presupposes "a genuine attempt … to engage in discussions … with a view to
   resolving the dispute" (*North Sea Continental Shelf*, para 85).
2. **Good offices** — a neutral third party trusted by both sides offers to facilitate efforts to enter into direct
   negotiations (e.g. the UN good offices mission in Lebanon).
3. **Mediation** — the third party takes a more direct role and suggests a framework of settlement or possible
   solutions (Richard Holbrooke, Bosnian War); the solutions are not binding.
4. **Inquiry or fact-finding** — when parties disagree over the facts; usually a non-binding report (UN Commission
   of Inquiry in Syria; the Joint Investigation Team on the 2014 shooting down of a Malaysian Airlines plane over
   Ukraine, which held Russia responsible).
5. **Conciliation** — a third party (e.g. a group of experts) reports on the facts and the parties' positions and
   suggests a solution, with non-binding recommendations (Timor-Leste and Australia, Timor Sea boundary, under
   UNCLOS).

The modes are often combined — e.g. mediation with fact-finding.

Exam use: identify the mode by the third party's role — none, facilitating, proposing, finding facts, or reporting
recommendations — and remember that none of them binds.
