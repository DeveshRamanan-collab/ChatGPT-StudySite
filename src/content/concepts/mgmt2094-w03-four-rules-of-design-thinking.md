In *Design Thinking: Understand, Improve, Apply* (2011), Christoph Meinel and Larry Leifer write that there are four
rules to design thinking (quoted in the reading):

1. **The Human Rule: all design activity is ultimately social in nature.** Successful innovation brings us back to the
   'human-centric point of view': solve technical problems in ways that satisfy human needs, and acknowledge the human
   element in all technologists and managers.
2. **The Ambiguity Rule: design thinkers must preserve ambiguity.** There is no chance for 'chance discovery' if the
   box is closed tightly, the constraints enumerated excessively and the fear of failure is always at hand; innovation
   demands experimentation at the limits of our knowledge.
3. **The Redesign Rule: all design is redesign.** The human needs we seek to satisfy have been with us for millennia;
   because technology and social circumstances change, understand how these needs were addressed in the past, then
   apply 'foresight tools and methods' to estimate conditions five, 10 or even 20 years ahead.
4. **The Tangibility Rule: making ideas tangible always facilitates communication.** One of their most recent
   findings: 'prototypes are communication media'.

The rules are principles that hold throughout; the five stages are the sequence of work.

Exam use: use a rule to justify a practice — the human rule for customer research, the ambiguity rule for not closing
the brief too early, the redesign rule for studying past solutions, the tangibility rule for prototyping.
