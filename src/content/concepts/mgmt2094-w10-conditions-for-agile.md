Agile is not a panacea. It is most effective and easiest to implement under conditions common in software
innovation: the problem is complex; solutions are initially unknown and requirements will probably change; the work
can be modularised; close collaboration with end users is feasible; and creative teams will typically outperform
command-and-control groups.

The reading's exhibit sets favourable against unfavourable conditions:

- **Market environment**: preferences and solution options change frequently — or conditions are stable and
  predictable.
- **Customer involvement**: close collaboration and rapid feedback are feasible — or requirements are clear at the
  outset and customers are unavailable.
- **Innovation type**: problems complex, solutions unknown, scope unclear, breakthroughs and time to market
  important — or similar work has been done before and plans can be forecast with confidence.
- **Modularity of work**: incremental developments have value and late changes are manageable — or customers cannot
  test parts until everything is complete.
- **Impact of interim mistakes**: they provide valuable learning — or they may be catastrophic.

These conditions exist in many product development, marketing, strategic-planning, supply-chain and resource
allocation activities, and less in routine operations such as plant maintenance, purchasing, sales calls and
accounting. At OpenView Venture Partners agile suited strategic planning and marketing but not selling. Agile also
needs training and a cadre of eager participants, so the payoff must justify the cost.

Exam use: test a scenario against all five rows before recommending agile.
