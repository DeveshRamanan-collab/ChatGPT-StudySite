Art 8: the conduct of a person or group is an act of a state if the person or group "is in fact acting on
the instructions of, or under the direction or control of, that State in carrying out the conduct". The
question came to the fore after 9/11 — could Afghanistan be responsible for Al-Qaeda?

Two situations. **Instructions** must be very specific, relating to the very conduct in breach and to each
operation — not general and vague. **Direction or control** means effective control over each operation;
the two were originally separate but are now assessed together.

- *Nicaragua* (ICJ, 1986): US participation "even if preponderant or decisive" in financing, organising,
  training, supplying and equipping the contras, selecting targets and planning the whole operation was
  insufficient; the US would need "effective control of the military or paramilitary operations in the
  course of which the alleged violations were committed" (para 115). A very high threshold.
- *Tadić* (ICTY Appeals Chamber, 1999): the degree of control may vary; an "overall control" test, less
  demanding, suits areas like international humanitarian and criminal law (para 117).
- *Application of the Genocide Convention* (ICJ, 2007): overall control "stretches too far, almost to
  breaking point" the link between a state's organs and its responsibility; effective control, reflecting
  customary law in art 8, applies.

Exam use: list what the state did (funding, training, targeting), then ask whether it instructed or
effectively controlled the specific operation in which the breach occurred.
