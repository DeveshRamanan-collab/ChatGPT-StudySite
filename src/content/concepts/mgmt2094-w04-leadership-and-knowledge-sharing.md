Carmeli, Gelbard and Reiter-Palmon (2013) ask how leaders improve employees' creativity. **Creativity** is the generation of new ideas that are novel (originality) and useful. **Creative problem solving** has a generation phase (problem identification and construction, information search and acquisition, ideation) and an implementation phase (idea evaluation, idea selection, implementation planning). **Creative problem-solving capacity** is the capability to do these, and it can be cultivated.

**Knowledge sharing** — activities aimed at transferring or disseminating knowledge from one person or group to another — is internal (colleagues inside the organisation) or external (people outside it). **Leader supportive behaviours**: modelling collaboration and knowledge sharing, and encouraging information exchange, openness and idea sharing.

Findings:

- Leader supportive behaviours relate to creative problem-solving capacity directly, and indirectly through internal and external knowledge sharing.
- Creative problem solving relates to both dimensions of creative performance: **fluency** (the number of ideas) and **originality** (ideas mentioned by fewer than 1% of the sample).
- In Study 2 it fully mediated only the link between internal knowledge sharing and originality.

For managers: include knowledge sharing in performance evaluation, train staff on what can be shared, and select and develop leaders who model it — like Robert Buckman of Buckman Laboratories in long online discussions with salespeople.

Exam use: trace the chain from leader to creative output, then note the limits of self-report surveys.
