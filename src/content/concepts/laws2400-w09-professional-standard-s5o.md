For professionals, special skill is taken into account. In *Rogers v Whitaker* (1992) 175 CLR 479 an eye surgeon failed to warn of a complication arising in one
in 14,000 cases. The High Court held the standard "is that of ordinary skilled person exercising and professing to have
that special skill", and rejected the *Bolam* principle — that the standard is set by medical practice.

Section 5O of the Civil Liability Act incorporates a modified version of *Bolam*. A professional does not incur a
liability in negligence arising from a professional service if it is established that they acted in a manner that, at
the time, was widely accepted in Australia by peer professional opinion as competent professional practice — unless the
court considers that opinion irrational (s 5O(2)). Wide acceptance need not be universal (s 5O(4)).

Do we use s 5B, s 5O or both? *Peterkin v Tadros* [2026] NSWSC 1185 [151]–[154]: **s 5O is a defence.**

1. The defendant bears the onus of its preconditions: practising in a profession, in a manner widely accepted in
   Australia by peer professional opinion as competent professional practice.
2. Where s 5O is pleaded, apply it first. If it is made out, it supplants the s 5B analysis: no breach.
3. If the s 5O defence fails, the plaintiff must still satisfy ss 5B and 5C.

Exam use: for a professional defendant, run s 5O first (onus on the defendant), then s 5B only if s 5O fails.
