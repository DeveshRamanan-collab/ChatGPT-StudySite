Fuller's imaginary case (1949): four explorers trapped in a cave for 32 days killed and ate Roger Whetmore on day 23; convicted, they are sentenced to hang. § 12-A: "Whoever shall willfully take the life of another shall be punished by death."

Five options:

1. **Truepenny CJ**: apply § 12-A regardless of the results; clemency can mitigate the rigours of the law.
2. **Foster J (first argument)**: do not apply the law. Positive law is predicated on a human society; *cessante ratione legis, cessat et ipsa lex*; the men were in a state of nature.
3. **Foster J (second argument)**: apply the law in the light of its purpose. "A man may break the letter of the law without breaking the law itself"; by analogy with self-defence, an implicit exception. A question of "intelligent and unintelligent fidelity".
4. **Tatting J**: more than one purpose, another explanation for self-defence, an exception of uncertain scope; he withdraws.
5. **Handy J**: a question of practical wisdom; treat forms and abstract concepts as instruments; common sense and public opinion.

**Keen J** also applies § 12-A, putting aside clemency and moral judgment. The supremacy of the legislature requires plain meaning: the question is "not the conjectural purpose of the rule, but its scope".

Fuller's Postscript: the case brings into focus "divergent philosophies of law and government", live questions of choice that are "among the permanent problems of the human race".
