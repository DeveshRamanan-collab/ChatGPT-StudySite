The blue hat manages the thinking process: control of thinking and the process, instructions for thinking, the organisation of thinking, control of the other hats, discipline and focus. It is the facilitator's, session leader's role. **Begin and end the session with the blue hat.** Kivunja (2015) calls it thinking about thinking, used at the beginning, in the middle and at the end.

Open: *What is our focus? What thinking is needed?* Steer: call the switches and keep people in their hat. Close: what we have achieved, outcome, conclusion, design, solution, next steps.

Using the hats:

- Use any hat, as often as needed; it is not necessary to use every hat.
- The sequence can be preset or evolving.
- Time under each hat: generally, short.
- It requires discipline from each person — while using it, stay in the idiom.
- It can be used by individuals and groups.

General hat issues: **direction, not description** ("Let's have some black hat thinking…"); **not categories of people** (not "He's a black hat thinker" — everyone can and should use all the hats); a constructive form of showing off, by being a better thinker rather than by right-vs-wrong argument; use in whole or in part.

The lecturer: the facilitator need not be the team's manager, and there is no best hat — the problem decides which hats matter most.

Exam use: in a scenario, say who wears the blue hat and what they ask to open and to close.
