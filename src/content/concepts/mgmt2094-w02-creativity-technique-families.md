The lecture sorts creativity techniques into four families:

- **Rational — « serious creativity »**: TRIZ (Genrich Altshuller); lateral thinking, 6 hats (Edward de Bono);
  mindmaps (Tony Buzan).
- **Gaming**: frame games (Thagi), improvisation, innovation games (Luke Hohmann), constructions (LEGOs, blocs…).
- **Idea gathering**: brainstorming, brainwriting, method 635, CPS; idea boxes, idea intranets, participatory
  innovation; crowdsourcing.
- **Irrational**: detour approaches, Synectics (analogical reasoning), random approaches.

Rational approaches stay logical. In TRIZ, each universal law of technological invention solves a contradiction
between physical quantities: identify contradictions, then apply the laws, which provide avenues to explore. Lateral
thinking escapes linear thinking, for example by introducing a random component in reasoning; the six hats
materialise the most common reasoning attitudes.

"Non-rational" techniques move away from the problem. Analog techniques (Synectics, William Gordon) — "making the
familiar strange, making the strange familiar" — use direct, symbolic and fantasy analogies, plus personal
identification with the situation. Detour techniques distance you from the problem (distanciation phase), then
return to converge (ideation phase: "to make it work, it would have to…").

For marketing and sales the lecture picks four: mindmaps (synthesis of information), innovation games (product
roadmap, growth drivers), lateral thinking/6 hats (problem solving, product ideas), brainstorming (product ideas,
names, slogans).
