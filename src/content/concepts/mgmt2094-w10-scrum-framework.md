The tutor slides separate three levels. **Agile** is the principles (collaboration, iterative delivery,
adaptation). **Scrum** is one framework that helps teams organise work into short, structured cycles called
**sprints**. A **sprint** is a fixed-length cycle of one month or less (typically one to four weeks) in which a team
works towards a **Sprint Goal** and produces a **usable increment**. Scrum is one Agile framework; not all Agile teams
use Scrum (Schwaber and Sutherland, 2020).

- **Roles**: the **Product Owner** represents the customer and maximises the value of the product; the **Development
  Team** is cross-functional and self-managing and delivers the work; the **Scrum Master** facilitates the process,
  removes obstacles and helps the team improve.
- **Events**: **Sprint Planning** agrees a Sprint Goal and selects achievable work; during the **Sprint** a 15-minute
  **Daily Scrum** inspects progress and adapts the plan; the **Sprint Review** inspects outcomes and gathers
  stakeholder feedback; the **Sprint Retrospective** reflects on how the team can improve its work.
- **Artifacts**: the **Product Backlog** (prioritised features and work, maintained by the Product Owner), the
  **Sprint Backlog** (work selected at Sprint Planning) and the **Increment** (a usable outcome at the end of the
  sprint).

The team then uses what it has learned to plan the next sprint. GE Digital's leaders run two-week sprints with COO
Brad Surak as initiative owner and an engineering executive as scrum master.

Exam use: name the role, event or artifact a scenario describes; keep the review (the product) apart from the
retrospective (how the team works).
