**The innovation process:** 1 recognising or scanning the environment; 2 aligning the overall
business strategy and the proposed innovation; 3 acquiring technology from outside; 4 generating technology in-house;
5 exploring and selecting the most suitable response to the environment; 6 executing and implementing innovation;
7 learning lessons for improvement; 8 developing the organisation. Steps 3 and 4 are a choice — buy, build or a
hybrid. Lego's materials search (Reed, 2018) — its own lab, about 100 new hires, some 200 materials tested — is
generating in-house, and exploring and selecting.

**Three levels.** Innovation occurs at the national level (government policies and support), the enterprise level
(enterprise policies, support and initiatives) and the individual level (individual and group innovation activities).
A team's solution can rise to the organisational level.

**Innovation management** is learning to find the most appropriate solution to the problem and managing that process
in the ways best suited to the organisation's circumstances; the search for effective routines; and managing the
learning process. Changing routines is hard: people at every level are used to them.

**Organisational process for innovation management:** market learning → building business models → creating a cause,
not a business → an open market for ideas, capital and talent → listening to new voices → unreasonable expectations
and stretching the business definition → partnerships and alliances → lowering the risks of experimentation → paying
the innovators well.

**Exam use:** say which step an organisation is at, and which management lever moves it on.
