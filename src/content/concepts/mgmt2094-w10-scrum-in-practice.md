The reading illustrates agile with scrum, used at least five times as often as other agile techniques.

- **Team**: small (usually three to nine people), mostly full-time, cross-functional (with all the skills it
  needs), self-managing and strictly accountable for every aspect of the work.
- **Initiative owner** (product owner): ultimately responsible for delivering value to customers and the business;
  builds a 'portfolio backlog' of promising opportunities and continually, ruthlessly rank-orders it. The owner does
  not tell the team who should do what or how long tasks will take.
- **Process facilitator** (often a trained scrum master): guides the process, protects the team from distractions
  and helps it put its collective intelligence to work.
- **Sprints**: the team breaks the highest-ranked tasks into small modules, plans in detail only what won't change
  before execution, defines 'done' and builds working versions in short cycles of less than a month.
- **Transparency**: brief daily stand-ups review progress and roadblocks; disagreements are resolved through
  experimentation and feedback, not endless debates or appeals to authority.
- **Test and improve**: small working prototypes are tested with a few customers for short periods; if customers get
  excited, a prototype may be released immediately. The team then brainstorms ways to improve future cycles.

GE Digital ran its leadership team this way: two-week sprints, stand-ups three times a week and a progress board any
employee can see.

Exam use: in a scenario, say who ranks the work, who protects the process and how long a cycle runs.
