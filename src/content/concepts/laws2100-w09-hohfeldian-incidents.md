Wesley Newcomb Hohfeld (1879–1918) identifies four different legal positions within the concept of a "right":

1. **Claim**: X has a claim-right against Y with respect to φ if and only if Y is under a duty to X to φ.
2. **Privilege**: X has a privilege to φ if and only if X has no duty not to φ.
3. **Power**: X has a (normative) power over Y if and only if X has the ability to alter (through volitional action) Y's normative position.
4. **Immunity**: X holds an immunity against Y (in respect of X's normative position) if and only if Y lacks the ability to alter X's Hohfeldian incidents.

Read the definitions closely. Claims and privileges are defined by duties about φ: a claim is someone else's duty owed to me; a privilege is the absence of my own duty not to act. Powers and immunities are defined by the ability to alter normative positions: a power is my ability to change yours; an immunity is your inability to change mine.

So "right" is not one thing. When someone says "I have a right", ask which of the four positions they hold, and against whom.

**Negative and positive rights**: consider the difference between "freedom from" (eg freedom from torture) and "freedom to".

Exam use: translate a rights claim into Hohfeld's terms before you argue about it.
