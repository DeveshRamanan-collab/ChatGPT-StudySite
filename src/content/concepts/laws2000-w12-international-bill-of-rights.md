The International Bill of Rights is three instruments: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR,
10 December 1948), the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) and the International Covenant on
Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), both dated 16 December 1966.

UDHR art 2: everyone is entitled to its rights and freedoms "without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour,
sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status".
Hansa Mehta of India is widely
credited with changing "All men are born free and equal" to "All human beings are born free and equal" in art 1. Its
rights include life, liberty and security of person (art 3), the prohibition on torture (art 5), freedom from
arbitrary arrest and detention (art 9), work (art 23) and education (art 26).

ICCPR: life (art 6), prohibition on torture (art 7) and on slavery (art 8), liberty and security (art 9), rights in
detention (art 10), no imprisonment for breach of contract (art 11), freedom of movement (art 12), rights of aliens
(art 13).

ICESCR: work (art 6), just and favourable conditions of work (art 7), trade unions (art 8), social security (art 9),
protection of the family (art 10), "adequate food, clothing and housing" (art 11), and the "highest attainable
standard of physical and mental health" (art 12).

Exam use: name the instrument and article for each right — the same number means a different right in each
Covenant.
