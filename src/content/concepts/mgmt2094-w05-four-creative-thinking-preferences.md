There are four ways to have a preference for a creative way of thinking:

1. **Clarifier** — asks "are we really solving the right problem?"; likes research and gathering information; attends
   to detail and is methodical; asks lots of questions because they crave information; wants to be quick to move to
   the solution.
2. **Ideator** — looks at the big picture and dreams of possibilities; toys with ideas, stretches the imagination,
   jumps from A to Z; adventurous.
3. **Developer** — takes an idea and tinkers with it, improving on others' ideas; puts together workable solutions and
   analyses competing ones, pros and cons. A perfectionist who can get stuck developing the perfect solution and may
   be seen as a nit-picker.
4. **Implementer** — quick to action, always moving forward; needs to see ideas hit fruition; decisive and
   determined. Can leap into action too soon, appear pushy and oversell their idea.

Understanding your preference can improve your creative performance. In teams, others who think differently should
lead to complementary ideas, solving a problem more effectively by taking into account the different paths to the
solution. The lecture linked this to emotional intelligence: knowing your strengths and weaknesses, and seeing them in
others, lets you match traits in a team.

Example: the Week 5 tutorial's group innovative (video) project — work with your team, create a schedule.

Exam use: identify the preference from behaviour, name its risk, and say which preference balances it.
