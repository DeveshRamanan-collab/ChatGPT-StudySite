Wallas' model sets out the creative process as stages:

1. **Preparation** — focuses on the problem and explores the problem's dimensions.
2. **Incubation** — subconscious mulling of the problem.
3. **Intimation** — an inkling that a solution may well be on its way.
4. **Illumination** — discovery, "eureka": the idea bursts through.
5. **Verification** — focus on the practicality, effectiveness and appropriateness of the idea.

It is a structured approach to creative problem solving: it needs time and a variety of people.

Graham Wallas' *Art of Thought* (1926) is usually summarised as four stages, without Intimation. Sadler-Smith (2015)
argues that Wallas gave Intimation detailed treatment, so a five-stage model is more authentic. The stages sit at
three levels of consciousness: Preparation, Illumination and Verification are conscious; Incubation is nonconscious
and not open to introspection; Intimation sits in the fringe of consciousness — "I often know that the solution is
coming, though I don't know what the solution will be".

Wallas drew the stages from Helmholtz (happy ideas arriving on walks after investigating a problem "in all
directions") and Poincaré, whose ideas could feel certain yet fail "the test of verification". Incubation can be
distraction (other work) or mental relaxation, which suits harder problems.

The lecture stresses Verification for people who generate lots of ideas: an idea that feels like the answer may not
fit the problem.

Exam use: identify the stage from what the person is doing and how conscious it is.
