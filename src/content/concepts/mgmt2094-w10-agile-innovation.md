Agile innovation is a term used to describe a series of methods which originated in the field of software
development. It involves high-intensity team work (often called scrums), which are self-managed; rapid prototyping,
testing and learning; methods more open-ended than traditional project management techniques; and creativity tools
such as design thinking. The slides name **two key roles**: the **team leader** and the **process facilitator**.

The tutor slides define **agile** as an approach to managing complex work through collaboration, iterative
development, feedback and adaptation: prioritise → deliver → review → adapt → repeat. It does not mean working without
a plan; it means planning in shorter cycles.

Rigby, Sutherland and Takeuchi (2016) call agile a radical alternative to command-and-control-style management: it
puts people in self-managed, customer-focused, multidisciplinary teams. Its varieties include **scrum**, **lean
development** (eliminating waste) and **kanban** (reducing lead times and work in process).

Its values come from the 2001 **Agile Manifesto** (Beck et al.), written as an alternative to 'waterfall'
development, where detailed plans are made up front and passed from function to function. In the manifesto's words
(the reading's adaptation in brackets):

- individuals and interactions over processes and tools (people);
- working software over comprehensive documentation (working prototypes);
- customer collaboration over contract negotiation (rigid contracts);
- responding to change over following a plan.
