States must respect the rights of people within their territory and those subject to their jurisdiction
(ICCPR art 2(1)), irrespective of nationality or legal status.

Obligations can also apply outside the state's territory. In the *Wall* Advisory Opinion (ICJ, 2004, para 109) the
Court said that while the jurisdiction of states is primarily territorial, it may sometimes be exercised outside the
national territory; given the ICCPR's object and purpose, states parties should be bound to comply with it even then —
the drafters "did not intend to allow States to escape from their obligations when they exercise jurisdiction outside
their national territory".

In *Armed Activities on the Territory of the Congo (DRC v Uganda)* (ICJ, 2005), Uganda was an occupying Power in Ituri.
Its responsibility was engaged both for acts of its military that violated its obligations and for any lack of
vigilance in preventing violations by other actors in the occupied territory, including rebel groups acting on their own
account (para 179). Human rights instruments apply "in respect of acts done by a State in the exercise of its
jurisdiction outside its own territory", particularly in occupied territories (para 216).

The slides add that obligations may apply even sometimes when the state has no effective control, citing the Human
Rights Committee in *Lopez Burgos v Uruguay* (para 12.3).

Exam use: ask whether the victim was within the state's territory or subject to its jurisdiction — nationality and
place of conduct are not decisive.
