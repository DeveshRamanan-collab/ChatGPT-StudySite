Without creativity we would have no progress and would be forever repeating the same experiences and patterns:
progress requires imagination. Since we are all somewhat creative already, the better question is how to become
**more** creative.

The slides list six methods that can assist us in being creative:

1. **Mind mapping** (Part 1 of the creative project is a mind map)
2. **Brainstorming**
3. **Sketching**
4. **Free writing**
5. **Thinking opposites**
6. **Seeking inspiration**

The lecture calls them an arsenal of tactics or tools, and stresses something even more important: how to unleash
creativity in a **group or an organisation**. Relying on a group of people is much more effective than relying only on
yourself.

Seeking inspiration can mean learning from creative people (business people, but also historical figures, politicians,
artists, even generals), for example by reading their biographies. In the reading, the relationship that most shaped
Bob Iger's thinking was his friendship with Steve Jobs: "Occasionally we would stand in front of a whiteboard and talk
about ideas … We'd just muse on business" (Lev-Ram 2015).
