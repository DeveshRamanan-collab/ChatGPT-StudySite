**Value innovation (VI)** is one of the most prominent programmatic approaches to innovation currently in use. It
is different from building layers of competitive advantage, and does not mean segmenting the market and accommodating
customer needs. Its main focus is offering products and services that create **superior buyer value in existing
markets** and enable a **quantum leap** for the firm to create new markets. It also differs from technology
innovation: new technology is not a value innovation unless it is **cheap enough for mass buyers**.

The lecture's examples were Uber and Airbnb: the market, the cars, the drivers and the internet were already there;
the innovation was rethinking the value.

**"What if" questions.** The status quo stifles imagination; asking "What if" of conventional assumptions provokes
and challenges our thinking. What if newspapers went purely digital — what are the issues, benefits and costs? What-if
questions are merely starting points: the challenge is to find the business model that could make the idea work, and
some will be too provocative to answer.

Answered what-ifs (Osterwalder and Pigneur, 2010):
- **IKEA** — flat packs and DIY furniture assembly in the 1950s.
- **Skype** — free internet voice calls from 2003; after five years, 400 million registered users and 100 billion
  free calls.
- **Grameenphone** — a telephone for every Bangladeshi villager; now the country's largest taxpayer.

**Exam use:** show the existing market, the rethought value, and the business model that answers the what-if.
