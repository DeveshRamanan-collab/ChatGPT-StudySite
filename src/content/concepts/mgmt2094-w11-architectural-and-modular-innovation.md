Innovation can also be divided by what changes inside the product.

- **Architectural innovation** is a change in product structure. The mobile phone becoming the smartphone is the
  slide's example. The lecturer explains that it departs quite a bit from what came before, yet grows out of the
  legacy product: essentially still a phone, but with a changed structure and many more capabilities.
- **Modular innovation** is a change in component technology. The steam engine of a car becoming a fuel engine is the
  example: the product stays the same, but one important component is replaced by a new technology.

The test is the level of change: has the way the product is put together changed (architectural), or has one part
been swapped for a new technology while the overall structure stays the same (modular)?

This is a different cut from the seven types of innovation. Both slide examples change a product, so both are also
product innovations; architectural and modular say what kind of change happened inside it.

Exam use: name the level of change and point to the slide example that matches — the smartphone (structure) or the
car engine (component).
