The hats can be used singly at any point in thinking — in general, this is the major use — as a convenience for directing thinking and for switching thinking. Simple sequences of two or three hats may be used together for a particular purpose.

Sequences on the slides:

- **Yellow, then black** — assess an idea: discover its positive and negative aspects. Yellow comes before black. You could follow up with green (new ideas) and then red (feelings and emotions).
- **Black, then green** — improve a design (the slide's example: an All Weather bike).
- **Blue, green and red** — design sequences: new ideas, products or improvements to existing designs.
- **Red, then white** — compares fact with opinion.
- **Black, then yellow, then green** — comparing and synthesising: coming up with new ideas from the known.
- **White, then blue** — what do we know in facts, and where do we plan to go.

The lecturer's red-then-white illustration: a team fears that shipping goods by air will fail because planes crash (red); the white hat brings the data that crashes are very rare. Sequences are not set in stone: a group can rerun the same problem with a different sequence. Kivunja (2015) agrees that there is no set sequence; it depends on the problem.

Exam use: name the purpose first, then the sequence that serves it, and justify the order.
