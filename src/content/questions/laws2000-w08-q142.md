prompt: The International Court of Justice (ICJ) has no so-called compulsory jurisdiction. Explain whether or not in the view of the ICJ its 'provisional measures' are binding.
model: |-
  Issue: are provisional measures indicated by the ICJ binding on the parties? Law: art 41 ICJ Statute gives the Court
  power "to indicate, if it considers that circumstances so require, any provisional measures which ought to be taken
  to preserve the respective rights of either party". The word "indicate" left room to argue they were merely
  hortatory. In LaGrand (Germany v United States) (2001, para 102) the Court rejected that view. The object and
  purpose of the Statute is to enable the Court to fulfil its functions, in particular the judicial settlement of
  disputes by binding decisions under art 59. Art 41 is to be read in that context: it exists to prevent the Court
  being hampered because the parties' rights are not preserved. So "the power to indicate provisional measures entails
  that such measures should be binding", and the contrary contention "would be contrary to the object and purpose of
  that Article". Application: measures are indicated only where jurisdiction is not manifestly absent, the rights are
  plausible and linked to the measures, and irreparable harm is imminent; once indicated, they bind. Conclusion: in
  the ICJ's view, provisional measures are binding.
