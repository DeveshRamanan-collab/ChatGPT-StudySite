model: |-
  The Council has primary responsibility for international peace and security (art 24) and its decisions bind all
  members (art 25). It first has its Chapter VI tools: it can investigate the situation (art 34), call on the parties to
  settle by peaceful means (art 33(2)) and recommend procedures (art 36(1)). The ABC Union's appeal brings the matter
  before it (any UN member could also do so under art 35).

  To act under Chapter VII, the Council must first determine the existence of a threat to the peace, breach of the
  peace or act of aggression (art 39). Although the fighting began internally, practice treats internal armed conflicts
  and serious human rights or IHL violations as threats, and here refugees are crossing borders and Belandia's and
  Ceria's arms, training and reported troops give the conflict an international dimension.

  Before deciding measures, it may call on the parties to comply with provisional measures — a ceasefire, withdrawal of
  foreign troops — without prejudice to their claims, taking account of any failure to comply (art 40). It then decides
  measures not involving armed force (art 41) and, only if it considers these would be or have proved inadequate,
  action by armed force (art 42). Any decision is subject to the P-5 veto (art 27(3)).
