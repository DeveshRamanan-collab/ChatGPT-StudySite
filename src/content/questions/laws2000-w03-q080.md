  Australia's treaty-making process runs through six steps: a mandate to negotiate from the Foreign Minister;
  negotiation of the text; a National Interest Analysis and Executive Council approval; signature; scrutiny
  by the Joint Standing Committee on Treaties (JSCOT), which reports within 15–20 joint sitting days; and
  entry into force once any implementing legislation passes. The 2021 ENNPIA shows the early steps: its NIA
  explained the agreement was needed because US and UK domestic law restricted disclosing naval nuclear
  propulsion information to Australia without it, required no changes to Australian law, and it entered into
  force by an exchange of diplomatic notes. The much longer-lived 2024 Agreement for Cooperation Related to
  Naval Nuclear Propulsion (in force until 2075) went through full JSCOT scrutiny — Report 224, 260
  submissions, four public hearings — before JSCOT recommended binding treaty action in its Recommendation 1.
  A significant criticism, raised by Professor Donald Rothwell, was that the Agreement's withdrawal
  provisions were asymmetric: only the UK or US can be an "Originating Party" entitled to withdraw based on
  their own defence and security assessment, while Australia's own capacity to withdraw is comparatively
  constrained — a point the Australia Institute also pressed, without JSCOT ultimately recommending against
  the Agreement.
