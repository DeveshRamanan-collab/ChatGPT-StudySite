prompt: Explain the differences between Jus ad bellum and Jus in bello.
model: |-
  Jus ad bellum is the law relating to the resort to force and war: it governs when a state is allowed to go to war
  and use force against another state. Today its source is the UN Charter — the prohibition on the threat or use of
  force in art 2(4), which is also customary international law and jus cogens (Nicaragua, para 34), and the two
  exceptions: Security Council authorisation (art 42) and self-defence (art 51), which requires an armed attack and must
  be necessary, proportionate and reported to the Security Council. It grew out of the just war doctrine (Aquinas's
  auctoritas, causa justa and recta intentio) and the efforts to limit war in the Hague Conventions, the League of
  Nations and the Kellogg-Briand Pact. Jus in bello is the law regulating behaviour during hostilities — what is
  permitted once a war has begun. It is international humanitarian law, the law of armed conflict associated with the
  Geneva Conventions, and this unit leaves it to the Law of Armed Conflict elective. The difference is one of timing
  and question: ad bellum asks whether force may be used at all; in bello asks how hostilities must be conducted. A
  claim of self-defence is an ad bellum question; the treatment of prisoners is an in bello question.
