model: |-
  The issue is whether Ivy's burns are too remote, so that they fall outside the scope of the owner's liability under CLA s 5D(1)(b).

  Remoteness of harm is concerned with the extent of the defendant's liability for losses. The leading case, The Wagon Mound (No 1), makes that extent depend on a reasonable foreseeability test. It differs from duty and breach because the court asks what damage was reasonably foreseeable as a consequence of the negligence, but 'reasonable foreseeability' means the same thing: a real risk, one a reasonable person in the defendant's position would not brush aside as far-fetched (The Wagon Mound (No 2)). The extent of the harm and the precise manner of its occurrence need not be reasonably foreseeable (eg Hughes v Lord Advocate; Jolley v Sutton LBC; Waller v James).

  The kind of harm Ivy suffered — burns — is exactly the kind of harm an uncapped fuel can beside a heater makes a real risk. The owner's arguments go to the manner (a flash explosion rather than an ordinary fire) and the extent (unusually severe burns). Neither needs to be foreseeable once the kind of harm was.

  Conclusion: the burns are not too remote; they fall within the scope of the owner's liability.
