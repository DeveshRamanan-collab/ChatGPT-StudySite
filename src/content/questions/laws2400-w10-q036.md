There must be a connection between the breach and the injury (Adeels Palace v Moubarak; Strong v Woolworths); the flowchart treats the statutory test as the common law 'but for' question: would Jono's injuries have been suffered but for the negligence?

  The answer depends on what Jono would have done had a sign been there, so s 5D(3) applies. It is decided subjectively, in the light of all relevant circumstances. His statement at trial that he would never have dived was made after the harm and is not against his interest, so under s 5D(3)(b) it is inadmissible. The friends' evidence of his past conduct is a relevant circumstance, and it suggests he might have dived despite a sign.

  If the court finds he would have dived anyway, the answer to the 'but for' question is Yes and the statutory question is No: factual causation is not satisfied. If it finds a sign would have stopped him, factual causation is satisfied and scope of liability follows. On this evidence, factual causation looks difficult for Jono to establish.
