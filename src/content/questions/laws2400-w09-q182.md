model: "Breach is measured against the reasonable person, a legal fiction judges use to apply an objective test. It is not completely devoid of particular characteristics, but only some characteristics count. Jai: under McHale v Watson, whose facts are close to these, the standard for a child is that of a normal child of comparable age — here, a normal 12-year-old. But Kitto J said it is no answer to say the harm was due to being abnormally slow-witted, quick-tempered, absent-minded or inexperienced; a child may rely only on limits of foresight or prudence characteristic of humanity at his stage of development. Jai's unusual absent-mindedness is therefore ignored: the question is whether a normal 12-year-old would have thrown a sharpened stick near a friend. Ruth: inexperience does not lower the standard. A learner driver owes the same standard as a licensed driver (Imbree v McNeilly); the standard is objective and impersonal, and varying it with experience would be unworkable. Age is different because childhood is a stage all humanity passes through — the reason Carrier v Bonham refused to adjust the standard for mental illness. Conclusion: Jai is judged as a normal 12-year-old; Ruth as any reasonable driver."
