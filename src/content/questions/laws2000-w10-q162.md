prompt: Explain the role of the General Assembly in the maintenance of international peace and security, including the limit in art 12(1) and the Uniting for Peace procedure.
model: |-
  The General Assembly is the UN's parliamentary body: all 193 member states, one state, one vote (art 18). It is seen
  as the most "democratic" international organ but has a minimal role in enforcement.

  It has a subsidiary responsibility for international peace and security, beside the Security Council's primary
  responsibility (art 24). It may discuss any question relating to peace and security and make recommendations to the
  states concerned, the Council or both, but must refer any question on which action is necessary to the Council
  (art 11(2)). It may recommend measures for the peaceful adjustment of any situation likely to impair friendly
  relations (art 14; Certain Expenses, 1962).

  Both powers are subject to art 12(1): while the Council is exercising its functions on a dispute or situation, the
  Assembly shall not make any recommendation on it unless the Council so requests (Wall Advisory Opinion, paras 27–28).

  Uniting for Peace (GA Res 377(V), 1950) responds to a deadlocked Council: if, because of lack of unanimity of the
  permanent members, the Council fails to exercise its primary responsibility, the Assembly shall consider the matter
  immediately with a view to recommending collective measures. The Cold War expanded the Assembly's role this way, and
  the ICJ confirmed the procedure in Certain Expenses. Even so, the Assembly recommends; only the Council decides.
