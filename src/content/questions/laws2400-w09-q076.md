At the breach stage, the question of reasonable foreseeability of risk involves an examination of the actual conduct of the defendant, and it is a more specific test, which requires the court to consider the actual risk of injury in the circumstances of the case. In NSW the breach-stage question is now framed by s 5B(1)(a) of the Civil Liability Act 2002: the risk must be one of which the person knew or ought to have known. Wyong Shire Council v Shirt remains good authority that foreseeable means not far-fetched or fanciful. McHugh J in Swain described reasonable foreseeability as \"a fact-value complex … with inherent questions of fairness, policy, practicality, proportion, expense, and justice\". That matters because the answer is not a bare prediction: the argument should identify the actual risk on the facts and explain, in evaluative terms, why it was or was not one the defendant ought to have foreseen."
