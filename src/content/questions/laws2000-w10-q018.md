model: |-
  The Security Council has primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security, conferred
  by the members on whose behalf it acts (art 24). It has 15 members — five permanent members with a veto (art 27(3))
  and ten elected for two-year terms. Its decisions bind all members (art 25) and prevail over conflicting treaty
  obligations (art 103). It acts under Chapter VI (peaceful settlement, arts 33–37) and Chapter VII (an art 39
  determination, art 40 provisional measures, art 41 non-forcible measures and art 42 force).

  The General Assembly — all 193 members, one vote each (art 18) — has a subsidiary responsibility: it may discuss and
  recommend (arts 11(2), 14; Certain Expenses), but not on a matter the Council is dealing with unless asked (art 12(1);
  Wall Advisory Opinion). Under Uniting for Peace (Res 377(V)) it may recommend collective measures when the Council is
  deadlocked by the permanent members.

  The Secretary-General, head of the Secretariat, may bring threats to the Council's attention (art 99) and exercises
  good offices. The ICJ settles legal disputes, which the Council should generally leave the parties to refer to it
  (art 36(3)).
