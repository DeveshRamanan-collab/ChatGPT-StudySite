prompt: "Q5: What is a risk described as obvious or inherent? Explain how each bears on breach of duty under the Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW)."
model: An obvious risk is one that, in the circumstances, would have been obvious to a reasonable person in the position of the person harmed (s 5F(1)). It includes patent risks and matters of common knowledge (s 5F(2)). An inherent risk is a risk of something occurring that cannot be avoided by the exercise of reasonable care and skill (s 5I(2)). At common law, obviousness went to the scope of duty (Kirby J in Romeo) and later to breach, especially in recreational activities. Either kind of risk changes how a reasonable person would respond. The Act now bears directly on the issue. A person harmed is presumed aware of an obvious risk unless they prove otherwise (s 5G); there is no proactive duty to warn of an obvious risk unless an exception applies, such as a request for information (s 5H); and there is no liability for the materialisation of an obvious risk of a dangerous recreational activity (s 5L). A person is not liable for harm from the materialisation of an inherent risk (s 5I(1)), though the duty to warn remains (s 5I(3)). Whether a risk is inherent is a question of fact.
