model: |
  ARIO art 2(a) defines an international organisation as one "established by a treaty or other instrument governed
  by international law and possessing its own international legal personality", which may include members other
  than states. The lecture breaks this into four elements: creation by treaty or other instrument; governance by
  international law; its own legal personality; and membership. Almost all IOs are created by treaty (the UN
  Charter), though some come from resolutions (OPEC; the ICTY and ICTR by Security Council resolution). Being
  governed by international law is what distinguishes an IO from an NGO such as Greenpeace or Amnesty
  International, which may be international in focus but is governed by domestic law.

  International legal personality means a personality distinct from the members': the capacity to conclude
  treaties, send and receive delegations, bring international claims and be responsible for wrongdoing. It may be
  conferred explicitly in the constitutive instrument — WTO art VIII, the EU and ASEAN, and the norm over the last
  30 years — or implicitly. The UN Charter is silent (art 104 gives only domestic legal capacity), but in
  Reparation for Injuries (1949) the ICJ found the UN an international person because of the functions it was
  intended to exercise, a principle later applied to other IOs.
