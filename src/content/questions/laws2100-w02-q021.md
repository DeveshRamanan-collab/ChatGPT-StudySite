model: |-
  Austin's account is coherent and simple, but as an account of all law it is too narrow. For Austin, law in general is
  a rule laid down for an intelligent being by an intelligent being having power over him, and laws properly so called
  are a species of commands. A command combines a wish that another shall do or forbear, an evil to be incurred for
  non-compliance, and the expression of the wish; command, duty and sanction are inseparable, and superiority means
  might. Positive law is the command of a political superior to political inferiors. The strongest case for the
  statement is that it captures what is visible about law: enforcement, jails, the power to use force, which Schauer
  calls the overwhelmingly coercive experience of law. Against it, Hart shows that the gunman obliges the clerk without
  the clerk having an obligation, and that power-conferring rules — wills, contracts — are not orders backed by threats.
  On balance the command model describes some duty-imposing rules well, but misses authority and the variety of laws;
  'simply' is the word that fails.
