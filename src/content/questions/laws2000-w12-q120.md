model: |-
  Issue: whether A's ICCPR obligations apply in the occupied province, and whether A can be responsible in relation to
  the armed group's attacks.

  Law: states must respect the rights of people within their territory and those subject to their jurisdiction (ICCPR
  art 2(1)), irrespective of nationality. In the Wall opinion (para 109) the ICJ said jurisdiction is primarily
  territorial but may be exercised abroad, and given the Covenant's object and purpose the drafters did not intend to let
  states escape their obligations when they do. In DRC v Uganda, human rights instruments applied to acts done in the
  exercise of jurisdiction outside a state's territory, particularly in occupied territory (para 216); as occupying Power
  Uganda was responsible for its military's violations and for any lack of vigilance in preventing violations by others,
  including rebel groups acting on their own account (para 179).

  Application: as occupier, A exercises jurisdiction in the province, so the ICCPR applies; the detainees' nationality is
  irrelevant, and detention without charge engages art 9. A need not control the armed group: standing by engages its
  own duty of vigilance.

  Conclusion: both of A's arguments fail.
