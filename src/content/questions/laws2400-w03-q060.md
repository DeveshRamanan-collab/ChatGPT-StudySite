The implied licence recognised in Halliday v Nevill allows any member of the public, including officials, to go up an unobstructed, unlocked driveway with no prohibiting sign, for the purpose of lawful communication with, or delivery to, an occupier. Placing an infringement notice on a car parked in the driveway is analogous to a delivery or communication directed at the occupier, and nothing on the facts suggests the officer’s sole purpose was outside that licence, that the licence had been revoked and the officer remained, or that the officer acted inconsistently with the licence (eg by entering the house). Chen’s personal dislike of the council is irrelevant; the licence is an objective one implied by law from the state of the driveway (unlocked, unobstructed, unmarked), not from the occupier’s subjective attitude. The officer has an implied licence and is not a trespasser.
