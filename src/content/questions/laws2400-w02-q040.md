  During an argument in a share house, D picks up a kitchen knife, points it at P from across the room, and says, "don't push me, or you'll regret it." P is frozen with apprehension. D never moves any closer. Advise whether P has an action in assault against D.
model: |
  Assault requires a positive act, direct interference, an intentional act, reasonable apprehension in the plaintiff of imminent harmful or offensive contact, and no lawful justification. D's act of picking up the knife, pointing it at P and threatening P is a positive, direct and intentional act. The central issue is whether P's apprehension of imminent contact was reasonable, applying an objective test: would a reasonable person in P's position have apprehended the threat? Given D is armed, has verbally threatened P, and P is within range, a reasonable person would likely apprehend imminent harmful contact, even though D never physically moves closer — as Zanker v Vartzokas shows, imminence does not require immediate contact, only that the threat could be carried out without effective means of escape, and being cornered in a room with an armed person satisfies this. P need not prove fear, only apprehension. On these facts D has no apparent lawful justification. P is therefore likely to succeed in an action for assault against D.
