  A financial adviser, knowing her client will rely on her advice to make a major investment decision, carelessly recommends an unsuitable investment, causing the client significant financial loss. Applying Hedley Byrne v Heller and the vulnerability requirement, advise on whether the adviser owed the client a duty of care.
model: |
  This is a claim for pure economic loss — financial loss not consequent on any physical injury to the client or damage to their property — so more than reasonable foreseeability of the loss is required to establish a duty of care (Perre v Apand Pty Ltd). The starting point is the Hedley Byrne v Heller principle, which requires, in addition to foreseeability: a special relationship between the parties; a voluntary assumption of responsibility by the party giving the advice; the other party's reliance on that advice; and the reasonableness of that reliance. Here, the adviser has a professional relationship with the client and has voluntarily undertaken to advise her; she knew the client would rely on the recommendation to make the investment decision, satisfying known reliance; and, absent any disclaimer of responsibility (unlike Heller in Hedley Byrne itself, who disclaimed responsibility for its reference), she has assumed responsibility for the advice. The client's reliance appears reasonable, since seeking a financial adviser's specific recommendation before a major investment is precisely the kind of matter of serious consequence in which reliance on professional skill is expected. Beyond the Hedley Byrne factors, the client must also show vulnerability — an inability to protect herself from the adviser's want of care (Woolcock Street Investments Pty Ltd v CDG Pty Ltd). If the client had no independent means of assessing the investment's suitability and reasonably deferred to the adviser's specific expertise, she is likely to be found vulnerable. On these facts, a duty of care is likely to be owed, and the adviser's careless recommendation would then need to be assessed for breach and causation in the usual way.
