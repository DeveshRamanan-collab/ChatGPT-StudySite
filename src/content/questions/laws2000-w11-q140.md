model: |-
  The issue is whether Varo's proceedings make the case inadmissible. The ICC is "complementary to national criminal
  jurisdictions" (art 1). Under art 17 a case is inadmissible where it is being investigated or prosecuted by a state
  which has jurisdiction over it, "unless the State is unwilling or unable genuinely to carry out the investigation or
  prosecution". Varo has territorial jurisdiction and is prosecuting, so the case is prima facie inadmissible. But
  unwillingness is assessed having regard to the principles of due process recognised by international law, by asking
  whether the proceedings are undertaken to shield the person from criminal responsibility, whether there has been
  unjustified delay inconsistent with an intent to bring him to justice, and whether they are not conducted
  independently or impartially in a manner inconsistent with that intent. Here a three-year unexplained adjournment is
  an unjustified delay, and a presiding judge who is the accused's brother-in-law suggests a lack of independence and
  impartiality; together they support the suspicion of shielding. Inability is not in issue — Varo's courts function.
  War crimes of this kind are likely grave enough. Conclusion: the case is likely admissible on the ground of Varo's
  unwillingness.
