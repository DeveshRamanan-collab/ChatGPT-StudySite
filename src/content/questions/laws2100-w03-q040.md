model: |-
  The account is presented as descriptive, but its key terms carry evaluation, and the essay should say what kind. Hart
  imagines a community with only primary rules and finds three main defects — uncertainty, static character and
  inefficiency — each remedied by supplementing primary rules with secondary rules of a different kind: recognition,
  change and adjudication (CL 94). The union is 'the heart of a legal system' (CL 98). The lecture treats this as a thought
  experiment, not a sociological investigation, and Hart's stated aim is a theory that is morally neutral with no
  justificatory aims (CL 240). The strongest counterargument is that calling uncertainty, stasis and inefficiency
  'defects' presupposes that certainty, adaptability and efficient enforcement are goods a society ought to want, so value
  enters the account at its foundation. In reply, these may be judgments about what a rule system needs to function, not
  moral judgments; Hart does not commend any legal system. On balance the account is not value-free, but it can still be
  morally neutral — which is all Hart claimed, and the distinction Question 1 turns on.
