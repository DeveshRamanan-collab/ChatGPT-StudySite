prompt: Explain the legal significance of the 'existence of a dispute' in the context of the International Court of Justice's jurisdiction.
model: |-
  A dispute must exist on the date proceedings are instituted; if it does not, the ICJ has no jurisdiction to deal with the case. That is why respondents often deny that any dispute exists. Treaty law gives no test, so the cases supply it.

  A dispute is "a disagreement on a point of law or fact, a conflict of legal views or interests" (*Mavrommatis*). Whether one exists "is a matter for objective determination", and "the mere denial of the existence of a dispute does not prove its non-existence" (*Peace Treaties*); otherwise a respondent could escape the Court simply by saying so. The claim of one party must be "positively opposed" by the other, and the respondent must have been aware, or could not have been unaware, of that opposition (*South West Africa*). Mere friction is not enough: there must be communication showing opposed views on a specific issue. The determination "requires an examination of the facts" (*Georgia v Russian Federation*, para 30), such as public statements and diplomatic exchanges. The threshold is low.

  Establish the dispute before turning to consent.
