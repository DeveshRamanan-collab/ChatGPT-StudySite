  **Issue**

  The issues are: (a) whether the ICJ has jurisdiction, and whether the Orun National Council can be a party; (b) whether a dispute existed when Mirovia filed, and whether Mirovia has standing although none of its nationals has been harmed; (c) whether the Court will indicate the provisional measures requested; and (d) the effect of a judgment that Teslan says it will ignore.

  **Rule**

  Only states may be parties in cases before the Court (art 34 ICJ Statute). The Court has no compulsory jurisdiction: it depends on consent, which art 36 allows states to give by special agreement, by a treaty clause, by optional clause declarations under art 36(2), or by forum prorogatum. Art IX of the Genocide Convention provides that disputes "relating to the interpretation, application or fulfilment of the present Convention, including those relating to the responsibility of a State for genocide", "shall be submitted to the International Court of Justice at the request of any of the parties to the dispute". The Court decides disputes about its own jurisdiction (art 36(6)).

  A dispute must exist on the date proceedings are instituted. It is "a disagreement on a point of law or fact, a conflict of legal views or interests" (*Mavrommatis*); its existence "is a matter for objective determination", and "the mere denial of the existence of a dispute does not prove its non-existence" (*Peace Treaties*). One party's claim must be "positively opposed" by the other, and the respondent must have been aware, or could not have been unaware, of it (*South West Africa*). This "requires an examination of the facts", including public statements (*Georgia v Russian Federation*, para 30). The threshold is low.

  The parties "undertake to prevent and to punish" genocide (art I). Genocide covers acts such as killing members of a group, inflicting conditions of life calculated to destroy it, and forcibly transferring its children, committed with intent to destroy a national, ethnical, racial or religious group in whole or in part (art II); "direct and public incitement" is punishable (art III(c)). A non-injured state may invoke responsibility for breach of an obligation owed to the international community as a whole (art 48(1)(b) ARSIWA), such as the prohibition of genocide.

  Provisional measures under art 41 require prima facie jurisdiction, plausible rights, a link between rights and measures, and a real and imminent risk of irreparable harm; they bind (*LaGrand* [102]). A judgment is "final and without appeal" (art 60), binds the parties (art 59), and must be complied with (art 94 UN Charter).

  **Application**

  *(a) Jurisdiction and the Orun National Council.* Neither state has made an optional clause declaration and there is no special agreement, so jurisdiction must rest on a treaty clause. Art IX is such a clause. Both states are parties to the Convention and neither has made a reservation. Mirovia's claim — that Teslan is committing genocide and failing to prevent it — concerns the "application or fulfilment" of the Convention and "the responsibility of a State for genocide". Because art IX allows submission "at the request of any of the parties to the dispute", Mirovia may file unilaterally. The Orun National Council cannot be a co-applicant: it is not a state, and only states may be parties in contentious cases (art 34).

  *(b) Was there a dispute?* Teslan's denial is not decisive, because the question is one for objective determination (*Peace Treaties*). In March Mirovia's Foreign Minister accused Teslan of committing genocide in breach of the Convention, and Teslan's ambassador replied that "no genocide has occurred". That is a conflict of legal views on a specific, identifiable issue, communicated between the parties — not mere friction. Mirovia's claim was positively opposed by Teslan, and Teslan could not have been unaware of the opposition, because its ambassador answered it in the same debate (*South West Africa*). Public statements in a multilateral forum count in the examination of the facts (*Georgia v Russian Federation*). Teslan's silence after the April note does not undo a dispute that already existed, and calling the exchange "political theatre" does not change what was said. The low threshold is met.

  *Standing.* Teslan's second argument confuses jurisdiction with injury. Art IX gives "any of the parties to the dispute" the right to submit it, and nothing in the clause limits that right to states whose nationals are harmed. Under the law of state responsibility, the prohibition of genocide is owed to the international community as a whole, so all states have a legal interest in its protection and Mirovia may invoke Teslan's responsibility as a non-injured state (art 48(1)(b) ARSIWA). The arrival of 40,000 refugees shows Mirovia is affected, but its standing does not depend on that.

  *(c) Provisional measures.* Teslan's third argument fails. The Court need only be satisfied that the absence of jurisdiction is not manifest, and art IX gives a clear prima facie basis. The rights asserted are plausible: UN agency reports describe killings, the destruction of food stores and wells, and the forced transfer of children — acts listed in art II — and the Minister's broadcasts describing the Orun as "vermin to be cleared" are relevant evidence of intent and may themselves be direct and public incitement (art III(c)). Each measure is linked to those rights: measure (i) protects the core obligation to prevent genocide (art I); measure (ii) targets incitement; and measure (iii) preserves the evidence the Court will need to decide the merits. Urgency is clear: the killings and transfers are continuing, and loss of life is the clearest example of irreparable harm. The Court is likely to indicate measures of this kind, and they will bind Teslan (*LaGrand*), whose contrary view would defeat the object and purpose of art 41.

  *(d) Teslan's threat to ignore the judgment.* A judgment is final and without appeal (art 60) and binds Teslan as a party (art 59), which as a UN member must comply (art 94(1)). If it does not, Mirovia may go to the Security Council, which may make recommendations or decide on measures to give effect to the judgment (art 94(2)). That route is political — a veto could block it — but non-compliance would itself engage Teslan's responsibility, since failing to comply with an ICJ decision breaches an international obligation.

  **Conclusion**

  The Court has prima facie, and probably full, jurisdiction under art IX of the Genocide Convention, a treaty route under art 36. The Orun National Council cannot be a party (art 34). A dispute existed when Mirovia filed: Teslan's General Assembly reply shows positively opposed views of which it was aware, and its later silence and denial do not change that (*Peace Treaties*; *South West Africa*). Mirovia has standing under art IX and as a state invoking an obligation owed to the international community as a whole (art 48(1)(b)). The Court is likely to indicate binding provisional measures to prevent genocide, stop incitement and preserve evidence (*LaGrand*). A final judgment will bind Teslan, and non-compliance can be taken to the Security Council (art 94).
