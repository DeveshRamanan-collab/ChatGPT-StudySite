  Pelora and Oriande are neighbouring island states in the Serin Sea. Three territorial disputes have arisen between them.

  Ashkar. In 2022 an undersea volcanic eruption created a new island, Ashkar, nine nautical miles off Pelora's coast, within Pelora's territorial sea. Ashkar is about two square kilometres in area and remains above water at high tide. In 2024 Oriande landed a detachment of marines on Ashkar, raised its flag and declared the island Oriandan territory "by effective occupation of land belonging to no state". Pelora protested immediately and has protested ever since. Oriandan coast guard vessels now patrol the waters around Ashkar and last month arrested the crew of a Peloran fishing boat there, taking them to Oriande for trial.

  Tallis Island. Under an 1890 treaty, a colonial power ceded Tallis Island to Pelora. Since the 1950s, however, Oriande has peacefully administered Tallis: it runs the island's school, post office and police station, and its residents vote in Oriandan elections. Pelora made no protest until 2020, when it first asserted that Tallis was Peloran.

  Reef K. Reef K is a coral formation 150 nautical miles from Oriande, within Oriande's exclusive economic zone, that is submerged at high tide. Oriande has piled sand and concrete onto it to build a permanent airstrip and a port, and now claims a 12-nautical-mile territorial sea around it.

  Last month, aboard an Oriandan-registered supply ship moored at Reef K's port, Leo Park, an Australian engineer employed by an Oriandan contractor, killed a Peloran worker in a fight. Oriande, Australia and Pelora have each announced an intention to prosecute Mr Park.

  Advise Pelora on its territorial claims to Ashkar and Tallis, the status of Reef K, the lawfulness of Oriande's arrest of the Peloran crew, and which states may exercise jurisdiction over Mr Park.
model: |
  **Issue**

  The issues are: (1) who has sovereignty over Ashkar; (2) whether Oriande has acquired Tallis Island by prescription; (3) whether Reef K can ground sovereignty and a territorial sea; (4) whether Oriande's arrest of the Peloran crew was lawful; and (5) which states may exercise jurisdiction over Mr Park.

  **Rule**

  Territory may be acquired in five ways. Cession is a transfer from one state to another, often by treaty; the acquirer gets no more rights than the ceding state had. Accretion is new land formed gradually by nature, as with a volcanic island formed in Japan's territorial sea in 1986. An island is "a naturally formed area of land, surrounded by water, which is above water at high tide" (UNCLOS art 121(1)). Occupation of terra nullius requires effective control and an intention to claim title over land never under any state's sovereignty; it is barely applicable today. Prescription gives title over another state's territory through peaceful control, acting as sovereign, on implied consent; sustained protest prevents it. Conquest has been prohibited since the UN Charter (art 2(4)); occupation by force triggers the belligerent occupation regime, which is temporary and transfers no sovereignty.

  Artificial islands "do not possess the status of islands", have "no territorial sea of their own" (UNCLOS art 60(8)), and, like low-tide elevations, cannot ground sovereignty (*South China Sea Arbitration*).

  On jurisdiction, *Lotus* allows states a wide discretion to prescribe their laws beyond their territory but prohibits enforcement "in any form in the territory of another State" absent a permissive rule. Grounds for prescriptive jurisdiction include territoriality, active nationality (with ships taking the nationality of their state of registration; *Nottebohm* describes nationality as a genuine connection), and passive personality, which is controversial and subsidiary (*US v Yunis*). Where jurisdiction concurs, there is no universal rule of priority; usually the state with the closest link is best placed.

  **Application**

  *(1) Ashkar.* Ashkar is naturally formed and above water at high tide, so it is an island under art 121(1). It was formed within Pelora's territorial sea. Like the volcanic island formed in Japan's territorial sea, it accrues to the state in whose territory it forms: Pelora acquired it by accretion. Oriande's claim of "effective occupation of land belonging to no state" fails at the first step, because Ashkar was never terra nullius — it was Peloran from the moment it formed. In any event, Oriande's method was a landing of armed marines on Peloran territory. Conquest is prohibited under art 2(4) of the UN Charter, and occupation by force cannot transfer sovereignty; at most it engages the belligerent occupation regime. Pelora's immediate and continuing protest also rules out any later argument of prescription. Ashkar is Peloran.

  *(2) Tallis Island.* Pelora's title derives from the 1890 cession. But Oriande has peacefully administered Tallis since the 1950s, running its school, post office and police and including its residents in Oriandan elections: that is peaceful control, acting as sovereign. Prescription turns on implied consent, and sustained protest prevents it. Pelora made no protest for about 70 years, which strongly suggests acquiescence. Its 2020 protest came too late to prevent the implied consent already shown. Oriande therefore has a strong argument that it has acquired Tallis by prescription. Pelora's best counter-argument is that the materials set no fixed period, so it can argue that the facts do not show Pelora knew of and accepted Oriande's conduct as sovereign; but on these facts Pelora's claim to Tallis is weak.

  *(3) Reef K.* Reef K is submerged at high tide, so it is not an island under art 121(1); it is at most a low-tide elevation. Oriande's sand and concrete make it an artificial island, which under art 60(8) has no territorial sea of its own. Following the *South China Sea Arbitration*, neither a low-tide elevation nor an artificial island can ground a sovereignty claim. Oriande may have rights in its exclusive economic zone to construct and regulate artificial islands (art 60(1)), but its claim to a 12-nautical-mile territorial sea around Reef K is unfounded.

  *(4) The arrest of the Peloran crew.* Because Ashkar and its surrounding waters lie within Pelora's territorial sea, the arrest was carried out in Peloran territory. Arresting persons is enforcement jurisdiction, the most intrusive form. Under *Lotus*, absent a permissive rule, Oriande "may not exercise its power in any form in the territory of another State". No permissive rule is suggested. The arrest was unlawful, and, like Israel's seizure of Eichmann in Argentina, it would found a claim for the crew's return and compensation.

  *(5) Jurisdiction over Mr Park.* The killing occurred aboard an Oriandan-registered supply ship. Ships take the nationality of their state of registration, so Oriande has jurisdiction on a nationality basis in respect of the ship, akin to territoriality. Oriande cannot rely on Reef K as its territory, since Reef K cannot ground sovereignty. Australia has active nationality jurisdiction, because Mr Park is an Australian national; the facts suggest a genuine connection. Pelora has passive personality jurisdiction, because the victim was Peloran, but that ground is controversial and better treated as subsidiary (*US v Yunis*). All three claims are concurrent and none has automatic priority. Oriande, as the flag state with custody of the scene and likely of the evidence, has the closest link, but the states should consult. Whichever state prosecutes, enforcement must respect *Lotus*: no state may arrest Mr Park on another's territory without permission.

  **Conclusion**

  Ashkar is Peloran by accretion; it was never terra nullius, and Oriande's forcible landing cannot transfer title under art 2(4). Oriande has a strong claim to Tallis by prescription, because Pelora acquiesced for some 70 years before protesting. Reef K is at most a low-tide elevation turned artificial island; it generates no territorial sea and cannot ground sovereignty (*South China Sea Arbitration*; UNCLOS arts 60(8) and 121(1)). Oriande's arrest of the Peloran crew in Pelora's territorial sea was unlawful enforcement jurisdiction (*Lotus*). Oriande (flag state), Australia (active nationality) and Pelora (passive personality, a weaker and subsidiary ground) may each assert prescriptive jurisdiction over Mr Park, with Oriande having the closest link.
