  The issue is how Kai recovers where two tortfeasors are jointly and severally liable, one of them insolvent.

  Where multiple tortfeasors are jointly and severally liable for damage, the plaintiff may choose to recover the whole amount against only one of them — for example, the one with the "deeper pockets" (Tort Law Principles [12.170]). The footnoted qualification for proportionate liability concerns claims for property damage and economic loss; Kai's claim is for personal injury, so it does not apply.

  Kai should therefore sue Lift Co, which is solvent, for the whole amount. Frame's liquidation does not reduce what Kai can recover.

  Between the companies, contribution is the statutory mechanism by which a tortfeasor held liable for the full amount may seek a contribution against another; in NSW the textbook cites the Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1946 (NSW) s 5(1)(c) and s 5(2). Lift Co may seek a contribution against Frame. Contribution in no way affects Kai, and because Frame cannot pay, the risk of its insolvency lies with Lift Co, not Kai.

  Conclusion: Kai recovers in full from Lift Co; Lift Co bears the loss of any contribution Frame cannot pay.
