model: |
  False imprisonment requires a positive act, direct interference, an intentional or negligent act, and total restraint of the plaintiff's liberty without lawful justification. The bouncer's positive act of standing in the doorway and telling P to wait directly restrains P's liberty; this is intentional conduct. The restraint is total because standing in the only doorway leaves P with no reasonable means of escape from the office — there is no suggestion of another exit or a safe alternative route. The central issue is lawful justification. Unlike Balmain New Ferry v Robertson, where a condition attached to entry gave a lawful excuse, here P did not enter the office on any accepted condition; the bouncer's belief P used a fake ID, though honestly held, was mistaken, and a reasonable but mistaken belief in a right to detain does not itself provide a lawful justification. Because all elements are satisfied and no true lawful excuse exists, P is likely to succeed in an action for false imprisonment against the club, which will typically be vicariously liable for its bouncer's conduct.
