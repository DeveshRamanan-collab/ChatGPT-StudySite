He checks the bolts as carefully as he knows how, but misses a loose bolt that a reasonable organiser would have found. A performer is hurt when a panel gives way. model: "The issue is whether Jordan's conduct fell short of the standard of care. The court measures the defendant's conduct against a standard of care; if it falls short — whether far short or just short — there is a negligent breach. The standard is objective: the court asks how a reasonable person would respond to a foreseeable injury, so a defendant may be in breach even though he did his personal best, if that best was not reasonable. Jordan's first argument therefore fails: the comparison is with a reasonable organiser, who would have found the loose bolt. The second is half right. The duty is only a duty to take reasonable care, not an absolute duty to avoid injury; the measure is reasonableness, not elimination of risk (Swain v Waverley Municipal Council). So remaining risk does not itself show breach — but nor does it excuse a failure to take reasonable care. Under Blyth v Birmingham Waterworks Co, negligence includes the omission to do what the reasonable man would do; failing to find a loose bolt that a reasonable organiser would have found is such an omission. Conclusion: Jordan likely fell below the standard of care."
