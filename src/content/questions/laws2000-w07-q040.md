  Article 2 ARSIWA defines an internationally wrongful act as conduct "consisting of an act or omission" that is
  attributable to a state and breaches an international obligation owed by it. A breach concerns a primary rule
  irrespective of its source (art 12): custom or treaty, it does not matter, but it must be international, not
  domestic. The state must also be bound at the time of the conduct (arts 13–14; Chagos), so there is no
  retroactivity. Omissions show the point clearly. In Tehran, protesters stormed the US embassy and took hostages;
  under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations the host state must protect the embassy, and Iran's failure
  to protect the embassy and staff was an internationally wrongful act. In Corfu Channel (1949), British warships
  struck mines in Albanian waters and 45 sailors died. Who laid the mines was conjecture, but Albania's attitude
  and the visibility of the minelaying from its coast showed it knew; its obligations were to notify shipping and
  warn the approaching warships, and its failure to do so was the breach. In both, the state answered for its own
  omission, not for the private actors' conduct.
