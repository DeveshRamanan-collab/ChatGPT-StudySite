prompt: Explain the TWO constitutive elements required to establish the existence of a rule of customary international law (CIL).
model: |-
  A rule of customary international law requires two elements, and each must be shown separately.

  The objective element is state practice: a general practice among states — consistent repetition of a particular behaviour over a period of time when states are confronted with the same facts. Practice need not be perfect. In *North Sea Continental Shelf* the ICJ required practice, including that of specially affected states, to be "extensive and virtually uniform", even over a short period. In *Nicaragua* it was enough that conduct was "in general" consistent with the rule, with departures treated as breaches rather than as a new rule. In the *Asylum Case* there was "so much uncertainty and contradiction" that no constant and uniform usage existed.

  The subjective element is opinio juris sive necessitatis: states follow the practice because they believe it is legally obligatory, not out of courtesy, convenience or tradition. *North Sea* rejected practice explained by mere "ceremonial and protocol".

  A widespread practice without a belief of legal obligation is not custom, and nor is a stated belief backed by inconsistent practice.
