prompt: Explain how the Security Council has used the three findings in art 39 in practice, and why "threat to the peace" has become its main route into Chapter VII.
model: |-
  Art 39 of the Charter of the United Nations requires the Security Council to "determine the existence of any threat
  to the peace, breach of the peace, or act of aggression" before it recommends or decides measures under arts 41 and
  42. Any one finding opens Chapter VII; without one, the Council can act only under Chapter VI.

  The three findings are used very unequally. The Council has never, or only very rarely, found an act of aggression.
  It has found a breach of the peace on a very few occasions, each involving an invasion of territory, such as Iraq's
  invasion of Kuwait in 1990. The categories overlap, since an invasion can be both.

  The threat to the peace is by far the most common finding because it is flexible and the Council's to make. Its
  reach has grown from traditional inter-state invasion to non-traditional threats: proliferation of weapons of mass
  destruction, terrorism, internal armed conflicts, serious human rights or IHL violations and the 2014 Ebola outbreak.
  During the Cold War, US–Russian tension made such determinations hard to agree; the Council has been far more active
  since, though the P-5 veto remains a limit. In a problem, argue for a threat to the peace and support it with the
  closest example from practice.
