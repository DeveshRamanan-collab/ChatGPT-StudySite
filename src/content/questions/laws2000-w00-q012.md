prompt: |
  In 2018 Sarenia and Tovald signed a Maritime Boundary Treaty dividing the continental shelf between their coasts. Sarenia's Minister for Energy signed after producing full powers issued by Sarenia's Foreign Minister; the treaty entered into force on signature, as its final clause provided. Annex A, a map prepared by Tovald's national survey office, plotted the boundary line and showed the Zaran gas field lying wholly on Sarenia's side. The Sarenian delegation had told Tovald throughout the negotiations that Sarenia would sign only if Zaran fell within its area.

  In 2023 new surveys revealed that Tovald's map was wrong: plotted correctly, the agreed coordinates in the treaty text place about 60 per cent of Zaran on Tovald's side. Sarenia now argues that the treaty is invalid. It also argues that its Energy Minister lacked authority because a 1962 internal regulation of the Sarenian Senate, never published outside Sarenia and never mentioned in the negotiations, requires Senate approval of boundary treaties. Finally, it has heard rumours that Tovald's state oil company paid for the Sarenian chief negotiator's daughter to study abroad in 2017, but has no documentary evidence.

  Meanwhile, at a televised press conference in 2020, Tovald's President — responding to Sarenian concerns about the field — declared: "Tovald solemnly undertakes that it will not drill in the Zaran field until any question about the boundary has been finally resolved between our two nations." In 2025 TovaldOil, a state-owned company empowered by Tovald's Petroleum Act to grant drilling licences on the state's behalf, licensed itself and began drilling in the part of Zaran that falls on Tovald's side of the treaty coordinates. It has already extracted gas worth USD 300 million. Tovald says the President's statement was "political, not legal", and that in any event TovaldOil is a company, not the state.

  Advise Sarenia on whether the Maritime Boundary Treaty is valid, whether Tovald is bound by its President's statement, and whether Tovald has incurred international responsibility and, if so, what Sarenia can claim.
model: |
  **Issue**

  The issues are: (1) whether the treaty is invalid for error; (2) whether Sarenia can rely on its Senate regulation; (3) whether corruption of its negotiator is a ground of invalidity on the facts; (4) whether the President's 2020 statement binds Tovald; and (5) whether TovaldOil's drilling engages Tovald's responsibility, and with what consequences.

  **Rule**

  A treaty properly concluded may still be invalid. The VCLT separates relative invalidity — error (art 48), a material factual mistake such as an erroneous map, important to the decision to join; fraud (art 49); and corruption of a representative (art 50), where a non-due advantage induces a representative to sign who otherwise would not have — from absolute invalidity, including coercion (arts 51–52) and conflict with jus cogens (art 53). A violation of internal law on competence to conclude treaties cannot be invoked "unless the violation was manifest and concerned a rule of its internal law of fundamental importance" (art 46; *Cameroon v Nigeria*). Under art 7 a person producing full powers represents the state.

  A unilateral statement can create binding obligations: a state may evidence "a clear intention to accept obligations vis-à-vis certain other states by a public declaration which is not an offer or otherwise dependent on reciprocal undertakings" (Crawford). In *Eastern Greenland*, Norway's foreign minister's promise bound Norway; in the *Nuclear Tests Cases*, French government statements of an intention to cease atmospheric testing were a legal undertaking.

  Under ARSIWA, a breach of an international obligation from any source (art 12) attributable to a state (art 2) is wrongful. Conduct of a non-organ empowered by the state's law to exercise elements of governmental authority, acting in that capacity, is attributable (art 5). The responsible state must cease and offer assurances of non-repetition (art 30) and make full reparation (art 31), which must wipe out the consequences of the illegal act (*Factory at Chorzów*), through restitution, compensation or satisfaction (arts 35–37).

  **Application**

  *(1) Error.* The mistake concerns a fact — where the agreed coordinates fall relative to the Zaran field — and derives from Tovald's own map in Annex A. It was material: Sarenia told Tovald throughout the negotiations that it would sign only if Zaran fell within its area, so the belief was important to its decision to be bound. This is the paradigm case of an erroneous map under art 48. Error makes the treaty voidable at Sarenia's instance (relative invalidity), rather than void ab initio. Tovald may reply that the treaty text's coordinates, not the map, are what the parties agreed, but that is exactly why the error matters: Sarenia consented to coordinates on the mistaken basis the map created. Sarenia has a strong argument that it may invoke error to invalidate its consent.

  *(2) The Senate regulation.* Sarenia's Energy Minister produced full powers from the Foreign Minister, so under art 7 he represented Sarenia. Sarenia's argument that the Senate regulation was breached falls under art 46. The regulation may concern a matter of importance, but the violation was not manifest: it is a 1962 internal regulation never published outside Sarenia and never mentioned in the negotiations. Nothing would have made it objectively evident to Tovald acting in good faith. *Cameroon v Nigeria* protects the other party's reliance in exactly these circumstances. This ground fails.

  *(3) Corruption.* Payment for the negotiator's daughter's studies by Tovald's state oil company could be a non-due advantage. But art 50 requires that the advantage induced the representative to sign when he otherwise would not have. Sarenia has only rumours and no documentary evidence, and its own delegation's position — that it would sign only if Zaran fell on its side — suggests that the negotiator's decision was driven by the map, not by any benefit. Sarenia should investigate, but on present facts corruption cannot be established.

  *(4) The President's statement.* The statement was made publicly, at a televised press conference, by Tovald's head of state. It was expressed as a solemn undertaking — "Tovald solemnly undertakes" — and was not an offer or dependent on anything Sarenia did in return. That shows a clear intention to accept an obligation towards Sarenia. As in the *Nuclear Tests Cases*, public statements of intention by the highest officials can amount to a legal undertaking, and as in *Eastern Greenland*, a senior official's promise concerning another state's claim can bind the state. Tovald's description of the statement as "political, not legal" is contradicted by its terms. Tovald is bound not to drill in Zaran until the boundary question is finally resolved — and, given Sarenia's error claim, it has not been.

  *(5) Responsibility.* TovaldOil is state-owned, but ownership alone does not make it an organ. It is, however, empowered by Tovald's Petroleum Act to grant drilling licences on the state's behalf — an element of governmental authority — and it was acting in that capacity when it licensed itself to drill. Its conduct is therefore attributable to Tovald under art 5, and Tovald's argument that it is "a company, not the state" fails. Drilling in Zaran breaches the obligation arising from the President's unilateral undertaking; under art 12 it does not matter that the obligation arises from a unilateral statement rather than a treaty. The obligation was in force from 2020, before the drilling began in 2025. The drilling is a breach even on Tovald's side of the treaty coordinates, because the undertaking applied to the whole field until the boundary question was resolved.

  *Consequences.* Tovald must cease drilling and, given its denial that it is bound, offer assurances of non-repetition (art 30). It must make full reparation (art 31). Restitution — halting operations and, where possible, restoring the position — comes first (art 35). The gas already extracted cannot be returned in kind, so compensation for the USD 300 million of gas taken is appropriate (art 36), subject to how the boundary is ultimately resolved, since that will determine how much of the field Sarenia was entitled to. A declaration of wrongfulness could provide satisfaction (art 37).

  **Conclusion**

  Sarenia can probably invoke error under art 48 to invalidate its consent to the treaty, because Tovald's erroneous map concerned a fact that was, to Tovald's knowledge, essential to Sarenia's decision to sign. Its Senate-regulation argument fails because the violation was not manifest (art 46; *Cameroon v Nigeria*), and corruption under art 50 cannot be established on rumour. The President's public, solemn undertaking binds Tovald (*Nuclear Tests*; *Eastern Greenland*). TovaldOil's drilling is attributable to Tovald under art 5 and breaches that undertaking. Sarenia can demand cessation, assurances of non-repetition and compensation for the gas extracted, with the final amount depending on the resolution of the boundary.
