model: "At common law, Rogers v Whitaker set the professional standard as that of the ordinary skilled person exercising and professing to have that special skill, rejecting the Bolam principle. Section 5O now incorporates a modified Bolam principle, and Peterkin v Tadros explains how it works with s 5B. Section 5O operates as a defence — a complete answer to the claim — so Dr Hale bears the onus of establishing its preconditions: that she was practising in a profession, and that she acted in a manner that, at the time, was widely accepted in Australia by peer professional opinion as competent professional practice. Because she pleads it, the court applies s 5O first. The patient's expert disagreeing is no obstacle: opinion need only be widely, not universally, accepted (s 5O(4)). The opinion cannot be relied on, however, if the court considers it irrational (s 5O(2)). If s 5O is made out, it supplants the s 5B analysis and there is no breach (Gould). If it fails, the patient must still establish breach under ss 5B and 5C. Conclusion: likely no breach if the GPs' evidence shows wide acceptance and is rational."
