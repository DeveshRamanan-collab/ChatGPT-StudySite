prompt: A gym leaves a heavy weight plate balanced on top of a high shelf. Kai, a member, climbs on a bench to reach a towel — something the gym's rules forbid — knocks the shelf, and the plate falls on his foot. The gym argues that (1) it never knew the plate was there, though staff walk past the shelf every day; (2) no one could have predicted the plate would fall in this way; and (3) it need not foresee members breaking the rules. Advise on s 5B(1)(a) of the Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW).
model: "The issue is whether the risk was foreseeable under s 5B(1)(a): a risk of which the gym knew or ought to have known. At the breach stage this is a more specific test than at the duty stage: the court examines the gym's actual conduct and the actual risk of injury in the circumstances. (1) Actual knowledge is not required. Staff walked past the shelf every day, so the gym ought to have known that a heavy plate was balanced there. (2) Kai need not show that the precise manner of his injury, or the particular injury, was reasonably foreseeable — only that the gym created a reasonably foreseeable risk of injury of some kind to someone such as him. A heavy plate balanced high above members creates a risk of falling-object injury that is not far-fetched or fanciful (Wyong Shire Council v Shirt). (3) A defendant must foresee and guard against inadvertent, negligent, or even extremely foolhardy conduct by the plaintiff, even though it is unlikely (Nagle v Rottnest Island Board; McLean v Tedman). Climbing on a bench against the rules is such conduct. Conclusion: s 5B(1)(a) is likely satisfied; (b) and (c) must still be addressed."
