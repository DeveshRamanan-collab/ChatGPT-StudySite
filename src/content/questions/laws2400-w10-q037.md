model: |-
  The issue is whether the GP's failure to refer was a necessary condition of Mia's harm (CLA s 5D(1)(a)), that is, whether there is a connection between the breach and her injury (Adeels Palace v Moubarak; Strong v Woolworths).

  At common law the question is whether Mia's injuries would have been suffered but for the negligence; under the Act, whether the fault was a necessary condition of the harm. A 'No' to the first and a 'Yes' to the second both mean causation is satisfied.

  On the experts' evidence, a referral leading to a scan would have prevented the harm. But the chain depends on what Mia would have done if referred, so s 5D(3) applies: it is decided subjectively, in the light of all relevant circumstances. Her statement at trial that she would certainly have gone was made after the harm and is not against her interest, so it is inadmissible under s 5D(3)(b). The court must weigh the other circumstances, including her dislike of hospitals.

  Conclusion: if the court finds Mia would have gone for the scan, factual causation is satisfied and scope of liability follows; if not, it fails.
