model: |-
  Art 25 of the Charter of the United Nations binds members to "accept and carry out the decisions of the Security
  Council". The binding force attaches to decisions only. A resolution can contain both recommendations and decisions,
  and often mixes them; which is which usually turns on the wording of each paragraph, chiefly the verbs, though
  borderline wording is debated. The Charter draws the same line: under Chapter VI the Council recommends (art 36(1)),
  and under art 39, once it has determined a threat to the peace, breach of the peace or act of aggression, it "shall
  make recommendations, or decide" measures under arts 41 and 42.

  The distinction therefore decides whether a state is legally obliged to act. A state may decline a recommendation; it
  must carry out a decision, whether or not it voted for it or sits on the Council.

  A binding decision is a Charter obligation, so art 103 applies: where Charter obligations conflict with obligations
  under any other international agreement, the Charter obligations prevail. A state bound by a trade or arms treaty
  with the target of Council sanctions must still implement the sanctions; the treaty cannot be relied on to avoid them.
