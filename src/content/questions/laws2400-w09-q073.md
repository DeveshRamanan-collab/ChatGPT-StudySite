model: "The issue is whether Lena breached her duty of care. Her conduct is measured against an objective standard of reasonable care: doing her best is no answer if it was not reasonable, and any shortfall, far or just, is a breach. Under the Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) s 5B, two questions arise. First, the risk. Under s 5B(1)(a) the risk must be foreseeable — one she knew or ought to have known of. A loose cord across a busy doorway creates a risk of tripping injury to customers that is not far-fetched or fanciful (Wyong Shire Council v Shirt); Sam need not show that the precise manner of his fall, or his particular injury, was foreseeable. Under s 5B(1)(b) it is not insignificant — a higher bar than not far-fetched (Ipp Report): a loose cord in a busy doorway is obvious, likely to be tripped on, and can cause real injury (Rail Corporation NSW v Donald). Second, the response. Under s 5B(1)(c), would a reasonable café owner in Lena's position have taken precautions? Considering s 5B(2): a trip in a busy doorway is not improbable; a fall can cause injury such as a broken wrist; taping the whole cord or rerouting it is a slight burden; and serving coffee does not depend on leaving the cord loose. Conclusion: Lena likely breached her duty."
