model: |-
  The issue is which routes Tilda has for her pure economic loss caused by misstatements. Against Coastline, the
  easier route is Australian Consumer Law s 18: "A person must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is
  misleading or deceptive or is likely to mislead or deceive." A dealer's sales statement is plainly in trade or
  commerce. Section 18 covers words as well as conduct, and negligent as well as intentional statements. Tilda need
  not establish a duty of care or prove negligence — only that the conduct was misleading or deceptive, plus a causal
  link (the loss arose "because of" it), which is nowhere near as demanding as at common law. She can also plead

  remains relevant. But a duty is unlikely: Lord Reid in Hedley Byrne said there can be no duty for opinions on social
  or informal occasions; Ross did not assume responsibility, and reliance on a casual comment is hardly reasonable,
  even with his past experience. Conclusion: Tilda should sue Coastline under s 18; a claim against Ross would very
  likely fail.
