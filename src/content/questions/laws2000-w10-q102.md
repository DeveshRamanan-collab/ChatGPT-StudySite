model: |-
  Once it determines a threat to the peace (art 39), the Council may decide on measures binding all members (art 25).

  Without force (art 41): the list is non-exhaustive. The Council could impose an arms embargo on Aeystan's factions
  and on Belandia and Ceria, whose supply of arms and training feeds the conflict. Because comprehensive sanctions have
  been criticised for their impact on populations (Iraq, 1990s) — a real concern where civilians are already suffering —
  targeted measures are preferable: travel bans and asset freezes on faction leaders and on the officials directing the
  support, with details set by a sanctions committee. It could also create an ad hoc tribunal (as with the ICTY and
  ICTR) or refer the situation to the ICC Prosecutor under Rome Statute art 13(b), as for Darfur.

  With force (art 42): if it considers these measures would be or have proved inadequate, it may authorise force — an
  exception to art 2(4) and a last resort. As art 43 was never implemented, it would authorise member states, or the ABC
  Union under art 53, to use "all necessary means" (Res 678), e.g. to secure humanitarian relief (Somalia, Res 794) or
  protect civilians (Libya, Res 1973). Alternatively it could deploy a UN peacekeeping operation, traditionally needing
  the parties' consent. Any measure may be blocked by a P-5 veto.
