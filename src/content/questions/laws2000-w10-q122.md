  Issue: whether the 2003 invasion was authorised by the Security Council under Chapter VII.

  Law: force is prohibited by art 2(4); art 42 is an exception, a last resort, under which the Council may act when art 41
  measures would be or have proved inadequate. Because art 43 was never implemented, the Council authorises states —
  as in Res 678 (1990), which authorised members co-operating with Kuwait "to use all necessary means".

  The argument: the US letter of 20 March 2003 claimed authority under Res 678 and 687. Res 687's disarmament
  obligations were conditions of the ceasefire; a material breach "removes the basis of the ceasefire and revives the
  authority to use force under resolution 678". Res 1441 (2002) found Iraq in material breach, gave it a "final
  opportunity" and recalled warnings of "serious consequences".

  Evaluation: Res 1441 contained no express authorisation; the coalition read authority into broad language — a revived
  or implied authorisation. Most other Council and UN members and the majority of scholars rejected this, and the UK's
  Chilcot inquiry found that not all peaceful options had been exhausted, undercutting the last-resort logic.

  Conclusion: the better view is that the invasion lacked Council authorisation.
