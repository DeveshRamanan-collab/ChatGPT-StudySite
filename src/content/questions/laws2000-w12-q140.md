model: |-
  Issue: whether each measure falls within ICCPR art 4.

  Law: art 4(1) permits derogation in a public emergency threatening the life of the nation and officially proclaimed,
  only "to the extent strictly required by the exigencies of the situation", consistently with other international
  obligations, and without discrimination solely on the ground of race, colour, sex, language, religion or social origin.
  Art 4(2) bars derogation from arts 6, 7, 8(1)–(2), 11, 15, 16 and 18. The four substantive requirements are necessity
  and proportionality (nature, duration, impact), consistency with other obligations (especially jus cogens), no effect
  on absolute or non-derogable rights, and notification. In Aksoy v Turkey, fourteen days without judicial intervention
  was "exceptionally long" and not shown to be necessary.

  Application: the threshold, proclamation and notification are met. (a) Thirty days without a judge is more than double
  the period rejected in Aksoy; unless V gives detailed reasons, it is not strictly required. (b) Beating suspects breaches
  art 7, which is non-derogable and jus cogens. (c) Detaining one religious minority is discrimination solely on the
  ground of religion, which art 4(1) forbids.

  Conclusion: none of the three measures is a valid derogation.
