model: |-
  Issue: whether a legal dispute existed between A and B at the date A instituted proceedings; if not, the Court has
  no jurisdiction. Law: a legal dispute is "a disagreement on a point of law or fact, a conflict of legal views or
  interests between persons" (Mavrommatis, PCIJ 1924). Whether one exists "is a matter for objective determination",
  and B's "mere denial of the existence of a dispute does not prove its non-existence" (Peace Treaties, 1950). The
  parties must "hold clearly opposite views": A's claim must be "positively opposed" by B, and B must have been aware,
  or could not have been unaware, of that opposition (South West Africa, 1962). It needs more than friction, and some
  communication (Marshall Islands v UK, 2016); the threshold is low. The determination "requires an examination of the
  facts" (Georgia v Russian Federation, 2011, para 30): public statements, diplomatic notes and the parties' conduct.
  Application: if A protested the pollution to B and B rejected the claim before A filed, the views were positively
  opposed and B knew it. Conclusion: a dispute existed and B's objection fails; if A never communicated its claim
  before filing, the objection would likely succeed.
