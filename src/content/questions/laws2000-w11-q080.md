prompt: Which States were mandated to cooperate with the ad hoc international criminal tribunals? Why?
model: |-
  The issue is the reach of the duty to cooperate with the ICTY and ICTR, and its source. All states were mandated to
  cooperate. Resolution 827, which established the ICTY, decided that "all States shall cooperate fully with the
  International Tribunal and its organs" and that "all States shall take any measures necessary under their domestic
  law" to implement the resolution and the Statute, "including the obligation of States to comply with requests for
  assistance or orders issued by a Trial Chamber under Article 29 of the Statute". The ICTR was likewise established by
  the Security Council under Chapter VII. Why: the Council acted under Chapter VII of the UN Charter, "Action with respect
  to Threats to the Peace, Breaches of the Peace, and Acts of Aggression", and under art 25 of the Charter "the Members of
  the United Nations agree to accept and carry out the decisions of the Security Council". So the duty did not depend on
  any state's consent to the tribunals. That contrasts with the ICC, a treaty court, where the general obligation to
  cooperate in art 86 binds States Parties. Conclusion: all states, because of the binding force of Chapter VII
  decisions under art 25.
