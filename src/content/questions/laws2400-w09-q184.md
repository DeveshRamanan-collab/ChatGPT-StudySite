model: "Section 5B(1)(b) is the second of three cumulative elements: a person is not negligent in failing to take precautions unless the risk was not insignificant. It reflects the probability of the risk materialising, and may not be satisfied where the probability of injury was very low. It is a deliberate change to the common law. Under Wyong Shire Council v Shirt a risk that was not far-fetched or fanciful was real and therefore foreseeable; the Act modified the probability of harm to \"not insignificant\". The Ipp Report explains the double negative: it indicates a risk of a higher probability than \"not far fetched and fanciful\", but not so high as \"a substantial risk\". So the threshold is still low, but slightly more demanding than the common law. In assessing significance, Rail Corporation NSW v Donald points to factors such as obviousness, likelihood of occurrence and seriousness of consequence, weighed together — a rare risk may still be not insignificant if it is obvious and grave. Benic stresses that (b) must be addressed separately from (a), and probability returns in s 5B(2)(a) as one factor in deciding what precautions were reasonable."
