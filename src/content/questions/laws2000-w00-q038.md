prompt: Distinguish between the UN Security Council's non-forcible measures under Article 41 and forcible measures under Article 42 of the UN Charter.
model: |-
  Both are Chapter VII measures: each follows an art 39 determination of a threat to the peace, breach of the peace or act of aggression, and each binds members as a Council decision (art 25).

  Art 41 measures are those "not involving the use of armed force". The list — interruption of economic relations and communications, severance of diplomatic relations — is non-exhaustive, giving the Council wide scope: comprehensive sanctions (Iraq in the 1990s; North Korea under Res 2397), targeted or "smart" sanctions such as asset freezes, travel bans and commodity bans, ad hoc criminal tribunals (the ICTY and ICTR), and referral of a situation to the ICC Prosecutor (Rome Statute art 13(b); Darfur, 2005).

  Art 42 measures involve force. If the Council considers that art 41 measures "would be inadequate or have proved to be inadequate", it "may take such action by air, sea, or land forces as may be necessary". Force is the last resort. Because art 43 was never implemented, the Council authorises member states, coalitions or regional organisations, typically to use "all necessary means" (Res 678, Kuwait 1990).

  Art 41 comes first; art 42 follows only if non-forcible measures are, or would be, inadequate.
