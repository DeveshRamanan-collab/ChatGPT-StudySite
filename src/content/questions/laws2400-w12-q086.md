model: |-
  The issue is whether Summit can establish volenti non fit injuria, which operates as a complete defence to
  negligence. The essence of the defence is the plaintiff's willingness to assume a risk she knew existed in a particular
  activity. Summit must show that Mel not only perceived the existence of the risk, but fully appreciated its nature and
  extent and voluntarily accepted that specific risk. The defence is narrowly confined to the specific risk and does not
  extend to an awareness of the general nature of the risk. Mel must have fully appreciated the nature and likely
  manifestation of the risk (Banovic v Perkovic; Carey v Lake Macquarie City Council), and effective knowledge extends
  beyond the mere existence of a risk to an acceptance of its materialisation (Carey; ICI v Shatwell). The specific risk
  that materialised was failure of an uninspected belay device. Mel's statement shows only awareness of the general
  nature of the risk of falling in climbing; nothing shows she knew the inspection had been missed, let alone accepted
  that the device might fail. Conclusion: the volenti defence is likely to fail. Summit's better argument, if any facts
  support it, would be contributory negligence, which only reduces damages.
