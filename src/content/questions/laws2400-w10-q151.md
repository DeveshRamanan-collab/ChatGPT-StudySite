Instead of putting her legs in plaster, which would have been the usual treatment given the nature of Lucy's fractures, and which would have probably allowed Lucy's legs to heal without any residual disability, Dr Sun was anxious to try a new technique of manipulation he had developed. This technique had never been used before in Australia. The technique was unsuccessful as it left Lucy with a permanent ankle disability in both legs. This so restricted her ability to engage in kickboxing that she gave up the sport. She started drinking heavily, which caused her to lose her job as an air traffic controller. Assuming the Hospital is vicariously liable for the negligence of Dr Sun, and that Dr Sun breached his duty, advise Lucy on causation."
model: |-
  Causation has two separate and distinct elements (CLA s 5D(1); Adeels Palace v Moubarak).

  Factual causation (s 5D(1)(a)): was Dr Sun's negligence a necessary condition of the permanent ankle disability — would she have suffered it but for his technique? Plaster "would have probably allowed" her legs to heal without residual disability. That is proof on the balance of probabilities (s 5E): more probable than not, the disability would not have occurred with proper treatment. Unlike Barnett, where timely treatment would not have saved the deceased, proper care here would probably have avoided the harm; and unlike Tabet v Gett, Lucy does not rely on a mere possibility or lost chance. Factual causation of the disability is likely established, and giving up kickboxing follows directly from it.

  Scope of liability (s 5D(1)(b)) is a separate, normative question: is it appropriate for liability to extend to each harm? For the disability, yes. The heavy drinking and loss of her job are harder: they follow Lucy's own later conduct, which may be argued to be a novus actus interveniens. In McKew the plaintiff's unreasonable conduct broke the chain; but not every later act does (March v Stramare). Under s 5D(4) the court considers whether and why responsibility should be imposed (Topic 11).

  Lucy is likely to recover for the disability and the loss of kickboxing; the drinking and job loss are arguable.
