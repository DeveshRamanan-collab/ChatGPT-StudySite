model: |-
  *Corfu Channel* shows that a state can be responsible for an omission — and so for harm it did not directly cause — where it fails to act on knowledge of a danger in its territory.

  In October 1946 two British warships struck mines in Albanian waters; 45 sailors died and 42 were wounded. Who laid the mines was never established. But the Albanian government's attitude, and the fact that the minelaying would have been visible from the Albanian coast, led the ICJ to conclude that Albania knew of the minefield.

  Albania's obligations consisted in notifying shipping of the minefield and warning the approaching British warships of the imminent danger. The Court also referred to every state's obligation "not to allow knowingly its territory to be used for acts contrary to the rights of other States". Albania's failure to warn breached those obligations.

  Under art 2 ARSIWA a wrongful act may be an act or an omission. Albania's own authorities failed to warn, so the omission was Albania's; it did not matter that others laid the mines. The "knowingly allow" duty is a question of due diligence.
