model: |-
  The issue is whether Jin is liable for the full extent of Kit's harm — the chronic condition and the loss of her career — or only for what an ordinary person would suffer from a bruised shoulder.

  Remoteness asks whether the kind of harm was reasonably foreseeable (The Wagon Mound (No 1)); the extent of the harm need not be. The egg-shell skull rule then applies: the defendant must take the plaintiff with all his weaknesses, beliefs and reactions as well as his capacities and attributes, physical, social and economic (Nader v Urban Transit Authority of New South Wales; see Kavanagh v Akhtar).

  Physical injury to a cyclist knocked off a bicycle is plainly a foreseeable kind of harm, so liability is established. Kit's undiagnosed shoulder weakness is a physical weakness Jin must take as he finds it, whether or not he knew of it. Her career as a professional cellist and her high earnings are capacities and attributes, social and economic, which the rule also says Jin takes as found. Jin's argument that he could not foresee the severity goes to extent, which need not be reasonably foreseeable.

  Conclusion: Jin is liable for the full extent of Kit's harm, including the chronic condition and her lost earnings.
