model: |-
  The issue is whether Cal's arson is a new intervening act (novus actus interveniens) that takes the loss outside the scope of Ari's liability under CLA s 5D(1)(b). 'But for' causation is arguably established — the truck was at the yard only because of the collision — but a later event has affected the situation Ari's negligence created, so the court must decide whether it breaks the chain of causation.

  The Oropesa asks whether the subsequent event is ultroneous, something unwarrantable, a new cause which disturbs the sequence of events, or something unreasonable, extraneous or extrinsic. To sever the connection, the intervening act must ordinarily be voluntary human action — conduct which is volitional — or a causally independent coincidence which is extremely unlikely (Haber v Walker).

  Cal's act is deliberate, planned and motivated by a grudge unconnected with the collision. It is volitional human action of the kind the flowchart treats as severing the chain (Rickards v Lothian; Yates v Jones; CES v Superclinics), and an extraneous new cause in The Oropesa's sense. Section 5D(4) supports this: there is little reason why Ari should bear responsibility for a stranger's deliberate crime.

  Conclusion: Cal's arson very likely breaks the chain, so the loss of the truck falls outside the scope of Ari's liability.
