model: |-
  The issue is whether Apex owes Nadia, a subsequent owner, a duty of care to avoid pure economic loss caused by a
  latent building defect. The defect reduces the townhouse's value without injuring anyone, so the loss is PEL. At
  common law, Bryan v Maloney held a builder owed a subsequent purchaser of a house a duty to take reasonable care in
  building it, using the proximity principle and distinguishing dwellings from other buildings; Woolcock Street
  Investments v CDG found no duty to a commercial purchaser with no reliance, no assumption of responsibility and no
  vulnerability. Nadia, a homeowner, is closer to Mrs Maloney. But in NSW she need not rely on the common law. Under the
  Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 s 37, a person who carries out construction work has a duty to exercise
  reasonable care to avoid economic loss caused by defects arising from that work, owed to each owner and each
  subsequent owner, with damages as if the duty were a common law duty. Apex's subcontracting does not help it: the
  duty is non-delegable (s 39). Nor does its contract clause: the duty cannot be contracted out (s 40). Apex carried out
  construction work, and the defect arose from it. Conclusion: Nadia has a strong claim against Apex under s 37,
  without needing to establish a duty at common law; breach and causation would then need to be proved.
