model: |
  Consent to be bound (arts 11–17 VCLT) is a state's own act — usually, for Australia, signature or (more
  often, given its dualist system) ratification under art 14. Entry into force (art 24) is when the treaty
  becomes legally operative "in such manner and upon such date as it may provide or as the negotiating States
  may agree" — a separate, later event that can depend on conditions like a minimum number of ratifications.
  UNCLOS shows why the gap matters: opened for ratification in 1982 with around 100 signatures, it did not
  enter into force until 1994, because art 306 required 12 months after the 60th ratification, not reached
  until then. A state that ratified UNCLOS in the 1980s was not bound by all its provisions during that gap —
  only by art 18's minimal duty to refrain from acts defeating the treaty's object and purpose. Article 25
  provisional application is different again: it lets a treaty (or part of it) apply in full before entry
  into force, if the treaty itself so provides or the negotiating states separately agree — a stronger,
  opt-in status than art 18's automatic floor. Getting these three concepts right matters because a problem
  question might place a state at any of these three stages, each carrying a different level of obligation.
