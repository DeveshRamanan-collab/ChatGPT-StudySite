model: "The issue is whether the Centre owed a duty in relation to the conduct of third parties — the escapees. Such a duty is only found in certain circumstances, usually a pre-existing relationship or a situation of control; there is a general reluctance to make defendants protect others from third parties' criminal acts (Modbury Triangle Shopping Centre v Anzil). Home Office v Dorset Yacht is directly in point: boys escaped from Home Office detention and damaged yachts nearby, and a duty was owed because damage on escape was reasonably foreseeable and the officers had control over the boys. Here the Centre controlled the detainees, and damage to boats at the jetty opposite the island that afternoon is foreseeable and within the immediate vicinity of the escape, so a duty is owed to the boat owners. Unlike Modbury, the Centre is not merely failing to prevent a stranger's crime: it controls the third parties. The householders are different. Dorset Yacht limited the duty to damage in the vicinity immediately after escape; for policy reasons — indeterminate liability — no duty existed for further damage while the escapees remained at large. Burglaries 50 km away two weeks later fall outside that scope. Conclusion: a duty to the boat owners; very likely none to the householders."
