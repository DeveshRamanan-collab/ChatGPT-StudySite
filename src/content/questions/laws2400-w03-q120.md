model: Farah is likely to succeed in private nuisance against Dev. She has title to sue as the owner of the house, unlike her son, a mere guest with no proprietary interest, who could not sue in his own right after Hunter v Canary Wharf. The substantial-and-unreasonable element is assessed objectively, by reference to an ordinary person in Farah’s position, not her own heightened sensitivity (Munro v Southern Dairies) — but nightly, loud engine noise until midnight for two years would likely be substantial and unreasonable even for an ordinary person, given its frequency, duration and timing. Dev, as the person carrying on the noisy activity on his own land, has created the state of affairs giving rise to the harm, satisfying the third element. Farah should therefore succeed, though she should frame her claim around the objective unreasonableness of nightly midnight noise rather than her own particular sensitivity, which the law disregards.
