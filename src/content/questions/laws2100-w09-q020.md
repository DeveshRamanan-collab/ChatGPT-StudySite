On the will theory a person has a right if they have control or choice over whether others must or must not act in certain ways; rights are tools for exercising free will (autonomy). An infant cannot exercise choice as adults can, so the theory seems to deny that infants have rights, which clashes with the idea of human rights as universal, belonging to all human beings. Against this, the will theory fits contractual rights well: a party can insist on or release the other side's duty. And the interest theory, which grounds rights in protecting important interests (welfare), explains infants' rights easily, so the debate is between families of theories, each capturing part of our practice. For human rights, which claim universality, the interest theory looks the better fit. Either way, the choice of theory should be stated, because it decides who can hold a right at all."
