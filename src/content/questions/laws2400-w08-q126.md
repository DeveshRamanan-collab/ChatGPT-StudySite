model: "The issue is whether a landlord owes a duty to an entrant who is not the tenant. Historically landlords owed no duty for defective premises (Cavalier v Pope), on the basis that the tenant in control could see defects, but the High Court rejected that rule in Northern Sandblasting v Harris. In Jones v Bartlett the High Court held that a landlord owes a duty; Gummow and Hayne JJ said the content of the duty to the tenant is that the premises be reasonably fit for habitation as a domestic residence: the landlord must not let premises with defects it knows or ought to know make them unsafe, and discharges the duty by taking reasonable steps to ascertain defects and to remove them or make the premises safe. The landlord also owes other entrants a duty, though narrower than an occupier's. Sam is a lawful visitor, so Ravi owes him a duty, narrower than the Nguyens' duty as occupiers. On breach: Ravi took no steps at all to ascertain defects, and a rail visibly rusted through is a defect he ought to have known about that made the premises unsafe. Unlike the glass door in Jones v Bartlett, this is a dangerous defect. Conclusion: a duty is owed, and it was very likely breached; the Nguyens, as occupiers, may also owe Sam a duty."
