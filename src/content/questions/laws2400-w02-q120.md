prompt: |
  P lends her camera to Q for the weekend. While Q has it, R — a stranger to both P and Q — grabs the camera out of Q's hands without permission, uses it to take a few photos, and then throws it into a bush, undamaged, before running off. Advise Q on a possible action in trespass to goods against R (ignore any other torts).
model: |
  Trespass to goods requires a direct interference with a chattel in the possession of the plaintiff at the time of the interference; because the action is in trespass, no proof of actual damage is generally required. Possession is distinct from ownership and requires an intention to exercise control and exclude others, plus actual control. Although P owns the camera, Q, as the borrower in physical custody of it for the weekend, has possession — an intention to control it and actual control over it, similar to the bailee's position in Penfolds Wines Pty Ltd v Elliott, where possession rather than ownership was decisive. R's grabbing of the camera from Q's hands is a direct interference with a chattel Q possessed at the time. Because trespass to goods does not generally require proof of damage, the fact the camera was undamaged does not prevent liability — the direct interference with Q's possession is enough on its own. Q therefore has a good claim in trespass to goods against R, notwithstanding that Q is not the owner of the camera.
