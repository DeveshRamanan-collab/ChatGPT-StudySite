model: |-
  The issue is whether Hana was contributorily negligent, so that her damages are reduced. A finding of
  contributory negligence results in a partial reduction in the damages otherwise recoverable, based on what is just and
  equitable having regard to her share in the responsibility for the damage (Law Reform (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act
  1965 (NSW) s 9(1)). Under CLA s 5R the principles of negligence apply to her own failure to take precautions: the
  standard is a reasonable person in her position, judged on what she knew or ought to have known at the time. A
  reasonable person crossing a busy road would check for traffic; Hana did not, and she ought to have known of the
  risk. The causal requirement is met: had she looked she would have waited, so her lack of care is causally linked to
  her injury (Caswell v Powell Duffryn Associated Collieries). On what is just and equitable, the court examines the
  whole conduct of each party and their relative blameworthiness (Pennington v Norris): Hana's inattention against
  Ollie's heavy speeding. Conclusion: Hana is likely to be found contributorily negligent and her damages partially
  reduced; the size of the reduction is for the court on that weighing. Volenti does not arise: nothing shows she
  accepted a specific risk.
