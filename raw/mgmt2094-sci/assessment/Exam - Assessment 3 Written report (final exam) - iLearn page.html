Completion requirements

This module gives you a quick rundown of the assessments you need to complete for this unit. You'll find the Assessment Guidelines, marking rubrics, and FAQs about the tasks.

Our purpose at MQBS is to provide inspiring and engaging business education and research that is useful to students throughout their careers and for solving society’s biggest problems.

Assessment 3: Written report

SUBMISSION DETAILS

WEIGHT (%): 30%

DUE DATE: Exam period

TASK DESCRIPTION: Written exam (Individual)

SUBMISSION METHOD: Turnitin

FEEDBACK MECHANISM(s): Rubric

FEEDBACK AVAILABLE: 2 weeks from submission

UNIT LEARNING OUTCOMES: ULO1, ULO3

ESTIMATED STUDENT WORKLOAD: 10 hours

ASSESSMENT TASK DESCRIPTION:

By completing this assessment, you will gain the following knowledge and skills:

Knowledge and skills in focus

Knowledge 

  • Basic knowledge of theories of innovation, why they are important, and how to apply them.
  • Systematic knowledge of a particular theory or theories.
  • You will consolidate core theories and concepts in creativity and innovation and demonstrate your ability to apply them to new organisational scenarios in a time-limited setting

Skills

  • Improve your writing and communication skills.
  • How to present your ideas about innovation and creativity.
  • You will develop exam-writing skills, including producing clear, well-structured arguments under time constraints, and translating theoretical concepts into actionable recommendations.

TASK DESCRIPTION

Assessment 3 is a final exam designed to assess your understanding of key creativity and innovation concepts covered across the unit. The exam emphasizes application: you will answer questions that require you to explain, compare, and apply innovation approaches in organisational contexts.

 

Submission Instructions:

You will follow the exam protocols.

LEARNING OUTCOMES:

This assessment supports the following unit learning outcomes:

    1. explain and apply key terms, concepts, frameworks and theories for strategy and innovation
    2. use appropriate language and structure to solve real-world management communication challenges.

    EXAM FORMAT AND CONDITIONS

    ·         Delivery mode: In-person or online invigilated exam, delivered via iLearn (if applicable).

    ·         Exam window: 10 minutes reading time (if applicable) + 120 minutes writing time + 30 minutes technical time (total window 2 hours 40 minutes).

    ·         Structure: You will answer 2 or 3 questions. One question will require an answer in essay style, and the other question(s) will require a short answer(s).

    ·         You must complete the exam individually. Collaboration, communication with others, or receiving assistance during the exam is not permitted.

    ·         Permitted materials: Closed book exam, no materials are permitted.

    ·         Academic integrity: Use of generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT) during the exam is prohibited.


    PREPARATION/INSTRUCTIONS:

    Before the exam, revise lecture material, tutorial activities, and required readings. Practice applying theories to unfamiliar scenarios rather than just memorising definitions. Details of the exam date/time and access link (if applicable) will be provided in on iLearn.

    Guidance

    ·         Answer concisely: define key terms, identify assumptions, and use examples to show understanding.

    ·         Justify your arguments using theory and evidence from examples.

    ·         Use subheadings and signposting (e.g., 'Theoretical justification', 'Implementation considerations', 'Risks/limitations').

    ·         Focus on depth rather than listing many concepts. Select the most relevant frameworks and apply them carefully.

    ·         Write in clear paragraphs. Avoid long quotations.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    ·         Describing innovation practices without linking them to course concepts or theories.

    ·         Answering with generic 'common sense' advice instead of theory-based reasoning.

    ·         Listing multiple approaches without explaining why one is more appropriate for the case.

    ·         Poor structure (no clear argument, no conclusion).


    MARKING CRITERIA & RUBRIC

    Refer to the following marking rubric:

    Assessment 3 – marking rubric

    Criterion

    Fail

    Pass

    Credit

    Distinction

    High Distinction

    1) Knowledge and understanding

    Demonstrates little understanding; major inaccuracies; key ideas missing or confused.

    Basic understanding with some accuracy; important gaps or simplifications.

    Sound understanding; mostly accurate; covers main ideas with some depth.

    Strong understanding; accurate and appropriately detailed; clear grasp of key distinctions.

    Exceptional understanding; precise, integrated grasp of ideas and distinctions; depth without unnecessary detail.

    2) Use of concepts and theory

    Concepts/theory absent, incorrect, or used inappropriately.

    Limited use; concepts applied loosely or descriptively.

    Appropriate use; concepts generally applied correctly; links to claims are present.

    Strong application; concepts/theory used to support and explain arguments clearly.

    Sophisticated application; concepts/theory are integrated seamlessly to develop compelling explanations.

    3) Analysis and judgment

    Little analysis; unsupported opinion; no evaluation or justification.

    Some analysis but largely general; limited justification or critical engagement.

    Clear reasoning with some evaluation; recognizes basic strengths/limits or conditions.

    Well-developed analysis; justified judgments; considers trade-offs, fit, or limitations.

    Highly analytical; nuanced judgment; explicitly considers alternatives, boundary conditions, and implications.

    4) Communication and response to task

    Off-task or incomplete; unclear and poorly structured.

    Addresses task at a basic level; structure and clarity are adequate but uneven.

    Addresses task well; mostly clear and organized; some synthesis.

    Strong, focused response; clear structure and coherent argument; effective synthesis.

    Outstandingly clear and persuasive; excellent structure and synthesis; highly focused and concise.

    USE OF RESOURCES & TECHNOLOGIES INC. GENERATIVE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE:

    For this assessment, students are prohibited to use GAITs 

    Please insert the following Acknowledgement Statement at the end of your assignment:

    “I acknowledge that I have not used GAITs (e. g., ChatGPT) in drafting and proofreading of this assignment.”
