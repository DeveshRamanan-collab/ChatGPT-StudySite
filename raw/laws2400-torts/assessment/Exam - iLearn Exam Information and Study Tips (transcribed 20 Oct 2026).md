## Assessment Overview

- The assessment will be marked out of 100 and will account for 50% of the final mark in this unit.
- The examinable topics are Topics 5 -12 .
- The exam consists of two (2) hypothetical questions: both questions will be worth 50% of the marks for the exam. Both questions must be answered. Both exam questions will ask you to assess the merits of a negligence claim in NSW - you will not be tested on course content relating to intentional torts.
- Support your answers with reference to the facts in the hypothetical questions and the applicable law.
- The law for the exam is to be drawn from the cases and legislation contained in a list provided with the exam paper. That list is also provided in the link on iLearn in the exam block.
- We strongly recommend you spend time preparing for the exam, including the preparation of your own notes (see tips below). We recommend organising your notes around the law included in the provided list. You will not have time during the exam to teach yourself the unit content and will likely have difficulty passing the exam if you are not adequately prepared.

## Referencing the law in the exam

- All references to the law (cases and legislation) should be in-text. Footnotes are not required.
- Use the following system for referencing cases and legislation:
  - **Case Law**: When citing a case for the first time, the full name of the case must be set out, but the rest of the citation is unnecessary. For example, for the decision in Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562, it is sufficient to cite it initially as 'Donoghue v Stevenson'. For subsequent references to that case, an abbreviated name is sufficient, for example, 'Donoghue'. Pinpoints for cases are not required.
  - **Legislation**: When citing legislative materials for the first time, the full name of the legislative material must be set out, for example, 'Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW)'. For subsequent references to that legislative material an abbreviated name is sufficient, for example, 'CLA'. Precise pinpoints to specific sections and subsections should be included as required, for example, 's 5B(1)(a) CLA'.

## Special Consideration

If you receive special consideration for the main exam, you will be granted permission to sit the supplementary exam at a time set centrally by the university (sometime in December). Please note that if you are then unable to sit the supplementary exam due to illness or misadventure, there will not be a second supplementary exam. In those circumstances, the remedy will be to sit the main invigilated exam in the next offering of this unit (ie session 2 2026).

## Assessment Standards

Grading for the final exam will be moderated against the following assessment standards (see MQU Assessment Procedure clauses 118-120):

- **High Distinction:** Provides a well-structured response, with no organisational or clarity issues. Demonstrates excellent identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with at most a single minor oversight that does not significantly impact the overall analysis. States applicable law accurately, completely and precisely with citations in accordance with instructions. Does not include irrelevant issues or law, demonstrating excellent discernment and focus. Applies the law to facts thoroughly and logically, addressing all relevant aspects.  Provides a clear, well-reasoned conclusion that follows logically from the analysis.
- **Distinction:** Provides a good structured response, with only minor organisational or clarity issues. Demonstrates strong identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with a few minor oversights that do not significantly impact the overall analysis. Mostly states applicable law accurately and in accordance with instructions with only minor omissions or errors in completeness or precision. May include an irrelevant issue or law but it does not undermine the response. Applies law to facts well, with only minor gaps in reasoning or analysis. Provides a sound conclusion that may include slight inconsistencies or lack of clarity.
- **Credit:** Provides adequate structure with some organisational or clarity issues present. Demonstrates adequate identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with some notable omissions or inaccuracies. States applicable law generally correctly but lacks some detail or precision. May include some irrelevant issues or laws, but not to the extent that significantly undermines the response. Satisfactorily applies applicable law to facts, but may contain moderate gaps in reasoning or analysis. Provides a reasonable conclusion but lacks some depth.
- **Pass:** Some attempt at structure but with organisational or clarity issues that impact the response to a significant extent. Identifies some relevant issues and/or sub-issues but misses key aspects of the problem. States some applicable law but with significant omissions or inaccuracies. Includes irrelevant issues or law to an extent that demonstrates a limited ability to discern importance. Attempts to apply law to facts but with significant logical gaps or misunderstandings. Provides a basic conclusion that doesn't fully align with the analysis.
- **Fail**: Lacks clear structure or is difficult to follow due to very poor organisation or writing. Fails to identify most of the relevant legal issues, demonstrating a lack of understanding of the core legal problems presented. Fails to state most of the applicable law or states them with significant inaccuracy. Includes a significant amount of irrelevant issues or law, as if reciting notes indiscriminately, significantly undermining the response. Fails to apply law to facts coherently or misapplies law significantly. Fails to provide a conclusion or provides one that doesn't follow from the analysis.
