2. Hypo 1 (short hypo):

   Peter was a maintenance linesman employed by State Rail. He was seriously injured at work when he fell down a railway embankment at night. He had been given a defective torch by his employer. He received workers’ compensation for some time then he was wrongfully cut off from workers comp by his employer. Peter worried about how to support his family and began to grow Indian hemp, intending to produce and sell marijuana. Before his personal injury trial, Peter was arrested, convicted and imprisoned for 9 months for cultivating Indian hemp. He wants to sue his employer for the imprisonment. **Advise Peter.**

6. Main Hypo:

   Lucy was late for work. She was running along the footpath when she came to a busy intersection that was controlled by traffic lights. She glanced quickly each way and, in spite of the don’t walk signal, thought she could beat the traffic as the lights had just changed. She failed to realise that a green arrow allowed oncoming traffic to turn right across her path. Fred started to turn right in accordance with the green arrow without first looking for pedestrians. He saw Lucy and stopped, slamming on his brakes and stopping short of Lucy. At this moment, Nancy, who was busy applying lipstick as she drove along a side street to the intersection, failed to notice the red light and drove into the intersection, colliding with Fred’s stationary vehicle and pushing his car into Lucy.

   Lucy suffered two broken legs and multiple cuts and abrasions in the accident but avoided more serious injuries as neither vehicle was speeding at the time of the accident. Before the ambulance arrived, and in the commotion, a nearby youth grabbed Lucy’s handbag and ran off. Lucy was unconscious when she was rushed by the ambulance to the North West Hospital.

   When Lucy was admitted to the emergency ward of the hospital, she was seen by Neville, a nurse, who disinfected and bandaged her cuts and abrasions in accordance with accepted practice, and her wounds healed without complication.

   Dr Sun, an employed surgeon working at the Hospital, then treated Lucy’s broken legs. Instead of putting her legs in plaster, which would have been the usual treatment given the nature of Lucy’s fractures, and which would have probably allowed Lucy’s legs to heal without any residual disability, Dr Sun was anxious to try a new technique of manipulation he had developed. This technique had never been used before in Australia. The technique was unsuccessful as it left Lucy with a permanent ankle disability in both legs. This so restricted her ability to engage in kickboxing that she gave up the sport. She started drinking heavily, which caused her to lose her job as an air traffic controller. Lucy now spends her days watching old martial arts films.

   **Assuming the Hospital is vicariously liable for the negligence of Dr Sun, advise Lucy of all possible actions available to her in negligence. Include in your answer reference to the relevant cases. Address duty, breach and causation.**
