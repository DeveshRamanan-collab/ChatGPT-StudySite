## Questions

Q1: Garling J in _Benic v NSW_ [2010] NSWSC 1039 usefully laid down steps to be followed in determining the breach question under the CLA. His Honour explained an initial step must be taken before going to the CLA provision on breach (s5B). Explain the initial step.

Q2: How do we understand foreseeability when assessing breach of duty? (i.e. CLA 5B(1)(a))

Q3: What does it mean if a risk is described as not-insignificant? (i.e. CLA 5B(1)(b))

Q4: How do we understand CLA 5B(1)(c), which tells us that a person is not negligent in failing to take precautions against a risk of harm unless ‘in the circumstances, a reasonable person in the person's position would have taken those precautions’?

Q5: What is a risk described as obvious or inherent?

## Hypothetical

Ariel went to the local aquatic centre to celebrate her 9th birthday. The centre has an exciting waterslide that is high in the air and loops around several times before the riders pop out into the exit pool at the bottom. The waterslide tube is fully enclosed, so once you step up into the entry port, you can’t see anything other than the dark tube in front of you. Industry standards for water parks say that a staff member must stand at the top of the slide and only allow riders onto the slide once the previous rider has left the exit pool at the bottom. This is to prevent against the risk that if one rider enters the slide before the previous rider has left the exit pool at the bottom, the two riders may collide and cause injury.

Medusa is a 16-year-old staff member who works at the aquatic centre. Medusa’s job is to stand at the top of the slide and watch for the riders to exit the slide, and to ensure the next rider doesn’t enter the slide until it is safe in accordance with the industry standards. There was a sign on the ride that said, ‘All riders must only enter the slide once staff tells them it is safe to do so, otherwise you may collide with the previous rider and injury could occur’. Ariel waited in line patiently for her turn on the slide and when it was her turn, she sat down at the entry to the slide and waited for directions. Medusa eventually said to Ariel, ‘it is safe to go now’, so Ariel pushed off into the slide.

Medusa hadn’t been watching properly. There was another rider still in the slide and Ariel hit the other rider headfirst and several of her teeth fell out, requiring several painful and costly surgeries and implants.

Ariel sues the aquatic centre for negligence. Focusing on the principles discussed in this week’s material, how would the court assess whether there has been a breach of duty? It is not necessary to consider in detail how the court would deal with any defences that arise in the CLA in relation to this potentially being a case of an obvious risk or a recreational activity (as we will learn about them further in a few weeks).
