# PART 1 — QUESTION 1: Can a general, descriptive theory of law be value-free?

## Q1-A — "Value-laden but morally neutral: Hart's claim, correctly stated"

**Thesis.** Not value-free, but morally neutral — and moral neutrality is all Hart claimed (*CL* 239–40: "morally neutral", "no justificatory aims"). Grant Finnis's selection objection in full; show that judgments of significance are evaluative without being moral; supply a *principled* criterion (participants' self-understandings + meta-theoretic virtues, falsifiable without moral premises; moral appraisal "staged", not postponed — Dickson 2015/2022).

**Refinement from the sweep.** Do not claim a single crisp criterion; the literature offers a two-part test. Strengthen premise (1) with Hart's own concessions (Sugarman interview 2005; Gavison 'Comment' 1987 if print-checked). **Most dangerous objection:** Priel 2010 / Pojanowski 2021 / Edmundson 2013 — participants' self-understandings are already morally loaded (law *claims* authority), so "importance to participants" either smuggles in moral evaluation or collapses into naturalistic social science. Answer this, not Finnis's cruder version.

## Q1-B — "The value hides in 'general', not in 'descriptive'"

**Thesis.** Hart's two coordinate claims — general and descriptive — have been audited asymmetrically. Generality is itself a value-laden achievement: the theory universalises a parochial (modern Western municipal) starting point, and its background assumptions about what counts as a *defect* in a pre-legal order (uncertainty, static character, inefficiency — *CL* ch V) are evaluations. A theory that smuggles a value into its starting point cannot be morally neutral downstream.

**Refinement from the sweep.** Tighten premise (5): the ch V defect-ascriptions are not mere "importance" judgments (which Raz, Dickson and Marmor concede are evaluative but not moral); they are substantive political valuations — certainty, adaptability and efficient enforcement are goods a society *ought* to want. Note that Q1 asks "value-free", which is wider than "morally neutral": Marmor's own concession that importance-judgments are "partly normative" (2006, 699 — single-sourced) already defeats value-freedom. **Most dangerous objection:** Raz 2005 / Dickson 2015 (TLT) / Marmor 2006 — parochialism of the *concept* is harmless because the *theory* concerns law wherever found, and functional/defect claims can be stated in neutral functionalist terms (Ehrenberg 2013). Answer: Ho 2018 and Kirkby 2018 show "uncertainty as defect" is a culturally local valuation; Green 1996 shows Hart locates the defects "in us".

## Q1-C — "The stalemate is the evidence: the question's object does not exist as assumed"

**Thesis.** The methodology debate's irresolution is data. ## Q2-A — "The two limbs are not exhaustive, and the second is a caricature"

**Thesis.** "Essential to counting as law" and "merely makes law more effective" are not contradictories. A condition can be constitutive without being moral (Raz's "virtue of the instrument as an instrument"). Hart never denied the principles' moral value; the poisoner objects to the *label* "morality" (provenance). Fuller's p 39 concerns *total* failure of a *system*; Hart's 207 concerns *content*; they are consistent. Conclusion: minimum respect is constitutive; its moral status is a separate question, tested against Kramer.

## Q2-B — "'Counts as law' does not take a yes-or-no answer"

**Thesis.** Both limbs presuppose that legality is binary. ## Q2-C — "Law is a reciprocal practice, which is why the poisoner and the instrument analogies do not transfer"

**Thesis.** Hart's poisoner and Raz's instrument model law as something done *to* subjects. Fuller models it as a relationship *with* them (reciprocity 39–40; agency 162). Purposive activity with an *addressee* who must be able to follow the rules differs structurally from purposive activity without one; a cooperative practice has internal goods (MacIntyre) the instrumental model cannot capture. A regime that abandons the principles has not become an inefficient ruler; it has stopped addressing anyone. Limit case: Rundle's exterminatory legality. So minimum respect is constitutive — and constitutive *because* of the moral feature the analogies deny. Optional hinge: the grudge-informer case was misreported by Hart (Pappe 1960; Dyzenhaus 2008).

**Refinement from the sweep.** Narrow the target: Bennett 2011 shows Hart and Raz denied that legality is *constitutive/intrinsic*, not that it has moral value — so premise (1) should say the analogies model law as a technique whose success conditions are indifferent to whether anyone is addressed. Do not lean on MacIntyre alone: Retter concedes reciprocity is *necessary* only if grounded in a political common good, which pushes past "minimum respect". Keep the claim structural (an addressee-directed practice has success conditions that are themselves respect-conditions). **Most dangerous objection — Kramer:** a self-interested tyrant has prudential reasons to satisfy all eight principles, so reciprocity buys no morality. Answer via Simmonds 2004 and C Murphy 2005 (motive does not change the *kind* of relationship compliance creates) and Rundle 2009 (the limit where the relationship is abandoned).
