## Exam

Exam Schedule: Visit the MQ Exam Timetable for the schedule of all your exams here.

Unit Weighting: 50%

The exam will be an invigilated hand-written exam taking place in-person on campus. Exam duration will be 2.5 hours plus 15 minutes reading time. Students will be allowed to take into the exam 10 A4 pieces of paper. What is included on that paper is up to each student. The content can be typed or handwritten, and can include content on both sides of each piece of paper. No additional resources are permitted in the exam and all notes will be collected at the conclusion of the exam.

The university sets the timetable centrally. For further information on Exams, including FAQ please visit: https://students.mq.edu.au/study/assessment-exams/exams

### Examinable topics

All topics covered in the unit are potentially examinable in the exam:

- The sources of international law.
- The law of treaties.
- Integration of international law in national law.
- The subjects of international law.
- Jurisdiction.
- Immunities.
- State responsibility.
- International dispute settlement.
- Use of force.
- Collective security.
- International criminal law.
- Human rights law

This does not mean that all topics in this list will be examined in the exam, but any of them could be included.

Assessable content includes material covered in lectures, tutorials and required readings.

### Referencing

Referencing treaties/ILC (draft) articles in the exam:

- All references to treaties/ILC (draft) articles are to be in-text. Footnotes are not required.
- Only refer to treaties/ILC (draft) articles that have been covered in the unit.
- When citing treaties/ILC (draft) articles for the first time, the full name of the item must be set out, for example, 'Charter of the United Nations'. For subsequent references to that item, an abbreviated name is sufficient, for example, 'UN Charter'. Pinpoints to specific articles, etc., should be included as required, for example, 'art 2(4) UN Charter'.

Referencing subsidiary sources in the exam:

- All references to subsidiary sources are to be in-text. Footnotes are not required.
- Only refer to subsidiary sources that have been covered in the unit.
- Scholarly writings: When citing scholarly writings, use the 'author (date)' convention. For example, if referencing the book, John Rawls, *Political Liberalism* (Columbia University Press, 2005), it should be cited as 'Rawls (2005)'. Pinpoints for secondary sources are not required.
- When citing a decision/advisory opinion of a court/arbitral body/tribunal for the first time, please provide both the name of the court/arbitral body/tribunal and the abbreviated name of the decision/opinion. The rest of the citation is unnecessary. For example, for *Right of Passage over Indian Territory (Portugal v India) (Merits)* [1960] ICJ Rep 6, it is sufficient to cite it as, e.g., 'the ICJ's *Right of Passage* case'. Pinpoints are not required. For subsequent references to the decision/advisory opinion, an abbreviated name is sufficient, for example, '*Right of Passage*'.

## Exam Information - Assessment Standards

Grading for the final exam will be moderated against the following assessment standards:

- **High Distinction:** Provides a well-structured response, with no organisational or clarity issues. Demonstrates excellent identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with at most a single minor oversight that does not significantly impact the overall analysis. States applicable law accurately, completely and precisely with citations in accordance with instructions. Does not include irrelevant issues or law, demonstrating excellent discernment and focus. Where applicable, applies the law to facts thoroughly and logically, addressing all relevant aspects.  Provides a clear, well-reasoned conclusion that follows logically from the analysis.
- **Distinction:** Provides a good structured response, with only minor organisational or clarity issues. Demonstrates strong identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with a few minor oversights that do not significantly impact the overall analysis. Mostly states applicable law accurately and in accordance with instructions with only minor omissions or errors in completeness or precision. May include an irrelevant issue or law but it does not undermine the response. Where applicable, applies law to facts well, with only minor gaps in reasoning or analysis. Provides a sound conclusion that may include slight inconsistencies or lack of clarity.
- **Credit:** Provides adequate structure with some organisational or clarity issues present. Demonstrates adequate identification of relevant legal issues and sub-issues, with some notable omissions or inaccuracies. States applicable law generally correctly but lacks some detail or precision. May include some irrelevant issues or laws, but not to the extent that significantly undermines the response. Where applicable, satisfactorily applies applicable law to facts, but may contain moderate gaps in reasoning or analysis. Provides a reasonable conclusion but lacks some depth.
- **Pass:** Some attempt at structure but with organisational or clarity issues that impact the response to a significant extent. Identifies some relevant issues and/or sub-issues but misses key aspects of the problem. States some applicable law but with significant omissions or inaccuracies. Includes irrelevant issues or law to an extent that demonstrates a limited ability to discern importance. Where applicable, attempts to apply law to facts but with significant logical gaps or misunderstandings. Provides a basic conclusion that doesn't fully align with the analysis.
- **Fail**: Lacks clear structure or is difficult to follow due to very poor organisation or writing. Fails to identify most of the relevant legal issues, demonstrating a lack of understanding of the core legal problems presented. Fails to state most of the applicable law or states them with significant inaccuracy. Includes a significant amount of irrelevant issues or law, as if reciting notes indiscriminately, significantly undermining the response. Where applicable, fails to apply law to facts coherently or misapplies law significantly. Fails to provide a conclusion or provides one that doesn't follow from the analysis.
